Take a course only if it makes you audit a real site; skip any program whose main selling point is the certificate. AI SEO (also called GEO or AEO) is too new for a credential to prove skill, and the good material is free. This page lists what a course must cover, four you can check today, and a self-study path. We have not taken these courses: everything about them is documentation-based, from each provider's public course pages, checked 3 October 2026.

## Should you take an AI SEO course?

Yes, if it saves you weeks of reading and forces practice. No, if you are buying a badge. The useful part of a course is a sequenced syllabus and a deadline. The useless part is the PDF at the end.

Start from what Google itself says. Its guide to generative AI features states that [SEO best practices still apply](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) because AI features are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems. If you already know technical SEO, you need a short course on what is new, not a long one on what you know.

That is our position: pay for coaching or feedback if you want it, never for information. Most of the information is public.

## What should an AI SEO curriculum cover?

A real curriculum teaches how engines pick sources, how crawlers reach your pages, how to measure answers, and makes you practice on a live site. If a syllabus is mostly prompt templates, you are buying a prompt course with an SEO label.

Six topics to demand:

1. **Citation mechanics.** How engines retrieve and choose sources, with the research behind it. The [GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) reported that some content strategies boosted visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses, and that results varied by domain. A course that quotes the 40% without the domain caveat has not read it.
2. **Crawler access.** The difference between training bots and search bots. OpenAI documents that [OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot are independent settings](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots): you can appear in ChatGPT search while opting out of training. Perplexity documents [PerplexityBot](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) as a search crawler that is not used for foundation model training.
3. **Content structure.** Answer-first sections, clear entities, honest schema. See [what AEO is and how to start](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization).
4. **Measurement.** Sampling prompts across engines, repeating runs, counting mentions. Our [guide to measuring AI visibility](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility) shows the metrics.
5. **Auditing.** A repeatable pass over a real site, like our [AEO audit checklist](/blog/aeo-audit-checklist).
6. **Limits.** What does not work. Google says you [do not need llms.txt, special markup or content chunking](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) for its AI features.

Two red flags: a course that promises guaranteed citations, and one that teaches llms.txt as a required step. We publish an llms.txt ourselves and still [call it overrated](/blog/llms-txt-is-overrated); we keep it because it costs almost nothing.

## Which AI SEO courses exist, and what do they cost?

Four options are verifiable today: HubSpot Academy, Ahrefs and Semrush offer free AEO or AI search courses, and Coursera hosts a nine-hour GEO course that is free to enroll in but needs paid access for the certificate. Details below, as of October 2026.

| Course | Provider | Cost | Length | Credential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [AEO Fundamentals Certification](https://offers.hubspot.com/academy-aeo-certifications) | HubSpot Academy | Free | Not stated on the page | Certification; "AEO Dual Certified" badge if you also complete the HubSpot-specific one |
| [Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) Course](https://ahrefs.com/academy) | Ahrefs Academy | Free | 12 lessons, 1 hr 26 min | No certificate shown on the page |
| [AI Search Operating System](https://us.semrush.com/academy/courses) | Semrush Academy | Free | 10 lessons, 41 min | Certification listed |
| [Introduction to Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/introduction-to-generative-engine-optimization-geo) | Edureka on Coursera | Free to enroll; certificate needs paid access or Coursera Plus (price not shown on the page) | 5 modules, about 9 hours, beginner | Shareable certificate, AI-graded assessments |

How to read the table:

- **HubSpot's** second certification, Implementing AEO With HubSpot, is a software course for people using HubSpot's AEO tool. Take the first one; the second only if you use the product.
- **Ahrefs and Semrush** are tool vendors that also sell AI visibility products (Ahrefs lists a separate Brand Radar course). Expect examples built around their tools.
- **Coursera's** course includes a live GEO audit project in module 4, which is the most valuable part. Our caution: AI-graded quizzes test recall, not results.

Udemy and other marketplaces also list GEO courses. Prices and ratings there change often and we could not verify them, so check the syllabus and recent reviews yourself.

## Is a GEO certification worth it?

No, not yet, as a career signal. Every certification above is issued by the company teaching the course, there is no shared syllabus between them, and we found no independent body that audits them. A certificate shows you finished; a client wants proof you moved a number.

Compare a product certification: it proves you can operate one tool, and the company that owns the tool stands behind it. A GEO badge implies skill across a whole discipline that the issuer does not own.

Our view on when it still makes sense:

- **A free certificate** is fine as a structured way to finish a syllabus. It costs nothing but time.
- **A paid certificate** is hard to justify. If the course is free to enroll in, as Coursera's is, the fee mostly buys the certificate.
- **For hiring**, a one-page case beats any badge: the prompts you tested, what you changed, what moved.

## What is the free self-study path?

Read the primary sources, then run one audit-change-remeasure loop on a site you control. The reading takes a weekend; the loop takes longer, because engines need time to re-crawl before the second measurement means anything. OpenAI says robots.txt changes can take about 24 hours to reach its search systems; content changes can take longer.

1. **Read the rulebook.** Google's [generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) and its AI features documentation, then the [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) and [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) crawler pages.
2. **Read the one academic paper.** The [GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) is short, and its tactics are testable.
3. **Study before-and-afters.** Our [GEO examples](/blog/geo-examples) show seven patterns.
4. **Baseline.** Ask the same 10 to 30 buyer questions in each engine, and record who is named. Our [AEO audit checklist](/blog/aeo-audit-checklist) gives the protocol. A free first pass is the [AEOeye audit](/), which asks 3 buyer questions to ChatGPT and shows the answers in full.
5. **Fix access first.** Check robots.txt and your firewall logs before touching content. The [AI robots.txt generator](/tools/ai-robots-txt-generator) writes the file.
6. **Change one thing, wait, re-measure** with the identical prompts, and write down the delta.

## What should you check first, whatever you learn?

Check whether AI crawlers can reach you. It is the cheapest fix and the one courses most often rush past. In AEOeye's crawler access study (October 2026, 384 sites, a convenience sample skewed toward established brands), 1 of 193 SaaS sites blocked any AI retrieval bot, against 27 of 38 news publishers. The [study hub](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory) has the data.

So for most brands the blocker is not robots.txt. It is absence from the pages AI engines read. But a CDN or firewall rule can still block bots silently: 24 of 88 e-commerce and consumer homepages (27.3%) in our study challenged our self-identified research crawler. Look at your logs.

Then measure from the engine side. Google's [generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) in Search Console shows impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode; it rolled out to all websites worldwide on 31 August 2026. It covers Google only, so you still need sampled prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity.

Our own practice is the same loop. aeoeye.com allows GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot and Applebot-Extended, and on 3 October 2026 we merged 165 pages into 42 because one strong page per question beats five thin ones. We did that after reading our own Search Console data, not after a course.
