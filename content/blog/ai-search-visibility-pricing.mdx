Paid credits never expire, and each re-audit uses another credit. On the listed pack prices, the per-audit arithmetic is $0.50, $0.38, and $0.245 respectively; the cheapest unit rate is not automatically the best fit if it leaves unused credits or mismatches your workload.

This is a documentation-based buyer guide based only on the public [pricing page](https://www.aisearchvisibility.ai/pricing) and homepage. No paid account was used and no audit output, coverage, or accuracy was tested. The plan details below describe published pricing and credit rules, not independently verified outcomes.

## How much does AI Search Visibility cost?

The published pricing uses one-time credit packs with no subscription: free provides 5 limited audits, Starter costs $5 for 10 audits, Growth costs $19 for 50, and Agency costs $49 for 200. The homepage and pricing page describe credits as never expiring.

| Option | Price | Audits | Simple price per audit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Free | $0 | 5 limited | $0 |
| Starter | $5 | 10 | $0.50 |
| Growth | $19 | 50 | $0.38 |
| Agency | $49 | 200 | $0.245 |

The per-audit figures are pack price divided by the audit count. They do not establish audit scope or whether results are comparable across runs. The free plan is described as limited, so its five audits should not be assumed identical to paid audits.

Because there is no subscription and paid credits never expire, the buying decision is primarily about expected volume and when you need it. That makes the service structurally different from a recurring monitoring platform with ongoing prompt runs. ## How do audit packs, expiry, and re-audits work?

Each pack buys a fixed number of audits, paid credits do not expire, and a re-audit consumes another credit. The credit model lets a team purchase capacity in advance without a monthly subscription commitment, while the re-audit rule means repeat checks count toward usage.

“Credits never expire” reduces pressure to use the full pack within a billing month, but it does not mean an audit can be repeated for free. If a page is checked, changed, and checked again, the second audit uses another credit according to the published rule. Include planned re-checks in the volume estimate, especially when auditing many pages or revisiting work after content changes.

The public pricing page does not say whether credits can be shared across users or workspaces, whether a cancelled account retains them, or what happens if an audit fails. Those are not details to infer from non-expiring credits. If they affect procurement, verify them before purchase and keep the confirmation with the order record.

The most useful measure of cost is therefore not only the pack’s unit price. Estimate the number of unique pages, the number of rounds per page, and how frequently changes will trigger another check. ## How should you estimate the number of audits?

Estimate audits as pages multiplied by the number of audit rounds per page, because each re-audit uses another credit. This simple model exposes the difference between an initial one-time review and a continuing optimization cycle.

For example, auditing 40 pages twice means **40 × 2 = 80 audits**. One Growth pack supplies 50, leaving a 30-audit shortfall. A Growth pack plus three Starter packs provides 80 audits total and costs **$19 + (3 × $5) = $34**. One Agency pack supplies 200 audits for $49, leaving **120** for later work. Both options cover the planned 80 audits based on the published pack sizes, but their tradeoffs differ: the smaller combination costs less now, while Agency leaves a larger unused balance for future audits.

This scenario assumes every page-round consumes exactly one audit and that packs can be combined as described. Confirm at checkout that the purchase flow permits that combination and that the scope of one audit matches the page-level unit in your estimate. The scenario does not account for any extra work, fees, or output limits not described in the public facts.

For a smaller workload, 10 audits cost $5 through Starter. That could cover 10 pages once, or five pages twice, under the same per-page assumption. For a larger project, Agency’s unit calculation is lowest among the paid packs, but buying 200 audits may still be a poor choice if the team expects to use only a small fraction. Budget against likely use, not a headline unit price.

## Which plan fits your audit workload?

Choose the pack that covers your expected audit rounds while keeping the total spend proportionate to likely use. The lowest per-audit price is a useful comparison, not a complete decision rule.

Free is a low-commitment way to access five limited audits, but do not assume it supports a full review without checking what “limited” means. Starter’s 10 audits for $5 suit a small batch or a short initial review. Growth’s 50 audits for $19 fits a more substantial batch and lowers the simple unit price to $0.38. Agency’s 200 audits for $49 has the lowest listed paid unit cost at $0.245 and may suit a larger site or repeated review cycle.

If the expected workload is around 80 audits, the worked example shows why pack choice depends on future demand: $34 covers the immediate estimate with a Growth pack plus three Starter packs, while $49 buys 200 audits and leaves 120. If those remaining audits will likely be used later, Agency can be reasonable. If not, the apparent savings per audit may not compensate for paying $15 more upfront.

This pricing model is best evaluated alongside the job itself. A one-time technical or content review is different from tracking whether a brand is mentioned, cited, or recommended across AI answers over time. ## What should you verify before buying?

Verify the meaning and limits of an audit, the treatment of credits, and any terms not stated publicly. The published pack prices and non-expiring-credit statement are useful, but they do not answer every operational question.

Before checkout, confirm:

- What counts as one audit and what the free plan’s “limited” audits exclude.
- Whether a re-audit always consumes one full credit, including after edits or an interrupted run.
- Whether credits can be combined across packs, users, or workspaces.
- Whether unused credits remain available if account access or a paid plan changes.
- Which payment, refund, or tax terms apply at checkout.

Then calculate a realistic workload: number of pages, first-pass audits, and anticipated re-audits. Compare the resulting total with available pack sizes. If repeat review is central to the project, include it from the start rather than treating the first batch as the entire requirement. A no-subscription model is useful when work is episodic; it should not be confused with an ongoing monitoring subscription or an unlimited audit promise.
