AI usually skips a brand for one of five reasons, and most of them are not on your website. The engine can't reach or index your pages, it can't tell what category you belong to, nobody independent names you, your own pages bury the facts, or the facts it holds are stale.

Our position: it is usually the rest of the web, not your site. Each cause below has a test that takes minutes.

## Why doesn't AI mention my brand?

An engine names a brand only if it can reach it, identify it and find it corroborated elsewhere. A failure at any of those three steps leaves you out of the answer. We rank causes by how likely they are and how cheap they are to rule out:

1. **Access:** the engine can't fetch or index your pages.
2. **Identity:** it can't tell what you are or who you're for.
3. **Corroboration:** nobody independent writes about you.
4. **Clarity:** your own pages don't state the facts plainly.
5. **Freshness:** the facts it holds are old or conflicting.

Engines answer from two places: what the model absorbed in training, and, when search is on, pages fetched live through [retrieval-augmented generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation) (RAG). Access problems hit the live layer. Identity and corroboration problems hit both.

One caution: answers vary. [SparkToro's study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) of 2,961 runs (November to December 2025) found under a 1-in-100 chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI returns the same brand list twice. Ask every question several times before you conclude anything.

## Is something blocking AI engines from reading my site? (Test this first)

Check access first: it is the cheapest cause to rule out and the only one where a single setting can make you invisible overnight. For live answers, three things must hold. The engine's search crawler may fetch you, your firewall lets it through, and your page is indexed.

- **robots.txt names the right bot.** OpenAI says [OAI-SearchBot is for search and GPTBot for training, and each setting is independent](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots). Anthropic says [disabling Claude-SearchBot may reduce your visibility in Claude's search results](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler). Perplexity says [PerplexityBot surfaces sites in its results and is not used for foundation-model training](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots).
- **A broken robots.txt blocks everything.** Under [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309), a crawler that can't retrieve robots.txt because of a server error must assume complete disallow.
- **Your firewall lets the bots in.** Perplexity notes that sites behind a web application firewall [may need to allowlist its bots explicitly](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots), and OpenAI recommends allowing its published IP ranges. Cloudflare announced on 1 July 2025 that it was [changing its default to block AI crawlers](https://blog.cloudflare.com/content-independence-day-no-ai-crawl-without-compensation/), so a setting you never touched may have changed.
- **The page is indexed.** Google says a page must be [indexed and eligible to show with a snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Bing Webmaster Tools added an [AI Performance report](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) in February 2026 that counts citations in Copilot and Bing's AI summaries.
- **Key facts are in the HTML.** A December 2024 [Vercel analysis](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler) found OpenAI and Anthropic crawlers fetch JavaScript files but don't execute them, so client-side-only pricing or product text may be invisible to them.

In AEOeye's crawler access study (October 2026), only 1 of 193 SaaS sites and 4 of 90 e-commerce and consumer brands blocked any AI retrieval bot, against 27 of 38 news publishers. For a brand, robots.txt is rarely the culprit. The firewall might be: 24 of 88 e-commerce homepages (27.3%) blocked or challenged our self-identified research crawler.

That is our crawler, not OpenAI's, on a convenience sample ([per-site data](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv), [bot directory and method](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory)). But it is the failure robots.txt hides. Search your WAF or CDN logs for OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot and look for 403 and 429 responses.

## Why does AI recommend my competitors instead of me?

Because independent sources name them more often than they name you. A 2025 study of AI search by Chen and colleagues found [a systematic and overwhelming bias towards earned media over brand-owned and social content](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919), a stark contrast to Google's mix. 

In AEOeye's recommendation benchmark (127 audits, 892 engine answers, June to September 2026), the brand was named in 17.6% of answers. Answers listed 2.9 competitors on average, and a ranked brand was first in 71.4% of 147 cases. Being named at all is the hard part. The sample is small and self-selected, so we don't compare engines with it ([method](/blog/ai-brand-recommendation-measurement)).

What moves it, in our view:

- **Get into the lists your buyer's query already retrieves.** Find the "best X for Y" roundups and pitch their authors with something real.
- **Earn reviews and community mentions** on the platforms in your category, honestly, not astroturfed.
- **Win narrow questions, not the head term.** You rarely beat an incumbent on "best CRM", but you can own "best CRM for solo real-estate agents".

Fifty more posts on your own blog is the wrong fix. See how [engines choose which brands to recommend](/blog/how-ai-assistants-choose-brands) and why [brand mentions beat backlinks in AI search](/blog/brand-mentions).

## Is my category or brand name ambiguous to AI?

Probably, if you describe yourself in invented terms or share a name with something else. Engines must tie each mention to one entity, and as Wikipedia notes, [entity names are often homonyms or polysemous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity_linking). "Growth platform" files you nowhere; "email marketing software for online stores" files you somewhere.

Test it in three fresh chats: ask "What is [your brand]?" and "What does [your brand] do?" Then read your homepage as a stranger. If you can't state your category and customer in one sentence, neither can the model.

Fix it in plain words:

1. Put the literal category and audience in the first screen of your homepage and About page.
2. Add Organization markup with [sameAs](https://schema.org/sameAs) links to your official profiles. Google says Organization markup can [help disambiguate your organization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization), but doesn't guarantee any feature.
3. Repeat the same category wording on every profile you control.

In our study 70.2% of 312 fetched homepages carried Organization schema and 60.9% carried sameAs links, so markup is table stakes, not an edge.

## Why does AI get facts about my brand wrong?

Because it reads old or conflicting copies of your facts. Wikipedia notes that when sources conflict, RAG systems may [merge outdated and updated details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation) into one misleading answer. Your pricing page says one thing, a review site and a 2023 press release say others.

The fix is dull and decisive:

- Publish one canonical, dated page for pricing, plans, location and category, in plain HTML.
- Update every third-party profile you control to match it.
- Redirect retired product names.

Our step-by-step method is in [how to fix AI getting your brand wrong](/guides/how-to-fix-ai-getting-your-brand-wrong).

## How do I diagnose which reason applies in 15 minutes?

Match what you see to the table, run the test, and fix only the row that fits. Most brands have one dominant cause.

| Symptom | Likely cause | 5-minute test | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Absent even when asked by name | Access | robots.txt, WAF logs, index status | Allow search bots, fix indexing |
| Confused with another company | Identity | "What is [brand]?" in three fresh chats | Literal category, Organization + sameAs |
| Known by name, absent from "best X for Y" | Corroboration | Search "[brand]" -site:yourdomain.com | Earn list, review and community mentions |
| Named, but price or features wrong | Freshness | Compare answer with your pricing page | One canonical page, update profiles |
| Right in some runs, missing in others | Variance | Repeat each question five times | Track the rate, not one run |

## In what order should I fix things?

Fix access first, then identity, then facts, then your own pages, then the slow work of independent mentions. Nothing else matters if engines can't fetch you.

1. **Access (an afternoon).** Allow the search bots, fix the firewall, confirm indexing. OpenAI says robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to be picked up for search.
2. **Identity (a few days).** Literal category, Organization markup, consistent profiles.
3. **Facts (a few days).** One canonical page, third-party profiles aligned.
4. **Your pages (weeks).** Lead with the answer, keep facts in HTML. Google says [no special markup is needed](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) for AI features, so don't chase tricks.
5. **Independent mentions (months).** Slowest and decisive. We have no measured timeline, so distrust anyone who sells one.

We practice the clarity step ourselves. On 3 October 2026 we merged 165 pages into 42, because one strong page per question beats five thin ones for Google and for AI retrieval.
