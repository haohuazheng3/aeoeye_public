Mailchimp pricing is easiest to forecast when you separate three drivers: stored contacts, monthly email sends, and optional channels such as SMS. The headline plan price is only the starting point. As your list grows, duplicate contacts, send frequency, overages, promotional pricing, and add-ons can change the real monthly cost.

For the current US display checked September 17, 2026, Mailchimp shows a free option below 250 contacts, Essentials starting at $13 per month, Standard starting at $20 per month under the shown promotion, and Premium at $350 per month for 12 months at the displayed selection. Treat those figures as starting points, not permanent quotes: Mailchimp says pricing varies by contact and send limits, promotions, taxes, and account selections. Check the [current Marketing pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) before purchasing.

## What should you budget for Mailchimp?

Budget Mailchimp by your highest billable contact count and your expected monthly send volume, then add a cushion for growth and overages. A small newsletter may remain free or fit Essentials, while a growing marketing program can move into Standard pricing before the team feels “large.”

The most important question is not “Which plan is cheapest today?” It is “Which plan will still fit when our list and campaign calendar are 30–90 days larger?” A low introductory price can be useful, but it should not become your long-term forecast unless you know when the promotion ends.

## How does Mailchimp count contacts?

Mailchimp charges based on the contacts associated with your account, and the same person can count multiple times when stored in multiple audiences. This is one of the easiest ways for a seemingly modest list to create a larger bill.

For example, imagine 2,000 people split across two audiences. If some subscribers appear in both, your billable contact count may be higher than the number of unique people in your CRM. A cleanup that removes duplicates, archives inactive records, or consolidates audiences can reduce cost without reducing your actual reach.

Before forecasting, answer these questions:

1. How many contacts are stored, not merely subscribed?
2. Are unsubscribed or inactive records still retained?
3. Does one person appear in multiple audiences?
4. Are separate lists being used where tags or segments would be enough?
5. How quickly will the contact database grow each month?

Do not wait until the invoice rises to inspect this. Export or review your audience structure, identify duplicates, and define a retention policy for inactive contacts. Mailchimp’s [pricing-change and pause guidance](https://mailchimp.com/help/change-or-pause-your-pricing-plan/) is useful when deciding how account changes affect billing.

## How does sending frequency change the cost?

Your monthly send pattern matters because Mailchimp plans include contact and send limits, and overages can apply when you exceed them. A weekly newsletter, a daily digest, and a newsletter plus several automated sequences can represent very different usage even with the same audience size.

Use this simple planning formula:

`monthly email sends ≈ recipients per campaign × campaigns per month`

Then add automated emails, resends, onboarding sequences, and transactional messages where applicable. If 1,000 recipients receive four campaigns, that is approximately 4,000 recipient sends before accounting for automation or other messages.

Three practical habits make the forecast more accurate:

- Estimate your busiest month, not your quietest month.
- Include automated journeys and resend campaigns.
- Leave room for launches, seasonal promotions, and testing.

A plan that fits ordinary months may generate overages during a product launch. That does not automatically make Mailchimp poor value; it means your budget should distinguish predictable subscription cost from variable usage cost.

## What is excluded from the headline price?

SMS is separate from the email plan and uses a credit-based add-on. If you plan to send text messages, forecast SMS credits independently from email contacts and sends. Email pricing alone will understate the cost of a cross-channel campaign.

Transactional email also deserves a separate review. Marketing plans are designed around campaigns, audiences, and automation. Transactional delivery can involve different products, limits, or billing considerations. Confirm the applicable product and price before assuming it is included in your marketing subscription.

### Scenario 2: A growing weekly publication

You have several hundred or a few thousand contacts, send weekly, and use basic automation. Essentials may be a reasonable starting point, with Standard worth considering if its additional capabilities replace other tools or manual work.

Forecast contact growth monthly. If you add 500 contacts per month, a plan that looks comfortable now may need to change before the next quarter ends.

## How should you evaluate Mailchimp before upgrading?

Run a 30-day usage forecast before committing:

1. Count stored contacts across every audience.
2. Remove or identify duplicates and inactive records.
3. List every planned campaign and automation.
4. Estimate recipient sends for a normal and peak month.
5. Add SMS, transactional, tax, and overage assumptions.
6. Compare the result with the current plan display and checkout quote.
7. Recheck the forecast whenever your audience grows materially.

Also test the workflow itself. If your team needs stronger reporting, integrations, or deliverability controls, a cheaper plan may create hidden labor costs. If you only need a simple newsletter, paying for advanced capacity may be wasteful.

## What is the verdict?

Mailchimp can be inexpensive at the beginning, but growth makes contact structure and send discipline decisive. The safest forecast combines your peak stored contacts, realistic monthly sends, duplicate-audience cleanup, and separate budgets for SMS or transactional needs.

Use Free for early validation, Essentials for a straightforward growing program, Standard when additional marketing capability earns its cost, and Premium only when the team genuinely needs its scale or controls. Recheck the official pricing pages before every upgrade because promotions and displayed starting prices are not permanent guarantees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Mailchimp free for small lists?

Yes. Mailchimp currently displays a Free option for accounts below 250 contacts, subject to the plan’s contact and send limits. Confirm the current limits and eligibility on the official pricing page before relying on it.

### What happens if I exceed my contact or send limit?

Overages can apply when you exceed the contact or send limits associated with your plan. Forecast peak usage and check your account terms so a campaign surge does not become an unexpected charge.

### Does one subscriber count twice?

Yes, the same person can count multiple times if stored in multiple audiences. Consolidating audiences and removing duplicates can lower your billable contact count.

### Is SMS included in the email plan?

No. SMS is a separate credit-based add-on. Budget SMS independently from your email subscription and verify the current credit pricing before launching a text campaign.
