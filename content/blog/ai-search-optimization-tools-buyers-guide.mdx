Choose an AI search optimization tool by the question it answers, not by its label. "AI SEO platform" and "AEO tool" cover three kinds of product, and only AI visibility measurement, wherever it is sold, shows whether ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity or Google AI Overviews name your brand when a buyer asks. Pick the type that fits your problem, then test it before you pay.

Vendor facts come from each vendor's own pages as of October 2026; we have not tested those products hands-on. AEOeye sells a measurement tool, so the last section covers where it falls short.

## What counts as an AI search optimization tool?

An AI search optimization tool helps a brand get named in answers that AI engines write. The practice is called [answer engine optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization) (AEO) or generative engine optimization (GEO), and three kinds of product sell under it.

| Type | Buy it when | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| SEO suite with AI features | Rankings or technical health are the problem | Semrush, Ahrefs |
| Content generator or optimizer | Publishing volume or page quality is the bottleneck | Surfer, Jasper |
| AI visibility measurement | You don't know if engines name you for buyer questions | Profound, Peec AI, Otterly, AEOeye |

Some teams pair a suite with measurement; few need all three.

The labels are converging, so judge the module, not the logo. [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/brand-radar) sells Brand Radar, a visibility tracker for AI answers, inside its platform. As of October 2026, [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/pricing/ai/) lists an AI Visibility Toolkit from $99 a month per domain, billed annually, with 25 tracked prompts. [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/) calls itself an AI visibility platform, and [Jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/) offers a GEO Diagnostic beside its content agents.

A suite's rank and click data can't show whether an answer names you, and no tool changes an answer by itself. Google says AI features need no special optimization or schema ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), and the GEO paper's strategies for content creators lifted visibility by up to 40% in its tests but varied by domain ([Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)). Tools measure or draft; what moves an answer is the content and evidence engines can retrieve.

## Which trade-offs actually matter?

Five trade-offs decide whether a measurement tool's numbers are worth acting on: what it asks, shows, tests, repeats and remembers. We take a side on each.

### Buyer questions or brand-name checks?

Buyer questions. A prompt containing your brand name hands the model the answer: it tests what the engine knows, not whether it would name you to a buyer who has never heard of you. In a peer-reviewed benchmark study (not brand queries), small formatting changes shifted an open-source model's accuracy by up to 76 points ([Sclar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)), so test each question in several wordings. The cost: more queries, a higher price.

### Raw answers or a score?

Raw answers, with the score as an index into them. A percentage means nothing without its denominator: in AEOeye's audit records (127 audits, June to September 2026), brands were named in 157 of 892 answers, or 17.6%.

Those brands chose to run an audit (some repeatedly); 509 of the 892 answers came from one engine; and our own analysis model judged each mention. So we publish only caveated [aggregates](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json). Ask any vendor the same: how many answers, which questions, who judged them, and whether "named" is kept apart from "recommended".

### The model buyers see or a cheaper stand-in?

The default tier your buyers see, per engine, with the model named and pinned; a stronger, weaker or unnamed model measures a different product.

[OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text) advises pinning apps to dated model snapshots for consistent behavior; [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/model-ids-and-versions) says a model ID stays fixed, though infrastructure changes can still shift output slightly. Ask which model, tier and access route (app or API, web search on or off) produced each row, and keep engines separate: absent everywhere is broad, absent on one is engine-specific.

### One run or repeated runs?

Repeated runs, reported as a range. In [SparkToro and Gumshoe.ai's study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/), 600 volunteers ran 12 prompts 2,961 times in late 2025, and the chance of two runs returning the same brand list was under 1 in 100. The authors called visibility across many runs a reasonable metric and ranking position unreliable.

A precise position from one run is noise with a decimal point. So breadth (five engines, one run each) shows where you are missing, and depth (repeated runs on fewer engines) shows whether the gap is real. The cost: more runs, a higher price.

### A snapshot or tracking?

Snapshot to diagnose, tracking to prove a fix worked. The same LLM service can change substantially within months: GPT-4's accuracy on one math task fell from 84% to 51% between March and June 2023 ([Chen, Zaharia and Zou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009)). So a trend counts only if prompts, models and run counts stay fixed.

Snapshot first; buy tracking once you have a fix to test. We sell snapshots, so discount our bias.

## What would we refuse to pay for?

We would refuse to pay for any number we can't trace to a full answer. Our walk-away flags:

- Keyword rankings relabeled as AI visibility, "all major AI engines" with no named engines, or thousands of near-duplicate prompts that pad the count.
- Sentiment as the headline. In our audit records, 144 of 157 mentions were positive and 71.4% of ranked mentions came first, so the usual problem is absence, not dislike.
- "Add schema" advice that is the same for every site. [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says AI features need no special schema.
- A guaranteed recommendation, or "optimization" with no before-and-after answers. [OpenAI's docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text) call model output non-deterministic, so nobody can promise placement.
- A subscription forced onto a one-time diagnosis, or no real output before you pay.

## How do you score tools side by side?

Score each tool, or each suite's AI add-on, 0 (no), 1 (partly) or 2 (yes) on the eight rows below, then double the rows that match your job. A 0 on Questions or Evidence is a walk-away, whatever the total. Skip History if you only need a one-time diagnosis.

| Row | A 2 looks like |
|---|---|
| Questions | Your buyer questions in several wordings, labeled by buying stage |
| Evidence | Each number opens to the prompt, full answer, cited URLs, competitors and run date |
| Engines | The ones your buyers use, shown separately |
| Model | Named per engine, with changes flagged |
| Repeats | Several runs per prompt, reported as a range |
| History | The same prompts and settings across dates |
| Next steps | Each fix names the prompt, the competitor ahead, the missing proof, the page to change and how to retest |
| Price | One clear unit (prompts, domains, report) and real output before you pay |

Which rows to double:

- First baseline: Questions and Evidence.
- Content team fixing pages: Evidence and Next steps.
- Monthly reporting to executives: Repeats and History.
- High-stakes or regulated category: Questions and Repeats; expect slower audits.

## How do you test a tool before you pay?

Run the same ten buyer questions through every shortlisted tool and read the raw answers yourself.

1. Write ten questions across five families: category discovery, best fit for a small team, A-versus-B comparison, a constraint ("under $50, no subscription") and an objection ("which tools show evidence for their score?"). Use buyers' words, not your brand name.
2. Run them in each tool's free tier, trial or sample report, or in one of the [free checkers](/blog/free-ai-visibility-checker).
3. Open five answers and compare the tool's label (named, recommended, absent) with the text.
4. Run the set twice. If verdicts flip, ask whether the tool repeats runs and reports a range.
5. Swap one question and watch how far the headline score moves.
6. Spend two minutes on the vendor's own site. When we checked 43 AI visibility vendor sites (ours included) on 3 October 2026 ([crawler access study](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory)), none blocked an AI crawler from the homepage in robots.txt, but 11 of 42 homepages served no JSON-LD in their HTML. JSON-LD is the [structured data format Google recommends](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data); search the page source for "ld+json". A miss may only mean JavaScript adds it: ask, don't judge.
