You probably already have Mention, Brandwatch, or a Google Alert pinging you every time someone tweets your company name. None of them can tell you what ChatGPT said when a prospect asked "what's the best project management tool for a 10-person team" — and that one AI-generated answer may have already shaped the buyer's shortlist before your sales team ever heard about the deal. Nobody's monitoring that conversation yet, and most brands have no idea what it even says.

## What is AI brand monitoring?

AI brand monitoring tracks how AI models describe, mention, and recommend your brand when users ask about your category — not just social or news mentions, but the actual sentences ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude generate when someone asks who's good at what you do.

This is a newer discipline than social listening, and it behaves differently too. A model pulls from training data, live retrieval, and whatever structured signals it can find about you, then generates a fresh answer for every single person who asks — favorable or not, accurate or not. There's no post to like, no comment to reply to, and no dashboard alert when it happens; you only find out by asking the same questions your buyers are asking.

It matters more every quarter this trend continues. As more people run their first product research inside a chat window instead of ten blue links, the AI's answer becomes the new first impression — and unlike a search results page, you can't buy your way to the top of it with an ad. Ignore it long enough and a competitor's name starts filling the gap where yours should be. Understanding why a model picks one brand over another in the first place is worth reading on its own — see [how AI assistants choose which brands to recommend](/blog/how-ai-assistants-choose-brands).

## AI brand monitoring vs. traditional brand monitoring

Traditional brand monitoring watches what people say about you across social media, news, and reviews; AI brand monitoring watches what AI models say about you when someone asks a question — a different, far less visible surface that most marketing teams aren't watching yet.

| | Traditional brand monitoring | AI brand monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| What it tracks | Social posts, news articles, reviews, forum threads | AI-generated answers, recommendations, and comparisons |
| Where it happens | X/Twitter, Reddit, news sites, review platforms | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Claude |
| What triggers it | Someone publishes something about you | Someone asks an AI a question in your category |
| Visibility | Public, searchable, easy to screenshot | Private, one-to-one, a new answer every time |
| Core metrics | Mention volume, sentiment score, reach | Mention rate, sentiment, share of model, ranking in the answer |
| How you respond | Reply, moderate, PR outreach | Improve the structured content, citations, and authority signals models pull from |

These aren't competing categories — they answer different questions. Traditional tools tell you what was said publicly, in the open, where you can respond. AI brand monitoring tells you what's being said privately, one conversation at a time, at a scale nobody can screenshot their way through. Most teams have spent years building the muscle for the first column and none for the second. If you've got the first covered and not the second, you have a real blind spot, not a minor gap.

## What these tools actually track

Most AI brand monitoring tools track four core metrics: mention rate, sentiment, share of model, and how you fare when the AI directly compares you to competitors. Between those four numbers, you get most of the picture — everything else is detail.

- **Mention rate** — how often your brand shows up at all when someone asks a relevant question in your category. Zero mentions is a worse problem than bad sentiment, because there's nothing left to fix. See the [mention rate glossary entry](/glossary/mention-rate) for how it's typically calculated.
- **Sentiment** — whether the AI describes you neutrally, favorably, or with caveats and comparisons that quietly undercut you. A brand that's mentioned constantly but always with a hedge isn't actually winning.
- **Share of model** — your slice of AI attention relative to competitors across a set of prompts, roughly the AI-era version of share of voice. See [share of model](/glossary/share-of-model).
- **Competitor comparison** — when someone asks "X vs. Y" or "alternatives to X" directly, whether you're named, and where you land in the answer. This is usually the highest-intent moment in the entire set, because the buyer is actively deciding.

Tracking these numbers is the easy half. The harder half is doing something about what you find, and most of these tools stop at the dashboard.
