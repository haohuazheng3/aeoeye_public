## What are the best free SEO audit tools?

The best free toolset depends on the evidence required. Search Console shows Google’s view, Screaming Frog simulates a crawl, PageSpeed Insights combines field and lab performance, Schema Validator checks structured-data syntax, and Bing Webmaster Tools adds another search engine’s diagnostics. Use them together through a repeatable [SEO audit checklist](/blog/seo-audit-checklist).

A practical stack includes:

- Google Search Console for indexing, queries, impressions, clicks, and Core Web Vitals.
- Screaming Frog SEO Spider for titles, canonicals, status codes, links, and crawl patterns.
- PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse for URL-level performance diagnostics.
- Schema Validator for parsing and syntax checks.
- Ahrefs Webmaster Tools for verified-site health and link evidence.
- Bing Webmaster Tools for independent crawl and search data.

A free score can help triage work, but it cannot certify that a site is healthy, indexed, fast for real users, or visible in AI answers.

## What can each free tool actually prove?

Each tool answers a bounded evidence question. A composite score can hide whether evidence came from your site, a simulated crawl, field users, or an estimate.

| Tool | Strongest evidence | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| Search Console | Google-reported indexing, queries, impressions, clicks, and coverage signals | Every ranking factor or guaranteed index inclusion |
| Screaming Frog | Crawl-visible URLs, metadata, links, redirects, canonicals, and response codes | What Google indexed or how users experience every page |
| PageSpeed Insights | Field Core Web Vitals when available and lab diagnostics | Rankings, conversions, or site-wide performance from one URL |
| Lighthouse | Simulated performance, accessibility, SEO, and best-practice checks | Real-user results under every device and network |
| Schema Validator | Whether structured data parses as markup | Rich-result eligibility, ranking impact, or AI citation |
| Ahrefs Webmaster Tools | Issues and link data for a verified property | Complete link coverage or Google’s ranking decisions |
| Bing Webmaster Tools | Bing’s crawl, indexing, and search-performance perspective | Google’s exact indexing and ranking behavior |

A crawler can find an unexpected canonical, but it cannot prove Google selected that canonical. Search Console is closer to the first-party answer, though it reports systems and samples rather than future ranking promises. Record the source and scope of each finding in your [technical SEO checklist](/blog/technical-seo-checklist).

## How do you audit indexing and crawlability for free?

Start with Search Console’s index and performance reports, crawl the site, then reconcile sitemap, canonical, status, and robots signals. This separates Google-reported outcomes from crawlable causes.

In Search Console, inspect priority URLs individually, review page and query performance, look for changes rather than isolated exclusions, and confirm that the XML sitemap contains the URLs you want indexed. Use [Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) as the primary source for Google-specific evidence.

Next, run a focused Screaming Frog crawl. Prioritize the homepage, key templates, money pages, recent content, and representative internal-link paths. Review status codes, redirect chains, indexability directives, robots signals, canonical targets, missing or duplicate metadata, weak internal links, and broken destinations.

Reconciliation is the valuable step. A successful response can still carry `noindex`; a sitemap can list a URL whose canonical points elsewhere; and a crawl can discover a page with little internal support.

## How do you audit speed without misreading the score?

Use field Core Web Vitals when available and lab diagnostics for debugging. Google’s good thresholds are LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds, and CLS under 0.1. A performance score is not an SEO grade, and one URL cannot represent every template or user.

PageSpeed Insights is the best starting point for a URL because it can show real-user data and a controlled lab assessment when relevant data exists. Field data reflects actual experiences; lab data helps reproduce causes. Use [Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) for the thresholds and test representative templates instead of only the homepage.

Use lab results to investigate render-blocking resources, image weight, script cost, and layout behavior. Treat a low score as a debugging signal, not proof of poor rankings. Prioritize issues affecting field data, important templates, and measurable business outcomes.

## How do you check schema and AI visibility?

Schema Validator confirms syntax, not eligibility or AI citation. AI visibility requires repeatable buyer questions and records of whether engines mention, recommend, or cite the brand.

Paste representative markup into [Schema Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) and check item types, required properties, visible-content accuracy, nested relationships, duplicate markup, and template consistency. Valid `Product`, `Article`, or `Organization` markup can still receive no rich-result treatment.

For AI visibility, create questions across discovery, comparison, and decision stages. Record the date, engine, brand mention, recommendation, category association, citation, accuracy, and omission. A starting point is AEOeye’s [free AI visibility checker](/blog/free-ai-visibility-checker); treat its output as an observation because prompts and answer formats vary.

## What should a 30-minute audit sequence include?

Run evidence in order—indexing, crawl, performance, rendering, structured data, links, then AI answers—and record raw findings before assigning priorities. Thirty minutes should produce a defensible shortlist, not exhaustive coverage.

1. **Minutes 0–5:** Check priority pages, changes, exclusions, and sitemap status in Search Console.
2. **Minutes 5–12:** Crawl representative pages and inspect status codes, canonicals, robots directives, metadata, and internal links.
3. **Minutes 12–18:** Test key templates in PageSpeed Insights; compare field data with lab diagnostics.
4. **Minutes 18–22:** Use Lighthouse to investigate JavaScript, resource loading, accessibility, and page-level SEO signals.
5. **Minutes 22–25:** Validate structured data against visible content.
6. **Minutes 25–27:** Review internal links and available external-link evidence.
7. **Minutes 27–30:** Ask repeatable AI-visibility questions and save exact observations.

Keep three note columns: “observed,” “inferred,” and “unknown.” “URL is excluded” is observed. “Canonical conflict may contribute” is inferred. “This change will improve rankings” is unknown. Use AEOeye’s [SEO tools](/tools) or [homepage](/) as workflow entry points.

## When is a paid tool justified?

Pay when URL scale, crawl scheduling, historical data, exports, collaboration, or multi-engine monitoring repeatedly exceeds free limits. A subscription should provide recurring operational value, not simply a larger score or longer issue list.

Free tools are usually enough for a small business site, focused technical review, new-site first pass, migration checkpoint, or handful of templates. Consider paid tooling for scheduled crawls, large or JavaScript-heavy sites, release comparisons, client exports, multiple projects, broader backlink discovery, competitor monitoring, or integrated reporting.

Screaming Frog lists current SEO Spider limits and pricing on its [pricing page](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/pricing/). [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/about) provides another search engine’s perspective. The editorial position is simple: buy scale and continuity, not false certainty. Paid tools organize evidence; they do not replace validation or judgment.
