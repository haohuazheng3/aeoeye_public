AI crawlers do one of three jobs: collect training data, build a search index, or fetch a page because a user asked. Major operators use a separate robots.txt token per job, so you can block one without touching the others. In [AEOeye's crawler access study](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.json) (October 2026), 1 of 193 SaaS sites blocked any AI retrieval bot, against 27 of 38 news publishers.

Vendor facts below come from each operator's own documentation, checked on 3 October 2026; treat those pages as the authority. This page replaces four earlier AEOeye pages: a crawler list, a robots.txt test suite, a log analyzer and a statistics roundup.

## Which AI crawlers should you know?

This table lists 22 tokens from eight operators, each tied to one job, and operators control each job separately. [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) puts it plainly: allow OAI-SearchBot to appear in search while disallowing GPTBot to stay out of training.

| Token | Operator | Job | What the operator says |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GPTBot` | [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) | Training | Signals content should not train foundation models |
| `OAI-SearchBot` | OpenAI | Search | Opted-out sites are not shown in ChatGPT search answers |
| `ChatGPT-User` | OpenAI | User-triggered | Robots.txt rules "may not apply" |
| `ClaudeBot` | [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler) | Training | Future materials excluded from training datasets |
| `Claude-SearchBot` | Anthropic | Search | Not indexed for search; may reduce visibility in Claude results |
| `Claude-User` | Anthropic | User-triggered | Claude cannot retrieve your page for a user's question |
| `PerplexityBot` | [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers) | Search | Surfaces sites in Perplexity search; not used for model training |
| `Perplexity-User` | Perplexity | User-triggered | Generally ignores robots.txt, since a user requested the fetch |
| `Googlebot` | [Google](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers) | Search | Affects Google Search and its features |
| `Google-Extended` | Google | Training and grounding control | Controls Gemini training and grounding use; no effect on Search inclusion |
| `GoogleOther`, `Google-CloudVertexBot` | Google | R&D; Vertex AI agents | Generic R&D crawler; Vertex AI crawls requested by site owners |
| `Google-Agent` | Google | User-triggered | Acts on user request; such fetchers generally ignore robots.txt |
| `Applebot` | [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119829) | Search | Powers Spotlight, Siri and Safari search; may also give AI answers context |
| `Applebot-Extended` | Apple | Training control | Opts content out of training Apple foundation models |
| `Meta-ExternalAgent` | [Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/sharing/webmasters/web-crawlers) | Training or indexing | Crawls for AI-model training and product indexing |
| `Meta-WebIndexer` | Meta | Search | Allowing it helps Meta AI cite and link your content |
| `Meta-ExternalFetcher` | Meta | User-triggered | May bypass robots.txt |
| `Amazonbot` | [Amazon](https://developer.amazon.com/amazonbot) | Product improvement | May be used to train Amazon AI models |
| `Amzn-SearchBot` | Amazon | Search | Eligible for Alexa search; not used for training |
| `Amzn-User` | Amazon | User-triggered | May not follow all robots.txt directives |
| `CCBot` | [Common Crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/ccbot) | Open dataset | Stops collection into the open Common Crawl repository |

Three traps account for most misconfigurations:

- **Google-Extended is not a crawler.** It has no user-agent string; Google crawls with existing agents and reads the token as a control. For AI Overviews and AI Mode, [Google says](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) a page only needs to be indexed and snippet-eligible, so Googlebot and snippet controls decide.
- **Many user-triggered fetchers sit outside robots.txt.** OpenAI, Perplexity, Google and Meta say theirs may or do ignore it, and Amazon says Amzn-User may not follow all directives. Only Anthropic documents Claude-User as controllable. A firewall rule stops the rest, but it also blocks the user who asked about you.
- **Unnamed bots inherit.** Apple says Applebot follows Googlebot's rules when robots.txt does not mention Applebot. Amazon says Amzn-SearchBot follows the rules given to other search bots.

## What do 384 well-known sites actually allow?

Brands almost never block AI search bots; publishers often do. On 3 October 2026 a self-identifying research crawler fetched robots.txt, `/llms.txt` and the homepage of 391 well-known sites. Seven were unreachable, leaving 384 usable. Download the [CSV](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv) and [JSON](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.json).

"Blocks" means the bot's effective rules disallow the homepage `/`. A partial block such as `Disallow: /checkout` does not count.

| Sites that block… | SaaS (n=193) | E-commerce (n=90) | News publishers (n=38) | All (n=384) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPTBot | 4 (2.1%) | 5 (5.6%) | 20 (52.6%) | 35 (9.1%) |
| OAI-SearchBot | 1 (0.5%) | 1 (1.1%) | 11 (28.9%) | 16 (4.2%) |
| PerplexityBot | 1 (0.5%) | 4 (4.4%) | 26 (68.4%) | 35 (9.1%) |
| ClaudeBot | 3 (1.6%) | 5 (5.6%) | 30 (78.9%) | 44 (11.5%) |
| At least one training bot | 12 (6.2%) | 9 (10%) | 32 (84.2%) | 64 (16.7%) |
| At least one retrieval bot | 1 (0.5%) | 4 (4.4%) | 27 (71.1%) | 37 (9.6%) |

None of 43 AI-visibility vendors blocked any tracked bot, and 5 of 20 UGC and review sites blocked a retrieval bot. Bytespider, left out of the table because we could not verify ByteDance documentation, was the most-blocked token (56 of 384). What stands out:

- **Publishers are closing the door on sources AI leans on.** 9 of 38 block OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot together. Reddit's robots.txt is two lines (`User-agent: *`, `Disallow: /`), making it the only site in our sample that blocks Googlebot.
- **The "split" setup is rare.** Only 19 of 384 sites (4.9%) block GPTBot yet allow OAI-SearchBot.
- **Missing robots.txt is common among stores.** 30 of 384 sites (7.8%) have none, including 15 of 90 e-commerce sites. Per [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309), a 4xx response lets crawlers access anything, but a 5xx means they must assume complete disallow. A broken robots.txt is worse than none.
- **Bot protection quietly blocks.** 60 of 375 homepages (16%) blocked or challenged our declared crawler, including 24 of 88 e-commerce sites (27.3%). A firewall that cannot tell a retrieval bot from a scraper can keep a store out of answers while robots.txt says yes.
- **Content Signals are a SaaS habit.** 36 of 384 sites (9.4%) carry [Cloudflare Content Signals](https://blog.cloudflare.com/content-signals-policy/) lines, 33 of them SaaS; 16 set `ai-train=no`.
- **llms.txt spread faster than evidence.** 173 of 330 checkable sites (52.4%) serve a valid one, but no major engine documents using it for answers; see [what llms.txt is](/blog/what-is-llms-txt).

## How does this compare with larger studies?

Larger datasets point the same way: training dominates crawling, and publishers restrict most. [Cloudflare's June 2026 report](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-internet-bot-report/) put AI training at 52% of crawler requests on its network, up from 22% in spring 2025. An [academic study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10315) found 60.0% of reputable news sites disallow at least one AI crawler versus 9.1% of misinformation sites. Our 84.2% publisher figure covers a smaller list, so do not compare them directly.

## Should you block AI crawlers?

If you want AI engines to name your brand, do not block search or user-triggered bots; training bots are a business decision. For brands, blocking search bots is almost always self-harm. For publishers with licensable archives, blocking is a negotiating position. aeoeye.com allows all of these bots.

![Hand holding a brass padlock, symbolizing security and protection](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36740854/pexels-photo-36740854.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

By bot type:

- **Search bots (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot):** allow. Blocking them drops you from that engine's answers.
- **User-triggered fetchers:** leave alone. Robots.txt often does not govern them, and a firewall block fails your prospects.
- **Training bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot):** your call. Anthropic says a ClaudeBot block covers future materials, so it is not retroactive.

By business type:

- **SaaS, stores, local and service businesses:** allow everything. Only 4 of 193 SaaS sites in our study block GPTBot.
- **Paywalled publishers and licensing-first sites:** block training tokens, keep search tokens open, and negotiate.
- **Worried about server load:** rate-limit. Anthropic supports `Crawl-delay`; Apple and Amazon say they do not.

Do not enforce a block by IP: Anthropic warns it can stop its bots reading your robots.txt, making opt-outs unreliable. For the full argument, read [why blocking GPTBot is usually a mistake](/blog/blocking-gptbot-is-usually-a-mistake), [how to block AI crawlers](/answers/how-to-block-ai-crawlers) and [ClaudeBot explained](/blog/claudebot).

## What robots.txt rules should you use?

Per [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309), a crawler obeys the group matching its token, and the `*` group applies only when no specific group matches. Start from the default that fits you.

**Brand default (allow all AI bots, protect private paths):**

```text
User-agent: *
Disallow: /api/
Disallow: /account/

Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml
```

**Publisher split (opt out of training, stay in search):**

```text
User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: Google-Extended
User-agent: Applebot-Extended
User-agent: CCBot
User-agent: Meta-ExternalAgent
Disallow: /

User-agent: *
Disallow: /api/
```

Three pitfalls:

- **A named group replaces `*`.** Add `User-agent: GPTBot` with one rule and GPTBot ignores your `*` rules. Repeat the rules you still want.
- **Rules live per host.** Anthropic asks for rules on every subdomain.
- **Changes lag.** OpenAI and Perplexity each say up to about 24 hours.

Check the file visitors actually get, not your repo's copy: [Cloudflare's managed robots.txt](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/managed-robots-txt/), for one, prepends its own rules. To draft yours, use our [AI robots.txt generator](/tools/ai-robots-txt-generator).

Then test them. Our [robots.txt test suite](/resources/ai-crawler-robots-txt-test-suite/README.md) holds 24 synthetic RFC 9309 cases (wildcards, `$` anchors, percent-encoding, merged groups, longest match) with expected output. We re-ran it on 3 October 2026 and it matched byte for byte. It tests parser logic, not bot obedience.

## How do you verify an AI crawler in your server logs?

A user-agent string is a claim, so verify the source IP. Every operator below publishes an IP list or a reverse-DNS pattern, and a spoofed "GPTBot" fails both. [Common Crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/ccbot) notes that some crawlers falsely identify as CCBot.

| Operator | How to verify |
|---|---|
| OpenAI | Published lists: [searchbot.json](https://openai.com/searchbot.json), [gptbot.json](https://openai.com/gptbot.json), [chatgpt-user.json](https://openai.com/chatgpt-user.json) |
| Anthropic | One [published list](https://claude.com/crawling/bots.json) |
| Perplexity | [perplexitybot.json](https://www.perplexity.com/perplexitybot.json) and [perplexity-user.json](https://www.perplexity.com/perplexity-user.json) |
| Google | [Reverse then forward DNS](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/verify-google-requests); the name must be under googlebot.com, google.com or googleusercontent.com |
| Apple | Reverse DNS in `*.applebot.apple.com`, or its CIDR JSON file |
| Common Crawl | Reverse DNS in `crawl.commoncrawl.org`, or [ccbot.json](https://index.commoncrawl.org/ccbot.json) |

For a sample of requests, record the exact `User-Agent` and IP, match the IP to the operator's list or DNS pattern, then confirm the bot fetched `/robots.txt` first. OpenAI may add a `robots.txt` marker to its user agent on those fetches, handy when logs lack paths.

Our [log analyzer](/resources/ai-crawler-log-analyzer/README.md) automates the first step. This dependency-free Python script keeps three fields apart: the claimed token, a category from a dated rules file, and an optional IP check against a CIDR file you supply. Without one it reports `not_checked`, never "verified". Its ten-row log is synthetic; we re-ran it on 3 October 2026 and the output matched.

A crawl is not a citation. Logs prove reachability; whether an engine names you needs [answer-level checks](/) like our free audit.

## How was the study done, and what are its limits?

We fetched each site once on 3 October 2026 and parsed robots.txt with the same matcher AEOeye's SEO audit uses. Our crawler identified itself as AEOeyeResearch/1.0 and waited one second between requests to a domain.

The sample, picked by us: 194 SaaS brands, 95 e-commerce and consumer brands, 43 AI-visibility vendors (aeoeye.com included), 39 news publishers and 20 UGC or review sites.

Limits:

- **Not a random sample.** It skews toward established brands; the publisher and UGC groups are small.
- **One fetch, one day, main host only.** Policies and subdomains differ.
- **A block is a request.** CDN-level blocking is invisible to robots.txt; we saw it only when our crawler was challenged.
- **Counts, not outcomes.** Nothing here shows a setting changed whether an engine recommends a site.
