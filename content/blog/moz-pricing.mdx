Moz does not have one universal “Moz cost.” Moz Pro, Moz Local, and Moz API are separate products, so the correct price depends on whether you need SEO research, local listing management, or programmatic data access.

The verified Google-rendered official snippets checked on September 13, 2026 show Starter at $49 monthly, Standard at $99 monthly, Moz API starting at $20 monthly, and a 20% annual saving for Moz Local. That distinction matters: a comparison that combines all three products into one neat number is hiding the decision you actually need to make.

## What does Moz cost?

Moz pricing must be split by product. Moz Pro Starter is $49 per month or a $39 monthly equivalent when billed annually; Standard is $99 monthly or a $79 annual equivalent; Moz API starts at $20 monthly; Moz Local has separate monthly and annual terms.

Published Starter capacity is one site, 50 tracked keywords, and one user. Standard expands to three sites and 300 tracked keywords. Moz Local advertises a 20% annual saving, but the verified official snippet did not establish its complete tier-by-tier price list, so this guide will not invent one.

Official references are the [Moz Pro pricing](https://moz.com/products/pro/pricing), [Moz Local pricing](https://moz.com/products/local/pricing), and [Moz API pricing](https://moz.com/products/api/pricing) pages. Always recheck live terms before purchasing.

## What do Moz Pro plans buy?

Moz Pro Starter is the sensible entry point when one person manages one website and needs a defined keyword set. Standard is the practical step up only when three sites or 300 tracked keywords solve a real operating constraint.

| Product | Monthly price shown | Annual-equivalent price | Published capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Moz Pro Starter | $49/month | $39/month | 1 site, 50 keywords, 1 user |
| Moz Pro Standard | $99/month | $79/month | 3 sites, 300 keywords |
| Moz API | Starts at $20/month | Not established here | Programmatic Moz data access |
| Moz Local | Separate monthly terms | 20% annual saving advertised | Local listing management |

Starter is not “cheap Moz”; it is a deliberately limited single-site plan. If your workflow needs more than 50 tracked keywords, treat that limit as a planning constraint, not a minor inconvenience.

Standard is not automatically better because it costs twice as much. It is better only when the additional sites or keywords are used. Buying capacity for hypothetical future work is still waste.

## Is Moz Local a separate purchase?

Moz Local is a separate local-search product, not an automatic upgrade bundled into Moz Pro. It addresses business-listing distribution and local presence, so a company may need Local without Pro—or reasonably need both.

Use Moz Local when the main problem is keeping business information consistent across location profiles and directories. Use Moz Pro when the job is keyword visibility, rank tracking, site-level research, and competitive analysis.

Those products can complement each other, but justify them independently:

- A single-location business may need Local but little broad keyword tracking.
- A national content site may need Pro but no Local subscription.
- A multi-location brand may need both, with each tied to a separate owner and outcome.

The useful question is not “Does Moz include Local?” It is “Which operational problem am I paying Moz to solve?”

## What does Moz API cost?

Moz API pricing starts at $20 per month according to the verified official snippet. It is for applications, internal dashboards, automated reports, or data pipelines—not a replacement for the normal Moz Pro interface.

API economics also include implementation. A low subscription can become an expensive project when a team still needs to build authentication, error handling, storage, alerts, analysis, and reporting.

Consider the API when you already have software that needs Moz metrics, can estimate request volume, and have an engineering owner. Do not choose it merely because $20 looks lower than Pro.

## Should you pay monthly or annually?

Annual billing is cheaper at the published Moz Pro equivalents, but the useful comparison is total commitment. For Starter, $49 × 12 is $588, while $39 × 12 is $468—a $120 annual difference. For Standard, $99 × 12 is $1,188, while $79 × 12 is $948—a $240 difference.

At those displayed rates, annual billing becomes favorable when you expect roughly ten months or more of use. Monthly billing buys flexibility for experimental or seasonal work. Annual billing fits an established, continuous workflow.

Do not let the discount decide the product choice. First verify that the site, keyword, user, and reporting limits fit. A discounted plan that cannot support the work is still wrong.

## How do you calculate the real Moz budget?

The real Moz cost is subscription spend plus the work required to turn data into decisions. Start with a workflow worksheet before opening checkout.

| Budget question | What to record |
|---|---|
| Websites | Domains and subdomains needing tracking |
| Keywords | Queries requiring recurring monitoring |
| Users | People needing access or reports |
| Local locations | Locations requiring listing management |
| API use | Endpoints, request volume, automation frequency |
| Reporting labor | Time spent reviewing and presenting data |
| Commitment | Expected subscription duration |

Then calculate only the products required. A one-site operator might begin with Starter. A three-site operator may justify Standard. A local business may need Local instead of Pro. A software team may need the API plus development and operating costs.

Account for overlap too. If you already pay for a rank tracker, listings platform, or SEO API, Moz may duplicate part of the stack. Compare total useful coverage, not a single pricing-page number.

## Which Moz product fits your situation?

The smallest fitting product is usually the best starting point. Choose Starter for one site and a modest tracked set; Standard for three sites or 300 meaningful keywords; Local for listings; API for software integration.

For AEO-focused teams, rank tracking is only one signal. Moz can explain conventional search visibility, but it does not prove that ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI, or Codex recommends a brand. Pair SEO data with direct buyer-question testing.

## How should you evaluate Moz in 14 days?

Use a 14-day evaluation window as a disciplined fit test, not as a claim about a current free trial. Verify purchase and trial terms directly with Moz.

During that window, answer:

1. Can you configure the right site, locations, and keyword set without hitting limits?
2. Do reports change a real decision about a page or query?
3. Can the intended user and reporting workflow operate without manual workarounds?
4. Does Moz duplicate data you already receive?
5. Can you explain the monthly cost in one sentence tied to an outcome?

If the last answer is no, postpone the purchase or narrow the scope. Tools become expensive when nobody owns the decision loop.

## What is the verdict?

Moz pricing is easiest to understand when you stop treating Moz as one product. Start with the workflow: Pro for SEO research and tracking, Local for listings, and API for programmatic data. Then choose the smallest plan whose published limits cover actual work.

Pay annually only when usage is durable. Pay monthly when the project is uncertain. Most importantly, do not buy a larger Moz footprint to compensate for an undefined SEO process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Moz Pro cost per month?

Moz Pro Starter is shown at $49 per month, while Standard is $99 per month. The displayed annual equivalents are $39 and $79 per month.

### Is there a free version of Moz?

This article does not assert a current free Moz plan or trial. Check the official product page for current access terms before relying on a free option.

### Are Moz Local and Moz Pro included together?

No. Moz Local and Moz Pro are separate products with different purposes and pricing. Evaluate and budget them independently.

### Is Moz Pro worth it for one website?

It can be if keyword tracking and SEO research change decisions. For one site, Starter is the logical published fit; it becomes poor value when reports do not change actions or limits do not match the workflow.
