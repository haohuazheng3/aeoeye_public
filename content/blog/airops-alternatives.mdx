AirOps is a strong fit when a team needs governed content production, workflow controls, and enterprise review around AI-assisted execution. It is not automatically the best choice for every AI-search visibility job. The right alternative depends on whether you need a lightweight monitor, enterprise intelligence, a broader SEO suite, or a one-time baseline before committing to ongoing tracking.

This is a documentation-based comparison of public product materials checked October 23, 2026, not a hands-on test. Validate coverage, limits, data quality, and contract terms with your own prompts before buying.

This guide compares seven AirOps alternatives by job, not by affiliate rank. Peec or Otterly usually suit lighter monitoring. Profound or Scrunch are better candidates for enterprise intelligence. Ahrefs Brand Radar and Semrush make more sense when AI visibility must live inside an existing SEO stack. AEOeye is designed for a one-time baseline, while AirOps remains compelling when governed execution—not measurement—is the bottleneck.

## Which AirOps alternative fits your actual job?

Start with the decision you need to make, not the feature list. If you need recurring prompts and trend lines, choose a tracker. If you need market-wide intelligence and team governance, choose an enterprise platform. If you need to understand your current position before buying a subscription, choose a baseline audit.

| Job to be done | Strongest fit | Why it fits | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lightweight AI visibility monitoring | Peec or Otterly | Focused prompt and mention tracking | Less governed execution than AirOps |
| Enterprise AI-search intelligence | Profound or Scrunch | Larger-scale visibility, competitive, and research workflows | Pricing and packaging may require a sales conversation |
| AI visibility inside an SEO stack | Ahrefs Brand Radar or Semrush | Connects AI signals with established SEO research | More suite complexity and plan dependencies |
| One-time visibility baseline | AEOeye | Free preview and $29 one-time report | Snapshot, not ongoing trend tracking |
| Governed content execution | AirOps | Workflow, controls, and production coordination | May be more platform than a measurement-only team needs |

## Is Peec a lighter alternative for teams that need monitoring?

Peec is a practical choice when the core requirement is recurring AI-search monitoring without adopting a full content-operations platform. Its public pricing page lists Starter at €89 per month, giving buyers a concrete entry point for a monitoring-led workflow.

The trade-off is scope. A monitoring product does not automatically provide AirOps-style governance for briefs, approvals, content generation, and publishing. Teams that need a controlled path from insight to approved output may still prefer AirOps, or pair Peec with their existing workflow tools.

Read AEOeye’s [Peec alternative](/alternatives/peec) page for a narrower comparison.

## Is Otterly the cheaper starting point for AI visibility tracking?

Otterly is a sensible starting point when price clarity and focused tracking matter more than enterprise workflow depth. Its public pricing page lists Lite at $29 per month, which makes it one of the clearest lower-cost options in this comparison.

Before choosing it, confirm the current prompt, engine, location, refresh, seat, and export limits on the official page. A low list price can still be a poor fit if your required coverage forces an upgrade. Otterly also addresses monitoring more directly than governed content execution.

See the [Otterly alternative](/alternatives/otterly) comparison for a focused decision aid.

## When should an enterprise team choose Profound or Scrunch?

Profound and Scrunch deserve consideration when AI visibility is a cross-functional intelligence problem rather than a single marketer’s dashboard. Their public materials position them for broader research, competitive analysis, and organizational use cases. Profound’s pricing page shows a free Trial and quote-based Enterprise offering; no universal public Enterprise amount is stated there.

Scrunch’s public pricing page lists Core at $250 per month. That published number provides a useful reference point, but enterprise fit still depends on coverage, seats, usage, integrations, and support. Verify the official page for current inclusions and any higher tiers.

Choose Profound when you expect a trial-led evaluation and need to assess enterprise intelligence before discussing a custom package. Choose Scrunch when the published Core positioning and price align with a team that wants a more explicit starting plan. In both cases, ask for a sample output using your own market, competitors, locations, and prompts.

Neither choice should be judged only by dashboard polish. Enterprise buyers should validate data freshness, engine coverage, prompt governance, exports, user permissions, API access, and how the vendor handles answer changes. A platform can be excellent for intelligence and still require another system for content production.

## Do Ahrefs Brand Radar and Semrush make more sense if SEO is already central?

Ahrefs Brand Radar and Semrush are strongest when AI visibility is one layer of an established SEO operating system. They let a team connect AI-answer observations to familiar research, competitive, and organic-search workflows instead of creating a separate measurement island.

Ahrefs Brand Radar is worth evaluating if your organization already uses Ahrefs and wants its AI visibility work close to existing brand and search analysis. Semrush’s Visibility Overview documentation is a better starting point for teams already invested in Semrush reporting and workflows. Feature availability and plan requirements should be verified on the vendors’ current pages; no additional price is assumed here.

## Is AEOeye a replacement for AirOps or a different first step?

AEOeye is a different kind of decision. It provides a free preview and a $29 one-time report to establish whether a brand is being recommended in buyer-style AI questions across supported engines. That makes it useful before a team commits to a recurring platform or changes its content roadmap.

The limitation is important: AEOeye is a one-time snapshot, not trend tracking. It will not replace a monitoring platform when you need weekly movement, alerts, longitudinal competitor comparisons, or an ongoing reporting cadence.

Use AEOeye when the immediate question is diagnostic: “What does AI say about us today, and where are the gaps?” Then choose a tracker or enterprise platform if the answer requires continuous measurement. ## Should you keep AirOps when execution is the bottleneck?

Keep AirOps when the hard problem is turning strategy into controlled, repeatable production. Its value is clearest where multiple contributors need governed briefs, reviews, approvals, and execution standards—not merely a visibility score.

AirOps may be the better choice for an enterprise content team that already knows which topics and audiences matter but struggles with consistency, handoffs, or safe scaling. AirOps Solo and Pro can start free on the public pricing page, but that page displays no USD subscription amount; verify the current offer directly before budgeting.

For buyer context, see the [AirOps buyer guide](/blog/airops-buyer-guide) and [AirOps pricing overview](/blog/airops-pricing). Decide which system owns the source of truth before connecting exports or reporting.

## How should you test an AirOps alternative before switching?

Run a controlled comparison using the same brand, competitors, buyer prompts, locations, and date window. Record what each tool can actually show, export, and explain—not just whether its landing page lists a feature.

Use this five-step check:

1. Define 10–20 buyer questions that matter to revenue, not vanity mentions.
2. Confirm which AI engines, regions, languages, refresh intervals, and sources are covered.
3. Check whether recommendations are reproducible enough for a team to investigate.
4. Export a sample report and test permissions, history, and handoff to content or SEO owners.
5. Price the required tier using current official documentation, including seats and usage.

Do not treat a single favorable answer as proof of visibility. AI responses vary by prompt, date, context, and engine. The best tool is the one whose evidence changes a decision your team can act on.
