Most SEO teams treat Bing as an afterthought — verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools, submit a sitemap, forget it exists. That habit made sense for a decade. It doesn't anymore, and not for the reason most guides give you.

## What is Bing SEO?

Bing SEO is the practice of optimizing a website to rank higher in Microsoft Bing's organic search results. The fundamentals overlap heavily with Google SEO — crawlable pages, relevant content, real backlinks — but Bing weighs several signals differently, and its index now feeds Microsoft Copilot's answers. That second part is the bigger reason to care.

Bing's share of search traffic is small next to Google's, but exact figures shift depending on the source and how search gets counted. What's changed isn't the share — it's the role. Bing isn't just a search engine competing for clicks anymore; it's the retrieval layer behind Copilot, which means Bing SEO now overlaps with a form of AI visibility that has nothing to do with blue-link rankings at all.

If you've already done solid Google SEO, you're most of the way to solid Bing SEO. The gap is smaller than people assume, the fixes are mostly mechanical, and closing it is cheap relative to almost anything else on a marketing team's to-do list.

## Bing SEO vs Google SEO: what's different

Bing and Google chase the same goal — serve the most relevant result — but they don't weigh every signal the same way. Bing leans more on exact-match keywords, social signals, and literal on-page optimization; Google leans harder on backlink profiles and inferred topical relevance. Bing Webmaster Tools also hands you more direct control than Google Search Console does.

| Factor | Bing tendency | Google tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword matching | Rewards exact-match keywords in titles and body copy | Leans on semantic and topical relevance over exact strings |
| Backlinks | Count, but domain-level authority carries less weight | Backlink profile and domain authority are heavily weighted |
| Social signals | Has historically factored in social shares and engagement | Treats social activity as an indirect signal at best |
| On-page optimization | Rewards clear, literal signals — title tags, meta descriptions, keyword placement | Values on-page work but tolerates more inference from context |
| Content freshness | Matters, but older content isn't punished as hard | Heavily rewarded for time-sensitive or trending queries |
| Competitive density | Fewer sites actively optimize for Bing's preferences | Every competitor is already fighting for the same rankings |
| Webmaster control | IndexNow plus Bing Webmaster Tools enable near-instant crawl requests | Search Console offers similar tools, with more friction |

None of this makes Bing automatically easier to rank on — a keyword's difficulty score is still its difficulty score. But the practical upshot is real: fewer people optimize deliberately for Bing's specific preferences, so literal, disciplined on-page work tends to go further there than it does on an already-crowded Google results page.

## How to optimize for Bing

Optimizing for Bing starts with what you've likely already built for Google — a technically sound site, clear content, real backlinks — plus a handful of Bing-specific moves: verifying in Bing Webmaster Tools, enabling IndexNow, and being more literal with keywords and metadata than you'd typically bother being for Google.

Concretely:

- **Verify in Bing Webmaster Tools.** It supports a direct import from Google Search Console, so setup takes minutes, not hours.
- **Submit your sitemap directly** instead of waiting for Bing to discover it on its own crawl schedule.
- **Enable IndexNow** so Bing hears about new or updated pages instantly instead of waiting for the next crawl.
- **Write literal title tags and meta descriptions** that include your target keyword plainly — Bing rewards precision here more than Google typically does.
- **Keep the technical basics tight**: crawlability, page speed, mobile rendering, structured data. If you haven't audited these recently, our [technical SEO checklist](/blog/technical-seo-checklist) walks through what actually moves the needle.
- **Keep earning real backlinks.** Bing counts them differently than Google does, but it still counts them.
- **Keep NAP and social profiles consistent** if you're a local business — Bing has leaned on this more than Google historically has.

None of this is exotic. It's mostly maintenance, done deliberately, on an engine most teams ignore by default.

## Why Bing SEO matters more than its market share

Bing's market share undersells its importance, because Bing isn't only competing with Google for search traffic anymore — it's the retrieval backbone behind Microsoft Copilot. If your site is effectively invisible to Bing, it's invisible to a growing slice of AI-generated answers too, no matter how well you rank on Google.

This is the part most SEO teams miss. They optimize exclusively for Google, assume that covers search in general, and never notice that Copilot — built into Windows, Microsoft 365, and Edge — is answering questions about their category using a completely different index. Our breakdown of [Bing vs. Google](/blog/bing-vs-google) covers the fuller picture of how differently the two engines actually behave, not just in ranking factors but in who uses them and why.

The practical risk is straightforward: optimizing only for Google leaves Bing-powered AI visibility on the table, for an audience — Windows and Microsoft 365 users who default to Copilot — that isn't small. You don't need to flip your priorities to fix this. You need to stop assuming Google SEO automatically covers Bing, because it doesn't. Check our [market share](/blog/search-engine-market-share) numbers if you want the real scale of what's at stake before deciding how much effort Bing deserves.

## Bing and AI search

Microsoft Copilot and Bing's own AI-generated answers draw from Bing's underlying search index — so being crawled, indexed, and reasonably well-optimized in Bing is a prerequisite for showing up in those AI answers, not an optional extra layered on top. An answer engine can't cite a page it can't find in its own index.

That's the same logic that applies across every AI answer surface, not just Bing's. The engine has to be able to find, parse, and trust your content before it can ever cite you — which is why classic technical SEO hasn't become irrelevant in the AI-search era. It's become a prerequisite for a new layer sitting on top of it. Our guide on [how to optimize for AI search](/blog/how-to-optimize-for-ai-search) covers that broader layer in more depth: what changes when the audience consuming your page is a language model instead of a human scrolling a results page.

Bing SEO, in that sense, isn't really a separate discipline from AEO. It's the specific version of AEO you're doing when Copilot, not ChatGPT or Perplexity, is the surface you're trying to reach.

## Should you invest in Bing SEO?

Yes, but as a secondary investment, not your primary strategy. Put your main effort into Google SEO first, since it still commands the larger audience for most businesses — then layer in Bing-specific work, most of which is cheap, fast, and reuses assets you already built for Google.

The honest case for Bing SEO isn't that it will send you more traffic than Google. For most sites, it won't, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The real case is that it's a low-cost way to also show up in Copilot and Bing's AI answers, at a bar that's often lower simply because fewer competitors have bothered to clear it. Bing Webmaster Tools takes minutes to set up. IndexNow takes an afternoon. Tightening title tags and meta descriptions is marginal effort you've probably already paid for once, when you did it for Google.

Skip Bing entirely only if you've genuinely run out of higher-value work on Google — which, if you're being honest with yourself, is rare for most sites.
