## What does the AI Search Add-On include?

The add-on is available only with SE Ranking Core, Growth, or Enterprise base plans. It extends AI Results Tracker and AI Research, and includes SE Visible. The five listed engines are AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini.

The key billing unit is a check: one prompt on one AI platform. That definition makes engine coverage important. A prompt checked on ChatGPT is one check; the same prompt checked separately on all five listed engines consumes five checks.

| Monthly checks | Monthly price | Annual monthly equivalent | Status |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 200 | $89 | $71.20 | Add-on only |
| 450 | $179 | $143.20 | Add-on only |
| 1,000 | $345 | $276 | Add-on only |
| More than 1,000 | By quote | Not publicly listed | Add-on only |

These are not standalone subscriptions. You need a qualifying base plan, and the add-on prices exclude that required subscription, taxes, and other add-ons. SE Ranking’s documentation does not establish that every customer will use all five engines in every workflow, so model your own prompt schedule before comparing vendors.

## How much does each check package cost?

The published add-on prices are $89 per month for 200 checks, $179 for 450, and $345 for 1,000. An annual billing option is shown as a monthly equivalent of $71.20, $143.20, and $276 respectively. These figures describe the add-on component only.

Simple monthly add-on arithmetic gives an effective price of $0.445 per check at 200, about $0.398 at 450, and $0.345 at 1,000. Those are package allocations, not necessarily a public overage price. Do not assume unused checks roll over or that additional checks can be bought at the same unit rate; those terms were not verified.

The five-engine structure is more useful than the nominal package number. At a full five-engine sweep, 200 checks represent 40 complete prompts, 450 represent 90, and 1,000 represent 200. A team monitoring only one engine can run more one-platform prompt observations, while a team requiring all five uses capacity five times faster.

## How many prompts fit in a five-engine sweep?

A complete sweep of one prompt across AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini costs five checks. Therefore, one-time five-engine capacity is 40 prompts on the 200-check package, 90 prompts on the 450-check package, and 200 prompts on the 1,000-check package.

That arithmetic assumes every check is assigned to a clean five-engine set. Real scheduling may include retries, different project configurations, or prompts monitored on fewer engines. Confirm how the interface counts a failed, repeated, edited, or partially completed observation.

## What could daily monitoring capacity look like?

At a daily 30-day cadence, the theoretical number of complete five-engine prompt slots is about 1.33 on 200 checks, 3 on 450 checks, and 6.67 on 1,000 checks. This comes from dividing the package by five engines and then by 30 days: 200 ÷ 5 ÷ 30, 450 ÷ 5 ÷ 30, and 1,000 ÷ 5 ÷ 30.

Those are arithmetic planning figures, not a product promise. Confirm actual scheduling, counting, engine cadence, and how prompt changes consume checks.

If you need ten complete five-engine prompts daily, none of the listed packages reaches that requirement without a quote above 1,000 checks or a less frequent schedule.

## How should you calculate total and annual cost?

The practical equation is:

`Total monthly cost = qualifying base plan + AI Search Add-On + taxes + other add-ons`

The base-plan amount is intentionally not filled in here because it was not verified in the cited add-on evidence. Do not multiply the add-on price and call that your SE Ranking bill. Confirm whether the base plan is already funded, required for each workspace, or subject to separate limits.

For the add-on alone, the displayed annual-equivalent totals are $854.40 for 200 checks, $1,718.40 for 450, and $3,312 for 1,000. These are $71.20 × 12, $143.20 × 12, and $276 × 12. Verify whether the annual option is paid upfront, how renewal works, and whether the monthly equivalent is only a display convention.

Taxes and other add-ons remain outside these figures. If procurement needs a committed annual number, request a quote that includes the base plan, add-on, tax treatment, currency, payment timing, and cancellation terms. ## Which team is each package suited to?

The 200-check package is the cautious starting point for a founder or small team with a tightly edited prompt set. At five-engine coverage, its 40-prompt capacity is enough for a compact baseline, not broad daily monitoring.

The 450-check package suits a team that needs about 90 complete five-engine prompts per package cycle or a larger single-engine program. It may fit a marketing team comparing priority topics, markets, or competitors, provided the required base plan is already justified.

The 1,000-check package is the clearest listed option for a larger monitoring program, with 200 complete five-engine prompts by simple arithmetic. It still does not establish output accuracy, citation quality, or a guarantee of business impact. More than 1,000 checks require a quote, so agencies or broad portfolios should ask for volume pricing and exact counting rules.

Choose by prompt-engine observations, not “prompts” alone. A 200-prompt claim can mean 200 checks on one engine or only 40 prompts observed across all five engines.

## What should you verify before buying?

Use this checklist in the account flow or procurement call:

1. Confirm that your chosen Core, Growth, or Enterprise base plan supports the add-on.
2. Ask exactly how a prompt, engine, retry, edit, failure, and rerun consume checks.
3. Verify scheduling, daily cadence, engine availability, and whether checks roll over.
4. Request the base subscription price, taxes, other add-ons, currency, and full monthly total.
5. Confirm whether annual billing is paid upfront and how renewal or cancellation works.
6. Define a prompt baseline and success metric before expanding volume.
7. For more than 1,000 checks, request written quote terms and overage treatment.

The responsible comparison is total cost plus measurable evidence. Start with a representative prompt set, observe all engines you actually care about, and scale only when the resulting data changes a decision.
