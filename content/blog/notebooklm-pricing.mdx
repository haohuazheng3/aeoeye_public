## What does NotebookLM cost now?

NotebookLM access is organized through Google AI plans: Standard is free, while Plus, Pro, and Ultra provide progressively higher limits. Google’s plan page explains the July 2026 branding change to Gemini Notebook, although “NotebookLM” remains the clearest search term. [Check Google’s current plan page](https://notebook.google/plans) before purchasing.

You are paying for capacity around notebooks, sources, chats, generated outputs, and sharing—not a guaranteed replacement for every research or writing tool. Standard suits occasional reading, study, meeting preparation, and question-answering over manageable source sets. Paid plans matter when several projects, large document collections, frequent generation, or collaboration repeatedly exceed free capacity. Compare the decision with our [NotebookLM review](/blog/notebooklm-review).

## What limits change between Standard, Plus, Pro, and Ultra?

The main difference is capacity. Google’s current table scales notebooks from 100 to 500, sources per notebook from 50 to 600, daily chats from 50 to 5,000, and generation limits by tier. Intermediate values and feature allowances can change, so verify the live limits before subscribing.

| Capability | Standard | Plus | Pro | Ultra |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Notebooks | 100 | Higher | Higher | Up to 500 |
| Sources per notebook | 50 | Higher | Higher | Up to 600 |
| Daily chats | 50 | Higher | Higher | Up to 5,000 |
| Reports, audio, other generations | Standard | Increased | Higher | Highest |
| Best fit | Occasional solo use | Light recurring research | High-volume individual work | Specialist capacity |

These limits interact. More chats do little if your project cannot hold its documents, while more source slots have little value if you ask only a few questions weekly. [Review Google’s official limits and data guidance](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16213268?hl=en); Google’s [support documentation](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16213268?hl=en) remains the controlling reference.

## Which plan solves which bottleneck?

Plus fits light recurring research, Pro fits high-volume individual work, and Ultra is a specialist capacity purchase. Choose the first tier that removes a measured bottleneck: identify the limit causing interruptions, then upgrade only far enough to restore a reliable workflow.

- Source bottleneck: required documents do not fit in one notebook.
- Chat bottleneck: you repeatedly reach the daily question allowance.
- Generation bottleneck: reports, audio, or other outputs stop during routine work.
- Collaboration bottleneck: sharing or workspace needs matter more than solo quotas.
- Quality bottleneck: you dislike answers despite having ample capacity.

The first four are pricing signals; a larger plan does not necessarily solve quality problems. Choose Plus when free is useful but cramped, Pro when NotebookLM supports daily professional work, and Ultra only when lower tiers demonstrably fail. ## What privacy and source limits matter before upgrading?

More capacity does not change the need to own upload rights or verify outputs. Each uploaded source can be up to 500,000 words or 200 MB, and data handling depends on account type. Confirm that documents may be processed, understand shared-notebook access, and review Google’s current language before upgrading.

The per-source limit is separate from the number of sources allowed in a notebook. More source slots cannot make one oversized file acceptable. Google describes NotebookLM as a source-grounded research and learning tool and notes support for more than 80 languages; that does not remove the need to test a particular source, language, or output. [See Google’s product definition](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16164461?hl=en).

Generated answers are not automatically authoritative. Check important claims against original sources, especially for legal, medical, financial, academic, or operational decisions. For upload-size and source-allowance wording, consult Google’s [source and notebook guidance](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16215270?hl=en).

## Who should stay free, choose Plus, Pro, or Ultra?

Most solo users should stay free until they hit a documented limit. Standard is the default, Plus is the sensible first upgrade, Pro is for sustained high-volume work, and Ultra is reserved for unusually large or intensive workloads.

- Stay with Standard for occasional use, modest source sets, and rare limit hits.
- Choose Plus when recurring research is constrained but not continuously heavy.
- Choose Pro when daily individual work has a clear economic cost when interrupted.
- Choose Ultra when you routinely need the highest published capacity.
- Reconsider the tool when your main need is discovery, automation, or another missing capability.

Teams should price collaboration, administration, retention, permissions, and compliance separately from generation quotas. If the subscription is part of a broader AI budget, compare it with AEOeye’s [pricing framework](/pricing) and record the precise workload each tool replaces. Capacity purchased without an assigned workflow is usually wasted capacity. Check account-specific controls before placing sensitive team material in a personal workflow. The [Standard FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en) can clarify limits, resets, and availability near the free-tier boundary.

The clearest pricing strategy is incremental: use Standard, record the limits interrupting real work, and buy the first plan that removes them. Reassess after a month. If paid capacity remains unused, downgrade; if it is consistently exhausted, consider the next tier.
