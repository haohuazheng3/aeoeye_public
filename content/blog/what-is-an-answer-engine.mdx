An answer engine reads sources for you and returns one written answer, usually with citations, instead of a ranked list of links. ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Claude with web search and Microsoft Copilot all work this way. For a brand, a shortlist of two to four names replaces a page of ten links, and you are either on it or you are not.

## What is an answer engine?

An answer engine takes a question in plain language, retrieves sources at the moment you ask, and writes one answer from them, usually with links to the pages it used. [Wikipedia files "answer engine" under question answering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Answer_engine), a field older than ChatGPT; what changed is that a language model now writes the answer from live web pages.

The label describes behavior, not a brand: ChatGPT is an answer engine when it searches and an ordinary chatbot when it answers from training data, and [OpenAI's developer documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search) says the model decides which, based on the prompt.

[AI search](/blog/what-is-ai-search) is the wider shift toward generated answers, and "generative engine" is the research term for the same mechanism (see the [GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)). Vendor details here come from public documentation checked on 3 October 2026; we have not tested every product hands-on.

## How is an answer engine different from a search engine?

A search engine returns a ranked list of pages and leaves the reading to you. An answer engine reads the pages and returns a written conclusion, so your job moves from choosing between sources to accepting, or challenging, one shortlist.

Here is "What's the best CRM for a five-person agency?" in each; the first row is an illustrative layout, not a recorded result.

| | Search engine | Answer engine |
|---|---|---|
| You get | Ads, listicles, a forum thread and vendor homepages: about ten links | One paragraph naming two to four CRMs, each with a reason and source links |
| A brand wins by | Ranking high on the page | Being named in the answer |
| A brand that misses out | Still gets some clicks | Gets no click from the answer |

There is no page two of an answer. [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) analyzed March 2025 browsing by 900 US adults: with an AI summary on the page, they clicked a regular result in 8% of visits, against 15% without one, and a link inside the summary in 1%.

## How do answer engines work?

Most answer engines run four steps: rewrite your question into searches, retrieve pages, write an answer from them, and attach citations. Researchers call the pattern [retrieval-augmented generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401): a model's trained memory combined with documents fetched at answer time. Retrieval decides which brands can appear at all.

1. **Rewrite.** One question becomes several searches. Google says [AI Overviews and AI Mode may use "query fan-out"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), running related searches across subtopics and data sources. Anthropic says simple factual queries typically use [one to three searches; comparative research can use ten or more](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool).
2. **Retrieve.** Searches return candidate pages from an index. For Google's AI features, a page must be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet.
3. **Write.** The model drafts one answer from the retrieved text. OpenAI distinguishes the pages a model [consulted from the few it cites inline](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search).
4. **Cite.** [Microsoft](https://blogs.bing.com/search/April-2025/Introducing-Copilot-Search-in-Bing) links whole sentences or passages of a response to their sources, and [Claude's API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool) returns the cited text with each citation.

Not every answer retrieves anything. Anthropic says Claude searches for current or changing facts, including facts about specific organizations and products, and answers stable knowledge directly, so whether an answer searched can change which brands appear. Our [RAG explainer](/blog/what-is-rag) covers the mechanics.

A citation is also not proof. A [2023 audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848) of four generative search engines (Bing Chat, NeevaAI, Perplexity AI and YouChat) found only 51.5% of generated sentences fully supported by their citations, and 74.5% of citations supporting their sentence. Treat it as a warning about the format, not a score for today's products. Retrieved, cited and named are three separate gates; a brand can pass one and fail the next.

## Which answer engines matter for brands, and where do their sources come from?

Six answer engines are worth testing. They draw on different indexes and crawlers, so one buyer question can produce different shortlists. The table summarizes each vendor's own documentation as of October 2026.

| Engine | Where its sources come from | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT search | [OpenAI's developer docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search): the model chooses whether to search and can open pages. [OAI-SearchBot](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) is the crawler that surfaces sites in ChatGPT search. | In the API: inline citations, plus a list of every page consulted |
| Perplexity | [Perplexity's Agent API docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/agent-api/quickstart) describe answers grounded in real-time web search. [PerplexityBot](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) surfaces sites; Perplexity-User fetches pages on request and generally ignores robots.txt. | Inline citations |
| Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | Google Search itself, queried through [fan-out searches](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537?hl=en). See [what an AI Overview is](/blog/what-is-an-ai-overview). | Supporting links, a wider set than classic Search |
| Gemini | [Google's Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search): the model decides whether Search would help, then runs one or more queries; in the app, [grounding uses Search-index content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers). | A [Sources button when available](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14143489?hl=en); not every response has links |
| Claude with web search | [Claude decides when to search](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool) and may search repeatedly. [Claude-SearchBot](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler) is Anthropic's search crawler. | [Citations on web-search responses](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10684626-enabling-and-using-web-search) |
| Microsoft Copilot | Microsoft's [enterprise docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy): Copilot generates a web query and sends it to the Bing Search service. | [Copilot Search in Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/search/April-2025/Introducing-Copilot-Search-in-Bing) links whole sentences or passages to sources |

"AI search" is not one channel, so a page one engine cites can be absent from another's answer. Our [engine comparison](/blog/best-ai-search-engines) goes deeper.

Access differs too. In [AEOeye's crawler access study](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.json) (fetched 3 October 2026, 384 sites), 11 of 38 news publishers blocked OAI-SearchBot, 19 blocked Claude-SearchBot and 26 blocked PerplexityBot. It is a convenience sample, and a robots.txt block is a request, not enforcement.

## What does this change for marketers?

It moves the goal from ranking on a page to earning a place on a shortlist. In AEOeye's recommendation benchmark, the audited brand was named in 157 of 892 answers. Answers named 2.9 competitors on average, and a named, ranked brand was first in 71.4% of 147 ranked mentions. Being named at all is the hard part.

The benchmark is small and self-selected (127 audits, 27 brand names, June to September 2026; repeat audits included; 509 of the 892 answers come from Claude). Read it as a pattern in our records, not a market statistic; the [aggregates are public](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json).

What to do about it:

- **Measure the shortlist, not the click.** Unlinked mentions leave no trace in analytics, and [Search Console folds AI Overview and AI Mode traffic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) into ordinary "Web" totals.
- **Stay readable to search crawlers.** Allow OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot. OpenAI says [crawler settings are independent](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), so blocking GPTBot (training) does not remove you from ChatGPT search.
- **Look beyond robots.txt.** In our study 1 of 193 SaaS sites blocked any AI retrieval bot; our reading is that the bigger risk for most brands is being absent from the sources engines read. Bot protection can block crawlers invisibly (ours was challenged on 60 of 375 homepages, 16%), so check firewall logs.
- **Write passages that survive being lifted out.** Open sections with the answer, name your brand and category consistently, and be specific. In the [GEO study's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) tests, adding quotations, statistics or source citations raised a visibility metric roughly 30 to 40% relative; keyword stuffing gave little to no improvement, and results varied by domain.
- **Do not chase special markup.** Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special schema.org markup, AI text files or other special optimization.

The full playbook is in [what is answer engine optimization](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization).

## How do you know whether answer engines recommend you?

Ask each engine your buyers' questions, then check three things: whether you are named, whether you are cited with a link, and whether the description is accurate. Do it engine by engine, because shortlists differ.

1. Write five to ten unbranded buyer questions: "best [category] for [use case]", "alternatives to [competitor]", "is [your brand] worth it".
2. Run each in every engine above, in a fresh session so earlier chats do not steer the answer.
3. Record whether you are named, where you rank, which competitors appear, which URLs are cited and what is wrong.
4. Repeat a few times; answers are generated, so one run is an anecdote.
5. Open the cited pages: they are the sources you need to appear on.
