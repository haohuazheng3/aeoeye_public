Search 'share of voice tool' and you'll land on a pile of platforms built for a media landscape that's already half gone. They'll tell you your share of ad impressions. Your share of social chatter. Your share of page-one rankings. None of them will tell you your share of the answer when someone asks ChatGPT which brand to buy — because that conversation happens inside a chat window, not on the open web, and it's where a growing slice of buyer research actually happens now. That's the gap this guide is built to close.

## What is a share of voice tool?

A share of voice tool measures what percentage of a channel's attention your brand captures against named competitors — it's always relative, never absolute. Twelve percent share of voice tells you nothing until you know who owns the other eighty-eight.

Three flavors of this have existed for years, each tied to one measurable channel:

- **Paid media SOV** — the percentage of ad impressions, spend, or auction wins you capture in a category, tracked by ad-intelligence platforms.
- **Social listening SOV** — the percentage of branded mentions and conversation volume you own across social platforms and forums, tracked by listening suites.
- **Search / SEO SOV** — the percentage of page-one visibility, combining rankings and estimated click share, you hold across a keyword set, tracked by rank-tracking platforms.

Each tool class is good at one thing: counting something that happens somewhere public, crawlable, or auction-based. That's also precisely where all three stop working — none of them can see inside a private exchange between a buyer and an AI model.

## The four arenas of share of voice

Share of voice now splits into four arenas, not three — and most teams have tooling for exactly none of the newest one.

| Arena | What's measured | Classic tool class | Blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ads | Share of impressions or spend won in paid auctions | Ad-intelligence platforms | Sees only paid media, nothing organic |
| Social | Share of branded mentions and conversation volume | Social listening suites | Public posts only; misses closed-door research |
| Search | Share of page-one visibility across rankings | Rank trackers / SEO platforms | Measures blue links, not AI-generated answers |
| AI answers | Share of AI responses that name your brand | AI visibility / AI answer monitoring | The newest arena — most stacks have zero coverage here |

Three of these rows have been instrumented for a decade or more. The fourth is where a growing share of buyer research now actually begins, and it's the row almost every share of voice stack still leaves blank.

## Why AI share of voice is the missing column

AI share of voice goes unmeasured because buyer research has quietly moved into chat interfaces that no classic SOV tool was built to read — a competitor can dominate the AI answer to 'best [category] for [use case]' while you're still winning the equivalent Google ranking sitting right next to it, and unless somebody manually runs that exact prompt, nobody on your team finds out.

That's the shift underneath this whole guide: buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews, or Claude directly, and act on whichever brands get named in the answer. No scrolling past ten results, no comparing open tabs — just an answer, and the handful of brands sitting inside it.

Classic listening tools were built to parse the open web: crawlable pages, indexed posts, public threads sitting at a stable URL. That breaks completely against AI answers, because a generated response isn't a page. It's assembled fresh per query, often varies by user and by day, and disappears the moment the chat closes. There's nothing sitting still for a crawler to find.

Which means **[AI share of voice](/glossary/share-of-voice-ai)** can't be listened for. It has to be actively measured — by asking the engines the same questions a real buyer would ask, then recording who actually shows up in the answer. That's a different discipline entirely: probing, not crawling.

Two metrics fall out of that discipline, and they're worth knowing by name:

- **[Share of model](/glossary/share-of-model)** — your percentage of the answer space across a set of buyer questions, the direct AI-answer equivalent of classic share of voice.
- **[Mention rate](/glossary/mention-rate)** — how often your brand gets named at all, independent of position or sentiment within the answer.

Track both. A brand can post a strong mention rate and still be losing on share of model, if it's the third name mentioned behind two competitors, every single time.

## How to measure AI share of voice

You measure AI share of voice by asking every engine the real questions your buyers ask, logging every brand each answer names, and dividing your mentions by the total. Five steps, and each one matters more than it looks:

1. **Build a buyer-question set.** Use the phrases a prospect actually types — 'best [category] for [use case],' '[you] vs [competitor],' 'is [category] worth it' — not just searches for your own brand name.
2. **Ask all five engines the same questions.** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Claude pull from different sources and phrase things differently; a brand named in one is often invisible in another.
3. **Record every brand named**, not just yours. Share of voice means nothing without knowing who's splitting the rest of it.
4. **Calculate your share.** Your mentions divided by total brand mentions across the question set is your share of model. Track mention rate — how often you appear at all — right alongside it.
5. **Re-run it on a schedule.** Answers shift as models update and as the pages behind them change; a reading from three months ago tells you where you stood, not where you stand.

For the mechanics behind step two — why engines diverge and what that means for how you measure — see [how AI visibility gets measured](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility).

## Choosing a share of voice tool

Which share of voice tool you need depends entirely on which arena you're trying to win — no single platform on the market scores ads, social, search, and AI answers equally well. Match the tool to the job:

- **Chasing ad auction visibility?** Use an ad-intelligence platform built for paid media SOV.
- **Chasing conversation share?** Use a social listening suite built to parse public mentions.
- **Chasing page-one visibility?** Use a rank tracker built for search SOV.
- **Chasing your share of AI answers?** That's a distinct, newer specialist category, built to probe engines and score who gets named rather than crawl pages or parse social feeds — it's the category AEOeye was built for.

Here's the buyer mistake worth avoiding: expecting one tool to cover all four. A rank tracker will never tell you who ChatGPT names first. A listening suite will never open a Perplexity answer and count your competitors inside it. Most teams end up running two tools instead of one — whichever class covers their strongest existing channel, plus something for AI answers, since that arena had no coverage at all until recently.
