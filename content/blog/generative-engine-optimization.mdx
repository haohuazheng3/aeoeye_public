Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of making your content the one an AI engine quotes when it composes an answer. Our view: GEO's useful core is being the most quotable, best-sourced answer to a question. Everything that looks like a trick has either failed to hold up or is simply SEO under a new label.

## What is generative engine optimization?

GEO is the practice of increasing how often and how prominently generative AI engines cite or name your content in their answers. The term was introduced in a [2023 paper by Aggarwal and co-authors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), presented at KDD 2024, and, as [Wikipedia's entry on GEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization) records, it now overlaps with the terms AEO and AI SEO.

A generative engine retrieves documents, then writes one synthesized answer grounded in them. That is [retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation). There is no ranked list to climb, only a few sources that make it into the answer. Clicks shrink too: in [Pew Research's March 2025 browsing data](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) (900 U.S. adults), visits with a Google AI summary clicked a result link 8% of the time, against 15% without one.

## What did the original GEO research find?

The 2023 study found that adding quotations, statistics and cited sources raised a source's visibility in generated answers by roughly 27 to 41 percent, while keyword stuffing reduced it. The authors tested nine rewriting methods on GEO-bench, a set of 10,000 queries, using gpt-3.5-turbo to write answers from the top five Google results.

Visibility was measured as the share of the answer attributed to your source, weighted by position. Here is the "Position-Adjusted Word Count" score from the paper's Table 1 (baseline 19.3); the percentages are our arithmetic.

| Method (what an LLM was told to do to the page) | Score | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Quotation Addition | 27.2 | +41% |
| Statistics Addition | 25.2 | +31% |
| Fluency Optimization | 24.7 | +28% |
| Cite Sources | 24.6 | +27% |
| Technical Terms | 22.7 | +18% |
| Authoritative tone | 21.3 | +10% |
| Keyword Stuffing | 17.7 | -8% |

![A close-up of a blue bar graph printed on paper, representing data analysis](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7947754/pexels-photo-7947754.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

Three findings matter most. First, the authors report no significant gain from an authoritative tone, so engines look largely unmoved by confident prose. Second, methods combined: Fluency plus Statistics beat any single method by more than 5.5%, though on a 200-query subset only. Third, the gain depended on where the page already ranked: Cite Sources raised visibility of the fifth-ranked source by 115.1% but cut the first-ranked source's by 30.3%. If you already sit at the top, GEO edits are no free lunch.

The authors also ran a check on the live Perplexity engine: Quotation Addition scored 29.1 against a 24.1 baseline, while keyword stuffing fell to 21.9. Which method works best also varied by topic, so test on your own category.

Their own limits: an LLM did the page rewriting, results came from one benchmark and one synthetic engine, methods "may need to adapt over time as GEs evolve", and they did not test effects on search rankings.

## How do generative engines pick and cite sources?

They break a question into sub-queries, retrieve pages through a search index, and write an answer from the passages they trust. Google calls this [query fan-out](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide): concurrent related searches generated by the model, such as "best herbicides for lawns" behind "how to fix a lawn full of weeds".

Three gates decide whether you are in the answer:

1. **Access.** A page must be indexed and snippet-eligible for Google's AI features. For ChatGPT search, [OpenAI recommends allowing OAI-SearchBot](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots); sites that opt out will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links. In [our crawler study](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory) of 384 sites, 16 (4.2%) blocked it, so access is rarely the problem for brands.
2. **Selection.** Engines favor passages that answer the sub-question directly and are backed by something checkable.
3. **Corroboration.** A [September 2025 comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919) of AI search and Google found a "systematic and overwhelming bias" toward earned media (third-party, authoritative sources) over brand-owned content.

That third gate is why your own pages are only half the job.

## Which GEO tactics actually work?

The tactics with evidence behind them all add verifiable substance; the ones that hold up are quotation-ready facts with real sources, plus third-party corroboration. Tricks aimed at the model rather than the reader are weak, risky, or both.

| Tactic | Evidence | Our verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Real statistics, quotes and cited sources | +27% to +41% in the GEO paper; Quotation Addition also led on Perplexity | Do it, with checkable facts only |
| Clear, fluent, specific writing | +28% for Fluency Optimization | Do it |
| Third-party mentions and reviews | Earned-media bias in the 2025 study | Do it; this is the long game |
| Keyword stuffing | -8% in the paper | Stop |
| Confident, "authoritative" tone | No significant gain in the paper | Skip |
| llms.txt, special markup, chopping pages into chunks | Google says its Search ignores llms.txt and that chunking is not required | Optional, never first |
| Hidden "strategic text" aimed at the model | [Worked on fictitious coffee machines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07981) in a lab | Don't; it is manipulation |

![A blue light bulb standing out among several yellow bulbs on a yellow background](https://images.pexels.com/photos/12198522/pexels-photo-12198522.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

Google's guide adds that [seeking inauthentic mentions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) is not as helpful as it seems, and that mass-producing pages for every fan-out query violates its scaled content abuse policy. Our own housekeeping agrees: on 2026-10-03 we merged 165 pages into 42, because one strong page per question beats five thin ones. See [seven before-and-after examples](/blog/geo-examples) of quotable rewrites.

## How do you measure GEO?

Measure whether you are named for real buyer questions, not rankings. Google's [Generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) in Search Console covers Google's features; for everything else, you have to ask the engines yourself, repeatedly, because answers vary from run to run.

Our own numbers show why. In AEOeye audits completed between 26 June and 7 September 2026 (127 audits, 892 engine answers), the audited brand was named in 17.6% of answers. When a brand was named and ranked, 71.4% of 147 ranked mentions were first place, and answers named 2.9 competitors on average. Being named at all is the hard part.

The caveat is large: these are self-selected brands, engine samples are unequal and not comparable with each other, and four engines had fewer than 10 mentions. Don't read engine-versus-engine rates from them; read the shape. Method and data are in the [benchmark write-up](/blog/ai-brand-recommendation-measurement) and the [aggregate file](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json).

To run it yourself, write 10 to 20 unbranded buyer questions and log who is named. AEOeye's [free audit](/) asks three of them on ChatGPT.

## How is GEO different from AEO and SEO?

GEO and AEO are two names for the same work; SEO is the foundation under both. Google's own guide says that from its perspective, optimizing for generative AI search "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO", and mentions AEO and GEO only as terms you may see.

We agree on the foundation and disagree on the conclusion. Indexing and helpful content get you retrieved; they do not get you quoted. That second step, plus how outside sources describe you, is what GEO adds. The full side-by-side is in [AEO vs GEO vs SEO](/compare/aeo-vs-geo-vs-seo), and the umbrella is in [what is answer engine optimization](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization).

## What is a practical GEO sequence?

Fix access, then evidence, then third-party presence, then measure. This order puts cheap, certain wins before slow ones.

1. **Check access.** Confirm your robots.txt and CDN do not block AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot.
2. **Rewrite your top pages** so each question has a heading and a direct answer, backed by real numbers and named sources. Our [AI-search content guide](/guides/how-to-optimize-content-for-ai-search) shows how.
3. **Replace vague claims** ("trusted by thousands") with specific, checkable facts.
4. **Find the sources engines already cite** for your category and make sure they describe you correctly.
5. **Re-run your question set** monthly and keep the log.
