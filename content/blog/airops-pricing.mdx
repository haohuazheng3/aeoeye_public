## What does AirOps pricing include in 2026?

AirOps pricing is structured around plan capabilities and included capacity, not a publicly posted per-seat dollar schedule. The official comparison names Solo, Pro, and Enterprise; the meaningful differences are answer-engine coverage, content-production task limits, knowledge and brand assets, integrations, seats, and service.

AirOps describes its broader product surface on the [platform overview](https://www.airops.com/platform), while implementation and feature details live in the [AirOps documentation](https://docs.airops.com/). Treat those pages as complementary: pricing summarizes packaging, and documentation explains how the product is intended to work.

If you are comparing tools, start with the job to be done. AEOeye’s guide to the [best AI visibility tools](/blog/best-ai-visibility-tools) can help frame the difference between monitoring visibility, evaluating citations, and producing content.

## Is AirOps free to start?

Yes. The published comparison says both Solo and Pro “start free,” but that wording should not be read as unlimited free access or as proof that every feature is free forever. The page lists plan limits and capabilities, so a buyer should verify eligibility, duration, upgrade triggers, and any restrictions directly with AirOps.

Solo is the clearest low-commitment entry point in the published plan summary. It includes ChatGPT Insights only, personas, regions, and languages, plus 35,000 content-production tasks, one Brand Kit, three Knowledge Bases, basic CMS and SEO integrations, community/live chat support, and a single user.

Pro also starts free in the comparison. It expands answer-engine coverage to 7+ engines and raises the listed content-production capacity to 100,000 tasks. It includes one Brand Kit, five Knowledge Bases, CMS, SEO, AEO, social, and project integrations, with unlimited seats.

The practical question is not simply “Can I sign up for free?” It is “Which limits apply while I am free, and what happens when my team reaches them?” Get those answers in writing before building a reporting process around the free tier.

## How do the AirOps plans compare?

Solo is designed for a single user who needs a narrower ChatGPT Insights workflow; Pro is designed for broader answer-engine coverage and team collaboration; Enterprise is designed for organizations that need custom limits, agent builds, and structured service. ## What counts as an AirOps task?

The official page lists content-production task allowances—35,000 for Solo and 100,000 for Pro—but does not define a universal dollar cost per task. Do not divide an assumed subscription price by those numbers, because no public price or task accounting rule is shown.

Before buying, ask AirOps what action consumes one task, whether retries or failed runs count, and whether task usage is shared across users, brands, workspaces, agents, or projects. Also ask whether unused capacity rolls over, whether limits reset on a monthly or annual cycle, and what happens at the limit.

A task may be relevant to content production, but the plan summary alone does not establish how every workflow is metered. If your business needs repeatable reporting, request an example usage calculation based on your actual workflow rather than relying on a headline allowance.

This distinction matters for AI visibility programs. A tracker may count prompts, responses, citations, or monitored queries; a content platform may count production actions. Those are different units. Use the [AI traffic analytics guide](/blog/ai-traffic-analytics) to separate visibility signals from downstream traffic and conversion signals.

## Does AirOps publish Pro pricing?

No public USD subscription price for Pro was displayed on the official pricing page checked on October 23, 2026. The page says Pro starts free and describes its included capabilities, but it does not provide a verified dollar amount that can be quoted as AirOps cost.

That means a serious comparison should use a quote checklist, not a guessed estimate. Ask for the billing currency, billing period, included task limit, overage policy, seat treatment, contract term, renewal terms, implementation fees, and taxes. Ask whether the quoted scope includes all answer engines and integrations shown in the plan description.

Enterprise is explicitly custom in several areas, including prompt/page limits and task limits. Its dedicated account manager, training, and 1:1 onboarding may be valuable, but their commercial treatment is not stated on the public page. Request an itemized proposal so software, services, and optional work are not blended together.

## Which AirOps plan fits an AI visibility workflow?

Choose Solo when one person needs a focused starting point and ChatGPT Insights is sufficient. Choose Pro when the team needs 7+ answer engines, unlimited seats, and broader CMS, SEO, AEO, social, and project integrations. Consider Enterprise when custom limits, agent builds, unlimited knowledge and brand assets, or dedicated enablement are requirements.

Use this decision sequence:

1. List the answer engines your buyers use and confirm whether ChatGPT-only coverage is enough.
2. Estimate your content-production workload, then ask how AirOps defines and measures a task.
3. Count brands, knowledge bases, regions, languages, and collaborators.
4. Map required integrations and identify any workflow that depends on an Enterprise-only service.
5. Request a quote that separates recurring software from onboarding, training, and custom work.

Do not select a plan solely because its task allowance is larger. A larger allowance does not automatically mean better citation quality, more qualified traffic, or a guaranteed improvement in recommendations. Define success metrics first, then test whether the plan gives you the data and workflow access needed to measure them.

## What should you ask before buying AirOps?

Ask for written answers to the questions that determine your effective AirOps cost: What is included in the free start? When does billing begin? What exactly is a task? How are retries, failed jobs, and shared workspaces counted? Are limits monthly, annual, or contract-specific? Which integrations require Pro or Enterprise? How are additional brands, knowledge bases, pages, prompts, or agent builds priced?

Also ask how results are exported and retained. If your team needs historical comparisons, API access, audit trails, or role-based governance, verify those details in the product documentation and proposal. Confirm who owns generated assets, what support response times apply, and whether onboarding is mandatory or optional.

Finally, define a review date. Pricing and packaging can change, so record the page date, quote date, plan name, included limits, and renewal terms. Recheck the official [pricing page](https://www.airops.com/pricing) and [documentation](https://docs.airops.com/) before signing or renewing.
