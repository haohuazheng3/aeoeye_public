SEO wins you a ranked position in a list of links. AEO and GEO win you a mention inside a generated answer, often with no list and no click at all. That single difference in the unit of success is the one that matters — not the acronym — and once you see it clearly, AEO and GEO turn out to be the same discipline wearing two different names, while SEO is a genuinely different game with different content, different metrics, and different ways to fail.

Search this exact phrase and the top result is a Reddit thread of practitioners arguing about what these terms even mean, not a clean vendor explainer. That's not an accident. "Answer engine optimization," "generative engine optimization," "AI search optimization," "LLM SEO" — most of these labels were coined by companies with software to sell, us included. So don't take our word for the distinction. Judge it by the two tables below, built around what you actually measure and what actually breaks when you get it wrong.

## What do AEO, SEO, and GEO actually mean?

SEO is the practice of ranking higher in a results page built from a crawled index; AEO and GEO are the practice of getting named, by product or brand, inside an answer an AI generates — on ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, or Google's AI Overviews. All three share a goal (be found when someone is deciding what to buy) but they chase it through completely different mechanics.

**SEO** is the oldest of the three. Organized optimization for search crawlers dates back to the mid-to-late 1990s, per [Wikipedia's history of the practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine_optimization) — you build pages a crawler can index, target queries with matching intent, earn links, and get paid in clicks and rank position.

**AEO** grew out of the voice-search and featured-snippet era: getting picked as the one answer Siri, Alexa, or a Google answer box reads aloud instead of listing ten options. The term found a second life once chat-based AI answers took over that same job.

**GEO** was coined specifically for that second job. "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization," posted to [arXiv in November 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), is the paper that named the discipline and tested which content tactics made a source more likely to be cited inside an LLM-generated answer — methods like adding statistics and citations improved visibility by as much as 40% in the paper's own benchmark. If you want the long version of either history, we've written full explainers on [what AEO actually is](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization) and on [generative engine optimization](/blog/generative-engine-optimization) specifically.

## Are AEO and GEO really the same discipline?

Yes — strip away the vendor branding and AEO and GEO optimize for the identical unit of success (a named mention inside a generated answer), reward the identical content (quotable claims, comparison tables, unambiguous entities), and get measured the identical way (how often you're mentioned when someone asks a buying question). The only real difference is which word the person in front of you learned first.

Teams with an SEO background tend to reach for "AEO," because it fits a naming pattern they already know. Teams closer to the research or ML side tend to say "GEO," because that's the term the original paper used. Neither group is optimizing for something functionally different — both are trying to raise the odds that an LLM includes their brand, unprompted, in its answer. Our full comparison of [AEO vs GEO](/blog/aeo-vs-geo) walks through the soft distinctions some vendors insist exist; in practice, they rarely survive contact with an actual buyer question. Treat AEO and GEO as one discipline and save your energy for the split that's real: AEO/GEO versus SEO.

## How do AEO, SEO, and GEO compare side by side?

The fastest way to see the real split is to compare what each discipline optimizes for, not what its acronym stands for. AEO and GEO both depend on how a [large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) decides what's worth mentioning, not on a ranking algorithm — which is why, lined up on the same dimensions, they come out nearly identical to each other and clearly different from SEO.

| Dimension | SEO | AEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit of success | Ranked position in a list of links | Named mention inside a generated answer | Named mention inside a generated answer |
| What you measure | Rank, impressions, organic clicks, CTR | Mention rate on buyer questions, sentiment, share of voice | Mention rate on buyer questions, sentiment, share of voice |
| What you build | Keyword-mapped pages, backlinks, page speed | Quotable claims, comparison tables, FAQ schema, entity clarity | Quotable claims, citable data, structured facts, entity clarity |
| Who owns it | SEO or organic marketing team | Content/marketing team | Same team, different job title |
| Typical failure | Ranking #1 for a term nobody buys on | Generic copy with nothing quotable | Generic copy with nothing quotable |

Notice the AEO and GEO columns are nearly identical. If you can swap one acronym for the other and lose no meaning, you're not looking at two disciplines — you're looking at one, described twice.

## Where do SEO and AEO/GEO genuinely overlap?

Good SEO fundamentals are necessary for AEO and GEO, but not sufficient — an AI that can't crawl, parse, or trust your site can't cite it, no matter how well-written your answer is. This is the one place all three disciplines truly share work, and it's why abandoning SEO to "do AEO instead" is a mistake.

Three things carry over directly:

- **Crawlability.** Most AI answer engines run their own crawlers, alongside the general web index they're trained or grounded on. If a page is blocked, slow, or hidden behind JavaScript the crawler can't render, it can't be cited — full stop. The [Robots Exclusion Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robots.txt), the standard your robots.txt file speaks, dates to 1994 and still governs whether these crawlers can even reach the page.
- **Entity clarity.** AI engines need to resolve who you are before they can recommend you — a consistent brand name, a clear "about" page, and an unambiguous web presence all reduce that friction.
- **Structured data.** Marking up FAQs, products, and articles with [schema.org](https://schema.org/) vocabulary — the shared standard Google, Bing, Yahoo, and Yandex built together starting in 2011 — makes facts easier for any machine reader to extract cleanly, whether that machine is a search ranking algorithm or an LLM's retrieval layer.

None of that guarantees a mention. It just removes the technical excuses that would otherwise make one impossible. For the deeper cut on where SEO and AEO/GEO diverge in day-to-day practice, see our dedicated [AEO vs SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo) comparison.

## Is it an SEO problem or an AEO/GEO problem?

Usually you can tell from the symptom alone: if you rank well but aren't named in AI answers, that's an AEO/GEO problem; if AI engines can't find or trust you at all, that's often still an SEO or technical problem underneath. Here's a fast triage table.

| Symptom | Likely problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| You rank top 3, but ChatGPT or Perplexity recommend competitors | AEO/GEO problem, not SEO | Add comparison content and quotable, citable claims |
| AI engines never mention you at all, even generically | Crawlability or trust problem | Check robots.txt and logs for AI crawlers; fix rendering and authority gaps |
| You're named in Google's AI Overview but not in ChatGPT | Source-diversity problem | Earn mentions on third-party sites the other engines actually pull from |
| Nobody mentions your brand in reviews, forums, or comparison posts | Earned-media / GEO problem | Invest in PR, comparison content, and community presence LLMs already quote |
| You rank well and get named in answers, but revenue hasn't moved | Not an AEO problem | Audit the post-mention experience; visibility isn't the same as conversion |

Run through that list before you spend a quarter rebuilding your SEO strategy for a problem SEO never caused — or vice versa.

## How do you measure AEO and GEO without lying to yourself?

Rank position and organic clicks tell you nothing about AEO/GEO performance, because there's often no rank and no click to count. The metric that matters instead is **mention rate**: how often your brand gets named, and how favorably, when someone asks an AI a real buying question in your category. A useful sanity check: if a named competitor consistently shows up on the same buyer questions and you don't, that gap is worth closing before you touch anything else.

Tracking that by hand means running the same buyer questions across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Google AI Overviews on a schedule, logging whether you show up, and comparing it to named competitors — which is tedious enough that most teams never do it consistently, and then wonder why their "AI visibility" is a guess instead of a number. ## Why is the AEO/GEO vocabulary still such a mess?

Because a wave of tool vendors — again, us included — have an incentive to make this sound like a newer, more separate category than it is. Nobody owns "AEO" or "GEO" the way the SEO industry eventually converged on shared terms; instead you'll see "AI SEO," "answer engine optimization," "generative engine optimization," and now "AI Overview optimization" all pitched as if they were distinct fields.

Don't wait for the industry to agree on a name before you act. The mechanics underneath every one of those labels are the same: be crawlable, be unambiguous about who you are, say things worth quoting, and get other credible sites saying them too. Get that right and it won't matter which acronym you filed it under.
