These prices describe subscriptions, not the final cost of every project. Credits are the working currency for model generation. A plan can look affordable while becoming expensive if you regenerate scenes, test models, or create longer sequences.

Do not ask only which plan is cheapest. Ask how much generation you need and how much iteration your workflow requires.

Occasional social work may fit Plus. Consistent creators with revisions usually fit Max. Generative is for projects where AI generation is the main production method, while Elite targets episodic or high-volume output.

## How does the credit system work?

Credits are spent for AI model use, charged at original API rates. Two videos of the same length can therefore have very different costs. Timeline-led editing may use no credits, while many generated clips can use thousands.

The key rules:

- eligible AI generation spends credits;
- timeline editing spends no credits;
- unused credits do not roll over;
- models consume different amounts;
- revisions and replacements multiply usage;
- generative projects may consume 3,000–30,000 credits.

If you write a script, assemble stock or existing footage, and edit on the timeline, credit use can stay low. If you generate many scenes, replace shots, explore styles, or rebuild sections, it rises quickly.

Seedance fast generation uses roughly 30 credits; a Nano Banana Pro image about 2.5. Small individual amounts add up across attempts. Project cost includes generations you keep and discard.

Estimate workflows in generations rather than finished videos.

## Which plan is best?

### Is Plus right for occasional creators?

Plus at $20 is the entry point for limited output and experimentation. It fits testing AI video, lighter projects, or occasional publishing.

The risk is practical capacity when frequent regeneration drives the workflow. A low subscription does not protect you when each scene takes several attempts.

### Is Max best for regular creators?

Max at $100 is the practical recommendation for regular creators. It is positioned for consistent production with normal generation, experimentation, and revisions.

It suits marketing videos, social content, explainers, and recurring channel work. These formats often require more than one pass without the full intensity of a short film.

Max works best with a repeatable process: lock the script, generate only needed shots, edit on the timeline where possible, and reserve expensive model calls for high-impact moments.

### When should you choose Generative?

Generative at $200 is designed for full short films and workflows where generated content is central.

A short film can involve many scenes, characters, treatments, and replacement shots. Even a short final video can require substantial production. The 3,000–30,000-credit project range shows the spread.

Choose Generative for extensive AI creation. If you need only a few generated elements in conventional editing, Max may fit better.

### Who needs Elite?

Elite at $1,000 is for episodic and high-volume work. It may fit when several projects are active, output is frequent, or generation belongs to a larger production operation.

For an occasional solo creator, the price is hard to justify unless commercial production clearly supports it. Choose for a repeatable workload, not one ambitious project.

## What does a real project cost?

### Scenario 1: timeline-led social video

Use existing assets, stock footage, or a prepared library, then apply AI only to scripting or selected elements. Since timeline editing costs no credits, generation stays comparatively low. This is most likely to fit Plus or Max.

### Scenario 2: marketing video with revisions

Generate a first cut, replace several shots, test alternate openings, and adjust visuals for audiences. Each revision adds model usage. If five scenes are regenerated twice, discarded attempts count too.

This makes Max more defensible than Plus. Its value is room for a normal revision cycle without making every change financially risky.

### Scenario 3: fully generative short film

A generative project may consume 3,000–30,000 credits. The range reflects models, shots, attempts, and continuity work.

Budget for development, not only export. Character consistency, camera variations, scene replacements, and failures add usage. Generative serves this work; several projects or an episodic pipeline make Elite relevant.

## How do revisions change the budget?

Revisions are the most underestimated cost. A first draft looks inexpensive until the work needed for an acceptable result appears.

Changing text or timing on the timeline uses no credits; replacing a generated shot can. Teams often revise selectively: a new opening, product angle, background, or transition.

Treat each generative shot as an experiment with replacement cost:

1. Lock the script before generating many scenes.
2. Use timeline editing when new media is unnecessary.
3. Generate lower-cost elements when sufficient.
4. Keep expensive generation for high-impact shots.
5. Review the sequence before regenerating clips.

Seedance fast at about 30 credits can help when speed matters; Nano Banana Pro images at about 2.5 credits can serve image-based elements. Choose by visual requirement, not only smallest number.

If you revise often, the lowest subscription is not always the lowest production cost. A plan supporting normal iteration can prevent premature compromises.

Track credits at the project level. Record the first-generation total, replacement shots, discarded attempts, and final approved output. After three projects, use the median rather than the best case to forecast next month. Because unused credits do not roll over, a realistic median is more useful than buying for a hypothetical peak.
