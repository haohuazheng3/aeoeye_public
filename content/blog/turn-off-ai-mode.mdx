Typing "turn off AI mode" into Google is its own small protest. You want the search engine you've used for years, not a chatbot summarizing it back to you before you've even scrolled. Here's the honest answer, plus what it means if your business depends on being found in that same search box.

## Can you turn off Google's AI Mode?

No — not completely, and not with one official switch. As of this writing, Google has not shipped a permanent, applies-everywhere toggle that turns off AI Overviews or AI Mode for good. What you can do is reduce how often an AI-generated answer shows up, by combining the "Web" results filter where Google offers it, account or Search Labs settings, third-party browser extensions, and query phrasing that's less likely to trigger a summary in the first place.

None of these are guarantees. Google decides when an AI answer appears, and it changes that logic often, sometimes without any announcement. Treat what follows as ways to tilt the odds in your favor, not a permanent fix you set once and forget.

## Ways to reduce AI answers in Google search

There's no single fix, but a handful of things reliably cut down how often you see an AI-generated answer: a built-in results filter, account settings, browser tools, and how you phrase the search itself.

- **Use the "Web" results tab.** On many searches, Google shows a "Web" filter next to "All," "Images," and "News" that strips out the AI-generated box and returns a classic, link-only results page. It's not labeled prominently, and as of this writing it doesn't show up for every query or in every market — but when it's there, it's the most reliable option.
- **Check your search settings or Search Labs.** Some accounts have experimental controls that dial back AI features. These are inconsistent across regions and account types, and Google has renamed, relocated, and removed similar toggles before. Don't assume a setting you find today will still be there — or still work the same way — next month.
- **Try a browser extension.** Several third-party extensions hide or collapse the AI Overview box by editing the page's code as it loads. They only work in the browser where they're installed, and they tend to break every time Google updates its page layout, which happens more often than most people notice.
- **Adjust how you phrase the query.** Narrow, specific, or clearly navigational searches — an exact model number, a phrase in quotation marks, a site-specific search — seem less likely to trigger a generated summary than broad, question-style searches. This isn't a documented rule, just an observable pattern, so don't count on it.

## Why there's no simple off switch

Google isn't withholding an off switch by accident. It doesn't want to give you one, because AI-generated answers aren't a side experiment anymore — they're the direction the entire product is moving, and have been since Google first tested this idea in 2023.

Keeping you inside Google's own answer box instead of sending you to a website is exactly what AI Mode is built to do. Every searcher who leaves for ChatGPT or Perplexity instead is someone Google isn't showing ads to. Building an easy, permanent, "take me back to 2019" button would work against that goal directly, so it isn't getting built — and nothing suggests that's about to change.

That's also why the controls that do exist feel scattered and half-hidden: a filter tab here, a Labs toggle there, a workaround that functions today and might not tomorrow. They're side doors, not a front door Google wants you using.

Here's how the main workarounds compare:

| Method | What it does | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| "Web" results filter/tab | Shows classic, link-only results and skips the AI-generated summary box | Not shown for every query or in every market; easy to overlook, not a default view |
| Search settings / Labs toggle | Dials back some AI features on accounts where it's offered | Availability varies by region and account type; Google has moved or removed these controls before |
| Browser extension | Hides or collapses the AI Overview box by editing the page as it loads | Third-party and browser-specific; breaks whenever Google changes its results layout |
| Query phrasing / operators | Narrow, specific, or navigational searches are less likely to trigger an AI summary | Inconsistent, with no published rule — Google decides case by case |

## AI Mode vs AI Overviews: what you're actually turning off

They're related, but not the same thing, and mixing them up is a big reason "turning off AI Mode" often doesn't do what people expect. AI Overviews are the AI-generated summary boxes that appear above the regular results on an ordinary search. AI Mode is a separate, more conversational search experience — closer to an open chat thread than a results page — that you can also land in directly.

Turning off, avoiding, or filtering one doesn't guarantee you've avoided the other; they're built differently and controlled through different settings. If you want a clear breakdown of what an AI Overview actually is and where its answer comes from, [what is an AI Overview](/blog/what-is-an-ai-overview) covers that in more depth.

## The other side: businesses can't opt out of being summarized

Here's the part most "how to turn off AI Mode" articles skip entirely: you can dodge AI-generated answers as a searcher, but your business cannot opt its own content out of being summarized by them.

There's no equivalent of a robots directive that says "don't include me in an AI Overview" the way there's a clean way to exclude a page from search results altogether. If your content is public and indexable, it's eligible to be pulled into an AI-generated answer — summarized, paraphrased, and shown to a searcher who may never click through to your site at all.

So the two sides of "turn off AI mode" pull in opposite directions. Searchers get a handful of imperfect workarounds to see fewer AI answers. Businesses get none to avoid being folded into them. The only real move left is adapting to be the source that gets cited when the summary is written, not hoping to be left out of it. ## What this means if you run a website

Wishing AI Mode away isn't a strategy — adapting to it is. Since you can't opt out of being summarized, the only lever left is influencing what that summary says when your brand comes up, and whether it mentions you at all.

The usual SEO fundamentals still matter here, but they're no longer sufficient by themselves. Getting cited inside an AI-generated answer rewards a lot of the same signals search engines have always valued — clear structure, direct answers, credible sourcing — just applied with an AI reader in mind, not only a human skimming down a results page. For specifics on what that looks like in practice, [how to rank in AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews) walks through it.

It also helps to know where this started. Google first tested AI-generated results under the name "Search Generative Experience," and understanding [what SGE was](/blog/what-is-sge) explains a lot about why today's AI Mode behaves the way it does. This has been the plan since 2023 — not a sudden pivot Google backed into.
