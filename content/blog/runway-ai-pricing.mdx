## What does Runway cost?

Runway offers a free plan with one-time credits, then monthly subscriptions with lower annual-equivalent rates. Value depends on model, format, and the number of attempts the workflow requires.

| Plan | Monthly price | Annual-equivalent price | Credits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Free | $0 | — | 125 one-time |
| Standard | $15 | $12/month | 625 monthly |
| Pro | $35 | $28/month | 2,250 monthly |
| Higher tier | $95 | $76/month | 9,500 monthly |

Free is a trial budget, not production capacity. Its 125 credits are useful for testing interface and output quality with a small proof of concept. Paid plans become clearer when expressed as seconds and images.

## What do Runway credits cost?

Credits have no universal cost per second because value changes by model and asset. A budget that looks large for one workflow can feel small for another.

Standard’s 625 credits at $15 are about 2.4 cents per subscription credit, or roughly 1.9 cents at the $12 annual equivalent. Pro’s 2,250 credits at $35 are about 1.6 cents, or 1.2 cents at $28. The 9,500-credit tier at $95 is about one cent, or 0.8 cents at $76.

Those calculations describe subscription economics, not finished output. Failed attempts and revisions consume the same underlying budget, so the cheapest credit can still produce an expensive approved clip.

## How many seconds do credits buy?

Model-specific conversion is the clearest plan comparison. Verified pricing gives these examples:

| Output | Standard: 625 credits | Pro: 2,250 credits |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gen-4.5 video | 52 seconds | 187 seconds |
| Gen-4 Turbo video | 104 seconds | 375 seconds |
| 1080p Gen-4 images | 78 images | 281 images |

Runway’s page lists the 9,500-credit tier as about 791 seconds of Gen-4.5, 1,900 seconds of Gen-4 Turbo, or 1,187 1080p Gen-4 images. Use the live page rather than extrapolating when models change.

These numbers are planning inputs, not guaranteed deliverable length. A 30-second commercial may require multiple variations, rejected artifacts, extended shots, transitions, and alternate openings. Measure usable seconds that survive editing.

## Are subscription and API credits separate?

Runway subscriptions and API usage are separate budgets. Subscription credits apply in the product experience; API usage costs $0.01 per credit.

**API cost = credits used × $0.01**

Ten thousand API credits therefore cost $100. Actual workflow spend still depends on model, duration, resolution, and attempts made by the application.

Subscriptions suit hands-on exploration. The API fits automated workflows, internal tools, batch systems, or product integration. If both are needed, budget them separately from the beginning.

## What revision multiplier should you use?

The biggest budgeting mistake is planning only for the final timeline. AI video is iterative, so finished output is one surviving version among attempts.

**Required credits = final-deliverable credits × revision multiplier**

A 2× multiplier means generating twice the final amount. Three or four times may be more realistic for exploratory work, difficult continuity, or multiple creative directions.

If 625 credits represent 52 seconds of Gen-4.5, Standard appears sufficient for a 30-second project. With a 2× multiplier, practical finished capacity is closer to 26 seconds; at 3× it is around 17 seconds.

The central question is not generated duration. It is how many seconds remain after revision.

## How do you build a campaign budget?

Start with the campaign rather than a plan card. Write down finished seconds, concepts, revision multiplier, model, resolution, alternate versions, and contingency.

| Planning item | Your estimate |
|---|---:|
| Finished video seconds | _____ |
| Visual concepts | _____ |
| Revision multiplier | _____× |
| Model and format | _____ |
| Alternate versions | _____ |
| Contingency allowance | _____% |

Calculate a theoretical minimum, working budget multiplied by revisions, and safe budget including feedback and late changes.

Run a storyboard test first: generate a close-up, moving-camera shot, subject interaction, and difficult transition. This reveals whether the revision multiplier is closer to 1.5×, 2×, or 4×. One impressive clip does not prove a production workflow.

## Who should choose each plan?

Free fits first-time evaluation. Standard fits controlled small projects. Pro fits regular production and multiple concepts. The higher tier fits high-volume schedules that genuinely use 9,500 credits.

Free should answer whether the interface and output style fit at all. Standard suits short social clips, storyboards, and occasional client work when the revision multiplier stays controlled. Pro creates more room for variations and recurring production, but still needs disciplined shot planning. The highest tier belongs to teams with enough scheduled work to consume its allowance rather than merely admiring a lower per-credit rate.

Plan fit also depends on when feedback arrives. A solo creator controlling the brief can often keep revisions lower. Agency and stakeholder work needs capacity for alternative directions, delayed feedback, and changed requirements. Put that contingency in the plan calculation before selecting a tier.

Paying for unused credits is not savings. Likewise, “unlimited” language should be treated as a condition question, not a promise of unlimited finished video. Verify applicable models, speeds, and features before relying on it.

### How should a first project be scoped?

Scope a first project around a short sequence that contains the hardest visual conditions expected later. A friendly five-second demo with no continuity is a weak predictor of a 30-second campaign with characters, camera motion, brand details, and product accuracy.

Keep an attempt ledger. Record model, duration, credits, result status, reason for rejection, and whether the clip reached the final edit. After ten to twenty representative generations, the acceptance rate becomes a defensible input. Divide generated seconds by accepted seconds to calculate the observed revision multiplier rather than guessing it.

If a project requires client approval, separate internal exploration from client-requested revisions. That distinction shows whether spend is driven by the tool’s output quality, an unclear brief, or a decision process that keeps changing. Buying a larger plan cannot repair the last two problems.

## What is the verdict?

Runway pricing is attractive when capacity matches the workflow, not merely a headline credit count. Standard is the sensible controlled starting point; Pro fits when revisions become the bottleneck; the higher tier requires consistently high usage.

Estimate usable seconds, multiply by revisions, and keep subscription and API budgets separate. Buy the footage that survives revision. If the separate problem is whether AI buyers can discover the resulting brand, compare [AEOeye pricing](/pricing) on that outcome.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Runway free?

Yes. Free costs $0 with 125 one-time credits and is best for testing models and workflow.

### How many seconds do Runway credits provide?

It depends on the model. 625 credits represent about 52 seconds of Gen-4.5 or 104 seconds of Gen-4 Turbo; 2,250 represent about 187 or 375 seconds.

### Does Runway offer unlimited generations?

Do not assume unlimited language means unlimited finished footage. Check plan terms, model availability, speed, and conditions.

### Are Runway API credits included with a subscription?

No. API credits are priced separately at $0.01 per credit and need their own budget.
