## SEO vs AEO vs GEO: the short answer

SEO gets you ranked in search results. AEO gets you chosen as the direct answer inside answer engines and voice assistants. GEO gets you cited or recommended inside generative AI answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. All three chase the same goal — visibility — just on three different surfaces, with three different scoreboards.

Here's the part most explainers skip: these aren't three competing strategies you pick between. They're three layers of the same problem, and each one is mostly built on top of the one before it. SEO ranks the page. AEO makes the page extractable. GEO makes the brand citable even when there's no page being extracted at all.

Confusing the three is understandable — the industry minted all three acronyms within about two years of each other, and plenty of agencies use them interchangeably because it's easier to sell one vague pitch than explain three specific ones. Don't let that sloppiness become your strategy. Each term has a distinct surface, a distinct unit of success, and mostly distinct tools for measuring it. Get the map straight once, and you stop wasting budget optimizing for the wrong scoreboard.

## Defining each one clearly

Each acronym optimizes for a different moment in a search: SEO wins the results page, AEO wins the answer box, and GEO wins the sentence an AI model writes about you. Here's what each one actually means.

**SEO — Search Engine Optimization.** Getting your pages to rank on Google, Bing, and other traditional search engines. The primary surface is the organic results list — the ten blue links, plus maps, image, and video packs. Success looks like a ranking position and a click.

**AEO — Answer Engine Optimization.** Structuring content so it gets pulled out and served as the direct answer, instead of just linked to. The primary surface is featured snippets, "position zero" boxes, and voice assistants like Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant — plus AI Overviews, which straddle AEO and GEO. Success looks like being the answer, not just a result.

**GEO — Generative Engine Optimization.** Making your brand and content citable enough that a generative AI tool references, quotes, or recommends it inside a synthesized answer. The primary surface is chat-based tools — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude — where there's no results page at all, just a paragraph a model wrote from what it read or retrieved. Success looks like your name showing up in someone else's answer.

If you want the fuller background on how AI search fits into all this, [what is AI search](/blog/what-is-ai-search) covers the landscape these three disciplines all operate in.

## The three side by side

The clearest way to see the differences is side by side, because the three disciplines share a goal but diverge on almost everything else — surface, tactic, content shape, and how you'd even know you're winning.

| Dimension | SEO | AEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | Search engine results pages | Featured snippets, answer boxes, voice assistants | AI chat tools (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot) |
| Unit of success | Ranking position and clicks | Being extracted as "the" answer | Being cited, named, or recommended |
| Core tactic | Keywords, backlinks, technical crawlability | Answer-first structure, schema markup, concise direct answers | Entity clarity, structured facts, third-party mentions |
| Content shape | Long-form pages matched to search intent | Short, extractable Q&A blocks near the top of the page | Fact-dense, source-backed content a model can quote accurately |
| How you measure it | Search Console, rank trackers | Snippet win rate, voice/answer share | Prompt testing, AI-visibility audits, brand-mention tracking |

Notice what doesn't change: all three still need a real, crawlable, well-written page underneath them. None of these are shortcuts around content quality — they're different filters content has to pass through afterward.

## How much do they actually overlap?

Enormously. All three reward the same underlying asset — crawlable, authoritative, well-structured content — so a page built right for one is already halfway built for the other two. This is the part the "SEO is dead, AEO is the future" crowd conveniently leaves out.

AEO and GEO in particular are close siblings, not opposites. Both want the same things from a page:

- Clear, factual, current content
- Structured markup and clean semantic HTML
- Direct answers stated in plain language, not buried in a wall of text
- Real sourcing an AI can point to instead of just paraphrasing a guess
- A consistent, unambiguous brand and entity identity

Where they split is destination and mechanism. AEO wins a specific UI element — a snippet, a box, a spoken response — determined largely by how you structure one page. GEO depends on being retrieved or remembered by a model synthesizing an answer from dozens of sources at once, with no guarantee any single page gets credit. That's a meaningfully different game, and it's why [AEO vs GEO](/blog/aeo-vs-geo) deserves its own breakdown.

SEO is the foundation both of them stand on, full stop. An AI model — whether it's powering a featured snippet or a ChatGPT answer — overwhelmingly favors content that's already indexed, trusted, and earning organic visibility. Pages that don't exist in crawlable, authoritative form don't get cited into existence just because you'd like them to be. For the two-way version of this argument, see [SEO vs AEO](/blog/seo-vs-aeo).

Here's the blunt take: most of the "SEO vs AEO vs GEO" content flooding the internet right now is marketing, not strategy. Agencies rebranded existing SEO service lines as "AEO packages" the moment ChatGPT got popular, because a new acronym sells better than admitting the work hasn't changed as much as the pitch deck implies. The disciplines are real and the distinctions matter — but the war between them is mostly noise.

## A unified playbook

Don't run three separate strategies. Build one content workflow that layers SEO fundamentals, then answer-first structure and schema for AEO, then entity clarity and citability for GEO — and measure all three surfaces at once instead of treating them as separate projects with separate budgets.

In practice, that means four steps on every page you build or rebuild:

1. **Lay the SEO foundation.** Fix crawlability and indexation, map the page to real search intent and keywords, build internal links, earn backlinks. Nothing downstream works without this.
2. **Add AEO structure.** Open each section with a direct, 40-60 word answer before the elaboration. Add FAQ and HowTo schema. Use a clean H2/H3 hierarchy so a machine — or a skimming human — can find the answer in seconds.
3. **Build in GEO citability.** Define your entities clearly and consistently — same brand name, same product names, every time. Cite real sources. Keep facts current, because outdated pages get quietly dropped from what models retrieve and trust.
4. **Measure across all three surfaces.** Search Console covers SEO. Snippet and voice-share tracking covers AEO. Neither covers GEO — for that you need prompt-based testing or an [AI-visibility audit](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility) built to check what AI tools actually say about you.

One workflow, three payoffs. You're not choosing which discipline to serve when you write a page — you're deciding how many of the three you're leaving on the table.

## Which should you invest in?

Not either/or — sequence them. Fix your SEO fundamentals first, layer in answer-first structure second, then start actively measuring AI-citation performance third. The right mix shifts over time as more of your buyers' questions get resolved inside an AI answer instead of a click.

If your site has real crawlability problems, or your core pages don't rank at all, GEO work is premature. A model that can't find or trust your page won't cite it either — you'd be optimizing for an answer engine that's never going to read you. Fix the foundation first.

If your fundamentals are solid but you're consistently losing snippets, voice answers, and AI Overviews to competitors who say the same thing you do but say it more clearly, invest in AEO next. That's usually a content-structure problem, not a content-quality problem, and it's often the cheapest of the three to fix.

Once both of those are in reasonable shape, GEO stops being optional. An increasing share of top-of-funnel questions never produce a click at all — they get answered directly inside a chat window, and you either get named in that answer or you don't. Ignoring that surface because it's newer and harder to measure doesn't make it smaller.

## Measure what Search Console can't show you

Search Console will tell you exactly how you're doing on SEO — impressions, positions, clicks. It has nothing to say about whether Claude recommends you over a competitor, whether Perplexity even knows your product exists, or whether the AI Overview above your perfectly-ranked page just answered the question without sending anyone your way.
