## How do you get cited by ChatGPT?

You get cited by ChatGPT when it runs a live web search for a query, finds your page among the most relevant and easiest to pull an answer from, and links to it in the response. That's the whole mechanism — no secret schema tag, no backdoor switch.

Citation is a retrieval event, not a memory event. When ChatGPT answers purely from training data, it might name your brand — that's a mention, and it works differently. When it opens a search, pulls in current pages, and grounds its answer in what it finds, the sources it shows are citations. That happens on queries where the model needs fresh information: current events, pricing, recent reviews, best-of comparisons, anything time-sensitive or too specific to trust from memory alone.

If your target query triggers a live search, the job is simple to state and harder to execute: be the page that gets retrieved, and be the page that's easiest to lift a clean answer from once it is.

## Mentioned vs cited: the difference

A mention is ChatGPT naming your brand from what it learned in training — usually with no link, no timestamp, no way to verify it in real time. A citation is a linked source pulled from a live web search, shown to the user as a clickable reference. Chasing one doesn't automatically get you the other.

Mentions live inside the model's weights. If your brand showed up often enough in the text ChatGPT trained on — press coverage, forum threads, comparison posts, your own site — it can recall your name when someone asks a relevant question. You can't audit that in real time, and you can't fix it this week; it changes on whatever schedule the next training run follows.

Citations live outside the model. They show up when ChatGPT decides a question needs current, verifiable information and goes to fetch it. What comes back is a short list of URLs the answer leans on — the small linked source cards you see under or beside the response. That's what this article is about.

A brand can be mentioned constantly and cited rarely, or barely known by name yet cited often because one page nails a specific question. This piece stays narrow: earning the linked citation specifically. For a closer look at how the mention side works on its own, see our piece on [brand mentions](/blog/brand-mentions).

## How ChatGPT picks citation sources

ChatGPT's search layer retrieves pages the way a search engine does — ranked by relevance to the query — then favors the ones that give a clear, direct, well-supported answer. Two things decide whether you make the cut: can the system find you as relevant, and can it extract a confident answer once it does.

Nobody outside OpenAI has the exact ranking formula, and anyone who claims they do is guessing. But the shape of it isn't a mystery — it rhymes with how search retrieval has worked for years, layered under a model that has to actually read and use what it finds.

Two filters, in practice:

- **Relevance and retrievability.** The page has to surface for the query in the first place. That means it's indexed, topically on-point, and carries enough authority on the subject to rank near the top of the retrieval set instead of buried on page four.
- **Extractability.** Once retrieved, the model has to pull a confident answer out of the page fast. A clear paragraph that directly answers the question beats a page that makes the reader — or the model — dig for it.

Neither substitutes for the other. A high-authority page that buries its answer under padding loses to a smaller page that states the answer in the first two sentences. A beautifully clear page that nothing links to or trusts on the topic never gets retrieved to begin with.

## The playbook to earn citations

Five moves earn citations: answer the exact question early, make sure ChatGPT's crawlers can reach and index you, structure the page for clean extraction, keep the content current, and build enough topical authority that you're the page retrieved for that query in the first place.

1. **Answer the exact question directly and early.** Put the real answer in the first sentence or two of the relevant section, in plain language. Don't make the model — or a human — dig past your brand story to find it.
2. **Be crawlable and indexed.** If OpenAI's crawlers can't reach the page, it doesn't exist for citation purposes. Check specifically for OAI-SearchBot — the crawler behind ChatGPT's search and citation feature — plus GPTBot and ChatGPT-User; a robots.txt block on any of them, or a page that errors out instead of returning clean content, kills your odds before extraction is even considered.
3. **Structure for clean extraction.** Use headers that match how people ask the question, short paragraphs, and lists for anything with steps or criteria. This is the same discipline covered in [what makes content quotable by AI](/answers/what-makes-content-quotable-by-ai) — write like you expect to be lifted out of context, because you do.
4. **Keep content current.** A page that hasn't been touched since it was published is exactly the kind of page a live search is designed to route around, especially for anything price-, version-, or date-sensitive. Update the numbers and examples on a real schedule.
5. **Build topical authority so you're retrieved for the query.** None of the above matters if you're never in the retrieval set. Publish enough depth on the topic — a cluster, not one lonely page — that your domain lands as a trusted source before extractability even gets judged.

Quick reference:

| Factor | Raises citation odds | Lowers them |
|---|---|---|
| Answer placement | Direct answer in the section's first sentence | Answer buried after intros, stories, or disclaimers |
| Crawler access | robots.txt allows OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot | Bots blocked, or the page errors/redirects |
| Page structure | Clear headers, short paragraphs, scannable lists | Wall-of-text with no headers, answer scattered throughout |
| Freshness | Dates and examples updated on a real cadence | Stale numbers, dead links, outdated examples |
| Topical depth | A cluster of pages covering multiple angles | One isolated page competing against full-coverage sites |
| Specificity | A precise answer to the exact query asked | A generic overview that half-answers many queries |

## Why you're not getting cited

Four causes explain almost every missing citation: ChatGPT never retrieves your page for the query, the answer is buried too deep to extract, your crawler access is blocked, or the content is too stale to compete with fresher pages. Fix the retrieval problem before touching anything else.

- **Not retrieved in the first place.** If your domain has no track record on the topic, or the specific page is thin, it never enters the pool ChatGPT chooses from. That's an authority and coverage problem, not a formatting one — clean headers don't fix a page nobody trusts to rank.
- **The answer is buried.** Your page might genuinely be relevant and get retrieved, but if the real answer sits in paragraph nine behind three paragraphs of company background, the model may pull the answer from a competitor who leads with it instead.
- **Your crawler is blocked.** This happens more than people expect — an old robots.txt rule, a security plugin, or a CDN setting meant to stop scrapers can quietly block the bots that make citation possible. Worth checking directly rather than assuming you're fine.
- **The content is stale.** A page that was the best answer a while ago and hasn't been touched since is exactly what a live search is built to route around, especially once a fresher page shows up.

## Tracking your citations

Track citations by running the actual queries you care about in ChatGPT with search on, watching whether your domain shows up in the linked sources, and repeating that on a fixed schedule — weekly or monthly, not once and done.

There's no dashboard inside ChatGPT that emails you when you get cited — you have to go looking. Pick the questions your target customer would actually ask, not just your brand name, and run them the way a real user would, with search enabled. Note which domains show up, not just whether yours does; a competitor beating you on the exact same query is the clearest signal of what to fix next.

Do this on a cadence, because retrieval isn't static. A page that gets cited this month can lose that spot the moment a fresher or better-structured competitor publishes.
