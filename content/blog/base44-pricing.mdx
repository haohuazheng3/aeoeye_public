## How much does Base44 cost?

Base44 has a free plan and four paid tiers, with annual-equivalent prices from $16 to $160 per month. The important difference is not only the subscription price, but also the monthly allowance of message and integration credits.

| Plan | Annual-equivalent price | Message credits/month | Integration credits/month | Best starting point |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Free | $0 | 25 | 100 | Exploring the product |
| Starter | $16 | 100 | 2,000 | Small prototype or solo experiment |
| Builder | $40 | 250 | 10,000 | Active solo launch |
| Pro | $80 | 500 | 20,000 | Larger app or heavier iteration |
| Elite | $160 | 1,200 | 50,000 | High-volume production work |

Annual billing advertises 20% savings, but that headline is not a cost-per-app promise. Consumption varies by action and model, and connected services may charge separately.

## What do message and integration credits buy?

Message credits generally support interactions with Base44, while integration credits support work involving connected services. Both are usage allowances, and one request should not be assumed to consume a fixed number of credits.

Message demand rises when you repeatedly revise layouts, fix behavior, change copy, or add workflows. Integration demand depends on how many outside systems your app uses and how frequently it interacts with them.

Base44’s rules are documented at [docs.base44.com/Account-and-billing/Credits](https://docs.base44.com/Account-and-billing/Credits). Review them before estimating usage because credit use varies and unused credits do not roll over.

The practical approach is to:

- Estimate messages and integrations separately.
- Track usage during a normal month.
- Leave room for debugging and revisions.
- Treat credit consumption as variable.

## Which plan fits your stage?

The best plan depends on iteration frequency and integration demand, not simply on the size of your idea. A prototype, solo launch, team workflow, and production app can create very different usage patterns.

### Is Free enough for a prototype?

Free is suitable for exploring Base44 before you know whether the workflow fits. Its 25 message credits and 100 integration credits can support an initial test, but may leave little room for extended refinement.

Use Free to sketch a concept or evaluate the builder. Do not treat a short experiment as a complete forecast for a production-like workflow.

### Is Starter enough for a solo launch?

Starter is a sensible first paid tier for a small solo project with moderate iteration. It includes 100 message credits and 2,000 integration credits monthly.

Choose it when your app is defined, integrations are limited, and you will monitor usage. If you expect frequent redesigns or several connected services, the lower price may simply lead to an earlier upgrade.

### When does Builder make sense?

Builder is the center of the range for an active solo launch or a serious prototype becoming a usable product. Its 250 message credits and 10,000 integration credits provide substantially more headroom than Starter.

Choose Builder when you expect regular changes, meaningful integrations, or a launch period in which product decisions are still moving. It is the plan I would examine first for a founder who wants room to learn without immediately choosing the highest tiers.

### Who needs Pro or Elite?

Pro and Elite are for higher-volume iteration and integration demand. Pro includes 500 message credits and 20,000 integration credits; Elite provides 1,200 and 50,000.

Pro is easier to justify when a project is already active and repeatedly improving. Elite requires a clearer usage case, such as frequent changes or substantial integration activity. Do not select it merely because it is the largest plan.

## What happens when credits run out?

When credits run out, check the current account behavior and upgrade terms rather than assume unlimited continuation. Base44’s documentation is the source of truth for restricted actions and available upgrades.

Running out once does not automatically mean Base44 is too expensive. It may mean your plan was chosen before your workflow stabilized, or that one unusually intensive build cycle distorted the month.

Before upgrading, ask:

- Was the usage normal or exceptional?
- Were repeated prompts caused by unclear requirements?
- Are integrations consuming more credits than expected?
- Would a lighter workflow reduce next month’s demand?
- Is interruption more expensive than the next tier?

Upgrade based on a recurring pattern, not panic from one large month.

## What costs sit outside Base44?

Connected third-party services can bill directly and separately from your Base44 subscription. The plan price therefore does not represent your complete operating budget.

This matters for apps using outside systems for data, communications, payments, automation, storage, or other functionality. Base44 may provide the connection, but the provider can impose its own pricing, limits, and billing terms.

Track each external service separately:

- Provider name and billing account.
- Subscription, usage, or combined pricing.
- Whether charges grow with users, requests, or data.
- Any free allowance or usage limit.

Read Base44’s terms alongside every connected provider’s terms. A low Base44 tier can still support an expensive application if external usage is high.

## How do annual billing and upgrades work?

Annual billing advertises 20% savings but requires a longer commitment. It makes the most sense after your usage pattern is understood and the commitment remains comfortable if the project changes.

Monthly billing preserves flexibility during discovery. Once your average usage is predictable and you expect to stay on the same tier, annual billing may improve the effective monthly price.

Before choosing annual billing, verify:

- The exact checkout charge.
- Whether the display is annual-equivalent pricing.
- How plan changes are handled.
- Whether credits expire at the monthly reset.
- That third-party billing remains separate.

If you are still discovering the product, preserve flexibility. If usage is predictable, compare the annual savings with the risk of paying for capacity you will not use.

## What budget worksheet should you use?

A useful budget separates the Base44 plan, message usage, integration usage, and external services. This avoids comparing only the monthly subscription price.

Track these fields each month:

1. Base44 plan and billing mode.
2. Included and used message credits.
3. Included and used integration credits.
4. Each connected service’s bill.
5. Whether usage approached a limit.
6. Next month’s expected workload.

Then compare actual usage with the available plans. If you repeatedly approach a limit, the higher tier may provide better predictability. If usage was caused by one unusual build sprint, waiting for another normal month may be wiser.

## Which plan is the rational default?

Builder is the rational default for an active solo project because it balances iteration room and integration capacity without assuming the highest-volume workload. Free is right for evaluation, while Starter suits a contained project.

My position is to choose by observed behavior:

- **Free:** evaluate the workflow.
- **Starter:** run a small prototype or light solo launch.
- **Builder:** support regular development and meaningful integrations.
- **Pro:** handle consistently high usage or a larger workflow.
- **Elite:** serve clearly established, high-volume demand.
