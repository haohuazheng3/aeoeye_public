## What does a GeoStars credit measure?

One GeoStars credit represents one successful prompt-model query. Failed attempts cost zero credits, so the credit balance is tied to successful query units rather than every attempted request.

That distinction matters when comparing plans. A headline prompt number can sound large while hiding how many platforms, models, or repeated runs it represents. GeoStars’ listed daily equivalents are expressed across all five platforms, while the credit definition gives you a more precise unit for forecasting.

For example, a monitoring brief might contain a fixed set of buyer questions that runs across several platforms each day. The relevant estimate is successful prompt-model queries per day multiplied by the active days in the billing period. Keep failed attempts separate from the forecast because the vendor says they cost zero credits, but do not assume that every other type of activity is free unless the documentation says so.

The credits are a capacity measure, not an accuracy guarantee. They do not establish how often a brand will be mentioned, cited, recommended, or visible. Use the [AI visibility metrics dictionary](/blog/ai-visibility-metrics-dictionary) to define the outcomes your reporting must distinguish from raw query volume.

The last column divides monthly price by included credits and multiplies by 1,000. It excludes VAT, top-ups, failed attempts, extra API models, and usage patterns. Treat recommended frequency as a planning reference, not an accuracy claim or required schedule.

## How much does annual GeoStars billing save?

Annual billing saves $220 on Single Brand, $500 on Growth, $870 on Team, $1,280 on Agency, and $2,800 on Agency Plus compared with twelve monthly payments.

| Plan | Monthly price × 12 | Annual price | Annual saving |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Single Brand | $95 × 12 = $1,140 | $920 | $220 |
| Growth | $200 × 12 = $2,400 | $1,900 | $500 |
| Team | $360 × 12 = $4,320 | $3,450 | $870 |
| Agency | $540 × 12 = $6,480 | $5,200 | $1,280 |
| Agency Plus | $1,050 × 12 = $12,600 | $9,800 | $2,800 |

The annual option is financially attractive when your monitoring program is already approved for a full year and the included credit level is credible. Monthly billing costs more over twelve months but gives a shorter initial commitment for a new workflow. This arithmetic does not establish renewal, cancellation, refund, or price-lock rights. Confirm those terms directly before committing.

## How do GeoStars top-ups work?

GeoStars lists four top-up packages, and each top-up is valid only through the end of the month. The published options are:

| Top-up credits | Price | Approximate price per 1,000 credits |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | $120 | $12.00 |
| 25,000 | $270 | $10.80 |
| 50,000 | $480 | $9.60 |
| 100,000 | $840 | $8.40 |

The expiry rule changes the calculation: unused top-up credits should not be treated as a long-term reserve. Compare the top-up price with your remaining days in the current month, not simply with the plan’s monthly unit price. Buying a large package late in the month can leave less time to consume it, while a recurring plan upgrade may be more predictable for sustained demand.

## Which GeoStars plan fits an agency?

Agency is the sensible starting comparison for a team that needs 75,000 credits, 500 listed daily prompts across five platforms, and white-label reporting without moving immediately to Agency Plus. Agency Plus doubles the included credits to 150,000 and raises the listed daily equivalent to 1,000, which may suit a larger client portfolio or denser monitoring schedule.

Single Brand and Growth can suit one brand or a smaller program, while Team sits between Growth and Agency for a broader internal operation. Because all plans advertise unlimited projects and users, the choice should be driven by query capacity and workflow—not by assuming a seat cap is the differentiator.

For an agency, map the plan to concrete operating requirements:

- How many client brands and projects need separate reporting?
- How many successful prompt-model queries must run each day?
- Which five platforms are in the standard coverage assumption?
- How much capacity will client-specific prompt sets consume?
- Do white-label reports need to be delivered on a fixed schedule?
- What happens when a client’s usage spikes near month end?

Choose the smallest plan that supports repeatable coverage and exportable evidence.

## What should you check before buying?

Verify the credit and expiry mechanics against a sample month before paying. Count successful prompt-model queries, identify the five platforms in your plan, and model both ordinary demand and a month-end spike. Then check whether failed attempts are clearly shown as zero-cost events in the reporting your team will use.

Ask about the seven-day trial, including what it requires, when it ends, and how billing proceeds. Prices exclude VAT, so your procurement team should confirm the applicable tax treatment rather than inserting an assumed rate. Likewise, do not infer cancellation, renewal, refund, or contract rights from the monthly and annual price display.

Extra API models also change the arithmetic. Claude costs 5 credits per query and Grok costs 10 credits per query, so the one-credit-per-successful-query rule does not apply universally to those models. Forecast their usage separately instead of multiplying every request by one credit.

Finally, request clarity on exports, white-label report permissions, platform coverage, and any Enterprise terms. Compare successful query units and expiry, not headline prompt labels alone.
