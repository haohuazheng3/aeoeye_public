## What does Gamma cost?

Gamma offers Free, Plus, Pro, and Ultra. The paid figures below are per-seat annual equivalents from the verified pricing page; Gamma advertises savings of up to 28% with annual billing. Do not treat them as monthly-toggle prices.

| Plan | Annual-equivalent price | Included credits | Best fit |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Free | $0 | 400 signup credits | Limited evaluation |
| Plus | $9/seat/month; $108 annually | 1,000 monthly | Regular individual work |
| Pro | $18/seat/month; $216 annually | 4,000 monthly | Frequent creation and revision |
| Ultra | $90/seat/month; $1,080 annually | 20,000 monthly | Measured high-volume production |

The key distinction is not simply “more AI.” It is how much creation and revision the workflow requires. Occasional users can evaluate Free; weekly presentation work may justify Plus or Pro.

Ultra is a serious production-capacity purchase. At $90 per seat monthly on an annual equivalent, it needs evidence rather than enthusiasm. Recheck [Gamma pricing](https://gamma.app/pricing) and the [help center](https://help.gamma.app/) before purchasing.

## How do Gamma credits work?

Credits are the usage unit separating Gamma from a conventional editor. Free includes 400 signup credits; Plus 1,000 monthly; Pro 4,000; Ultra 20,000.

Free is useful for first evaluation, not unlimited work. Pro provides four times Plus’s allowance, and Ultra five times Pro’s, but that does not mean every user needs the largest plan.

Usage changes with content type and revision pattern. A single polished deck may be inexpensive compared with one that goes through many generated sections, visual alternatives, and brand corrections.

Do not ask for a universal “decks per month” estimate. Track AI generations per approved deck, revisions after first review, audience variants, and rebuilds. Your own approval workflow is more useful than a generic promise.

## What does each seat cost?

A paid seat costs $108 annually for Plus, $216 for Pro, or $1,080 for Ultra at stated equivalents. Two Pro seats are $432 annually; five are $1,080.

Simple arithmetic is not a business case. Each seat should belong to someone who creates, revises, or approves enough work to benefit.

Start with people facing the most presentation friction: sales teams, marketers, consultants, founders, enablement teams, and anyone repeatedly turning briefs into drafts. Occasional reviewers may not need the same paid access; confirm current collaboration rules before rollout.

## Which costs appear after the first draft?

First-draft speed is visible, but brand alignment, factual corrections, stakeholder feedback, exports, and quality checks often consume more time. Gamma is valuable when it reduces those later steps.

Ask whether output reflects the brand, reaches review quickly, exports appropriately, preserves versions, and avoids a full rebuild in another app. A fast draft requiring extensive cleanup is less valuable than a slightly slower one that receives approval sooner.

Export compatibility matters when the final asset must leave Gamma. Test the actual handoff to a client, executive, event, or office-file workflow—not just the editor demo.

## How do you calculate cost per approved deck?

Cost per approved deck is more useful than cost per generated draft.

**Cost per approved deck = monthly plan cost ÷ approved decks that month**

Add meaningful manual cleanup time when comparing Gamma with current tools. A $9 Plus seat producing two approved decks and saving hours may be inexpensive; a $90 Ultra seat used for one lightly edited deck may be difficult to justify.

Measure time from brief to reviewable draft, revision rounds, layout correction, export preparation, and first review to approval. These signals turn pricing into an operating decision.

## How should teams and agencies buy Gamma?

Teams should separate generation volume from review volume. Not every reviewer needs the same plan and not every producer needs Ultra.

An agency may benefit from Pro when it creates multiple client variants and frequent revisions. Ultra may fit a high-volume function, but its $1,080 annual-equivalent seat cost demands a clear answer for who consumes 20,000 monthly credits and why.

Include client friction. Fast generation followed by manual reformatting for every client may create little savings. A repeatable brief, review, brand-check, and export process can make a higher plan valuable.

For internal teams, define who creates, edits, approves, and merely comments before expanding seats.

## How do you run a one-week pilot?

A one-week pilot can support a plan decision when it uses real presentation work rather than a demo.

Record the source brief, time to reviewable draft, AI generations, major revisions, correction time, stakeholder rounds, export issues, and time to approval.

If more credits let the team test alternatives and reach approval faster, an upgrade has a rationale. If credit is barely used and approval time does not improve, the larger plan is premature.

## When do Slides or PowerPoint win?

Gamma is not automatically a replacement for Google Slides or PowerPoint. Established templates, detailed manual control, office file requirements, complex charts, and familiar approval workflows can keep conventional tools ahead.

Gamma’s advantage is turning notes or research into a structured presentation. A practical workflow can use Gamma for early iteration, then refine elsewhere. Judge the complete handoff cost.

## Which Gamma plan is worth it?

Choose Free to test. Choose Plus when one creator regularly needs 1,000 monthly credits. Choose Pro for frequent or revision-heavy work. Choose Ultra only after 20,000-credit demand or approval-time savings are measured.

My view is simple: Gamma pricing is attractive when it removes revision friction, not when it merely generates a quick first draft. Start with the smallest plan supporting a real workflow; upgrade only when demand and approval savings are visible. ### Is Gamma Plus worth it?
