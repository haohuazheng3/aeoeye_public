## How many people use ChatGPT in 2026?

ChatGPT had 800 million weekly active users as of October 2025 — double the 400 million it reported just eight months earlier. That's the headline number, and the trajectory behind it is what should really get your attention: this isn't a plateauing product, it's one still compounding.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman announced the 800 million weekly active user figure at OpenAI's DevDay on October 6, 2025 [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/sam-altman-says-chatgpt-has-hit-800m-weekly-active-users/). Rewind to February 2025 and weekly actives stood at 400 million, up 30% in under three months at the time [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/openais-weekly-active-users-surpass-400-million-2025-02-20/). Volume tells the same story from a different angle: users now send 2.5 billion prompts per day — roughly 29,000 every second — based on data OpenAI shared with Axios [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/chatgpt-users-send-2-5-billion-prompts-a-day/).

Site traffic backs all of this up independently. chatgpt.com grew from 2.6 billion to 5.7 billion monthly visits — a 117% jump — in twelve months, making it the 6th-largest website on the entire internet, ahead of properties that have been fighting for search rankings for two decades [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/insights/ai-news/chatgpt-turns-3/). Four independent measurements — announced users, prior users, prompt volume, and third-party traffic tracking — all point the same direction. ## Who uses ChatGPT?

34% of U.S. adults have used ChatGPT — about double the share in 2023 — and adoption skews heavily toward younger adults. That's from a Pew Research Center survey of 5,123 U.S. adults conducted February 24–March 2, 2025, one of the more rigorous public polls on the topic [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/).

The generational split is stark: 58% of adults under 30 have used it, compared with 25% of those 50–64 and just 10% of adults 65 and older [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/). That's not a gentle taper across age brackets — it's a cliff. The under-30 group is using it at nearly six times the rate of the oldest group.

Our take: this stopped being a "tech early adopter" tool a while ago. When more than half of under-30s have used it, ChatGPT is closer to a mainstream search habit than a novelty — and it's the group entering the workforce, forming brand preferences, and making first-time purchase decisions right now. Marketers who still treat it like a side experiment, something you check on quarterly instead of monitor weekly, are already behind on reaching exactly the audience they'll need in five years.

## What do people use ChatGPT for?

About 80% of ChatGPT conversations fall into three buckets — practical guidance, information-seeking, and writing help — and more than 70% of all usage is non-work. That's according to an NBER working paper analyzing real ChatGPT conversation data at scale, one of the more detailed independent looks at actual usage patterns rather than self-reported survey answers [NBER Working Paper 34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255).

That "information-seeking" bucket is doing more work than it sounds like on the surface. Asking ChatGPT "what's the best project management tool for a 10-person team," "is this supplement brand legit," or "should I use a credit union or a bank for a mortgage" is information-seeking in the technical sense — but functionally, it's purchase research. That's exactly the query territory brands have spent two decades fighting over in Google's top 10 results, and a meaningful share of it is now happening inside a chat window instead of a search results page, with no ten blue links and no guarantee your site is even mentioned.

The non-work skew matters too. This isn't primarily an office productivity tool that stays confined to work hours — it's woven into how people research everyday decisions on their own time, which is precisely when purchase-intent questions get asked.

## ChatGPT as a traffic source

ChatGPT doesn't just answer questions and keep users inside its own window — it sends them out to the open web, and that outbound traffic is growing fast. Referral traffic from ChatGPT to websites grew 206% year over year, comparing January 2025 to January 2026, based on more than 1 billion lines of U.S. clickstream data drawn from a 200-million-user panel [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-search-insights/).

That's the number that should reframe how you think about ChatGPT. It's not a black box that absorbs your traffic and gives nothing back — it's a distribution channel with a growth rate most marketing channels would kill for. Whether that referral traffic reaches your site specifically depends entirely on whether ChatGPT knows enough about your brand, product, or content to cite it when someone asks a relevant question.

What that means in practice:
