Every few years, someone decides the web browser is overdue for a rethink. This time it's Perplexity, and the product — sometimes called Perplexity Comet, usually just Comet — is an early, serious entry in the [AI search](/blog/what-is-ai-search) wave, not a one-off gimmick. Here's what the Comet browser actually is, what it does differently from Chrome or Safari, and whether switching your daily browser for an AI one is a good idea right now.

## What is Comet?

The Comet browser is Perplexity's AI-powered web browser — a Chromium-based browser with Perplexity's answer engine and an AI assistant built directly into the browsing experience. Instead of opening a search engine in a separate tab, the answer engine lives inside the browser itself, alongside an assistant that can act on the pages you're viewing.

Perplexity built its reputation on being an "answer engine" instead of a traditional search engine — see our breakdown of [how Perplexity's answer engine works](/blog/how-does-perplexity-work) for the mechanics. Comet takes that same idea and moves it one layer down the stack: from a site you visit to the software your whole session runs inside. As of this writing, Comet launched to paid users first and access has expanded since, which is worth checking directly given how quickly that kind of detail changes.

## What does Comet actually do differently?

The short version: Comet turns browsing into something closer to a conversation with the page you're on, instead of a page you silently read. It can summarize what's in front of you, answer questions scoped to that specific content, and carry out assistant-style tasks inside the browser rather than you clicking through them by hand.

In practice, that shows up in a few recognizable ways:

- **Page-aware summaries** — ask what a long article, thread, or product page is actually saying, instead of reading the whole thing yourself.
- **In-context Q&A** — ask follow-up questions about the exact page you're on and get an answer scoped to that content, not a generic web search.
- **Agentic tasks** — the assistant can take actions inside the browser on your behalf, not just answer questions about what's there.

That last one is the real departure from a normal browser. A search engine answers questions; an assistant that can act inside your browser is doing something closer to using the web for you. Whether that's a convenience or a liability depends entirely on what it's doing and how much you trust it to do correctly — and that's exactly the question the Comet browser forces you to sit with, not a side detail.

## Comet vs a normal browser

A normal browser is a window onto pages you read yourself. Comet is a window with an assistant reading — and sometimes acting — alongside you. Here's the practical difference, dimension by dimension:

| Dimension | Normal browser (Chrome, Safari, etc.) | Comet |
|---|---|---|
| Search default | Whatever engine you've set — typically a traditional results page | Perplexity's answer engine is built in, not bolted on |
| Page understanding | None — you read, skim, or hit Ctrl+F yourself | Can summarize and answer questions about the page you're on |
| Task automation | Extensions, if you choose to install and trust them | An AI assistant that can act inside the browser natively |
| Privacy questions to ask | What does this browser sync, and to whom? | What does the assistant see, store, and send to do its job? |
| Who it fits | Anyone — it's the safe, boring default for a reason | People who want an assistant woven into browsing and are comfortable with a newer, faster-moving product |

None of these individual pieces are new on their own — summarizer tools and browser extensions have existed for years. What's different is having them native to the browser, which is a bigger trust decision than installing an extension you can delete in ten seconds if you don't like it.

## Is Comet safe/private?

An AI browser has to see your browsing to summarize pages and act on them — that's not a flaw, it's the entire mechanism behind the features working at all. So the honest question isn't "is Comet browser safe" as an abstract yes-or-no, it's "am I comfortable with what it needs to see to do this."

A browser with an assistant built into the page-rendering layer sits in a structurally different position than a search engine you occasionally visit. A search engine sees what you type into it. A browser with an assistant can potentially see what you read on every page, in every tab, unless you've deliberately configured it otherwise. That's the tradeoff that makes "summarize this page for me" possible in the first place — the browser has to be able to read the page.

That's not a knock on Comet specifically; it's how every AI browser and AI-assisted extension works, by design. The sensible move is to read Perplexity's current privacy documentation directly, check exactly what's collected and what isn't, and decide for yourself. As of this writing, we're deliberately not stating specifics here — this is exactly the kind of detail that changes fast, and getting it wrong is worse than leaving it to the source.

## Should you switch?

If you're a researcher or an early adopter who likes trying new tools, yes — worth trying now. If you're a mainstream user who just wants your browser to work without thinking about it, there's no rush; the AI browser wars are just getting started, not ending.

Switching your daily-driver browser is not a small decision. It's the one piece of software that touches nearly everything else you do online — your passwords, your logged-in sessions, your history. Doing that for a v1-era product purely because the pitch sounds exciting isn't a call we're going to pretend is obviously correct.

Dismissing it outright would be its own kind of bias, though. Comet isn't a one-off gimmick — it's part of a wider wave of AI browsers, which means multiple serious companies are betting browsing itself is changing, not just search. If you're still deciding whether Perplexity's whole approach even suits you, our [Perplexity vs. ChatGPT comparison](/blog/perplexity-vs-chatgpt) is a reasonable place to start before you commit to their browser specifically.

Our actual take: try the Comet browser if switching costs you an afternoon, not your entire workflow. Keep your old browser installed. Don't make Comet your only browser until the parts you personally depend on have had real time to mature. That's not hedging for its own sake — it's just how anyone should evaluate a v1 product that sits this close to everything they do online.

## Why AI browsers matter for brands

If the browser itself can summarize your page before a visitor really reads it, the browser has become part of the answer layer — and being citable inside it now matters as much as ranking in search results used to. That's a genuinely new front, not a rebrand of an old one.

For years, being "found" meant ranking in a search engine results page. Then answer engines like Perplexity started summarizing content directly inside a chat interface, and getting cited accurately inside that answer became its own discipline. Comet moves that same shift one layer further inward: the summarizing can now happen at the browser level, on any page, whether or not the visit even started with a search. For the wider landscape this fits into, see our rundown of the [best AI search engines](/blog/best-ai-search-engines).

That has a blunt implication for anyone running a website: a page can technically get a visit from a Comet user and still lose most of the value of that visit if the browser answers the visitor's question for them before they ever read the actual content or notice the brand name. Whether you're represented accurately — and whether your brand gets named at all when an assistant does the summarizing — is no longer just a search-ranking question. It's happening inside the browser now too.

This is exactly the blind spot [AEOeye](/) audits: whether AI systems recommend or even mention your brand when someone asks the kind of question your buyers actually ask. If the browser is quietly becoming an answer layer on top of your site, it's worth knowing that today, not after your traffic numbers quietly stop meaning what they used to.
