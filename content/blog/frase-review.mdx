Frase is one of the cheapest doors into the AI content-optimization category, and that's really the whole pitch. It builds a brief in minutes, scores your draft against pages that already rank, and throws in AI-writing tools so you don't have to leave the app to draft a section. None of that means it will out-write someone who actually knows the topic, or that a high score means anything to ChatGPT or Google's AI Overview — and that gap is worth understanding before you subscribe to anything in this category. This Frase review covers what it actually does well, where its limits sit, how it stacks up against the tools people compare it to, and where to look if what you really need is AI search visibility rather than a better content score.

## What is Frase?

Frase is a content-brief and on-page optimization tool: point it at a target keyword, and it pulls the top-ranking pages, extracts their headings and shared topics, and hands you a brief to write against.

As you draft, it scores your content against those same competitors and offers AI-writing tools to fill gaps or draft sections outright. It sits in the same category as Surfer and Clearscope — tools built on the premise that studying what already ranks tells you what to write next. Frase's angle is doing that at a lower price than most of the category, spread across a few subscription tiers depending on how many documents and how much AI-writing you actually need.

In practice, that means solo bloggers, freelance SEO writers, and small in-house teams who need a repeatable brief process without hiring an editor to build one by hand. That positioning — the value option, not the deepest one — shapes almost everything else in this review.

## What Frase does well

Frase is genuinely fast at producing a usable brief, and it's upfront about being the wallet-friendly pick in a category that otherwise prices out solo writers and small teams. Both of those are real strengths, not marketing spin.

- **Brief-building speed.** Point it at a keyword and get a structured outline back — headings, subtopics, competitor word counts — in a couple of minutes, instead of opening ten browser tabs and doing it by hand.
- **Research aggregation.** It pulls “People Also Ask” questions, competitor headings, and topic gaps into one view, which is the part of content research that eats the most time and is hardest to enjoy doing manually.
- **Value positioning.** Frase is consistently the more affordable name mentioned alongside Surfer and Clearscope, which matters if you're a freelancer or a two-person content team rather than an enterprise SEO department with a tooling budget.
- **A flatter learning curve.** The interface asks less of you upfront than some of its more configurable rivals — a real advantage if you want a brief today, not a new tool to master this week.
- **A reasonable ceiling for simple content.** For straightforward, informational pages — not competitive, high-stakes commercial pages — Frase's brief-plus-score loop is often enough to produce something solidly average, which is a real outcome at a low price, even if it isn't a remarkable one.

## Where Frase falls short

Frase's optimization score has the same ceiling every tool in this category has: it measures how closely your draft resembles pages that already rank, not whether the content is original, accurate, or actually good. That's a category-wide limitation, not a Frase-specific bug, but it's worth naming plainly rather than glossing over.

- **Scoring rewards echoing what's already there.** Chase the number hard enough and you'll converge on the same headings and phrasing as everyone else you were trying to outrank — competitive sameness, not competitive advantage.
- **The AI-writing features increasingly compete with tools you already own.** If your team has ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini access, Frase's built-in drafting has to be meaningfully better than a well-written prompt to justify its own line item on the invoice — and that's a harder bar than it sounds.
- **Feature depth in this category shifts month to month.** Treat any single review, including this one, as a snapshot. Check what's actually included in the current plan tiers during a trial before committing budget to it.

None of this makes Frase a bad tool — it makes it a tool with a job description narrower than “write good content,” and it's worth holding it to that narrower job when you judge it.

## Frase alternatives

If Frase's scoring feels too shallow or its AI-writing tools feel redundant, the adjacent options split cleanly by what you actually need — deeper optimization, editorial polish, topical planning, or just a good assistant and your own process.

- **Surfer** is more technical, with more configurable scoring, and it's the common upgrade path once Frase's guidance starts to feel thin. If you're weighing it directly, our [Surfer SEO review](/blog/surfer-seo-review) covers where it's stronger and where it isn't.
- **Clearscope** leans editorial — cleaner reports, readability-first scoring, and little interest in writing the content for you. It's a better fit for teams where the writer wants a checklist, not a technical dashboard to manage.
- **MarketMuse** is oriented around planning and topical authority mapping rather than scoring a single draft — useful when your real problem is deciding what to write next, not optimizing what you've already started.
- **General AI assistants** — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — can approximate a brief with the right prompt and draft the piece too. It's the free-ish path if you don't want another subscription, though you lose the automated, competitor-scored feedback loop.

## Is Frase worth it?

Frase is worth it for solo writers, freelancers, and small content teams who want briefs and light AI-writing help without paying premium-tier prices. It's a weaker fit for teams that need deep technical scoring or expect its AI-writing tools to replace a general assistant they already rely on.

There's a simple test worth running before you buy anything in this category: open three articles that already rank for your target keyword, read them as a human, and ask whether you actually need software to tell you what they have in common.

If your bottleneck is “I don't know what to cover,” Frase closes that gap quickly and cheaply. If your bottleneck is “I need my content to actually get cited or recommended by AI systems,” a strong Frase score doesn't answer that question — it was never built to. ## The question Frase can't answer

Frase can tell you how closely your draft resembles pages that already rank in Google. It cannot tell you whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, or Google's AI Overview would ever cite or recommend that page — that's a different question, scored by different signals entirely.

Ranking-similarity and AI-citation-worthiness overlap less than the category likes to imply. AI answer engines favor content that states a clear answer early, is easy to extract from, and carries some signal of trust — not necessarily content that mirrors ten other pages targeting the same keyword.
