## ChatGPT user statistics (2026)

ChatGPT had roughly **800 million weekly active users (WAU)** as of October 2025, according to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and reported by TechCrunch — up from about 400 million WAU in February 2025 (Reuters). Those same users are now sending an estimated **2.5 billion prompts per day** (TechCrunch, July 2025).

That's the short, sourced answer to 'how many people use ChatGPT.' Everything below unpacks where those numbers come from, what they actually measure, and why the size of ChatGPT's audience should change how you think about being found online — not just how impressed you are by the headline.

Usage volume backs up the user count: ChatGPT was handling around 2.5 billion prompts per day as of mid-2025. OpenAI hasn't published a precise prompts-per-user breakdown, so we won't estimate one — but at that volume, ChatGPT is processing a meaningful share of the everyday questions that used to go straight to a search bar.

Three things are true about these numbers at once:

- They're large enough to matter to almost any consumer or B2B brand with online visibility goals.
- They're changing fast enough that any statistic older than a few months should be treated as outdated.
- They're frequently misquoted, rounded, or mismatched with the wrong time period in other 'statistics' roundups — which is exactly why this page cites a source and a date for every figure.

## The growth trajectory

ChatGPT's weekly active users roughly doubled in 2025 — from about 400 million in February to about 800 million in October. That's real, large-scale growth, not a rounding artifact.

What we can't tell you is the shape of the curve in between. OpenAI doesn't publish a monthly active-user drip feed, so any chart you see plotting 'ChatGPT users by month' between those two points is an estimate, an interpolation, or a fabrication dressed up as data. Treat it accordingly.

What we can say with confidence:

- Growth from February to October 2025 wasn't a one-time spike tied to a single event — it held up across roughly eight months.
- A platform doesn't double its weekly active users at a base of 400 million by accident. That kind of growth curve, at that scale, is rare even among the biggest consumer tech products.
- OpenAI's own public statements (via Sam Altman) treat 800 million as a confirmed, current figure, not a projection — which is why it's the number to cite going forward, until a newer figure replaces it.

It's also a reminder of how quickly 'current' statistics go stale in this space. A number that was accurate in February 2025 was already half the real figure eight months later — which is exactly the trap this page is trying to help you avoid.

## WAU vs MAU vs registered: what the numbers actually mean

Not all 'ChatGPT user' numbers measure the same thing, and mixing them up is where most bad statistics come from. Here's the difference:

- **Weekly active users (WAU)** — unique users who did something in ChatGPT in the trailing 7 days. This is the figure OpenAI has been citing (800 million, October 2025).
- **Monthly active users (MAU)** — unique users active in the trailing 30 days. Usually higher than WAU for the same product, since it captures more occasional users. OpenAI hasn't published a current MAU figure alongside the WAU number.
- **Registered users / accounts created** — a cumulative, lifetime count of everyone who ever signed up, whether or not they still use the product. This number only goes up and tells you almost nothing about current engagement.

If you see a ChatGPT 'user' statistic that doesn't specify which of these three it means, treat it as unreliable until you can confirm the methodology. This matters even more once you bring paid tiers into the picture: most of that 800 million weekly total is presumed to be free-tier usage, with Plus, Team, and Enterprise subscribers representing a smaller, paid slice — OpenAI hasn't broken out an exact split. ## Why ChatGPT's user base matters for your brand

Here's the pivot most 'ChatGPT statistics' posts miss entirely: 800 million weekly users isn't just a vanity metric to drop in an investor deck. It's 800 million people who are now asking a chat window the kind of questions they used to type into a search bar — including 'best [category] for [use case]' and 'is [brand] any good.'

That shift is exactly why ChatGPT's growth curve matters to marketers, not just to AI industry watchers. Every one of those 2.5 billion daily prompts is a potential moment where a buyer forms an opinion about your category — and possibly your brand — without ever visiting a website, clicking an ad, or landing on a search results page you optimized for.

This is also where user-count statistics and market-share statistics diverge. Raw user numbers tell you how big the audience is; they don't tell you how much of a category's buying conversations ChatGPT actually controls relative to Google, Perplexity, or Gemini. ## Where to verify ChatGPT numbers

Go to the source first, then cross-check with dated press coverage — never trust a 'statistics' page (including this one) without both.

- **OpenAI's own channels** — official blog posts, and public statements from executives like Sam Altman, are the primary source for user and usage figures.
- **Dated, reputable reporting** — outlets like TechCrunch, Reuters, Axios, and Bloomberg typically confirm figures directly with OpenAI before publishing, and they timestamp their reporting.
- **The publish date, always** — ChatGPT's user base has moved fast enough in 2025 that a 'statistics' article from even six months ago may already be citing the wrong number. Check the date before you cite the figure.
