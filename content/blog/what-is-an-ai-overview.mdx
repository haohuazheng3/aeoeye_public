## What Is an AI Overview?

**An AI Overview is Google's AI-generated summary that appears at the top of many search results, pulling an answer together from several web pages instead of just listing blue links.** It sits above the normal organic results, usually with a handful of source links attached directly to the summary.

Google introduced AI Overviews as the public, default version of what it had spent over a year testing as the Search Generative Experience (SGE) — an opt-in feature tucked inside Google Labs. As of this writing, AI Overviews are no longer experimental or optional for most U.S. searchers; they're just part of how Google search looks now.

The short version: you type a question, Google's AI reads across the top-ranking pages for that query, and it writes you a synthesized answer instead of making you click through and piece one together yourself.

That's a bigger shift than it sounds. For two decades, ranking #1 meant your blue link sat at the top of the page. Now the top of the page is a generated paragraph, and your site's job isn't just to outrank the competition — it's to be one of the sources that paragraph is built from.

## How Do AI Overviews Work?

**AI Overviews work in three steps: Google retrieves the most relevant pages for a query, a language model reads and summarizes them, and the summary cites the sources it drew from.** It's retrieval-augmented generation applied to search — the AI isn't inventing an answer from whatever it memorized during training, it's grounding the response in pages that are actually ranking right now.

Broken down, that looks like:

- **Retrieval.** Google's normal ranking systems pull a set of candidate pages for the query, the same way they would for a standard search.
- **Synthesis.** An AI model reads those pages and drafts a short, direct answer, often blending facts from more than one source into a single response.
- **Citation.** The Overview links out to the pages it used, typically shown as a small carousel of source links next to or beneath the summary.
- **Display.** The result renders above the standard organic listings, so it's the first thing most users see before anything else.

This whole system is the shipped version of a concept Google spent years testing as SGE. For the fuller backstory on how that experiment became today's AI Overviews, see our guide to [what SGE is](/blog/what-is-sge). AI Overviews are also just one piece of a much bigger shift — search engines answering questions directly instead of only indexing pages for you to search through yourself — which we unpack in our explainer on [generative search](/blog/generative-search).

## AI Overviews vs. Featured Snippets vs. AI Mode: What's the Difference?

**AI Overviews, featured snippets, and AI Mode all put an answer near the top of the results page, but they're built differently and they behave differently.** A featured snippet quotes one passage from one page. An AI Overview blends information from several pages into new, generated text. AI Mode goes further still, turning search into an ongoing conversation.

| Feature | What It Is | How It Differs |
|---|---|---|
| Featured snippet | A single passage or list quoted directly from one ranking page | No generation involved — it's a verbatim quote tied to one URL, so attribution is simple |
| AI Overview | An AI-generated summary synthesized from multiple pages, with linked sources | Combines several sources into new text; it's not a quote from any single page |
| AI Mode | A conversational, chat-style search experience built for multi-step questions | Supports follow-up questions in a thread, closer to a chatbot than a static results page |

The practical difference for site owners: a featured snippet rewards having the single clearest passage on the page. An AI Overview rewards being one of several credible sources on a topic. AI Mode rewards being useful across a whole line of follow-up questions, not just the first one someone asks.

## What Do AI Overviews Do to Your Traffic?

**Honest answer: AI Overviews can reduce clicks on queries where the summary already answers the question, because users get what they need without ever visiting a site.** That's not speculation — it's the pattern showing up across independent studies of AI Overview click behavior.

Ahrefs, for one, has published research finding that pages ranking below an AI Overview see meaningfully lower click-through rates than the same positions earned before AI Overviews existed, especially on informational queries with a short, factual answer. Directionally, that matches what most SEOs are reporting from their own data, as of this writing.

A few things worth keeping straight:

- **Zero-click search keeps growing.** More searches now end with no click at all, because the AI Overview, the snippet, or the knowledge panel already answered the question.
- **Not every query loses equally.** Simple factual questions lose more clicks than complex, comparative, or commercial queries, where people still want to read a full page before deciding anything.
- **Visibility is shifting, not vanishing.** Getting cited inside an AI Overview is a new form of visibility — you're in front of the user even without a click, and that still matters for brand recall and trust.
- **Being the cited source beats being invisible.** Pages that get cited in the Overview generally fare better than pages that rank on page one but get skipped by the AI entirely.

We don't think the right response to any of this is panic. Ranking is no longer the whole game — it's table stakes. Getting cited inside the Overview is the second, parallel goal now, and it's the one most sites haven't started optimizing for yet.

## How Do You Appear in AI Overviews?

**To show up in an AI Overview, you generally need to already rank well, write in a format the AI can lift cleanly, and make your site's topic and entity unmistakable.** AI Overviews draw heavily from pages that are already ranking near the top of page one, so there's no shortcut around solid, relevant rankings first.

Past that baseline, a handful of things consistently help:

- **Answer-first content.** Put the direct answer to the query in the first sentence or two of the relevant section, then explain further. AI systems lift cleanly written, self-contained answers far more easily than answers buried under a long lead-up.
- **Clear structure.** Use headers phrased as real questions, short paragraphs, bullet points, and tables. Structured content is simply easier for an AI model to parse and quote accurately.
- **Schema markup.** FAQ, HowTo, and Article structured data give the AI explicit signals about exactly what a page is answering, which can improve how cleanly it gets summarized.
- **Entity clarity.** Use consistent naming for your brand, product, and key terms, and define concepts plainly on first use. Ambiguous entities are hard for an AI to cite with any confidence.
- **Topical depth.** Pages that thoroughly cover a subject, rather than skimming it in one thin paragraph, give the AI more accurate material to pull from — and lower the odds it reaches for a competitor instead.

## Can You Turn AI Overviews Off?

**Users can sometimes reduce how often they see AI Overviews — through certain search settings, browser extensions, or search operators that tend to suppress them — but there's no permanent, official Google toggle that turns them off everywhere.** Google has also tested, in some markets, a separate 'Web' results filter that shows a more traditional, link-only page, as of this writing.

For businesses, though, hunting for a way to turn AI Overviews off is energy spent in the wrong place. You can't opt your site out of being summarized, and most of your customers have no plans to turn Overviews off either. The only lever that actually matters for a business is adapting — earning a spot as a cited source when the Overview appears — rather than wishing the feature away.
