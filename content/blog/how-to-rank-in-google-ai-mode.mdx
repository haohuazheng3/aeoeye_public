To rank in Google AI Mode, make sure a page is indexed and snippet-eligible, then make it the most useful, least generic answer to the question and its obvious follow-ups. Google's documentation says AI Mode has no extra technical requirements and no special markup. Every claim below about how Google works comes from Google's own pages, checked on 3 October 2026.

## What is Google AI Mode, and how is it different from AI Overviews?

AI Mode is Google's conversational search experience: you ask a complex question, it searches several subtopics at once, and it answers with links to supporting pages. AI Overviews are shorter summaries on the regular results page, shown only when Google's systems judge them additive. Since May 2026 they are one flow, not two products.

Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says AI Mode suits queries that need exploration, reasoning or complex comparison. At I/O on 19 May 2026, Google said you can [ask a follow-up from an AI Overview and continue in AI Mode](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) with your context kept, worldwide.

So plan for one surface, with one caveat: Google says the two features may use different models and techniques, so the links they show differ. For the summary side, see [what an AI Overview is](/blog/what-is-an-ai-overview) and our [AI Overviews playbook](/guides/how-to-rank-in-ai-overviews).

| | AI Overviews | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|
| How a user gets there | Appears on the results page when Google's systems judge it additive | Reached by choosing AI Mode, or by a follow-up from an AI Overview |
| Best at | Getting the gist of a complex topic | Exploration, reasoning, complex comparisons |
| Search Console | Performance (Web) and Generative AI report | Same |
| Opt-out | Search generative AI control | Same control |

## How does AI Mode choose which pages to link?

AI Mode starts from Google's normal Search index and ranking systems, then uses retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out to build the answer. A page can be linked only if it is indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Beyond that, it competes on how well it answers the fanned-out searches.

[Retrieval-augmented generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) (RAG) means a model pulls in retrieved documents instead of relying on memory alone. In Google's [generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), those documents come from the Search index.

Query fan-out is the retrieval step: a set of concurrent, related queries the model generates. Google's example: for "how to fix a lawn that's full of weeds", the model may also search "best herbicides for lawns", "remove weeds without chemicals" and "how to prevent weeds in lawn".

Our reading, not Google's: you are competing for several searches per question, so a page that best answers one subtopic can earn a link without ranking first for the head query. Google does not publish how links are picked across the fan-out.

## How do you get cited in AI Mode?

Treat it as normal SEO done with more discipline. Google says unique, useful content will likely influence your presence in generative AI search more than any other suggestion in its guide. These five steps follow that guide, in order of leverage.

1. **Confirm eligibility.** The page must be indexed and snippet-eligible. Check for stray `noindex`, `nosnippet` or restrictive `max-snippet`, make sure robots.txt and any CDN or firewall let Googlebot in, and confirm your site is not excluded in Search Console.
2. **Write non-commodity content.** Google calls common-knowledge listicles commodity content and asks for a unique point of view, such as first-hand experience. A summary of what other pages already say gives the model no reason to link you.
3. **Answer the next three questions on the same page.** Typical follow-ups are cost, alternatives, who it is for and risks. Give each its own heading and a direct answer.
4. **Open sections with the answer.** Google says you need not chunk content for AI. We do it anyway, because a reader decides in seconds whether a page solves their problem.
5. **Keep business data current.** Google says generative AI responses can include product listings and local business information. It points stores to Merchant Center feeds and local firms to Google Business Profile.

One correction to common advice, including ours: an earlier version of this page told you to build a separate page per sub-question. Google's guide now says creating content for every fan-out variation primarily to manipulate responses violates its scaled content abuse policy. One strong page that covers the follow-ups beats ten thin ones.

## Which popular AI Mode tactics can you skip?

Skip llms.txt, content "chunking" and writing in a special style for AI. Google's guide lists all three as things you can ignore for Google Search, and much AEO advice is [recycled SEO](/blog/most-aeo-advice-is-recycled-seo) plus rituals like these.

- **llms.txt:** Google says Google Search itself does not use such files. In AEOeye's [crawler access study](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory) (October 2026), 173 of 330 sites we could check, 52.4%, publish one. Adoption is not effect; we publish one because it is cheap, and [rate it overrated](/blog/llms-txt-is-overrated).
- **Blocking Google-Extended to leave AI Mode:** it does nothing for that goal. Google says Google-Extended governs Gemini model training and grounding in Gemini apps, and [does not affect Search inclusion](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers). In our study, 31 of 384 sites block it, yet only 1 blocks Googlebot from the homepage. Per-site data: [study CSV](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv).
- **Chasing mentions:** Google says inauthentic mentions help less than they seem to. Genuine coverage in blogs, videos and forums differs, because these features can show what is said about you across the web.

A firewall can quietly undo all of this. In our study, 60 of 375 homepages (16%) challenged our self-identifying research crawler. That was our crawler, not Googlebot, but it is why Google tells you to check your CDN.

## How do you track AI Mode visibility?

Use Search Console's Generative AI performance report for the numbers Google itself reports, then check your key questions in AI Mode by hand. Google warns that no third-party tool has access to its internal ranking or AI systems, so treat tracker output as sampling.

**Search Console.** The [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) reached all sites on 31 August 2026. It reports impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode, by page, country, device and date. Google documents impressions there, not clicks,. AI Mode also counts in the normal Performance report under Web, where a [follow-up question counts as a new query](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828).

**Manual checks need repetition.** AI answers vary by run, and in AI Mode by user, since people can connect apps like Gmail for personalized answers. [SparkToro's study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts 2,961 times across ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI features. It calculated under a 1-in-100 chance that two responses to the same prompt, in ChatGPT or Google's AI, listed the same brands, and argued that visibility percentage across many runs beats rank.

A protocol that works: pick 10 buyer questions, run each at least five times on different days, and log whether your page is linked and your brand named. Report a share, not a position.

## Can you opt out, and what can't you control?

Yes, you can opt out. You can't control which links Google picks on a given run or how many clicks follow. Eligibility, content quality and the opt-out are yours; the rest is Google's ranking and generation, which varies by person.

**The opt-out.** Search Console's [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024) sits under Settings. Excluding your site removes its links and content from AI Overviews, AI Mode and generative AI features in Discover, within a few days. Google says it is not a ranking signal elsewhere in Search and does not limit model training, which is Google-Extended's job. Our stance: unless your revenue depends on page views from your own content, stay included.

**Clicks.** Pew Research tracked 900 US adults in March 2025: 8% of visits to pages with an [AI summary ended in a click on a result](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), versus 15% without one, and 1% clicked a link in the summary. That is AI Overviews, not AI Mode. Google says clicks from those pages are higher quality.

**Being named is the hard part.** Across AEOeye's audit records (127 audits, June to September 2026), Google AI named the audited brand in 1 of 74 answers. The sample is small and self-selected and engines are not comparable, so treat it as a reason to test your own questions, not a rate.
