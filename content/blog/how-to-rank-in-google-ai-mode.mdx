If you've been treating Google's AI Mode as "AI Overviews, but bigger," you're optimizing for the wrong target. Ranking in Google AI Mode means something different: it's a separate, fully conversational search experience with its own retrieval logic, and most of the AI Overview advice circulating right now doesn't fully transfer to it. Here's what's actually different, how ranking works inside it, and the playbook for getting surfaced.

## What is Google AI Mode (and how is it different from AI Overviews)?

AI Mode is Google's full conversational search experience; AI Overviews are the summary box that sits on a normal results page. Ranking in AI Mode means being retrievable across a multi-turn answer, not just winning one snapshot — and confusing the two is the biggest reason teams misallocate their effort right now.

AI Overviews sit inside classic search results: you type a query, get ten blue links, and sometimes an AI-generated summary appears above them, citing a handful of sources. AI Mode replaces the entire results page with a chat interface. You ask a question, Google answers conversationally, and you can follow up — "what about for a small team," "how much does that cost" — without starting a new search. Every follow-up triggers new retrieval, which means your content has to hold up across a conversation, not just match one query to one page.

| Aspect | AI Overviews | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | Above traditional results, on the normal results page | Its own full-page, chat-style experience |
| Interaction | One query, one static summary | Multi-turn, with follow-up questions in the same session |
| What it pulls | A handful of cited snippets per query | Synthesized passages from many sources, refreshed each turn |
| Ranking logic | Close to featured-snippet retrieval | Query fan-out across sub-questions (more below) |
| Can you opt out | No AI-Mode-specific control | Same controls as AI Overviews — governed by nosnippet/noindex |

Both surfaces draw from the same underlying index, and both can cite your pages — but AI Mode's follow-up behavior is the real shift. A page built to answer one keyword well can still earn a normal AI Overview citation and lose every follow-up turn in AI Mode, because nothing on the page answers what the user asks next. If you're already watching [how AI Overviews are affecting your traffic](/blog/ai-overviews-and-your-traffic), treat AI Mode as the harder version of that same pressure — not a duplicate of it.

## How ranking works in AI Mode

Google uses a technique it calls query fan-out: it breaks your question into several related sub-queries, retrieves results for each, and synthesizes the strongest passages into one answer. You win a fan-out slot by comprehensively answering the sub-questions around a topic, not just the headline query.

Query fan-out, defined plainly: a single user question gets expanded, behind the scenes, into multiple underlying searches — covering related angles, comparisons, definitions, and likely follow-ups — before the AI assembles a response. Ask "best CRM for a 5-person agency" and AI Mode almost certainly isn't running one retrieval pass. It's more likely pulling separate results for something like "best CRM for small teams," "CRM pricing for agencies," and "CRM vs spreadsheet for small business" — then stitching the strongest passages together into a single answer.

What that means for how you should think about ranking:

- **A single page can't win a whole conversation.** If your content answers only the head query and ignores the adjacent sub-questions, a competitor's page picks up those fan-out slots instead — even if you outrank them for the main keyword.
- **Passages matter more than pages.** AI Mode extracts and cites specific paragraphs, not whole URLs. A page can sit at #1 in traditional search and still lose every fan-out slot because no single passage directly answers the sub-question being asked.
- **Topic depth beats keyword matching.** Coverage of the whole cluster — definitions, comparisons, pricing, use cases, objections — gives you more surface area across the sub-queries Google generates than one narrowly optimized page ever will.
- **Specificity compounds.** Vague, generic paragraphs get passed over for passages with concrete numbers, dates, and direct claims that are easy to lift and cite cleanly.

## The AI Mode playbook (step-by-step)

There's no separate "AI Mode SEO" toolkit. It's disciplined, comprehensive execution of fundamentals that fan-out retrieval rewards far more heavily than traditional ranking ever did. In order of leverage:

1. **Build topical depth, not just one page.** Map every sub-question a buyer would actually ask around your topic — definitions, comparisons, pricing, alternatives, edge cases — and cover each one, either as sections in a pillar page or as a linked cluster. One page per keyword means one shot per fan-out slot.
2. **Write passage-level direct answers.** Open each section with a self-contained 1–2 sentence answer that doesn't depend on the paragraph before it. AI Mode lifts passages out of context; a sentence that only makes sense mid-flow won't get cited, even on a page that otherwise ranks well.
3. **Add structured data Google can parse cleanly.** FAQPage, HowTo, and Article schema won't force a citation on their own, but they make your content's structure unambiguous to machine parsing — which is exactly what fan-out retrieval leans on. Google's own structured data documentation is worth following closely for implementation details.
4. **Strengthen E-E-A-T signals.** Real author and organization identity, cited sources, original data, and a clear reason to trust the page all feed the same helpful-content systems that Google has said inform its AI features, not just traditional ranking.
5. **Keep technical crawlability clean.** AI Mode draws from the same index as regular Search. If Googlebot can't crawl it, render it, or reach it — broken internal links, blocked JavaScript, orphaned pages — it can't be fanned out to, no matter how well the page is written.

None of this is exotic. It's the boring fundamentals, done at a level of comprehensiveness most sites still skip, because most sites are still writing for one query instead of a conversation.
