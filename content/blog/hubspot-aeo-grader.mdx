## What is HubSpot's AEO Grader?

HubSpot's AEO Grader is a free, one-time check of how your brand shows up when AI tools are asked about it. It sits inside HubSpot's broader push into answer engine optimization — the practice of earning citations and recommendations inside AI-generated answers, not just rankings in blue links. (If the term itself is new to you, we cover it in full in [what answer engine optimization actually means](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization).)

As of this writing, HubSpot describes the Grader as a snapshot: you give it your brand, it checks your current AI search visibility, and it hands back a grade reflecting that moment — not a subscription, not a dashboard you log into weekly, closer to a diagnostic than a monitor. It sits next to a growing shelf of HubSpot AEO educational content, which tracks: a free grader is a smart top-of-funnel move for a company that wants marketers thinking about AEO on HubSpot's terms.

Credit where it's due. A major marketing platform putting a free AI-visibility check inside a product and content ecosystem that marketers already trust does something smaller AEO vendors can't: it legitimizes the category. For a lot of teams, this grader will be their first honest introduction to “AI search visibility” as a thing worth measuring at all. That's a genuinely good outcome, regardless of who built the tool.

## What an AEO grade actually tells you (and what it can't)

A single AEO grade tells you how one set of prompts, run against your brand, scored on the day you ran it. It can't tell you which engines were actually asked, how the prompt wording shaped the answer, or what the same check would return tomorrow — let alone how you stack up against a named competitor.

That's not a knock on HubSpot specifically. It's a structural limit of any one-time grader, and it's worth understanding before you treat a single grade as a verdict on your AI visibility.

AI answers aren't static search rankings. Ask ChatGPT the same buyer question two different ways and you can get two different sets of recommended brands. Ask Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Claude that same question and you'll often get four different answers, because each engine pulls from different sources and weighs them differently. A grade built from an unspecified set of prompts, against an unspecified set of engines, on one particular day, is a real data point — but it's one still frame from a video that never stops moving.

Three things a one-time grade structurally can't capture:

- **Citation variance.** Whether your brand shows up consistently across repeated, differently-worded questions, or only under one lucky phrasing.
- **Competitor comparison depth.** Who AI engines recommend instead of you, and why — a grade tells you your score, not the gap between you and whoever's winning.
- **Buyer-question coverage.** Real buyers rarely ask “tell me about [brand].” They ask “best [category] for [use case]” or “[brand] vs. [competitor].” A brand-name check and a buyer-intent check are different questions with different answers.

None of this makes the Grader useless — it makes it a starting point, not an ending one. Yes — run HubSpot's free grade, then verify what it tells you with a proper per-engine audit. The two aren't competing tools; they answer different questions at different depths, and doing both takes less than an hour combined.
