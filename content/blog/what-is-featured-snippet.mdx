Google still shows a full-answer box above the first organic result on a huge share of question-based searches, and most sites either ignore it or chase it with vague advice like "write better content." Neither works. Winning that box is a specific, learnable skill — and as AI search reshapes how people find answers, it turns out to be the same skill that gets your content quoted by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. Here's what a featured snippet actually is, how the different formats work, and why this decade-old SEO tactic just became more relevant, not less.

## What is a featured snippet?

A featured snippet is a short answer Google pulls from a ranking page and displays in a box at the top of results — the spot SEOs call "position zero." Google extracts a sentence, list, or table straight from your page and quotes it back to the searcher, along with your title and URL underneath.

That's the entire mechanism. There's no submission form and no separate settings panel. Google's algorithm scans the pages already ranking on page one, decides which one answers the query most directly, and lifts that exact passage into the box above result #1. You don't apply for a featured snippet — you write the page that makes the choice obvious.

Snippets show up on a meaningful share of Google searches, concentrated heavily on question-based and how-to queries — "what is," "how to," "vs," and "best" searches are where they cluster most. If you've ever typed "how many teaspoons in a tablespoon" into Google and gotten your answer without clicking anything, you've used one. That's the box you're optimizing for.

## Types of featured snippets

Google formats a snippet differently depending on what the query is asking for. Matching the format to the query's intent is the single biggest lever you control.

- Paragraph snippets — a short text answer, used for definition and "what is" queries.
- List snippets — numbered steps or bulleted items, used for "how to" and ranking queries.
- Table snippets — rows and columns, used for comparison, pricing, and spec queries.
- Video snippets — a timestamped clip, used for demonstration and tutorial queries.

| Snippet type | Best for | How to format |
|---|---|---|
| Paragraph | Definitions, "what is X" questions | One tight, direct answer placed right under the heading |
| List | Steps, rankings, "how to" queries | Numbered list for sequences, bullets for unordered items |
| Table | Comparisons, pricing, specs | An HTML table with clear column headers, one row per item |
| Video | Demonstrations, tutorials | Descriptive title, timestamps in the description, transcript on the page |

Guess wrong on format and you can write the most accurate answer on the page and still lose the box — to a competitor who simply used a table where Google wanted a table.

## Why featured snippets matter

A featured snippet places your page above every organic result, including the #1 spot, and often above paid ads. That's the visibility case in one sentence.

Three reasons it's worth chasing:

- Top visibility. You occupy the most prominent block on the results page, before anyone scrolls.
- Voice-answer source. Smart speakers and voice search typically read the featured snippet aloud as the answer — it's often the only result a spoken query returns.
- Authority signal. Ranking in the box works as third-party validation: Google picked your page as the clearest answer out of everyone ranking on page one.

The honest caveat: featured snippets can lower your click-through rate. If your paragraph fully answers the question, some searchers never click through — Google already gave them what they came for. That's the same tension AI Overviews create now, and it isn't a reason to skip snippets. Fewer clicks paired with more qualified intent usually beats more clicks with less.

## How to win featured snippets

You win a featured snippet by making your answer the easiest one on page one for Google to lift and quote. Four things matter, roughly in order of importance.

1. Answer the question directly and concisely, near the top. Put the actual answer in the first sentence or two after the heading, not buried after three paragraphs of preamble.
2. Use the format the query wants. Check the current SERP before you write — if a table already holds the snippet, a paragraph won't dislodge it.
3. Structure the page clearly. Use the exact question as an H2 or H3, and place the answer immediately beneath it so Google can isolate it from the rest of the page.
4. Already rank on page one. Google pulls snippets almost exclusively from top-10 results, so snippet optimization is a refinement on existing rankings, not a shortcut around them.

None of this requires new content. Most of the time it means restructuring a page you already have — moving the answer up, trimming the throat-clearing, adding a table where you had a paragraph — so the answer is unmistakable to both the reader and the algorithm scanning it.

## Featured snippets were the original AEO

Here's the part most SEO guides skip: winning a featured snippet and winning an AI citation are the same skill wearing different clothes.

When your page gets pulled into position zero, Google's algorithm has already done what an AI answer engine does — it read multiple candidate pages, decided which one stated the answer most clearly, and extracted that exact passage to display without a click. That's not adjacent to AEO. That is AEO, running on an older, narrower engine.

Featured snippets were the training ground. For years, they rewarded pages that answered questions in extractable, self-contained chunks: a direct sentence up top, clean lists, labeled tables. AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, and Google's AI Mode reward the same structural habits, just at a larger scale and pulling from more sources per answer. If you already know how to write a snippet-winning paragraph, you already know most of what it takes to get quoted by an [answer engine](/blog/what-is-an-answer-engine).

The pages that show up in AI Overviews today are disproportionately the same pages that were already winning featured snippets. That's not a coincidence — both systems are scoring for the same trait: extractability.

## Featured snippets vs AI Overviews

A featured snippet quotes one page. An AI Overview synthesizes several. That's the core difference — and it matters less than what they have in common.

- Featured snippet: pulls one exact passage from one ranking page and displays it with attribution and a link.
- AI Overview: generates a synthesized answer drawing on multiple sources, often citing several pages instead of one.

Both systems are scanning for the same trait: a clear, direct, well-structured answer they can lift without editing it first. Optimize for one and you're most of the way to the other. The reverse isn't guaranteed, though — some pages that win snippets still get skipped by AI Overviews if the surrounding page lacks the topical depth or source credibility the AI weighs on top of extractability.
