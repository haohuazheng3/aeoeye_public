A full SEO audit checklist covers four areas: technical health, on-page quality, off-page authority, and — the one most checklists still skip — AI visibility. Miss any of the four and you're only auditing part of how people actually find you today.

Most SEO audit checklists stop at three areas. They walk you through crawl errors, title tags, and backlink profiles, then call it done. That was a complete audit in 2019. It isn't anymore. Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews the same questions they used to type into a search box, and a checklist that never checks whether those engines recommend you is only auditing half your funnel.

This checklist covers all four areas, gives you a tool for every check, and tells you how often to actually run it — because an audit that happens once and gets filed away isn't an audit, it's a snapshot.

## The SEO audit checklist at a glance

A full SEO audit checks four things: can search engines and AI crawlers reach your site (technical), does each page match what people are searching for (on-page), does the rest of the web vouch for you (off-page), and do AI engines actually cite you (AI visibility). Here's the checklist grouped by area — skim it, then jump to the section you need.

**Technical:**
- [ ] Site is crawlable and every important page is indexed
- [ ] Core Web Vitals pass on mobile and desktop
- [ ] Mobile experience matches or beats desktop
- [ ] HTTPS is site-wide with no mixed-content warnings
- [ ] Structured data validates without errors

**On-page:**
- [ ] Title tags and meta descriptions are unique per page
- [ ] Heading hierarchy is logical, with one H1 and nested H2s
- [ ] Content actually matches search intent, not just keywords
- [ ] Internal links connect related pages in both directions
- [ ] Images have descriptive alt text

**Off-page:**
- [ ] Backlink profile is growing and clean, not spammy
- [ ] Brand gets mentioned across the web, linked or not
- [ ] Reviews and citations skew positive

**AI visibility:**
- [ ] AI engines cite you when buyers ask relevant questions
- [ ] AI crawlers like GPTBot and PerplexityBot can access your site
- [ ] Content is quotable: clear claims, defined terms, real data

Work through all four before you fix anything. A site can pass every technical check and still lose the sale because ChatGPT never mentions it — better to know that before spending a quarter chasing Core Web Vitals.

## Technical audit

The technical audit answers one question: can search engines and AI crawlers actually find, load, and understand your site? If the answer is no, nothing else on this list matters, because you can't rank or get cited from a page that never gets crawled.

Check these five things first:

- **Crawl and index status.** Check Google Search Console's Coverage report for crawl errors, and confirm important pages aren't accidentally set to noindex or blocked in robots.txt.
- **Speed.** Core Web Vitals — loading, interactivity, and visual stability — affect both rankings and how willing visitors are to stick around.
- **Mobile experience.** Google indexes the mobile version of your site by default, so a fix that only works on desktop doesn't count.
- **HTTPS.** The entire site should be secure, with no mixed-content warnings on any page.
- **Structured data.** Schema markup should validate cleanly; broken schema can quietly disqualify a page from rich results.

## On-page audit

The on-page audit checks whether each page actually deserves to rank: does it match what the searcher wants, and is it structured so both readers and search engines can follow it? This is where most content quality problems live.

Work through these checks page by page:

- **Titles and meta descriptions.** Every page needs a unique title and description that describes what's actually on the page; duplicates confuse search engines about which page to rank.
- **Heading hierarchy.** One H1 per page, with H2s and H3s that follow a logical outline instead of jumping around.
- **Content quality.** Is it accurate, complete, and well-written, or does it read like it was written to hit a word count?
- **Intent match.** A page can be well-written and still fail if it answers the wrong question — check it against what's actually ranking for that keyword right now.
- **Internal links.** Related pages should link to each other; pages with no internal links pointing to them rarely rank well.
- **Alt text.** Every meaningful image needs descriptive alt text, both for accessibility and for image search.

Intent mismatch is the one teams miss most often. A page can nail every other box on this list and still underperform because it's a product page competing for an informational query, or the other way around.

## Off-page audit

The off-page audit looks at what the rest of the web says about you — your backlink profile, brand mentions, and reputation — because search engines and AI models both treat third-party validation as a trust signal.

Three things to check:

- **Backlink profile.** Is it growing over time, from relevant sites, without a spike of spammy or unrelated links that could trigger a manual action?
- **Brand mentions.** Search your brand name across the web. Unlinked mentions still count as a trust signal for AI engines, which often cite brands without a hyperlink at all.
- **Reputation.** Reviews, forum threads, and third-party citations shape both what Google shows and what AI engines say when someone asks for a recommendation.

Off-page signals move slower than on-page ones, so don't expect a week of link building to change anything. Treat this as a trend to watch over months, not a box to check once and forget.

## The AI-visibility audit (the part most checklists skip)

An AI-visibility audit checks whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI actually recommend you when someone asks a buyer question — and it's the one part of a full SEO audit that most checklists still leave out. Skipping it means auditing your search traffic while ignoring where a growing share of research and recommendations now happens.

Run these three checks:

- **Ask the buyer questions yourself.** Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini and ask the exact questions your buyers ask — the best tool for your category, you versus your closest competitor, whether you're worth the price. Note whether you show up, and who shows up instead.
- **Check crawler access.** AI engines can't cite what they can't read. Confirm GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and the other major AI crawlers aren't blocked in robots.txt — our [AI crawler list](/blog/ai-crawler-list) covers exactly which ones to allow.
- **Check quotability.** AI engines pull sentences, not pages. Content with clear, standalone claims, defined terms, and real data is far easier to quote than vague marketing copy. Reread your key pages and ask whether any single sentence could be lifted out and still make sense on its own.

Doing this manually works for a spot-check but doesn't scale past a handful of questions. For a repeatable way to track it over time, see our [guide to measuring AI visibility](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility).

## How often to run a full audit

Run the full four-part audit quarterly, spot-check technical and AI visibility monthly, and always re-audit after a migration, redesign, or CMS change — those are the moments things break silently and nobody notices for months.

A practical cadence:

- **Quarterly:** the full audit, all four areas, start to finish.
- **Monthly:** a lighter spot-check — new crawl errors, Core Web Vitals drift, and a quick re-ask of your top buyer questions in ChatGPT and Perplexity.
- **After any migration, redesign, or CMS change:** a full re-audit within a week. Redirects break, structured data gets dropped, and robots.txt rules get overwritten more often than teams expect.
