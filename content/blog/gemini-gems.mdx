## What Are Gemini Gems?

Gemini Gems are customized versions of Google's Gemini that you set up once with your own instructions, then reuse anytime you need that specific job done. Think of a Gem as a saved AI persona built for one task instead of a general-purpose chat. It's Google's answer to custom GPTs — same basic idea, different name, running on Gemini instead of GPT.

A Gem is not a separate, smarter model. It's Gemini running with a system prompt you wrote and saved. That's the whole mechanism: instructions, a role, maybe some ground rules, wrapped into something you can open with one click instead of retyping everything every time.

As of this writing, Gems live inside the Gemini app, sit alongside your regular chats, and each one keeps its own instructions separate from the others. Open your "Editor" Gem and it behaves like an editor. Open your "Code Reviewer" Gem and it behaves like a code reviewer. Same underlying model, different job description.

Here's the part people miss: a Gem's value isn't personality, it's not repeating yourself. If you find yourself pasting the same three paragraphs of context into a new Gemini chat every week, that's a Gem waiting to happen.

## What Can Gems Do?

A Gem can do anything regular Gemini can do — the difference is scope. You write instructions once for a specific job, and Gemini follows them every time you open that Gem, instead of you re-explaining context from scratch in every new conversation.

In practice, that covers most repeated knowledge work:

- **Writing helper** — house style, preferred tone, words to avoid, formatting rules baked in
- **Coding assistant** — preferred language and framework, code style, comment conventions, no unsolicited explanations
- **Research aide** — how to structure a summary, which angles to prioritize, how to flag uncertainty
- **Brainstorming partner** — a fixed set of questions it always asks, or instructions to push back and play devil's advocate

Google also ships a handful of premade Gems for common jobs — things like brainstorming or learning support — so you can see the format before building your own. They're a reasonable starting point. But premade Gems are generic by design; they don't know your style guide, your codebase conventions, or your specific research process. The real payoff shows up once you write one for a task you actually repeat.

## How Do You Create a Gem?

Creating a Gem takes four real steps: open the Gem manager, write clear instructions for the job, give it a defined role plus explicit rules, then test it against a real example and revise.

1. **Open Gems and start a new one.** As of this writing this lives inside the Gemini app's side menu — exact placement can shift as Google updates the interface, so look for "Gems" if a menu label has moved.
2. **Name it for the job, not for fun.** "Blog Editor" tells you what it does six months from now. "Ed" doesn't.
3. **Write instructions like you're onboarding someone on day one.** State the job, what a good result looks like, and what to avoid. Vague instructions produce vague output — this is where most Gems fail before they even launch.
4. **Set boundaries.** Tone, length, format, and anything it should never do. A Gem with no constraints is just Gemini with extra steps.
5. **Test it on a real task immediately.** Instructions that look fine on paper often have gaps that only show up once you feed the Gem something real.
6. **Revise based on what comes back.** Treat the first version as a draft. The gap between a mediocre Gem and a genuinely useful one is almost always a second and third editing pass on the instructions, not a cleverer initial prompt.

## Gems vs. ChatGPT Custom Instructions vs. Projects: What's the Difference?

Gemini Gems, ChatGPT's custom instructions, and ChatGPT Projects solve the same underlying problem — not wanting to re-explain yourself every chat — but they package the solution differently. Gems are multiple saved personas you switch between. Custom instructions are one global default. Projects add shared files and grouped chat history on top of instructions.

| Feature | Gemini Gems | ChatGPT-Style Custom Assistants |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Multiple saved AI personas, each with its own instructions | [Custom instructions](/blog/chatgpt-custom-instructions): one global instruction set. [Projects](/blog/chatgpt-projects): a workspace with instructions plus shared files |
| Best for | Switching between distinct repeated tasks — writing, coding, research, each kept separate | A single consistent tone everywhere, or ongoing work that needs shared files and history together |
| Persistence | Instructions persist per Gem; each new chat inside it still starts fresh | Custom instructions carry into every new chat by default; Projects keep related chats and files grouped together |

The short version: if you want one consistent voice everywhere with minimal setup, the lighter custom-instructions approach wins. If your work depends on shared files and history alongside instructions, a Projects-style workspace is the closer match. Gems sit in between — multiple distinct personas, but as of this writing, no shared file library the way a Projects workspace has.

## What Are Good Use Cases for Gems?

Gems earn their setup time on tasks you repeat at least weekly with the same rules every time — not one-off questions you'd ask once and move on from.

Where they actually pay off:

- **Repeated workflows** — a weekly report summarizer, a meeting-notes cleaner, an email drafter that already knows your tone
- **Brand voice enforcement** — a Gem that always writes in your company's style guide, so you stop correcting the same tone issues in every output
- **Structured outputs** — instructions to always return the same format, like a table or a fixed set of headers, which cuts down on cleanup work after the fact
- **Role-based assistants** — a "code reviewer" Gem that checks the same five things every time, a "research aide" Gem that structures findings identically across topics

The mistake people make is building a Gem for something they'll run once. That's not a Gem, that's a wasted setup step — just ask Gemini directly and move on. Save the effort for anything you'd otherwise be copy-pasting the same preamble into a fresh chat.

## The Marketing Angle: What Do Gems Mean for AI Visibility?

Every custom assistant people build — a Gem, a ChatGPT Project, a saved workflow in any other tool — is one more surface where your brand either shows up in the answer or doesn't. That's worth understanding even if you never build a Gem yourself.

People increasingly do product research inside these tools instead of a traditional search bar. Someone setting up a "shopping research" Gem is writing instructions like "compare options, check for red flags, summarize trade-offs" — and Gemini answers using whatever it knows and can find. If your brand isn't part of that answer, your website quality is beside the point.

A Gem's instructions shape tone and workflow, but they don't expand what the model actually knows about your brand — that gets decided upstream, the same underlying-knowledge question that governs [how Claude works](/blog/how-does-claude-work) regardless of what custom instructions sit on top of it. The wrapper changes the delivery. It doesn't change the facts the model has to draw from.

That upstream question is also why it's worth watching more than one platform. Whether the people researching your category lean toward Gemini or [Claude](/blog/gemini-vs-claude) changes where your visibility gaps actually are — and that's a more useful question for a marketer than which model wins a general benchmark.

There's a fast, free way to get a rough read on your own brand: build a Gem instructed to act like a buyer in your category, then ask it to recommend options. What it says — and whether your brand makes the list — tells you something in under five minutes.
