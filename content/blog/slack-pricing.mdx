Slack’s real cost is the number of active members you need to support—not the headline plan price. For a 10-person team, Pro costs $87.50 per month on monthly billing or $870 per year at the annual rate. Business+ costs $180 per month monthly or $1,800 per year at the annual rate. Free costs $0, while Enterprise+ requires a quote.

These figures use Slack’s published prices checked September 17, 2026. Recheck [Slack’s official pricing page](https://slack.com/pricing) before purchasing because prices, features, and promotions can change. Limited-time discounts are excluded from the baseline math.

## How much does Slack cost directly?

Slack’s listed plan prices are:

| Plan | Published price | Best fit |
|---|---:|---|
| Free | $0 | Small teams testing Slack or working within limited history and app access |
| Pro | $8.75/user/month monthly, or $7.25/user/month annually | Teams needing paid collaboration features and unlimited history |
| Business+ | $18/user/month monthly, or $15/user/month annually | Larger or more demanding teams needing additional business-grade capabilities |
| Enterprise+ | Quote-only | Organizations that need a negotiated enterprise deployment |

The Free plan includes 90 days of message history and supports up to 10 apps. Paid tiers show unlimited message history. That history difference can matter more than the nominal monthly price if your team regularly needs to recover old decisions, customer context, technical discussions, or project files.

Slack bills around active members. Its billing policy also says Slack credits inactive prepaid members, and activity is evaluated over a 28-day period. In other words, your invoice may not simply equal the number of people who have ever been invited. Your practical planning number is the set of members who are active under Slack’s billing rules, with attention to how inactive seats are handled.

## Which Slack plan should your team choose?

The decision is usually a sequence of gates rather than a feature shopping exercise.

1. Start with Free if 90 days of history and up to 10 apps are sufficient.

2. Move to Pro when your team needs paid-tier functionality or unlimited history, and the team does not require Business+ controls.

3. Choose Business+ when your operational, administrative, security, or organizational requirements exceed Pro.

4. Contact Slack for Enterprise+ when you need a quote-based enterprise arrangement or requirements that cannot be evaluated from public list pricing.

5. Recalculate using active members, not a rough employee count.

6. Test the workflow during the 14-day evaluation period, when available, before making an annual commitment.

This approach keeps the plan decision tied to a real operational constraint. “More expensive” does not automatically mean “better,” and the Free plan can be entirely adequate for a small team with modest history needs.

## What does Slack cost for 10, 50, or 200 active people?

The following table shows baseline pricing for active seats. It excludes taxes, negotiated terms, temporary promotions, and any non-seat charges.

| Active people | Pro monthly | Pro annual-rate total | Business+ monthly | Business+ annual-rate total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | $87.50/month | $870/year | $180/month | $1,800/year |
| 50 | $437.50/month | $4,350/year | $900/month | $9,000/year |
| 200 | $1,750/month | $17,400/year | $3,600/month | $36,000/year |

“Pro annual-rate total” means the annual price multiplied by 12 months: $7.25 × seats × 12. “Business+ annual-rate total” uses $15 × seats × 12.

For a 10-person active team, the annual Pro rate saves $180 compared with paying Pro monthly for 12 months. Business+ saves $360 compared with its monthly equivalent.

For 50 active people, Pro is $5,250 per year on monthly billing versus $4,350 at the annual rate—a difference of $900. Business+ is $10,800 versus $9,000, a difference of $1,800.

For 200 active people, Pro is $21,000 per year monthly versus $17,400 at the annual rate. Business+ is $43,200 per year monthly versus $36,000 at the annual rate.

## Do AI, history, and security requirements change the plan?

Yes. Plan selection should begin with the capabilities your team cannot compromise on.

History is the clearest first gate. Free includes 90 days of message history. Paid tiers show unlimited history. If your team treats Slack as a searchable operating record, 90 days may be too short. If Slack is only used for temporary coordination and important records are stored elsewhere, Free may be enough.

AI is a separate gate. Do not assume that paying for Pro or Business+ automatically answers every question about Slack’s AI functionality. Check the current official plan comparison and confirm which AI features, limits, eligibility rules, and administrative controls apply to your workspace.

Security and administration should also be verified directly against the current plan details. The right question is not “Does this plan sound secure?” It is “Does this plan meet our specific identity, access, retention, audit, and administrative requirements?” For a regulated or distributed organization, that distinction is important.

Slack’s [paid-versus-free comparison](https://slack.com/pricing/paid-vs-free) is the right starting point for checking current differences. Treat feature labels as a checklist to verify, not as a substitute for reviewing your organization’s requirements.

## How do guests and external collaborators affect the calculation?

Guests, contractors, agencies, clients, and partner organizations deserve a separate review. Do not automatically count every external collaborator as a normal employee seat, and do not assume that a guest arrangement has no billing or access implications.

Before inviting external users, verify:

- Whether the collaboration model is supported on your selected plan.
- Whether the person is active under Slack’s billing rules.
- Whether their access should be limited to selected channels or a specific project.
- Whether message history, files, retention, and administrative controls apply differently.
- Whether an external user needs a full workspace account or another Slack-supported collaboration method.

Your safest budget should include the active internal seats you know you need, then separately validate external-access costs and restrictions with Slack’s current documentation. The [Slack billing policy](https://slack.com/help/articles/218915077-Slacks-billing-policy) is the authoritative source for billing behavior.

## Is Slack’s 14-day evaluation enough?

A 14-day evaluation can be enough to make a practical decision if you test real work rather than merely clicking through the interface. Confirm the evaluation’s eligibility, timing, and conversion terms before starting.

During the evaluation, measure:

1. How many members actively use Slack after the first week.

2. Whether the team needs more than 90 days of history.

3. Which apps and workflows are essential.

4. Whether search reliably recovers decisions and documents.

5. Whether administrators can manage access and external collaborators as required.

6. Whether the selected AI capabilities match the team’s actual use case.

7. Whether the monthly and annual prices fit your active-seat forecast.

At the end, compare the plan’s measurable value with the cost of communication gaps, lost decisions, duplicated work, and fragmented external collaboration. A paid plan is easier to justify when the team can point to specific work it makes faster or safer.

## What is the final Slack pricing verdict?

Choose Free when your team can work with 90 days of history and up to 10 apps. Choose Pro when unlimited history and paid collaboration features solve a clear need. Choose Business+ only when its additional business requirements are necessary for your organization. Treat Enterprise+ as a quote-based procurement decision.

For baseline budgeting, plan around active members and use these reference points:

- 10 people: Pro $870/year at the annual rate; Business+ $1,800/year.
- 50 people: Pro $4,350/year; Business+ $9,000/year.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Slack Free really free?

Yes. Slack’s Free plan is listed at $0. It includes 90 days of message history and supports up to 10 apps. Confirm current limits before relying on it for long-term records.

### Does Slack charge for every invited user?

Slack’s billing policy is based on active members, and Slack credits inactive prepaid members under its billing rules. Activity is considered over a 28-day period, so calculate with care.

### Is Pro or Business+ better for a 50-person team?

Pro costs $4,350 per year at its annual rate for 50 active people. Business+ costs $9,000. Choose Business+ only if its additional requirements justify the $4,650 annual difference.

### Should a small team pay annually?

Pay annually when the active-seat forecast is stable and you are comfortable with the commitment. Stay monthly while usage, headcount, or plan requirements are still changing.
