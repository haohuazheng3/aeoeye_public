## How Do You Show Up in AI Search?

You show up in AI search when an engine can crawl your content, decide it's relevant and authoritative enough to trust, and lift out a specific answer worth quoting. That's the whole mechanism, reduced to three gates: reachable, trustworthy, quotable. Clear all three and ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI, and Claude can all cite you. Miss one, and the other two stop mattering — you stay invisible.

Most "how to rank in AI search" advice jumps straight to formatting tricks: add an FAQ block, wrap it in schema, done. That's real, but it's the last of three steps, not the first. A perfectly formatted page on a domain that blocks AI crawlers still earns zero citations. We've covered the underlying mechanics of [what AI search actually is](/blog/what-is-ai-search) elsewhere; this piece is the checklist for getting found by it, one gate at a time. Read it in order — skipping ahead to the polish step is the most common mistake we see.

## The Three Gates to Showing Up

Showing up in AI search comes down to three sequential gates — reachable, trustworthy, quotable — and you have to clear all three, not just the one you're already good at.

- **Reachable** means AI crawlers can physically access and index your pages. If GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, or Google-Extended can't fetch a page, it doesn't exist for that engine, no matter how good it is.
- **Trustworthy** means the engine recognizes you as a real, credible, clearly defined entity — not just a domain with words on it. That's built from consistent branding, clear identity signals, and mentions from sites you don't control.
- **Quotable** means the actual answer is easy to lift out. A page can be reachable and trustworthy and still get skipped if the useful sentence is buried under three paragraphs of throat-clearing.

| Gate | What It Means | How to Pass It |
|---|---|---|
| Reachable | AI crawlers can access and index your pages | Confirm robots.txt allows GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and Google-Extended; submit a current sitemap; don't hide key content behind JavaScript-only rendering |
| Trustworthy | The engine treats you as a credible, clearly defined entity | Use one consistent brand name everywhere, keep a real About page, add Organization schema, earn mentions on sites you don't own |
| Quotable | A specific answer can be lifted cleanly for a response | Open each section with a direct one- or two-sentence answer, use headers and lists, name specifics instead of hedging |

Notice the order isn't arbitrary. There's no point polishing your quotability if the crawler can't reach the page in the first place — fix gates in sequence, not all at once.

## Why You Might Be Invisible Right Now

If you're not showing up in AI search, it's almost always one or more of five problems: blocked crawlers, buried answers, a fuzzy brand identity, no outside corroboration, or a domain too new to have earned trust yet.

- **Blocked AI crawlers.** A security plugin, a CDN default, or an old robots.txt rule can silently disallow GPTBot and its counterparts. Check your access against the [full list of AI crawlers](/blog/ai-crawler-list) — it's a five-minute fix that a surprising number of sites never make.
- **Thin or buried answers.** The information exists, but it's wrapped in a long introduction, a cookie banner, or a slider before the actual answer ever shows up. Engines extract what's easy to extract.
- **A weak or ambiguous entity.** If your brand name changes across pages, or there's no clear statement of who you are and what you do, the engine can't confidently connect your content to one trustworthy source.
- **No third-party corroboration.** Nobody outside your own site says you're good at what you do. AI engines weight independent confirmation far more heavily than self-description — your homepage claiming you're "the best" is not evidence.
- **A brand-new domain with no authority.** Zero history, zero backlinks, zero mentions elsewhere. That's not a permanent problem, but it does mean you're starting the trust gate from nothing.

Most sites we audit have at least two of these stacked on top of each other, which is why single fixes rarely move the needle by themselves.

## The Cross-Engine Reality

Showing up in ChatGPT does not mean you show up in Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI, or Claude. Each one runs its own crawler, its own index, and its own model — a presence you've earned in one is simply invisible to the rest until you earn it there too.

The mechanics differ more than most people assume:

- Perplexity leans on its own real-time index and rewards fresh, well-structured pages.
- Google AI Overviews draws heavily on Google's existing search index, so classic technical SEO still carries weight there.
- ChatGPT's browsing and search features combine multiple sources with its own crawling.
- Claude uses web search when it's enabled, pulling live results rather than relying only on training data.
- Gemini draws on Google's index and knowledge graph, but the model makes its own call about what to surface.

Treat each engine as a separate audience with its own gatekeeper, not as one monolithic "AI search" target. The practical takeaway: don't assume. Verify per engine, because "we show up in AI search" is meaningless without saying which one.

## A Simple Action Plan

Fixing AI search visibility is a five-step sequence: unblock the crawlers, fix your entity signals, earn outside corroboration, rewrite your key pages answer-first, then test across every engine you actually care about.

1. **Audit crawler access.** Check robots.txt, your CDN, and your WAF for rules blocking GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended. Unblock anything that shouldn't be blocked.
2. **Fix entity clarity.** Settle on one canonical brand name, write a real About page, and add Organization schema so engines know exactly who's speaking.
3. **Earn corroboration.** Get mentioned, reviewed, or linked from sites you don't control — this is the step most brands skip, and it's the one AI engines weight hardest for trust. Our guide to [tracking brand mentions](/blog/brand-mentions) covers how to monitor this over time.
4. **Rewrite for quotability.** Put the direct answer in the first two sentences of every section. Structure the rest with headers, lists, and tables so an engine can lift a clean, self-contained chunk.
5. **Test and iterate.** Ask each engine the exact questions your buyers type, note who gets cited, and go fix the gap.

Steps two through four have a lot of moving parts on their own. We walk through the deeper mechanics in [how to optimize for AI search](/blog/how-to-optimize-for-ai-search) if you want the fuller version of this plan.

## How to Know If You Show Up

The only reliable way to know if you show up in AI search is to ask the engines the questions your buyers actually ask, and log who gets named. There's no impressions dashboard for ChatGPT or Perplexity the way there is for Google Search Console.

Here's a workable process:

- Write down ten to twenty real buyer questions: "best [category] for [use case]," "[you] vs. [competitor]," "is [you] worth it."
- Run every question through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Claude.
- Log whether you're named, cited with a link, or absent entirely — and note who shows up instead of you.
- Repeat monthly, or after any major content change. Models update and competitors publish constantly, so a one-time check goes stale within weeks.
