## Which is better: SE Ranking or Semrush?

SE Ranking is the stronger value choice for teams that need daily rank tracking, audits, and AI-search monitoring in one plan; Semrush is stronger when database breadth and a mature toolkit ecosystem justify the premium.

The practical choice depends less on brand familiarity than on how much of each platform you will use. SE Ranking is no longer simply a low-cost rank tracker. Its current Core plan combines project management, daily keyword tracking, website audits, competitive research, and GEO-oriented capacity in a relatively direct workflow.

Semrush remains the more expansive platform. Its connected tools cover competitive research, keyword discovery, backlinks, content, advertising, reporting, and broader market analysis. That depth can support sophisticated workflows, but it also creates a higher bar for onboarding, ownership, and budget control. For a wider feature overview, compare [SE Ranking’s review](/blog/se-ranking-review) with [Semrush alternatives](/blog/semrush-alternatives).

## How do current prices compare?

As of August 29, 2026, SE Ranking lists Core at $129 monthly or $103.20 on annual billing, while Semrush SEO Toolkit Pro lists $139.95 monthly; plan limits make that narrow sticker gap much wider in practice.

The headline monthly difference is only $10.95, but the included capacity is not equivalent. SE Ranking’s annual rate lowers the effective monthly cost, while Semrush’s Pro price represents its SEO Toolkit entry tier. Billing frequency, add-ons, extra users, and the number of tracked sites can materially change the total bill.

The fairest comparison starts with the workflows you actually need. If your team mainly tracks rankings, audits sites, monitors competitors, and reviews emerging AI-search visibility, SE Ranking may cover the job with less unused functionality. If you need Semrush’s specialized databases and connected toolkits every week, its higher cost can be rational. See the [official SE Ranking subscription page](https://seranking.com/subscription.html) and [Semrush pricing documentation](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1547-seo-toolkit-pricing-and-plans) before committing.

## What capacity do you get?

SE Ranking Core lists 10 projects, 2,000 daily keywords, 100 daily prompts, and 250,000 audited pages; Semrush Pro lists five websites, 500 tracked keywords, and 100,000 crawled pages monthly.

| Capability | SE Ranking Core | Semrush Pro | Buying implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Listed monthly billing | $129 | $139.95 | Similar headline range |
| Annual-billing monthly equivalent | $103.20 | Check current terms | Annual plans change the gap |
| Projects or websites | 10 projects | 5 websites | SE Ranking offers more listed containers |
| Daily tracked keywords | 2,000 | 500 tracked keywords | SE Ranking has greater stated tracking capacity |
| AI-search prompts | 100 daily prompts | Depends on AI Visibility setup | Compare prompt and engine limits |
| Audit capacity | 250,000 pages | 100,000 pages monthly | Limits use different measurement periods |
| Research ecosystem | Core SEO and GEO workflow | Broader connected toolkit | Semrush trades capacity for breadth |

These figures require an apples-to-apples warning. A “project” is not necessarily the same thing as a “website,” and daily keyword limits do not measure the same workload as monthly crawl limits. Semrush’s tracked-keyword allowance may also be distributed across campaigns and reporting needs, whereas SE Ranking’s listed daily capacity describes a different operating model. The [Semrush plan comparison](https://www.semrush.com/kb/856-subscription-plans-comparison) explains how its limits are structured.

## Which has better research depth?

Semrush’s advantage is the breadth and maturity of interconnected competitive, keyword, backlink, content, advertising, and reporting toolkits; SE Ranking’s advantage is usable capacity in a simpler plan.

Semrush is the better fit for teams that move between many research questions. You may begin with a competitor domain, inspect keyword opportunities, examine backlink profiles, review content gaps, investigate advertising activity, and build reports in one larger environment. That breadth is valuable when SEO is part of a wider acquisition or market-intelligence process.

SE Ranking is more compelling when the core job is operational SEO. A team can maintain rankings, audit technical issues, compare competitors, and organize recurring work without paying primarily for a large ecosystem. The trade-off is that a broader or more specialized research program may eventually require additional tools or a higher-tier workflow.

## Which is better for agencies?

SE Ranking exposes an Agency Pack and manager-seat model, while Semrush scales sites, tracked keywords, rows, reports, and extra-user fees; agencies should price their actual client roster.

For an agency, the decisive question is not simply which subscription is cheaper. Calculate the number of active clients, tracked terms per client, audit frequency, reporting rows, users, and expected growth. SE Ranking can be attractive when many accounts need dependable recurring monitoring and the agency wants clear capacity in the base workflow.

Semrush can make more sense for agencies selling deeper competitive research, content planning, market analysis, or integrated reporting. Its broader toolkit may support a more expansive service proposition, but the agency should assign ownership for training, templates, and usage controls. Otherwise, the team may purchase breadth that only one specialist uses.

Review the [SE Ranking pricing details](/blog/se-ranking-pricing) and [Semrush pricing guide](/blog/semrush-pricing) alongside a simple client-by-client capacity model. A platform that fits today’s roster but becomes expensive after two new retainers is not the lower-cost choice in practice.

## Which is better for AI-search tracking?

SE Ranking’s current plans include GEO prompts and five LLMs, with a larger AI Search add-on; Semrush offers AI Visibility as a toolkit or bundle, so neither comparison is complete without prompt and engine limits.

AI-search monitoring changes the buying decision because visibility is no longer limited to traditional blue-link rankings. Teams may want to track whether a brand, product, or organization appears in generated answers, which sources are associated with those answers, and how visibility changes across prompts and models. That requires comparing the number of prompts, supported engines, refresh frequency, and reporting detail—not just whether a product says “AI.”

SE Ranking’s [AI Search add-on documentation](https://help.seranking.com/hc/en-us/articles/22120452776476-AI-Search-Add-on) describes the relevant expansion path, while Semrush’s [Semrush Questions resource](https://www.semrush.com/semrush-questions/) helps clarify how its broader platform positions search and visibility workflows. AEOeye should enter this decision only when AI-search visibility is a real part of the team’s buying criteria; it should not be treated as a substitute for ordinary SEO capacity.

## Who should choose each platform?

Choose SE Ranking when capacity and cost discipline matter most; choose Semrush when a team will repeatedly use its deeper ecosystem and can assign an owner to it.

Choose SE Ranking if you want a comparatively focused operating base for rank tracking, audits, project management, competitor monitoring, and current GEO work. It is especially persuasive when the listed limits fit your workload and you would otherwise leave much of Semrush’s ecosystem unused. The annual Core rate strengthens that value case, provided you are comfortable with the billing commitment.

Choose Semrush if your work depends on a broad set of interconnected research tools, larger competitive investigations, advertising context, content workflows, or established reporting processes. Its premium is easier to defend when several people use those capabilities regularly and the platform supports revenue-generating services.

The final decision should be based on a written workload, not a feature-count contest. List your sites, keywords, prompts, users, reports, audits, and recurring research tasks. Then compare the plan that handles those requirements with the fewest paid expansions and the least operational friction.

## FAQs

### Is SE Ranking cheaper than Semrush?

Usually, SE Ranking is cheaper on annual billing and offers higher listed tracking capacity at its Core level. The real difference depends on users, add-ons, billing frequency, and whether Semrush’s broader toolkit replaces other software.

### Which tracks more keywords?

On the stated plans, SE Ranking Core lists 2,000 daily keywords, while Semrush Pro lists 500 tracked keywords. These limits use different wording and should be mapped to your actual rank-tracking schedule before purchase.

### Which is better for agencies?

SE Ranking is generally the stronger choice for agencies prioritizing capacity and recurring SEO operations. Semrush is stronger when the agency repeatedly sells deeper competitive, content, backlink, advertising, or market-research work.

### Which is better for AI search?

Neither wins universally. SE Ranking presents clear GEO prompt and LLM capacity in its current plans, while Semrush offers AI Visibility within its wider toolkit; compare engines, prompts, refreshes, and reporting against your specific workflow.
