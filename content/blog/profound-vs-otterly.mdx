"Profound" and "Otterly.ai" get typed into the same search bar constantly, and it's easy to see why — both show up in nearly every "AI visibility tool" roundup. Both promise the same broad thing: visibility into how AI answer engines talk about your brand. The similarities mostly end there — these two are built for nearly opposite teams, and picking the wrong tier wastes more money than picking the wrong brand ever will.

## Otterly.ai at a glance

Otterly.ai is an AI search monitoring tool aimed at small businesses and solo marketers — quick to set up and affordable, with some engines offered as add-ons rather than bundled in from day one.

That's the whole pitch, and it's a good one. If you're a solopreneur, a two-person marketing team, or an agency running lean for a handful of clients, you don't need a six-figure contract to find out whether AI answer engines mention your brand when someone asks for a recommendation. You need something you can set up this afternoon and start reading results from tomorrow.

There's also a simpler reason small teams gravitate here: the alternative to using any monitoring tool at all is guessing. Before tools like this existed, the honest answer to "does ChatGPT recommend us" was "we have no idea." A fast, affordable way to replace that guess with a real answer is worth something on its own, before you even get into feature comparisons.

The tradeoff shows up in coverage. Otterly's entry-level plans tend to focus on a core set of engines, with others — Gemini and Claude are the two people ask about most — sold as add-ons instead of included by default. That's not a knock; it's a pricing model that lets small teams pay for exactly what they track instead of subsidizing coverage they'll never look at. We broke down [what the Gemini and Claude add-ons actually cost](/answers/otterly-ai-gemini-claude-add-on-cost) if you want current numbers before you commit.

Is it worth paying for at all? For the audience it's built for, generally yes — see our full [Otterly.ai worth-it verdict](/answers/is-otterly-worth-it) for the reasoning. The short version: it earns its keep when your question is "are we showing up," not "why aren't we showing up, across every market, for every product line."

Otterly.ai isn't going to give a ten-brand agency the multi-client infrastructure it needs, and it's not trying to — being excellent for one audience beats being mediocre for everyone, and Otterly.ai clearly picked its audience on purpose.

## Profound at a glance

Profound is enterprise-grade: deeper, broader, and pricier than the small-business tools in this category, built for large brands and agencies that need to track AI visibility at scale rather than for a single site.

That scale is the whole point. A large retailer or a global agency managing AI visibility for a dozen client brands isn't just asking whether they show up in AI answers — they're asking it across more prompts, more markets, and more stakeholders who each need their own view of the data. That requires infrastructure, not a dashboard one person checks once a week.

The other thing enterprise teams weigh that small teams usually don't: procurement. Legal review, security questionnaires, multi-seat contracts, invoicing that matches how a large company actually pays for software — enterprise-grade pricing exists partly because enterprise-grade buying is its own project. That's less about a feature checklist and more about which vendor is built to survive that process at all.

It also requires budget. Profound's pricing isn't the kind of number you'll find on a self-serve signup page — as of 2026, you'll need to check directly with the vendor for current plans, and, like most enterprise software, that usually means a sales conversation rather than a credit card form. That's normal at this end of the market, and it's exactly why comparing Profound's price tag to Otterly's is a bit like comparing a fleet lease to a monthly car payment — the numbers were never meant to look similar.

Profound isn't the right fit if you're a single-location business watching one product category — you'd be paying enterprise rates for capacity you'll never use. That's not a flaw in Profound; it's a mismatch in who's buying.

## Key differences

The fastest way to see the split: Otterly.ai wins on price and speed, Profound wins on depth and scale.

| | Otterly.ai | Profound |
|---|---|---|
| Built for | Small businesses, solo marketers | Large brands, agencies |
| Setup | Self-serve, fast | Sales-assisted onboarding |
| Pricing | Affordable, entry-level | Premium/enterprise — contact vendor |
| Typical commitment | Month-to-month, self-serve | Annual or custom contract |
| Engine coverage | Core engines included; Gemini/Claude often add-ons | Broad coverage as standard |
| Depth of reporting | Lightweight, straightforward | Deeper analytics, multi-stakeholder views |
| Best-fit team size | Solo to small team | Cross-functional org |

Pricing and packaging change often in this category — treat the table as directional, and confirm current plans on each vendor's site before you buy. Notice what's not on this table: neither vendor is objectively "better" in the abstract. The table describes fit, not quality — the same way a moped and a delivery van aren't ranked against each other, just matched to different jobs.

## Which should you choose?

Pick Otterly.ai if you're a small team or solo marketer on a tight budget who wants to start monitoring AI mentions today. Pick Profound if you're an enterprise brand or agency where deeper, broader coverage justifies a bigger line item.

The mistake we see more often than "wrong vendor" is "wrong size." A five-person startup signing an enterprise contract ends up paying for seats and dashboards nobody opens. An enterprise team trying to run multi-brand, multi-market monitoring on a starter tool ends up exporting spreadsheets by hand because the product was never built for that scale. Both situations are avoidable if you're honest about which category you're actually in before you look at either vendor's homepage.

A few signals worth weighing before you pick a tier:

- **Team size.** One to three people handling marketing? Start with Otterly.ai. A cross-functional team spanning SEO, brand, and PR? Lean toward Profound.
- **Number of brands or markets.** Tracking one brand in one market favors Otterly.ai's simplicity. Multiple brands, languages, or regions favor Profound's scale.
- **Budget cycle.** Paying out of a small monthly card limit? Otterly.ai's self-serve pricing fits. Working from an annual enterprise software budget? Profound's model fits better.
- **Reporting audience.** A solo marketer reporting to themselves needs far less structure than an agency reporting to five different clients every month.
