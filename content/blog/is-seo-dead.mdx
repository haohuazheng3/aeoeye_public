No, SEO isn't dead, and ChatGPT isn't replacing Google. Google says searches are still growing; ChatGPT is a second front door. What's dying is lazy SEO: rank a generic page, collect the click, now that AI summaries answer many questions above the results. What's growing is being the source an AI answer names, and that still runs on SEO fundamentals.

## Is SEO dead?

No. SEO has been declared dead before and keeps outliving the obituaries. What's breaking is narrower: the assumption that a ranking automatically brings a click. [Search engine optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine_optimization) itself, making pages findable and trustworthy, still matters.

The click was already scarce before AI. [SparkToro's 2024 clickstream study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/) found that 58.5% of U.S. Google searches ended with no click.

- **Shrinking:** thin "what is X" explainers, same-five-products lists and several pages per intent.
- **Growing:** being the named source in AI answers, from [Google AI Overviews and AI Mode](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (Google's AI summaries and AI search) to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini.

## Is Google actually losing searchers?

Not on the public evidence we can check. Google said in March 2025 that it sees [more than 5 trillion searches a year](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ai-personalization-and-the-future-of-shopping/), and in July 2026 that AI experiences are [driving growth in queries](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/), with Search usage at an all-time high during the World Cup. As of September 2026, [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share) gives Google 89.94% of worldwide search-engine referrals.

Neither is a clean count. Google's figures are self-reported, and StatCounter [counts clicks](https://gs.statcounter.com/faq) from search engines to sites that use its tracker, so searches that end without a click never appear. Gartner [predicted in February 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-02-19-gartner-predicts-search-engine-volume-will-drop-25-percent-by-2026-due-to-ai-chatbots-and-other-virtual-agents) that traditional search volume would fall 25% by 2026; we can't verify volume independently, but Google's statements point the other way.

It feels like Google is dying because of SEO filler, AI summaries on the page and ChatGPT habits. Each is real, but none shows up in these figures.

The open web still behaves as if Google matters most. In AEOeye's [crawler access study](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory) (3 October 2026, 384 well-known sites), only Reddit, via a blanket disallow, blocked Googlebot, while 9.1% blocked GPTBot and 4.2% blocked OAI-SearchBot, the crawler behind ChatGPT search. None of 38 news publishers blocked Googlebot, yet 27 blocked at least one AI retrieval bot.

Our reading: owners treat Google as the channel that sends visitors and AI crawlers as takers. The sample is convenience-picked, and robots.txt is a request, not enforcement.

## Will ChatGPT replace Google?

No, it's a second front door. OpenAI said at its 29 September 2026 DevDay that ChatGPT has [1.2 billion weekly users](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/). Yet in a mid-2025 Gartner survey of 377 U.S. consumers, [only about one-third](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/gartner-survey-finds-only-one-third-of-consumers-say-genai-rivals-search-engines-marketers-must-optimize-for-both-ai-driven-and-traditional-search) said chatbots are as effective as search engines for learning new information.

Scale check (our arithmetic): an [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255) co-written by OpenAI researchers counted 18 billion ChatGPT messages a week in July 2025, about 49% of them "Asking" (seeking information or clarification), or roughly 8.8 billion. Google's [5 trillion-plus searches a year](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ai-personalization-and-the-future-of-shopping/) is about 96 billion a week.

Even if every Asking message were a lost Google search, that is at most about 9% of Google's volume. Scale that for user growth since (700 million to 1.2 billion) and it is about one in six at most. Messages aren't searches and the dates differ, so read it as an order of magnitude.

But the shift is real. In the same paper, "Seeking Information" grew from 14% to 24% of messages in a year, which the authors say looks like a close substitute for web search. People use both, though: [Semrush's U.S. clickstream panel](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-search-insights/) found over 21% of ChatGPT's outbound referral traffic going to Google by early 2026, and ChatGPT searching the web on only 34.5% of queries in February 2026.

Google adds AI to search while ChatGPT adds search to [AI](/blog/what-is-ai-search), so your buyers now have two front doors, and each builds its own shortlist.

## What is AI search really changing?

Three things: clicks, the shortlist and measurement. AI summaries cut clicks on the queries they answer, AI answers name a handful of brands instead of ten links, and your traffic reports can't show whether you're named. AI search doesn't replace SEO; it forks it into two scoreboards: rankings and clicks, and names and citations.

- **Clicks.** In [Pew's browsing data](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) (900 U.S. adults, March 2025), people clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without, and a link inside the summary in 1%. Ahrefs [estimates](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) the top-ranked page's click-through rate is 58% lower when a Google AI Overview appears (300,000 keywords, December 2025 data). Treat a citation as a brand impression, not the old click.
- **The shortlist.** Ranking isn't citing. [Ahrefs found](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/) that on average only 12% of links cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot appear in Google's top 10 for the same prompt (15,000 prompts, July 2025), partly because assistants search variations of your question (query fan-out). In [AEOeye's audit records](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json) (June to September 2026), answers named about three competitors, and a ranked brand came first in 71.4% of 147 ranked mentions. That is a snapshot of 127 audits of self-selected brands, mostly Claude answers, not a benchmark.
- **Measurement.** [Google's documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (updated December 2025) counts AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic inside the ordinary Web report in Search Console and describes no separate AI report. Nothing in Search Console shows what ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity say about you, so you have to ask them your buyers' questions.

Google says total organic clicks were [relatively stable](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/) year over year as of August 2025, with traffic moving between sites. Both can be true: Google reports an aggregate, while Pew and Ahrefs measure rates on the queries summaries answer ([click data](/blog/ai-overviews-and-your-traffic), [wider statistics](/blog/ai-search-statistics)).

## Is SEO still worth investing in?

Yes, if your pages earn their place. SEO is still the entry ticket: Google says there are [no additional requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) for AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond being indexed and eligible for a snippet, and [ChatGPT search won't show sites that block OAI-SearchBot](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

**Keep investing if:**

- Your demand is short, specific queries. Pew found summaries on 60% of searches starting with who, what, when or why, but only 8% of one- or two-word searches.
- You can publish what a summary can't flatten: original data, first-hand testing, tools, a stance. Google says people are [increasingly clicking](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/) forums, videos and posts with first-hand perspectives.
- You want to be named, not only clicked. In the [GEO research benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), adding statistics, quotations and cited sources lifted visibility in generated answers by up to 40%; keyword stuffing gave little to no improvement.

**Rethink if you publish several pages per intent.** We retired 234 of our own posts in September and merged 165 pages into 42 on 3 October; this page replaces four. One strong page per question beats five thin ones, for Google and for AI retrieval.

To run both scoreboards:

1. **Keep the fundamentals.** Crawlable, indexed, fast pages, and a CDN that doesn't turn bots away: 16% of the 375 homepages we requested blocked or challenged our declared research crawler, and a challenge can hide you from AI retrieval bots without any robots.txt rule.
2. **Answer first.** Open every question heading with a self-contained 40–60 word answer.
3. **Measure names, not only rank.** Search Console covers rankings and clicks. For names and citations, ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI the questions your buyers ask, and repeat after big changes. That second job is called [answer engine optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization) (AEO); [AEO vs SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo) covers what carries over.

## What will search look like in the next few years?

Our prediction for the future of search, and it is an opinion: search decentralizes instead of disappearing. Google stays the biggest source of visits, while more questions start in chat windows and with agents. The winners get named in all of them.

| Stage | How people ask | How you get found |
|---|---|---|
| Classic search | Keywords, then scan ten links | Rank and win the click |
| AI answers | One question, one synthesized answer | Be named or cited in it |
| Conversation | Follow-ups that carry context | Stay consistent across turns |
| Delegation | "Handle this for me" | Be the option an agent picks |

Google says it is adding [agents, personal intelligence and notebooks](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/) to Search, and on 29 September 2026 OpenAI introduced [Dots](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/), agents that each get their own cloud computer and browser.

An [AI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent) pursues goals and takes actions, so with agentic search the reader of your page may be a model choosing for someone else. It's still early; we won't guess dates.

So the useful question isn't "is SEO dead" but "am I named where my buyers ask?"
