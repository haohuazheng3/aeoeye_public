The official page advertises 20% savings with annual billing and a 14-day trial. This is a documentation-based buyer guide, not a hands-on review; AEOeye did not run a paid account or test accuracy. Confirm the final charge, taxes, and add-ons at checkout.

For an in-house brand or agency, the useful comparison is prompt-by-model coverage plus repeatable answers, not just the headline subscription. Searchable AI pricing should be weighed against how many buyer questions the team needs to track, how often it needs fresh observations, and which engines matter for monitoring brand mentions, citations, and recommendations.

## What does Searchable cost in 2026?

Searchable lists Pro at $125/month, Scale at $400/month, Enterprise at $999/month, and Custom pricing by quote. Its pricing page advertises 20% savings for annual billing and a 14-day trial.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pro | $125 | 100 | 9,000 | 200 | 20 |
| Scale | $400 | 500 | 45,000 | 1,000 | 80 |
| Enterprise | $999 | 1,250 | 112,500 | 3,000 | 200 |
| Custom | Quote | Confirm with vendor | Confirm with vendor | Confirm with vendor | Confirm with vendor |

The three included engines listed publicly are ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity. Additional engines are paid add-ons or custom arrangements; the page does not establish their prices. ## What does each plan include?

Every listed tier increases the prompt, analyzed-answer, audit, and article limits. Scale also lists white-label and API access. The public allowances do not specify how often prompts run or how an analyzed answer is counted.

Pro lists 100 prompts per model, 9,000 AI answers analyzed monthly, 200 audits, and 20 articles. Scale lists 500 prompts per model, 45,000 answers, 1,000 audits, and 80 articles, plus white-label and API access. Enterprise lists 1,250 prompts per model, 112,500 answers, 3,000 audits, and 200 articles. Custom is quote-based, so confirm its limits and terms directly.

Ask how “analyzed answer” and “audit” are defined, whether unused capacity carries over, and how repeated runs consume the allowance. For context, see AEOeye’s guide to [measuring AI visibility](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility).

## How does annual billing change the cost?

The official page advertises a 20% saving with annual billing. Applying that percentage to twelve months of list-price billing yields an expected amount; confirm the actual total and payment terms at checkout.

Worked Scale calculation: $400 × 12 = $4,800 at monthly list price for a year. Applying the advertised reduction gives $4,800 × 0.80 = $3,840 expected annual cost, equivalent to $320/month. For Pro, $125 × 12 = $1,500; applying 20% implies $1,200 per year, or $100/month equivalent.

These are calculations, not confirmed invoices. Check whether annual payment is upfront, renewal and cancellation terms, and whether the discount applies to add-ons or only the base plan. No tax or add-on cost is included because no price is established for them here.

## What does “prompts per model” mean for budgeting?

The prompt allowance is stated per model, so plan capacity should be mapped across the engines you intend to monitor. Searchable lists ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity as the three included engines.

For example, Pro’s 100 prompts per model across those three engines represents 300 prompt-engine pairings if the full allowance is used for each. That is a capacity illustration, not a statement about run frequency or answer volume. The separate 9,000-answer monthly limit also applies; ask how repeated runs, variations, or failed responses count.

Build the prompt set from brand and product names, category discovery, comparisons, and high-intent buyer questions. Set a refresh cadence suited to the decisions being made. See AEOeye’s [AI search monitoring guide](/blog/ai-search-monitoring).

## Which plan fits a brand versus an agency?

Pro may fit one brand with a focused prompt set; Scale is more plausible when capacity, API access, or white-label reporting matters. Enterprise offers larger listed limits. Choose based on the required prompts, engines, and workflow rather than the tier label.

An in-house team should estimate prompt-engine pairings for priority products and markets, then compare them with the monthly answer allowance. If Pro covers the important questions at a useful cadence, unused capacity is hard to justify. If a fourth engine is needed, confirm availability and price before comparing plans.

An agency should estimate prompts, audits, and reporting needs for each client. Scale’s white-label and API features may fit a service workflow, but its 80 articles do not establish how many monitoring projects are supported. Ask whether limits are shared across clients or workspaces. Enterprise’s higher ceilings matter only if the program can use them.

A large answer count without clear coverage and rerun behavior can create false precision. See AEOeye’s [best AI visibility tools guide](/blog/best-ai-visibility-tools) for a broader comparison.

## What should you verify before buying?

Confirm limit definitions, engine access, overage behavior, and annual terms in writing. The published ceilings are useful, but operational details affect the effective Searchable cost.

Before the trial ends, ask:

- How often does each prompt run, and can the schedule be controlled?
- What counts as an analyzed answer, including retries or duplicate responses?
- Are prompt limits shared across projects, users, or clients?
- Which additional engines are available, and what is their add-on or custom pricing?
- What happens when a monthly limit is reached?
- What are the annual payment, renewal, cancellation, and refund terms?
- Can observations and citations be exported, and how long is history retained?

Use the 14-day trial to assess workflow fit; this guide did not test outputs. Compare fixed buyer prompts across relevant engines, save dated examples, and check whether a teammate can reproduce the conclusion. See AEOeye’s [AEO audit checklist](/blog/aeo-audit-checklist).
