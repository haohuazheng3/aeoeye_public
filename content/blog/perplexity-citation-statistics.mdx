Perplexity is citation-forward, not citation-perfect. The best platform-specific evidence available in 2026 says that it returned the wrong article, publisher, or URL in **37% of a controlled 200-query news test** ([Tow Center/CJR, March 2025](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). That is the useful answer to “How accurate are Perplexity citations?”: visible source cards make checking easier, but they do not remove the need to check.

It separates source retrieval, claim support, and Google overlap; these have different denominators.

## Why do source-overlap studies disagree?

The apparent conflict—28.6% URL overlap in Ahrefs versus 82% in Semrush—is mostly a denominator and design problem. Ahrefs asked assistants the same **15,000 long-tail prompts**, extracted visible citations, and checked whether each cited URL ranked in Google's top 10 for the original prompt ([methodology](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/)). Semrush counted overlapping domains and URLs against Google's top 10 in a 5,000-keyword study with more than 150,000 citations ([methodology](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-mode-comparison-study/)).

Those are not interchangeable percentages. Query wording, geography, language, date, citation definition, and URL-versus-domain comparison can move the result. A domain overlap can be high while the exact article differs.

The practical conclusion is narrower: Perplexity appears more aligned with Google's results than the other assistants in both studies, but still retrieves beyond the exact top result. Ahrefs found only **12% of AI citations overall** in Google's top 10 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/)); Perplexity was the outlier.

## How can researchers reproduce a Perplexity citation comparison?

Start with a frozen prompt set and write down the exact wording, locale, date, account tier, selected model, and search mode. Save the complete answer, every visible citation, and the destination URL before judging quality. Normalize URLs by removing tracking parameters, but preserve redirects and publisher paths so syndicated copies remain distinguishable. Then score each result in separate columns: citation present, URL resolves, publisher matches, claim is supported, and answer is factually correct. Publish the rubric with the results. This prevents a later interface change from silently changing the denominator, and lets another researcher rerun the same protocol against [Perplexity's documented search behavior](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352895-how-does-perplexity-work).

## Does a citation prove the claim is correct?

No. Citation correctness is claim-level support, not the mere presence of a clickable card. The foundational 2023 verifiability study defines **citation recall** as the share of externally checkable statements fully supported by citations and **citation precision** as the share of citations that support the associated statement ([Liu, Zhang, and Liang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)). Across four systems, only 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported and 74.5% of citations supported their associated sentence ([same study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)). Those are aggregate figures, not a current Perplexity-only score.

The Tow Center test exposes a different failure: source identity. Researchers supplied excerpts that would return the original article in Google's first three results, then asked for the headline, publisher, date, and URL ([CJR methodology](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). Perplexity could display a source card and still identify the wrong article or a copied version. Audit retrieval success and claim support separately.

Perplexity's source-label documentation makes the same distinction: a Government, Academic, or Trusted label describes the website as a whole, **not the accuracy of an individual page or claim**, and is not an endorsement ([Perplexity Help Center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/20260806-understanding-source-labels)).

## What can we say about free versus Pro?

Only what has been measured. The Tow Center included both Perplexity and Perplexity Pro in its 2025 eight-tool test ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). It reported that premium systems generally produced more confidently incorrect answers, but the public article's headline gives the 37% error rate for Perplexity and does not provide a separate Pro percentage in that summary. Therefore, “Pro is more accurate” is not supported by that source.

Feature differences can change results. Perplexity says Pro Search performs multiple web searches across articles, papers, forums, videos, and other sources ([Perplexity Help](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352903-what-is-pro-search)). That describes a retrieval path, not an accuracy lift. A fair comparison needs the same prompts, date, locale, settings, and denominator.

## How should publishers and brands use these statistics?

Treat citation visibility as an observable opportunity, not an accuracy certificate. A page can be discoverable, selected, cited, and still be misattributed—or cited for a claim it does not support. Verify the exact sentence, the date, the publisher identity, and whether the linked page is the original source.

For measurement, keep a prompt log with four fields: answer mention, cited URL, claim support, and publisher attribution. Repeat prompts because indexes, model routing, and page availability change. Perplexity describes real-time web search and citations as core behavior ([official help](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352895-how-does-perplexity-work)); yesterday's citation snapshot is not permanent.

## How should a Perplexity citation be verified?

Use a repeatable five-step check instead of trusting the source card at face value. First, open the cited URL and confirm it resolves to the original publisher rather than a copied or syndicated version. Second, match the answer's claim to the exact passage on the page. Third, check the publication and update dates, especially for prices, product features, policies, and personnel. Fourth, record whether the answer names the brand and source correctly. Finally, rerun the same prompt on a later date and save both outputs; retrieval indexes and product routing change.

For team reporting, keep citation presence, URL validity, publisher attribution, claim support, and answer accuracy in separate columns. This prevents a high citation count from hiding a weak evidence chain. It also makes corrections actionable: a broken URL calls for a technical fix, a wrong publisher calls for entity clarification, and an unsupported claim calls for better primary evidence.
