If you searched “Perplexity market share,” you probably want one clean number. Here's the honest version: Perplexity is smaller than ChatGPT or Gemini in overall AI-chatbot usage, but it isn't really competing in that race. ## What Is Perplexity's Market Share?

Perplexity holds a smaller overall share of AI-chatbot usage than ChatGPT or Gemini, but it's a fast-growing specialist within AI search specifically. As of mid-2025, Perplexity was handling roughly 780 million queries per month, according to [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/perplexity-780-million-monthly-queries-month-456725) (June 2025).

That figure is a query count, not a percentage — and the distinction matters more than most coverage admits. Precise market-share splits between AI tools shift monthly and depend heavily on what's being measured: web visits, app opens, API calls, or total sessions all produce different numbers. Rather than repeat a stale percentage, check [StatCounter's AI chatbot market share tracker](https://gs.statcounter.com/ai-chatbot-market-share) directly — it updates continuously and is one of the few public, methodology-transparent sources tracking this space at all.

Quick summary of where things actually stand:

- **Confirmed:** roughly 780 million monthly queries, as of June 2025 (Search Engine Land).
- **Directionally true:** a smaller overall share than ChatGPT and Gemini, but larger and faster-growing than most other AI-search challengers.
- **Not confirmed here:** any specific percentage split — check StatCounter's live tracker for current figures.
- **For scale:** see how that compares to the broader [search engine market share](/blog/search-engine-market-share) landscape, where Google still dominates by a wide margin.

Perplexity launched publicly in December 2022, which makes its growth trajectory genuinely fast by any startup standard. But treat that as two separate claims: fast growth within its niche is real, and overtaking ChatGPT's total scale is not — don't let one imply the other when you're reading stats about either company.

## Perplexity's Niche: AI Search, Not General Chat

Perplexity competes as an answer engine — a search replacement — not as a general-purpose chatbot. That's a fundamentally different contest than the one ChatGPT and Gemini are fighting over assistant share.

Open Perplexity and the product tells you exactly what it's for:

- A query box styled like a search bar, not a chat window
- A sourced, cited answer up front, before any conversation starts
- A thread of follow-up questions, not open-ended chit-chat
- No character personas and no “write me a poem” positioning front and center

That focus is why comparing Perplexity's numbers directly to [ChatGPT's market share](/blog/chatgpt-market-share) can be misleading. ChatGPT serves an enormous range of use cases — coding, writing, brainstorming, tutoring — while Perplexity's traffic skews toward one thing: people who typed a question expecting a researched, cited answer back. Smaller total usage, narrower job, a genuinely different competitive set.

That narrowness is a strategic choice, not a limitation. Perplexity isn't trying to out-feature ChatGPT on coding help or Gemini on Workspace integration. It's betting that a meaningful share of searches — the ones where people want a fast, sourced, ad-free answer — are better served by a dedicated answer engine than by a general chatbot or a traditional page of ten blue links.

## Reading the Numbers Carefully

Before you repeat any AI usage statistic — Perplexity's or anyone else's — check three things:

- **What's being counted.** Queries, unique users, and sessions are three different denominators. A “queries” figure and a “market share” figure aren't interchangeable, even when headlines use them that way.
- **Who's counting it.** Self-reported company numbers and third-party analytics platforms (like StatCounter) don't always agree, because they're measuring different things by design.
- **When it was measured.** This category moves fast. A chart from six months ago can already be stale — check the date on the underlying source, not just the date on whatever article is citing it.
- **Whether it's a live source or a screenshot.** Static images of “market share” charts circulate for months after the underlying data has changed. A live tracker beats a screenshot every time.

The 780-million figure in this article is dated and sourced deliberately, for exactly that reason: it's a specific, checkable number from a specific month, not a rounded-off vibe.
