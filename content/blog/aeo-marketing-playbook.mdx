Search "aeo marketing" right now and Reddit outranks every martech vendor, HubSpot, and course platform pitching a framework — proof the people doing this work don't trust vendor pitches, and they're right not to. Most "AEO marketing" content is a reheated SEO checklist with "AI" swapped in for "Google": same keyword research, same on-page tips, same push to "create quality content."

That's not AEO. It's SEO in a costume, and the stakes are bigger than a checklist implies: [44% of US adults now use ChatGPT, and 60% read the AI-generated summary atop search results](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/) instead of clicking through — a habit that barely existed two years ago.

Real AEO marketing is a demand-capture discipline, not a content-production one. The unit of success isn't a ranking position — it's whether your brand gets named out loud when ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, or Gemini answers a buyer's question. That shift changes what you measure, what you build, and what you stop paying agencies to do. Here's the actual playbook, not the checklist.

## What Does AEO Mean in Marketing?

AEO stands for Answer Engine Optimization: earning a named mention when an AI system answers a buyer's question directly, instead of returning a list of links to click through. The AEO meaning in marketing terms is simple — stop asking "did we rank," start asking "did we get named."

The term is young and genuinely messy. [Wikipedia's entry on generative engine optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization) notes that AEO, GEO, and "AI optimization" get used interchangeably by practitioners, with no settled distinction between them yet. That looseness is exactly why so much AEO content is relabeled SEO — nobody has forced a real definition, so vendors reuse whatever checklist they already had lying around.

Here's the distinction that actually matters for planning a marketing team's work:

| Dimension | SEO | GEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank a URL on page one | Get quoted inside an AI-generated answer | Get your brand named as the recommendation |
| Unit of success | Position #1–#10 | Citation or inclusion in generated text | Mention rate on buyer questions |
| Primary surfaces | Google, Bing results pages | AI Overviews, Perplexity, Bing Copilot | ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews |
| Core tactic | Keywords, backlinks, technical fixes | Structuring facts for extraction | Third-party proof: reviews, comparisons, PR |
| How you check it | Rank tracker | Citation or inclusion tracker | Ask the buyer question yourself, across models |

Treat GEO as the mechanical cousin — formatting content so a generator can lift and cite it — and AEO as the outcome: being the name the model says out loud when nobody typed your name into the prompt. The research that coined the term found the right approach can lift visibility in generated answers by as much as 40% ([arXiv:2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)).

## What Actually Changes for a Marketing Team?

The biggest shift: AEO work looks more like PR and analyst relations than classic content marketing. You stop optimizing a single page for a crawler and start managing what independent sources say about you across the whole web, because that's the input models actually weight.

Language models are trained and grounded on the open web — review sites, forums, comparison posts, news coverage — not on your homepage's opinion of itself. That mirrors something marketers already know about human buyers: [Nielsen's global trust research](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2012/global-trust-in-advertising-and-brand-messages-2/) found 92% of consumers trust recommendations from people they know above every form of advertising, and the bias transfers directly into what models repeat.

Practically, this moves the budget:

- Comparison content and third-party review platforms outrank your own product pages as trust signals.
- One well-argued Reddit or G2 thread can outweigh ten blog posts you wrote about yourself.
- Digital PR — getting named in roundups and journalist coverage — becomes a core AEO channel, not a nice-to-have.
- Your own site still matters, but as a source of clear, quotable facts, not as the only voice arguing your case.

## What Should You Measure for AEO Marketing?

Track mention rate: the share of realistic buyer questions where an AI answer names your brand, out of a fixed set you test on repeat. Impressions and rankings don't apply here — there's no results page to count position on, only a yes-or-no of whether you got named.

Build a simple, repeatable panel:

1. Write 15–25 real buyer questions your prospects would ask before purchase — "best [category] for [use case]," "[you] vs [competitor]," "is [you] worth it."
2. Run each one across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Overview.
3. Log whether you're named, whether a competitor is named instead, and which source the model leaned on.
4. Re-run monthly. Treat a drop in mention rate as seriously as a ranking drop.

Doing this by hand across five models is tedious at scale, which is why a dedicated [AI-visibility audit](/) exists instead of a spreadsheet: it runs the buyer-question panel across engines and shows exactly which competitor is getting named in your place. ## What Should You Build First?

Build the things a model can actually cite as evidence, not the things a crawler merely indexes. In practice that means comparison pages, an active review footprint, and a handful of quotable, specific claims — roughly in that order of leverage for most B2B brands.

Start here, this week:

1. **Ship one honest comparison page** for your top "X vs Y" query. Not a hit piece — a fair breakdown a buyer could actually use, because models detect and downrank one-sided pitches.
2. **Claim and actively work your review profiles** — G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, or the category-relevant equivalent. Reviews are exactly the earned-media signal buyers already trust more than ads, and models mine that signal the same way people do.
3. **Publish one data point nobody else has.** A stat, a benchmark, an original number — [content built on real data earns citations that generic advice doesn't](/blog/content-marketing-statistics), because models need something concrete to quote.
4. **Get named in independent roundups.** Pitch journalists and niche newsletters the same data point. A mention in someone else's article is worth more to a model than a mention on your own site.

This works because buyers already do their research this way before you ever hear from them: [Gartner's 2026 sales survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) found 67% of B2B buyers now prefer a rep-free purchase process. Miss that self-serve moment and there's no second pitch.

## What Should You Stop Doing?

Stop treating AEO as a technical trick bolted onto existing content — most of the "hacks" being sold right now don't survive contact with how these systems actually work. Even Google's own guidance for its AI features admits there's no special technical checklist, just the same search fundamentals.

Its [generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) states plainly that "optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO" — and it explicitly waves off tactics like publishing an llms.txt file or chopping content into rigid Q&A fragments as unnecessary.

Here's what shouldn't be on an AEO invoice:

- **"llms.txt optimization."** No model provider has confirmed it changes citations. It's a courtesy file, not a growth lever.
- **Rewriting working pages into robotic Q&A chunks** because someone claimed models "prefer" that format. Clarity helps; mechanical fragmentation just reads as thin content.
- **FAQ schema chased for the old reason.** Google [stopped showing FAQ rich results in Search](https://searchengineland.com/google-to-no-longer-support-faq-rich-results-476957) in 2026 — but the underlying [FAQPage markup](https://schema.org/FAQPage) still helps AI systems parse clean Q&A pairs. Keep it for that, not for a snippet that no longer exists.
- **An "AEO audit" that's a rebranded rank report.** If it doesn't test real buyer questions across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini and report a mention rate, it's SEO reporting with a new cover page.

This isn't permission to ignore technical SEO — crawlability and indexing are still table stakes. It means redirecting budget from AI superstition to the proof work above.

## A One-Week AEO Marketing Checklist

You can start capturing AEO data and fixing the biggest gaps inside a single week — this is not a quarterly initiative that needs a steering committee and a slide deck. Here's the order that gets you a usable signal the fastest, with the least wasted motion:

1. **Day 1–2:** Write your buyer-question panel and run it manually across the major models, or run one [AI-visibility audit](/) to get a mention-rate baseline in minutes instead of days.
2. **Day 2–3:** List every competitor named instead of you, and note which page or source the model cited for each.
3. **Day 3–5:** Ship the one comparison page or review push that plugs the biggest gap you found.
4. **Day 5–7:** Pitch one journalist, newsletter, or forum thread with a specific, quotable data point — not a press release.

This is also where AEO overlaps with the broader shift toward [zero-click marketing](/blog/zero-click-marketing): buyers increasingly get their answer without visiting any site at all. Fighting that trend is a losing game; winning the mention when the click never happens is the actual game, and a cheaper one than most agencies price it at.
