Pick Surfer if your bottleneck is writing and refreshing pages. Pick AthenaHQ if it is finding out, and fixing, what AI engines say about your brand. This guide is based on public documentation as of October 2026; we have not tested either product hands-on.

One correction to the usual advice: "Surfer does content, AthenaHQ does AI visibility" is out of date. [Surfer now sells daily AI prompt tracking](https://surferseo.com/ai-tracker/), so the two overlap.

## Which should you choose, Surfer or AthenaHQ?

Choose by the job you do every week, not by the category label. Both now track AI answers, so the real split is where each workflow starts: Surfer starts from the page you are writing, AthenaHQ from the prompt where you are missing.

- **Choose Surfer** if you publish or refresh pages weekly, want an editor and score to hold writers to, and need AI tracking for a modest prompt set.
- **Choose AthenaHQ** if tracking is the main job, you need more than five engines, or you want a free plan to test before paying.
- **Choose neither yet** if you have never checked whether AI names you. A one-off look costs less than a subscription and shows which job you actually have.

## What does Surfer do in 2026?

Surfer is a content-optimization platform that has added AI visibility tracking. Its [homepage](https://surferseo.com/) now calls it an "AI Visibility Platform", but the writing workflow is still the core of the product.

- **Content Editor and Content Score:** a live score for a draft, which Surfer says draws on [500+ ranking factors](https://surferseo.com/content-editor/) and competing search results.
- **Page tools:** Auto-Optimize, automatic internal links, Topical Map, Content Audit with ranking-drop alerts, and Surfy, an AI writing assistant.
- **AI Tracker:** prompts followed across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews and Gemini, with a Visibility Score, mention gaps, sentiment and share of voice.
- **Joined-up advice:** since 5 August 2026, [Recommendations include AI Tracker signals](https://surferseo.com/updates/ai-visibility-recommendations-august2026/): which mentions to pursue, which sentiment to fix, which topics to cover.

Surfer says it scrapes real AI interfaces rather than using APIs. That is a vendor claim we cannot verify.

## What does AthenaHQ do?

AthenaHQ is a platform for [generative engine optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization) (GEO): it tracks whether AI answers mention and cite your brand, then points to fixes. Its workflow starts from a visibility gap, not from a draft.

- **Plans:** per its [pricing page](https://athenahq.ai/pricing), Free ($25 of credit, 300 credits), Starter ($295 per month, 3,600 credits) and custom Enterprise.
- **Credits:** 1 credit equals 1 AI response, so cost scales with prompts times engines times runs.
- **Coverage:** five platforms on Free, 11 models on Starter, including Claude, Grok and Meta AI.
- **Action layer:** content recommendations on Free, a content optimization agent on Starter, plus Google Analytics 4, Search Console, Shopify and Webflow integrations.

Customer results on its site, such as a 6x share-of-voice lift in 60 days, are vendor-reported. For the credit and overage math, see our [AthenaHQ guide](/blog/athenahq-review).

## How do Surfer and AthenaHQ compare on price and coverage?

For AI tracking alone, Surfer's cheapest tracking plan costs less on paper; AthenaHQ covers more engines. Prices below are US dollars as of 3 October 2026, taken from each vendor's pricing page.

| | Surfer | AthenaHQ |
|---|---|---|
| Starts from | A draft and its Content Score | A prompt where you are missing |
| Tracking-only plan | AI Search Analytics: $82 per month billed yearly, 50 prompts, daily refresh | Free: $25 credit, 300 credits |
| Plan with content tools | Standard $99 (25 prompts, weekly); Pro $182 (50 prompts, daily); Peace of Mind $299 (100 prompts, daily); all billed yearly | Starter: $295 per month, 3,600 credits, content optimization agent |
| Engines tracked | Five (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Mode, AI Overviews, Gemini) | Five on Free, 11 on Starter |
| Writing editor | Yes: Content Editor, Surfy, 120 to unlimited documents by plan | No live-score editor listed |
| Unpublished | Enterprise pricing | Annual price, extra credits, API add-on |

Three caveats. Surfer's cheapest content plan, Discovery ($49 per month billed yearly), includes no AI prompt tracking. Its [pricing page](https://surferseo.com/pricing/) words Standard's 25 prompts as "across ChatGPT", so check whether that plan tracks one engine. And its page data lists a higher monthly-billing figure, $95 for AI Search Analytics.

Now the arithmetic, which is ours. At AthenaHQ's unit, 50 prompts on five engines refreshed daily is about 7,500 responses a month, roughly double Starter's 3,600 credits. Run the same set weekly and it is about 1,000. That assumes Surfer's daily refresh queries all five engines, as its page describes.

## Will a higher content score get you cited by AI?

No tool can promise that, and the evidence points to different edits than a keyword-coverage score rewards. Google's documentation says [no special optimization is needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): a page must be indexed and snippet-eligible, and normal SEO fundamentals apply.

For other engines, the best-known study is the [GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735). Its best edits (adding statistics, quotations and cited sources) lifted visibility by 30 to 40% on one metric. Keyword stuffing performed about 10% worse than baseline on Perplexity.ai.

Surfer's score is not keyword stuffing, and we have not tested what it rewards. Its homepage says content optimized with Surfer is 25% more likely to be cited by AI; that is vendor-reported, and we did not verify the study behind it.

Our position: use a content score to keep pages complete, and judge AI results only by tracking answers over time. For edits that match the evidence, see [how to optimize content for AI search](/guides/how-to-optimize-content-for-ai-search).

## What should you ask before you buy either one?

Ask how each tool collects its answers, because that decides whether the numbers match what your buyers see. Surfer says it scrapes real interfaces; AthenaHQ's pricing page does not say how it queries engines, so ask.

Put these five questions to both vendors:

1. Do you query consumer interfaces or APIs, and from which country and logged-out state?
2. Which exact model tier does each engine run on? Free users usually get a default tier, not the strongest one.
3. Do I choose the prompts, or does the tool generate them from my site?
4. What happens when I exceed my prompt or credit allowance, and at what price?
5. Can I export raw answers, so I can check a result myself?

If a vendor cannot answer the second question, treat its mention rates as indicative only.

## Do you need both Surfer and AthenaHQ?

Usually not. Because both now track AI answers, paying for both mostly buys two dashboards for the same prompts. Pick the one that matches your weekly job and add the second only for a specific gap.

Buy both only if:

- Your writers already live in Surfer's editor and you need AthenaHQ's 11-model coverage or source analysis.
- An agency runs content production and AI measurement as separate workstreams.

If you are unsure, test before you commit. AthenaHQ's Free plan costs nothing. AEOeye's [free audit](/) asks ChatGPT three unbranded buyer questions and shows the real answers; the $29 one-time report covers 10 questions across five engines. Both are snapshots, not daily tracking. For daily monitoring across many prompts, compare the options in our [AI visibility tools guide](/blog/best-ai-visibility-tools).

## What do the two vendors' own sites show to AI crawlers?

Both sites let AI crawlers in, but they differ on llms.txt and structured data. In [AEOeye's crawler access study](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory) (fetched 3 October 2026, 384 sites, [per-site CSV](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv)), we checked each vendor's robots.txt, /llms.txt and homepage once.

| | surferseo.com | athenahq.ai |
|---|---|---|
| AI bots blocked in robots.txt | None | None |
| llms.txt | No (404) | Yes |
| Homepage JSON-LD in served HTML | Includes Organization, WebSite, SoftwareApplication, Offer | None found |

"None found" can mean the schema is injected by JavaScript, which Google renders and many AI crawlers do not. This is one fetch of one day, not a verdict on either product.
