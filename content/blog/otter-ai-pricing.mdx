## How much does Otter AI cost?

The [official pricing page](https://otter.ai/pricing) separates monthly billing from annual equivalents. Our September 18 browser check switched both tabs without starting a subscription. These are USD reference prices, not a verified tax-inclusive checkout total.

| Plan | Monthly billing, per user | Annual billing, displayed per user/month |
|---|---:|---:|
| Basic | Free | Free |
| Pro | US$16.99 | US$8.33 |
| Business | US$30 standard; US$24 for the displayed three-month promotion | US$19.99 |
| Enterprise | Quote | Quote |

The public page also contains lower Pro figures of US$8.49 monthly and US$4.17 annually, but explicitly limits that offer to **India-issued credit cards**. They are not the standard US price. The Business US$24 offer is temporary, not its annual rate; confirm checkout eligibility and renewal terms. Do not annualize a three-month discount as if it lasts forever. Annual equivalents may be rounded, so confirm the actual yearly charge before paying.

The visible price primarily reflects recording and transcription capacity. However, the best plan depends on workflow friction: imported recordings, meeting length, simultaneous meetings, review requirements, sharing, and administrative expectations. A free account may be sufficient for occasional personal notes, while a paid seat can be easier to justify when one missed decision or repeated correction creates downstream work.

## What limits matter on each Otter plan?

The decisive limits are monthly recording minutes, per-meeting duration, file imports, concurrent meetings, and administrative controls. The practical differences are summarized below; confirm current details in Otter’s [feature matrix](https://otter.ai/pricing) and help documentation before purchasing.

| Plan | Key limits and controls |
|---|---|
| Basic | 300 monthly minutes; 30 minutes per conversation; 3 lifetime imports |
| Pro | 1,200 in-app minutes; 10 monthly imports; up to 90 minutes per meeting |
| Business | Unlimited in-app recordings; unlimited imported file count but 6,000 imported minutes/month; 4 hours per conversation; 3 concurrent meetings |
| Enterprise | SSO, SCIM, domain capture, and HIPAA add-on |

Basic can be inconvenient for frequent or file-based recording; its [official help page](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360047538094-Conversation-import-and-app-limits-on-the-Basic-free-plan) confirms the three-import limit is lifetime, not monthly. Business removes the file-count limit, **not** the imported-minute limit. That distinction matters if you plan to upload a historical archive rather than record new meetings inside Otter.

## What is the real cost per useful meeting?

Divide subscription spend by reviewed, shareable meeting records—not by nominal transcription minutes. A useful record is one that has accurate names, decisions, owners, and follow-up context, and can be shared with the right people.

A simple formula is:

`effective cost per useful meeting = monthly subscription cost ÷ useful meeting records completed`

Illustrative assumption: a Pro user pays $16.99 monthly and produces 20 reviewed, shareable records. The subscription-only cost is about $0.85 per useful record. If four records require substantial correction or cannot be shared because of consent or governance constraints, the effective cost rises to about $1.06 before labor.

For two Business users who qualify for the displayed monthly promotion and stay twelve months, a planning estimate is **2 × [(3 × US$24) + (9 × US$30)] = US$684** before tax. This assumes the offer ends after three months and the standard rate remains unchanged; it is not an actual invoice. Compare that with the annual checkout total rather than twelve months at the temporary discounted rate.

That labor matters. A lower-priced plan can cost more when limits force manual imports, split workflows, or repeated processing. Conversely, a higher tier may be wasteful if meetings are infrequent and records do not need centralized oversight. This is the same distinction procurement teams should apply when evaluating [ChatGPT for business](/blog/chatgpt-for-business) or an [AI content strategy](/blog/ai-content-strategy): price the completed outcome, not the headline allowance.

## How much correction and processing time should you budget?

Transcription is not finished when processing ends; names, decisions, and action owners still require human review. Otter’s official processing example says a 30-minute imported recording typically takes 4.5–6 minutes for the transcript and 5.5–7 minutes for the summary, depending on conditions. See the [processing-time FAQ](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360048322493-Transcription-processing-time-FAQ).

Budget two separate resources: system processing time and human correction time. Processing affects when a record becomes available. Review time affects whether the record is dependable enough to circulate. The second cost is often harder to see because it appears as a manager checking names, a project lead clarifying ownership, or an employee reconstructing a decision from audio.

Imports also deserve attention. Otter’s [Quick Start Guide](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360049722894-Otter-Quick-Start-Guide) explains supported workflows and imports. If your organization records elsewhere, test the volume and review path against the plan’s import allowance before treating transcription minutes as the main buying metric.

## Which privacy and governance questions affect the plan?

Before inviting a bot to meetings, define notice, consent, retention, sharing, admin access, and regulated-data rules. These questions determine whether a plan is operationally usable, even when its minutes appear sufficient.

Start with consent: who must be notified, who can decline, and what happens when a meeting includes external participants? Then define retention and access: how long records remain available, who can search them, and whether meeting owners or administrators can remove or review them. Finally, identify regulated workflows. Enterprise provides the higher control tier through capabilities such as SSO, SCIM, and domain capture, with a HIPAA add-on listed in the plan matrix.

This does not mean other tiers are inherently unsafe. It means governance requirements can make them unsuitable for a particular workflow. Otter’s product definition and supported use cases are described in [What is Otter](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360035266494-What-is-Otter). Teams unable to establish meeting-recording consent should pause the rollout regardless of price.

## Which Otter plan should you choose?

Basic is a continuing free plan, not merely a time-limited trial. Pro can fit a frequent individual recorder; Business adds team capacity; Enterprise is the governance conversation. Choose by workflow:

`occasional personal notes → Basic`

`one frequent recorder with moderate imports → Pro`

`multiple users, longer meetings, or simultaneous recording → Business`

`identity, domain, compliance, or centralized administration → Enterprise`

Delay adoption if you cannot establish meeting-recording consent or nobody owns review and retention. Choose a paid tier for a demonstrated constraint: longer conversations, file uploads, concurrent meetings, or administration. Extra minutes alone do not make a transcript dependable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Otter AI free?

Yes. Basic remains free with 300 monthly transcription minutes and three lifetime file imports, checked September 18, 2026. Its limits can make frequent or file-heavy workflows impractical.

### How many minutes are included?

The plan matrix distinguishes meeting recordings from imported files. Business has unlimited in-app recordings but a 6,000-minute monthly file import allowance, so unlimited file count does not mean unlimited imported minutes.

### Does Otter charge per user?

Yes. Paid public plan prices are per user. Annual amounts are displayed as monthly equivalents and require annual billing. Regional or introductory offers must not be treated as the standard ongoing price.

### Is Otter suitable for confidential meetings?

It can be considered only after your organization defines notice, consent, retention, sharing, and administrative-access rules. Enterprise adds governance-oriented controls, with a HIPAA add-on listed. Teams that cannot establish recording consent should not invite a meeting assistant into those sessions.
