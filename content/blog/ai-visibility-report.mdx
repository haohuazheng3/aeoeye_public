AI Visibility Report: The Monthly Template Executives Can Actually Use

An AI visibility report should answer three practical questions: where the brand appeared in buyer-facing answers, whether those answers were accurate and well-supported, and what the team should change next. A proprietary score without the raw answers behind it is not enough. Executives need a repeatable prompt set, clear denominators, competitor context, and a decision-ready record of changes.

![Executive reviewing an analytics report](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7947757/pexels-photo-7947757.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

## What should a monthly AI visibility report answer?

The report should make answer visibility observable, not mysterious. It should show the exact questions asked, the engines queried, the number of runs, the brands mentioned, the sources cited, and the material errors found.

Use a fixed monthly question set divided into buyer stages:

- Category discovery: “What are the best tools for [job]?”
- Shortlist evaluation: “Which [category] products are suitable for [use case]?”
- Comparison: “[Brand] vs. [competitor]: which is better for [constraint]?”
- Risk and trust: “What are the limitations or complaints about [brand]?”
- Purchase readiness: “What should a team check before buying [category]?”

Keep wording, location, language, and account settings consistent. Add a small rotating set only when a real product, market, or campaign change creates a new question. Otherwise, a month-to-month change may reflect a different prompt rather than a real visibility shift.

## How should you lock the methodology before running it?

Write a methodology box at the top of every report so a reader can reproduce the measurement. Record the prompt version, collection date, engines, model or interface where known, geography, language, personalization assumptions, and run count.

For each prompt-engine pair, define the denominator before reviewing results. If 20 runs were completed and the brand appeared in 7 answers, mention rate is 7/20, or 35%. If the brand appeared in 4 of 12 completed answers because 8 runs failed, report 4/12 and separately disclose the failures; never silently use 4/20.

Run the same set across the engines that matter to the business, such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI experiences, or other relevant surfaces. A result in one engine is not a universal market position. The [Semrush Brand Performance Reports documentation](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1595-brand-performance-reports) and [Visibility Overview guidance](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1596-visibility-overview-report) are useful reminders to separate coverage, mentions, and competitive context rather than collapsing every signal into one label.

## What belongs on the one-page executive layout?

The first page should be readable in two minutes and useful in the next leadership meeting. Put the period, scope, and three decisions at the top, followed by a compact methodology box and a KPI table.

| KPI | This month | Last month | Denominator / note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Brand mention rate | 35% | 28% | 7 of 20 completed runs |
| First-position recommendation rate | 20% | 15% | 4 of 20 completed runs |
| Citation rate | 30% | 25% | 6 of 20 answers with a source |
| Accurate-answer rate | 85% | 80% | 17 of 20 reviewed answers |
| Competitor lead rate | 45% | 50% | Competitor led in 9 of 20 runs |
| Material factual errors | 3 | 5 | Reviewed answer set |

The numbers above are a reporting example, not an industry benchmark. Avoid invented “good” thresholds. A 35% mention rate may be excellent for a narrow category or weak for a dominant brand; interpretation depends on the prompt set, market, and business objective.

Use a short executive narrative beneath the table: “Visibility rose because two product pages were cited more often; accuracy improved after the pricing page was corrected; the largest remaining gap is comparison prompts in Perplexity.” Every sentence should point to a row, answer, source, or change log entry.

## How should the KPI table measure answer visibility?

Answer visibility is the presence and quality of a brand in generated answers. It is not the same as website traffic, clicks, impressions, or revenue. Measure the answer first, then connect it to downstream analytics without pretending the relationship is perfectly attributable.

Track at least these fields:

1. Mention rate: runs where the brand is named divided by completed runs.
2. Recommendation rate: runs where the brand is explicitly recommended divided by completed runs.
3. Share of answer: a consistent rubric for the brand’s prominence relative to named competitors.
4. Citation rate: answers citing an owned or controlled source divided by answers that contain any citation, with both denominators shown when useful.
5. Accuracy rate: reviewed answers without a material factual error divided by reviewed answers.
6. Competitor gap: the difference between the brand’s rate and the strongest competitor for the same prompt and engine.

Define review rules before scoring. For example, a citation that merely names a homepage is not equivalent to a citation supporting a specific claim. A mention buried in a list is not equivalent to a recommendation with a reason. The [Rank Math AI brand visibility guide](https://rankmath.com/kb/ai-brand-visibility-report/) offers a practical framing, but your report should disclose its own rubric and preserve the underlying answers.

For statistical language, be modest. Precision and recall concepts from [NIST’s precision-recall reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) can help teams discuss classification quality, but a small monthly sample does not justify false precision. Show counts, not just percentages, and flag small denominators.

## How do source and citation views reveal the real gaps?

The source view should show which pages an engine used, what claim each source supported, and where the brand was absent or misrepresented. This is often more actionable than a blended score.

Create a citation worksheet with columns for prompt ID, engine, answer ID, quoted claim in your own notes, cited URL, source type, source freshness, ownership, and support rating. Classify sources as owned, partner, editorial, community, or uncited. Mark whether a source supports the exact claim, only part of it, or nothing relevant.

Add a narrative-accuracy review. Record factual errors such as an outdated price, discontinued feature, incorrect integration, wrong geography, or conflation with a competitor. Include severity and a proposed owner. Do not rewrite an answer to make it look cleaner; retain the raw response or a stable capture reference so the finding is auditable.

## How should the report connect visibility to traffic and revenue?

Keep answer visibility and click/referral analytics in separate sections, then connect them cautiously. AI answers may influence awareness without producing a tagged click, while referral traffic may represent only the small subset who choose to visit.

The traffic and revenue appendix can include AI referral sessions, landing pages, assisted conversions, demo starts, pipeline, and revenue where tracking is available. Label the source and time window, and distinguish directly observed referrals from modeled or self-reported influence. Do not call the report a GA4 replacement: GA4 can measure web behavior, while an answer audit measures what a user was told before or instead of visiting.

Use the appendix to ask useful questions: Did cited pages receive qualified visits? Did an updated comparison page improve demo conversion? Are high-visibility prompts associated with sales conversations? ## What should the monthly agenda decide next?

End with a change log and a decision agenda, not a generic “keep monitoring” conclusion. List what shipped, when it shipped, the intended effect, and the next observation window.

| Change shipped | Date | Target gap | Owner | Next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Updated pricing and limits | Oct 8 | Factual errors | Product marketing | Next monthly run |
| Added comparison evidence | Oct 12 | Competitor gap | Content | Two prompt cycles |
| Corrected integration documentation | Oct 16 | Citation support | Product | Next monthly run |

The agenda should make five decisions explicit:

- Which two pages or claims need evidence updates?
- Which competitor gap matters commercially rather than cosmetically?
- Which factual error requires an urgent correction?
- Which prompt or engine should be added because the market changed?
- What will be considered a meaningful improvement next month?
