## What Is Profound?

Profound positions itself, as of this writing, as an enterprise-grade AI answer engine analytics platform — software built to track how brands are mentioned, cited, and recommended across AI systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Overviews. It belongs to a newer software category that goes by a few overlapping names: AI visibility, generative engine optimization (GEO), or answer engine optimization (AEO).

Traditional SEO tools track where a page ranks on a results page full of blue links. AI answer engines work differently — they generate one synthesized answer, sometimes with citations and sometimes without, drawing on training data and live retrieval. Knowing whether your brand shows up inside that answer requires different tooling: repeated prompting across many queries, response parsing, and citation extraction rather than a rank-position crawl. Tools in Profound's category, typically, are built around that specific problem.

Why does this category exist at all? Because visibility inside an AI-generated answer behaves nothing like visibility on a search results page — there's no fixed set of ten blue links to rank inside, and a brand can be a strong SEO performer while being effectively invisible inside AI-generated answers, or the reverse. That gap is what tools like Profound are built to expose.

## What Is Profound Built For?

Profound is typically positioned toward larger marketing, SEO, and brand teams — organizations that already run enterprise SEO or brand-monitoring software and want an AI-visibility layer added on top. That's a fair, category-consistent read of an enterprise-grade platform, even without confirming specific plans or pricing.

In practice, that usually means: agencies managing AI visibility for multiple client brands at once, in-house teams at larger companies who need cross-team reporting, and organizations comfortable with the procurement side of enterprise software — security review, contracts, sales-assisted onboarding. Enterprise platforms in this category also tend to be built for continuous tracking rather than a single check; our breakdown of [ai search monitoring](/blog/ai-search-monitoring) covers what that ongoing approach typically involves and why some teams want it.

The trade-off is real. Enterprise tools are usually built for teams with dedicated headcount to run and interpret them, and the onboarding is rarely instant. If you don't have a team dedicated to AI visibility, that depth can go unused.

## What Category Does Profound Compete In?

Profound competes in the AI-visibility tracking category — a fast-growing group of tools built specifically to measure brand presence inside AI-generated answers instead of traditional search rankings. The category exists because the way people find information has changed: a growing share of questions now get answered directly by ChatGPT, Perplexity, or an AI Overview instead of a list of links.

Profound isn't the only vendor here. Other platforms, like Rankscale, take different approaches to the same underlying problem — different engine coverage, different dashboard styles, different definitions of what counts as a "result." Our [Rankscale review](/blog/rankscale-review) looks at one of those alternate approaches in more detail. The category also isn't standardized yet. There's no shared, industry-wide definition of what separates a "citation" from a "mention," and no common benchmark methodology across vendors — which is exactly why the next section matters more than any single vendor's marketing page. When you're comparing tools, ask each vendor directly how they define a citation, and ask for a sample report before you buy — not just a demo.

## Enterprise Platform vs. Self-Serve Audit: What's the Real Question?

The real question isn't "Profound or AEOeye" — it's whether you need an ongoing enterprise analytics platform or a fast, affordable answer to one specific question: does the AI actually recommend us right now. Those are different jobs, and they usually call for different tools.

| Dimension | Enterprise AI-Visibility Platform (Profound's category) | Self-Serve Audit (AEOeye's category) |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Typically sales-assisted, with onboarding before first data | Sign up and run — typically minutes, no sales call |
| Price posture | Usually custom or contract-based, quoted per account | Usually flat, published self-serve pricing |
| Best-fit team | Dedicated marketing/SEO org, or an agency tracking multiple brands | Solo founders, small teams, individual marketers |
| Speed to first insight | Slower by design — built for ongoing, longitudinal monitoring | Fast — built for an immediate, repeatable check |
| Depth vs. accessibility | Deeper historical data and configuration, more to learn | Narrower scope, easier to read in one sitting |

Neither posture is a trick. Enterprise pricing tends to be quote-based because enterprise deals vary by seats, brands tracked, and integrations; self-serve pricing tends to be flat because the product is standardized. Knowing that in advance saves you a sales call you didn't need, or a corner you'd otherwise cut.

Read the table by what you actually need this month, not by brand name. If you're running AI-visibility tracking across a dozen client accounts and need a shared dashboard for your team, the enterprise column fits. If you're a founder or a lean marketing team who wants a direct answer today, the self-serve column fits better — and costs a lot less to find out.

## How Do You Evaluate Any AI-Visibility Tool?

Judge any AI-visibility tool — Profound, an alternative, or a self-serve audit — on four things: which engines it actually covers, whether it separates citations from mentions, how often the data refreshes, and whether the output tells you what to fix. These four hold regardless of vendor size, price, or how polished the sales deck looks.

- **Engine coverage.** Which AI systems does the tool actually query — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Claude? Coverage varies a lot between vendors, and a tool that only checks one or two engines gives you a partial, potentially misleading picture.
- **Citation vs. mention tracking.** Being cited — linked as a source in an AI-generated answer — is different from being mentioned by name without a link. A tool worth using distinguishes between the two instead of lumping them into one score.
- **Cadence.** AI answers shift as models get updated and retrieval indexes refresh. A one-time snapshot ages fast; ask how often the tool actually re-checks.
- **Actionability.** Does the report just hand you a score, or does it explain why you're missing and what to change — content, structure, or citations — to close the gap?
