## What are GEO tools?

GEO tools help your content get pulled into generative AI answers from engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. Instead of tracking blue-link rankings, they track whether an AI engine mentions, cites, or recommends your brand when someone asks a relevant question.

Most GEO tools do some combination of three jobs: run realistic, buyer-style prompts across multiple AI engines to see if your brand shows up, benchmark that visibility against named competitors, and diagnose why you're missing when you are — thin content, no comparison pages, missing structured data, weak third-party citations.

Under the hood, generative engines aren't ranking your page — they're synthesizing an answer from whatever text they can access: your site's crawled content, structured data markup, and, just as often, what other people say about you on review sites, forums, and comparison articles you don't control. That's why a GEO tool that only checks your own site is only doing half the job.

Some tools stop at the dashboard and leave the fixing to you. Others go further and recommend, or even help generate, the content changes. Neither approach is wrong — it depends on whether you want a mirror or a mechanic. ## GEO vs AEO vs SEO: are they different?

In practice, GEO and AEO describe the same goal with different labels. Both differ from classic SEO by optimizing for a synthesized answer, not a ranked list of links.

Concretely: rank #1 on Google and you still have to win the click. Get cited inside a ChatGPT answer and the user may never see your URL at all — just your brand name and your claim, delivered directly in the response.

The term "Generative Engine Optimization" traces back to academic research proposing it as a formal discipline, published around 2023, around the same time generative AI (see Wikipedia's overview of [generative artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_artificial_intelligence)) started reshaping how people search. "Answer Engine Optimization" grew up more informally, out of SEO and marketing circles reacting to the same shift. Neither camp fully adopted the other's term, and honestly, they probably never will — GEO shows up more in technical and academic writing, AEO more in marketing content. That's a naming split, not a strategy split.

Our take: don't spend energy picking a side in the label war. Judge any tool — GEO-branded or AEO-branded — on the same three questions: does it track the engines your buyers actually use, does it refresh often enough to catch real changes, and does it explain *why* you're cited or ignored, not just whether you are.

## How to choose a GEO tool

Match the tool to the question you're actually asking, not the other way around. Run a free or low-cost audit first to confirm GEO is a real problem for your brand, then decide whether you need always-on enterprise monitoring.

1. **Know what you're testing for.** A one-time sanity check ("does AI even know we exist?") needs a different tool than continuous competitive monitoring.
2. **Confirm engine coverage.** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Claude don't behave the same way, and not every tool checks all five.
3. **Look for the "why," not just the "if."** Being told you're invisible does little good without a diagnosis — missing structured data, no comparison content, weak citations.
4. **Check refresh frequency.** AI answers shift as models update and competitors publish; a monthly snapshot can miss a lot of movement.
5. **Weigh price against team size.** An enterprise platform makes sense for a marketing org defending share. It's overkill for a two-person startup still testing a hypothesis.
6. **Make sure it benchmarks competitors, not just you.** Visibility is relative — "we're mentioned sometimes" means nothing without knowing what your rivals get.
7. **Check if it exports cleanly.** If GEO reporting needs to sit next to your other marketing dashboards, confirm you can pull the data out — not just view it inside a walled-garden UI.
