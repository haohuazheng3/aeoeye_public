## What is Sora pricing in 2026?

Sora cost is now a shutdown and migration question, not a subscription comparison. The issue is not finding the cheapest way to generate one more video; it is whether spending can produce enough value before service ends.

For most buyers, do not buy Sora for a long-term workflow. The web and app product has ended, and the API has a scheduled end date. Temporary remaining access cannot provide the reliability or continuity required for a production dependency.

That matters for client video products, automated content pipelines, libraries needing revisions, team workflows, products depending on generated output, or a repeatable long-term visual style.

A short experiment may be useful if you already have access and an export plan. A new commitment is difficult to justify because the time to recover setup costs, train a team, and migrate is nearly gone.

## What happens to remaining credits?

Remaining purchased ChatGPT or Sora credits may be usable for Codex under OpenAI’s flexible credit guidance. Unused credits should not automatically be treated as Sora-only value. Review the current [credit usage guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12456897-using-credits-for-flexible-usage-in-chatgpt-free-go-plus-pro).

The practical sequence is to identify remaining credits, confirm whether they are purchased and eligible elsewhere, check current account behavior, use eligible value where useful, and avoid buying more merely to preserve a discontinued workflow.

Credits and access are separate. A balance does not guarantee Sora after an end date. An alternative use for credits also does not mean video assets, prompts, settings, or integrations remain available.

### What should happen to your data?

Export data and assets before access ends: generated videos, source files, prompts, metadata, project notes, reference material, and documentation needed to reproduce important work.

Do not wait until the last day. Exporting can reveal missing files, naming problems, incomplete downloads, or dependencies trapped inside the original workflow.

A useful archive preserves filenames and creation dates, prompt text and meaningful settings, approved videos, useful alternate takes, references, usage notes, client approvals, and identifiers matching assets to projects. If an asset cannot be recreated from the export, migration is incomplete.

## Does Sora still make sense for a new purchase?

For a long-term workflow, no. The scheduled API shutdown makes a new production commitment indefensible, while consumer access is already past its stated end date.

Paying in final days might make sense only when an urgent deliverable is clearly defined, access is available now, work can finish and export before shutdown, and the spend creates no expectation of future service. That is a completion decision, not an investment.

Before spending, ask whether the whole project can finish before access ends, every output can be downloaded, future revisions are required, a client expects continuity, credits have another eligible use, and migration takes longer than remaining access.

If ongoing dependency appears, stop treating Sora as the answer. Compare replacements on workflow fit, exportability, consistency, API continuity, commercial terms, moderation behavior, and team productivity.

## How should you migrate a Sora workflow?

Start with inventory, then asset export, requirements, replacement testing, and cutover. Preserve the business output rather than every interface detail.

### Migration checklist

- List every project, integration, and recurring use case.
- Mark each as critical, useful, or disposable.
- Export final videos and source material.
- Save prompts, references, settings, and project context.
- Verify files open and play.
- Record rights, approvals, and delivery requirements.
- Separate must-have capabilities from preferences.
- Test replacements with representative prompts.
- Compare consistency across several generations.
- Confirm export of files, metadata, and revisions.
- Update automation and team instructions.
- Set a final checkpoint before September 24.
- Remove Sora API calls after validation.
- Archive migration decisions and results.

A replacement must pass a real workflow test, not one attractive demo. Test difficult cases: brand consistency, camera movement, text in scenes, recurring characters, aspect ratios, revision speed, and the editing handoff.

## What should you evaluate next?

Evaluate alternatives by the job, not the Sora brand.

Pricing is only one row. A lower price can cost more when it creates manual work, inconsistent output, or another migration.

Run the replacement in parallel with the final Sora workflow before cutover. Compare three representative projects, document failures, verify commercial terms, and confirm that another teammate can reproduce the result from the saved instructions. Migration is complete only when the replacement works without hidden Sora dependencies.
