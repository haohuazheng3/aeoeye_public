## What is known about Yext Scout’s free and paid boundary?

Yext’s September 2026 release says basic Scout key performance indicators are available on the free tier, while full drill-down and exports require paid Scout. That is the clearest current public boundary, but it is not a public price.

The safe interpretation is:

- Basic KPIs: documented as available on the free tier.
- Full drill-down: documented as requiring paid Scout.
- Exports: documented as requiring paid Scout.
- Dollar amount: not publicly verified in the sources reviewed.
- Commercial scope: not publicly verified for region, currency, term, locations, limits, or add-ons.

Do not convert “free tier” into a $0 product-price claim for every Scout capability. It describes the documented availability of basic KPIs. Conversely, do not label paid Scout custom-priced or quote-only without a current commercial document saying so. The responsible buying step is to request a written offer that defines the paid package.

## What does paid Scout unlock?

Paid Scout is documented as the requirement for full drill-down and exports, and AI Citations are identified as paid-Scout-only. Those capabilities matter when a team must inspect why a KPI changed, preserve evidence, or share findings outside the product.

The Help Center describes Scout as supporting map, location, and competitor analysis. That makes it relevant to organizations with local footprints as well as brands tracking competitive visibility. The documentation does not, however, establish how many locations, competitors, scans, users, or exports belong to any paid package.

Ask whether paid Scout includes every metric’s drill-down, AI Citations across models and locations, usable exports, competitor limits, and map-analysis scope.

The distinction between seeing a KPI and inspecting its evidence is important. A dashboard number may be useful for a quick pulse, while a durable visibility program needs a reproducible methodology and an exportable record. See AEOeye’s [measuring AI visibility](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility) guide for the questions that methodology should answer.

## Which metrics and AI models does Scout analyze?

The official metrics documentation lists AI Rank, AI Visibility Score, sentiment, and citations. It also says Scout analyzes Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. AI Citations are specifically described as paid-Scout-only.

These labels should not be treated as interchangeable outcomes. AI Rank may describe a position or ordering concept; an AI Visibility Score may aggregate visibility; sentiment describes a qualitative classification; and citations concern references in answers. Ask Yext how each metric is calculated, what the denominator is, and whether historical values remain comparable when a model changes.

The model list establishes coverage, not equal behavior. The sources do not specify scan frequency, prompt volume, geographic or language sampling, retention, or every free-tier metric.

For a consistent evaluation, define the prompts, observation unit, citation and competitor rules, successful-scan definition, and treatment of model changes before comparing vendors.

Use the [AI visibility metrics dictionary](/blog/ai-visibility-metrics-dictionary) to keep metric names precise. A larger list of labels is not proof of more accurate measurement.

## How has the Scout documentation changed over time?

The source chronology matters: an older Help Center article describes Scout through a beta and CSM access path, while Yext’s September 2026 release confirms a free tier for basic KPIs and a paid tier for full drill-down and exports.

That change explains why older references may say Scout is in beta or available through a customer success manager. They should not be used as the current public pricing answer without checking their date and context. The newer release clarifies the free/paid capability boundary but still does not publish an amount in the sources reviewed here.

Chronology also guards against a common mistake: treating a product announcement as a complete rate card. The September release establishes availability and access distinctions, not a normalized price per location, prompt, scan, model, citation, or export.

## How can you model total Scout cost without inventing a price?

Use a transparent formula with unknown variables instead of fabricating a total:

**Total cost = base Scout fee + location units × location rate + add-ons + onboarding + applicable taxes.**

Ask the quote to state the billing currency, billing period, minimum term, renewal behavior, number of locations, scan cadence, model scope, history, seats, exports, API or MCP access if needed, onboarding, support, and any add-ons. If Yext uses a different unit than locations, replace that variable explicitly rather than silently assuming a per-location model.

## What should you verify before buying?

Start with access: confirm which basic KPIs are available without payment and which drill-down screens, exports, and AI Citation views require paid Scout. Ask for a current feature matrix tied to your account, region, and intended number of locations.

Next, request definitions for AI Rank, AI Visibility Score, sentiment, citations, prompt source, model sampling, scan frequency, geography, history, model changes, and exportability.

Finally, verify commercial terms. Obtain a written amount and currency, term and invoice cadence, location and user limits, support and onboarding scope, API or MCP availability, renewal and cancellation language, and all add-ons. Do not call the plan $0, custom, quote-only, or included unless the current written offer uses those terms.

The buying standard is simple: do not compare a vendor without a written normalized quote and measurement methodology. Public documentation can establish the free/paid boundary; it cannot substitute for the commercial and measurement details your team needs.
