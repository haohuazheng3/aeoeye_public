## How much does Microsoft Copilot cost?

There is no single Copilot price. Microsoft offers a free consumer experience, Copilot within personal Microsoft 365 offers, included Copilot Chat for eligible organizations, and paid Copilot Business. The license and commitment term matter as much as the headline price.

For individuals, free access provides Copilot without buying a Microsoft 365 subscription. Copilot may also be included in a broader personal productivity offer. The experience depends on the account, product, and current offer, so confirm the live [Copilot for individuals](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/try-copilot/individuals) page before subscribing.

For organizations, Microsoft separates Copilot Chat from paid Copilot Business. Its US business pricing page listed Copilot Chat as included for eligible customers, but that does not mean every organization receives every capability or that Microsoft 365 requirements disappear.

Microsoft listed Copilot Business at $18 per user per month when paid yearly, shown as a promotion from $21, or $25.20 per user per month with a monthly commitment. These prices were checked on September 2, 2026, and promotions can change. Recheck checkout before approval. See our broader [pricing](/pricing) coverage for recurring software costs.

## What is free, bundled, or sold as an add-on?

Free chat tests the assistant; a personal Microsoft 365 plan connects Copilot to productivity apps; commercial Chat may be included; and Copilot Business adds deeper work context and controls.

“Copilot” is not one product. Access, context, application integration, administration, and pricing differ.

| Offer | What it generally represents | Main buying question |
|---|---|---|
| Free Copilot | Consumer chat access without a paid Microsoft 365 Copilot subscription | Is basic chat enough? |
| Personal Microsoft 365 offer | Copilot connected to eligible personal productivity applications | Will Office work justify the bundle? |
| Copilot Chat | Commercial chat included for eligible customers | Does the existing license qualify? |
| Copilot Business | Paid business capability with work context, app access, agents, and controls | Will integrated workplace use repay the seat cost? |

Microsoft’s explanation of [free versus Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/what-s-the-difference-between-microsoft-copilot-free-and-copilot-in-microsoft-365) clarifies these boundaries.

Individuals should ask whether Copilot supports their Microsoft 365 workflow. Companies should ask whether employees use Teams, Outlook, Word, PowerPoint, and Excel enough for in-app assistance to matter. Otherwise, a business add-on may duplicate browser-based chat.

## What does Copilot Business really cost per employee?

The real annual cost includes the Copilot seat, qualifying Microsoft 365 license, rollout, governance, training, and human review. An $18 seat costs $216 yearly before those costs; monthly commitment at $25.20 costs $302.40 yearly.

Microsoft states that a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan is separate, so organizations must include that prerequisite. The relevant calculation is the cost of an enabled, governed workflow, not simply the Copilot price multiplied by employees.

Twenty-five annual-commitment seats would equal $5,400 per year at the listed promotional price, before separate Microsoft 365 licensing and implementation overhead. At the listed monthly-commitment price, the same seats would equal $7,560 over twelve months. These are arithmetic illustrations based on listed prices.

Value depends on utilization. A rarely used seat produces little return. A seat used to summarize recurring meetings, draft documents, retrieve permitted work context, or prepare repeatable deliverables may save time after review and correction.

Microsoft says Copilot Business adds Work IQ, access inside Teams, Outlook, Word, PowerPoint, and Excel, agents, and enterprise controls. Check the [Microsoft 365 Copilot business pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing) page for current prices, terms, and eligibility.

## When is Microsoft Copilot worth paying for?

Copilot earns its price when employees repeatedly summarize meetings, draft in Office, retrieve permitted work context, or build routine deliverables faster. It is harder to justify when workers stay in browser chat or rarely use Microsoft 365 source material.

The strongest case is operational repetition. Teams moving between meeting notes, email, documents, spreadsheets, and presentations may benefit from assistance inside existing workflows. Reducing copying, pasting, and tool switching can matter more than improving one isolated answer.

The weaker case is buying Copilot because everyone should have AI. That creates unused seats and review work without a defined workflow. Occasional browser brainstorming may not justify a business add-on.

Work context is not authority. Copilot can surface information from permitted organizational sources, but users must assess whether it is current, complete, and appropriate. Microsoft’s [validate Copilot output](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/Microsoft-365-Copilot/validate-copilot-output) guidance emphasizes checking source, verification, context, and resilience.

Measure how quickly an employee produces an accurate, usable result after checking it—not how quickly Copilot produces text. A fast draft requiring extensive repair may not save time.

## How should a business run a Copilot pilot?

Pilot Copilot with a small, role-based cohort and measure review-ready output time against the same tasks without it. Adoption alone is weak evidence; decision-ready output, corrections, and avoided tool switching show whether seats create value.

Choose employees whose work matches Copilot’s strengths, including meeting summaries, customer communications, internal reports, presentations, or spreadsheet analyses. Define repetitive tasks before assigning seats.

Record baseline task time, application switching, correction frequency, and manual source gathering. During the pilot, repeat comparable tasks and record the same measures.

A useful scorecard can include:

- Time to a review-ready deliverable
- Number and severity of corrections
- Percentage requiring substantial rewriting
- Time saved by avoiding application switching
- Frequency of use for defined tasks
- Confidence compared with verified accuracy

Set review rules before launch. Decide which outputs require source checking, which data may be used, who approves external communications, and how employees report questionable results. Copilot should support the workflow, not become an unexamined authority.

## Which Copilot plan should you choose?

Use Free for exploration, rely on an existing personal bundle for individual Office work, and buy Business only when work-context integration repays the full seat cost. Recheck Microsoft checkout because promotions and commitment prices change.

Free Copilot limits financial exposure while showing whether recurring use cases exist beyond experimentation. A personal Microsoft 365 offer can make sense when an individual already depends on Microsoft productivity applications and expects to use Copilot there.

Commercial customers should determine whether their existing Microsoft 365 environment qualifies for included Copilot Chat. Included access may satisfy basic organizational chat needs, but it is not the same as Copilot Business.

Copilot Business is defensible when the organization has or will maintain the qualifying Microsoft 365 license, employees work extensively in Microsoft applications, and expected time savings exceed seats, administration, training, and verification costs.

The listed annual promotional price is lower than the monthly-commitment price, but the lower monthly equivalent may provide less flexibility. Confirm whether the organization can reduce, cancel, or reassign seats under the agreement. Monthly billing does not automatically mean cancellation without conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Microsoft Copilot free?

Yes, Microsoft offers a free Copilot experience for eligible users. Free access is not identical to Copilot features included with Microsoft 365 or Copilot Business. Check the current [individual Copilot page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/try-copilot/individuals) for account-specific experience and terms.

### How much is Microsoft 365 Copilot Business?

Microsoft’s US business page listed Copilot Business at $18 per user per month paid yearly as a promotion from $21, or $25.20 per user per month with a monthly commitment. A qualifying Microsoft 365 plan is separate. Recheck checkout because prices and promotions may change.

### Does Copilot require Microsoft 365?

Not always. Free Copilot can be available without a paid Microsoft 365 Copilot subscription, while personal and business features may depend on the relevant plan or eligibility. Copilot Business requires a qualifying Microsoft 365 license in addition to the Copilot seat.

### Can you cancel a Copilot business seat monthly?

The monthly-commitment price is listed separately from the annual paid-yearly price, but cancellation and seat-reduction rights depend on the agreement and purchase channel. Confirm current terms before buying; monthly billing does not automatically mean cancellation without conditions.
