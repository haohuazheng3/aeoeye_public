Someone's pitching you "AI SEO services" — a retainer, a deck full of jargon about large language models, a promise to get your brand "featured in ChatGPT." You nod, sign nothing yet, and quietly wonder what you'd actually be buying that's different from the SEO pitch you got in 2019. That confusion is reasonable — right now a lot of agencies are still figuring out what "AI SEO" means themselves, and the price tags don't always track the value.

## What do AI SEO services actually include?

Most AI SEO services bundle three things — AI-assisted content, technical automation, and increasingly AI-visibility work — but quality and transparency vary wildly from one agency to the next. If you are packaging that last category for clients, this [AEO service framework for agencies](/for/agencies) separates the audit, remediation, and measurement scopes. What lands on your invoice as "AI SEO" usually maps to some combination of the following.

- **AI-assisted content**: blog posts, product copy, or landing pages drafted with an LLM and, ideally, edited by a human before you ever see them. The gap between "AI-assisted" and "AI-dumped" is enormous and rarely disclosed upfront.
- **Technical automation**: the same crawling, schema markup, site-speed, and indexation work SEO agencies have always done, now run through AI-powered tooling so it's faster to produce — not necessarily better.
- **AI-visibility / AEO work**: a genuinely newer category — tracking and improving whether your brand gets mentioned or cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews, and Claude, rather than just where you rank in blue links.
- **A rebranded dashboard**: reporting labeled "AI-powered insights" that's really the same analytics agencies have shipped for a decade, with new terminology.

Here's the uncomfortable part: a meaningful share of what's sold as "AI SEO" is [recycled SEO advice](/blog/most-aeo-advice-is-recycled-seo) wearing an AI sticker, priced at an AI premium. That doesn't make it worthless — technical SEO and good content still matter — but it means you should ask exactly which of the four things above you're paying for, and how much of each.

The fastest way to tell which one you're being sold: ask for three writing samples produced under the contract, and ask what share of deliverables are templated versus genuinely researched for your brand. Agencies doing real work will show specifics — which AI platforms they've verified you appear in, what changed after a fix shipped. Agencies reselling old SEO under a new label tend to answer in generalities.

## How much does an AI SEO agency cost?

Reported pricing ranges widely, from a few hundred dollars a month for tools-plus-templates to five figures a month for full-service retainers — the number matters less than what's actually delivered for it. Because "AI SEO" isn't a standardized service, agencies price it however the market will bear.

| Tier | Commonly reported monthly range | What you're typically getting |
|---|---|---|
| DIY / tool-only | Low hundreds | Software access, templates, self-serve audits — you still do the work |
| Light-touch service | Low-to-mid thousands | Some content or technical fixes, a shared strategist, monthly check-ins |
| Full-service agency | Mid thousands to low five figures | Dedicated strategist, content team, technical implementation, reporting |
| Specialized AI-visibility / enterprise | Five figures and up | Custom AI-mention monitoring, multi-market coverage, dedicated team |

Treat these as rough bands, not quotes — actual pricing depends on your market, content volume, and how much hand-holding you want. The bigger issue isn't the number; it's that the word "AI" doesn't reliably predict which tier you're in. It's entirely possible to pay full-service prices for what amounts to a ChatGPT subscription and a template library.

Two more things worth digging into before you sign: contract length and pricing model. Agencies increasingly offer month-to-month terms instead of annual lock-in, and some price partly on performance — citations gained, traffic lift — rather than a flat retainer. Both are reasonable asks, not favors. If an agency insists on a long lock-in with no visibility into what you get monthly, that's a red flag regardless of the price.

## Do you actually need one? (a decision framework)

You need an agency when you genuinely lack the in-house time or expertise and the business stakes justify the spend; you don't need one when a tool plus a few focused hours a month would do the same job.

Lean toward hiring when:

- You have no in-house marketing capacity and no realistic time to build the skill
- Your category is competitive enough that the revenue at stake justifies a real budget
- You need sustained content or technical output at a volume you can't produce alone
- You've already tried DIY, hit a ceiling, and can't diagnose why

Lean toward DIY when:

- You're a small team or solo founder who can spend a few hours a month on this
- Your site has a handful of specific, fixable issues rather than deep structural problems
- You want to understand what's actually broken before anyone pitches you a fix
- Every dollar of budget needs justifying, and you're not ready to commit to a retainer

There's also a middle path worth naming: hire narrowly instead of broadly. Plenty of agencies will take on a defined, scoped project — fix technical debt, rebuild a content cluster, set up schema — without locking you into an open-ended monthly retainer. That's often the better fit for a mid-size team that needs expertise but not a permanent headcount replacement.

If you're leaning DIY, a [handful of AI-visibility tools](/blog/best-ai-visibility-tools) can cover most of what a light-touch retainer would do, for a fraction of the cost, with you in control of what gets published.

## The DIY alternative: audit first, then decide

Before you hire anyone, run a [free AI-visibility audit](/#audit) so you know exactly what's broken — then either fix it yourself with the right tools, or brief an agency with specifics instead of hoping they diagnose it correctly. To sanity-check the budget side first, our [SEO ROI calculator](/tools/seo-roi-calculator) shows what a retainer has to return just to break even.

This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that saves the most money. An audit — AEOeye's free scan included — checks how your brand actually shows up when people ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI, and Claude buyer-intent questions in your category, turning a vague worry into a concrete list. Specifically, you'll typically see whether your brand gets mentioned at all for relevant buyer questions, whether your content has the structured data and clear answers AI models tend to pull from, and where competitors are showing up instead of you. None of that requires trusting anyone's sales pitch — it's observable.

From there, you have two honest paths:

1. **Fix it yourself.** If the gaps are things like missing schema, thin comparison pages, or no clear answer to "is [product] worth it," those are fixable without an agency, especially once you understand [how AEO differs from traditional SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo).
2. **Hire with precision.** If the audit turns up structural problems beyond your bandwidth — content debt across hundreds of pages, technical issues you can't safely touch — you now walk into that agency conversation with a baseline instead of blind trust. Either way, you stop paying someone for a diagnosis you could've gotten for free.

## Questions to ask any AI SEO agency (checklist)

A short list of direct questions will expose whether an agency is doing genuine AI-visibility work or just reselling standard SEO with new vocabulary and a bigger invoice — ask before you sign anything, not after.

1. Which specific AI platforms do you track or optimize for — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Claude — and how do you measure success on each one?
2. What's the actual split between AI-assisted content, technical work, and AI-visibility work in my retainer?
3. Can you show me a baseline audit before we start, so I know what's broken before you start billing?
4. Who reviews AI-generated content before publication, and what does that editing process look like?
5. Does your reporting show AI citations and mentions, or only traditional keyword rankings?
6. What happens to my content, schema, and technical changes if I cancel?

If an agency can't answer the first two clearly, that's worth noticing. Google itself has been consistent that content gets judged on whether it's genuinely helpful, not on whether an AI or a human produced it — so "AI-generated" alone was never the value proposition to begin with.
