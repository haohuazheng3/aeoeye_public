Ask five people "what's Gemini's market share?" and you'll get five different numbers — because they're all measuring something different. Here's what's actually verified, what isn't, and why the percentage matters less than you'd think.

## What Is Gemini's Market Share?

Precise AI-chatbot market-share percentages shift from month to month, and different trackers disagree because they measure different things. The safest honest answer: ChatGPT still leads the category by most public measures, and Gemini is a strong, fast-growing second — with a distribution advantage no competitor can match.

That's not a dodge. It's the accurate picture. Anyone who hands you a confident "Gemini has X% of the market" without naming a source and a date is guessing, rounding, or marketing.

What we can say with confidence:

- Gemini's user base is enormous and still climbing — roughly 750 million monthly active users as of February 2026.
- ChatGPT remains the largest standalone AI chatbot by most public measures.
- The exact percentage gap between them depends entirely on which metric you pick — see the next section.

Why not just print a number? Because any specific percentage published today is stale within weeks. AI chatbot usage is growing too fast, and trackers already show meaningfully different splits depending on whether they count web visits, app installs, or figures the companies self-report.

For a live, continuously updated percentage split, StatCounter's AI chatbot market share tracker is the most commonly cited public source. Bookmark it instead of trusting any static number — including this one, six months from now.

## Why Does Gemini's Distribution Matter More Than Its Share Number?

Gemini ships pre-installed on Android phones, sits inside Google Search, rides along in Chrome, and is embedded in Workspace apps hundreds of millions of people already use for work. That's distribution ChatGPT has to earn one download and one signup at a time — and it means Gemini's real-world reach is almost certainly wider than any single market-share percentage implies.

Consider where Gemini shows up without anyone deciding to "try an AI chatbot":

- Default assistant on most new Android devices
- Surfaced directly inside Google Search's AI Overviews and AI Mode
- Built into Gmail, Docs, and Sheets for Workspace users
- Pre-loaded on phones and Chromebooks — zero download friction required

None of that requires anyone to have heard of "Gemini" as a brand. They just start typing into a box that's already open.

Google's core product has dominated [search engine market share](/blog/search-engine-market-share) for two decades, and that same installed base is now carrying Gemini into hundreds of millions of hands that never searched an app store for an AI chatbot. A smaller "share" number next to a much bigger distribution footprint is the real Gemini story — share alone undersells it.

## How Do You Read AI Market-Share Data Without Getting Fooled?

Two numbers that sound alike rarely measure the same thing. Monthly active users, weekly active users, total prompts, and website traffic share are four different metrics that can produce four different rankings from the same underlying reality — so the first question for any market-share claim is "which one is this?"

Quick guide to what each metric actually tells you:

- **MAU (monthly active users):** the broadest window and the easiest number to grow — good for scale, weak for engagement.
- **WAU (weekly active users):** a tighter engagement signal; it's the metric OpenAI has reported for ChatGPT.
- **Query or prompt volume:** measures usage intensity, not headcount — a small, heavy-usage base can out-query a larger, casual one.
- **Web traffic share (StatCounter and similar):** tracks visits to chatbot websites — it undercounts app-only and OS-embedded usage, which is exactly how most people now reach Gemini.

Sources disagree because they're built on different measurement windows, different self-reported versus third-party methodologies, and different blind spots. A company blog post announcing "hundreds of millions of users" is almost always the largest defensible number available — compare that to a third-party analytics tool counting weekly web visits, and you're comparing a best-case figure to a conservative one. Neither is "wrong." They're answering different questions.

Before you cite a number, check what was actually measured and who's reporting it. The phrase "market share" hides a lot of methodology.

## Where Can You Find Current Gemini Market-Share Data?

For a live percentage split, StatCounter's AI chatbot market share tracker is the most commonly cited public source — free, continuously updated, and transparent about its methodology. Treat it as one strong data point, not the final word, since it measures web traffic and misses app-only or OS-embedded usage.

Reliable places to check, roughly in order of authority:

1. **Company disclosures** — earnings calls and official blog posts from Google and OpenAI. Most authoritative for user counts, least frequent.
2. **Reputable tech press** — outlets like TechCrunch, The Verge, and Reuters, which usually name their source. Good for context; always verify the original number.
3. **StatCounter** — best for a live, comparable percentage split across chatbots, updated monthly.
4. **Academic and industry research** — slower to publish, but useful for cross-checking the methodology behind the claims above.

Be skeptical of SEO listicles quoting a precise percentage with no source or date attached. If a number can't be traced to one of the sources above, don't repeat it.
