HubSpot pricing is easiest to budget when you model four separate variables: contacts, seats, usage credits, and one-time onboarding. For a defensible first-year estimate, treat promotional prices as temporary, use standard pricing for renewal planning, and ask sales to confirm which limits apply to your exact portal.

This guide uses HubSpot’s published US pricing and catalog information rechecked September 18, 2026. Prices and promotions can change, so use the official [Marketing Hub pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/marketing) and [Product and Services Catalog](https://legal.hubspot.com/hubspot-product-and-services-catalog) as the commercial references, then confirm your order.

## What should you budget for HubSpot in year one?

A realistic first-year budget is:

**software subscription + additional seats + contact-related costs + credits + required onboarding + tax or negotiated adjustments.**

The headline monthly price is not the whole cost. Marketing Hub Professional displays at $800 per month on the annual payment option or $890 with monthly payments; the latter still indicates an annual commitment. It also requires $3,000 onboarding. Enterprise displays at $3,600 per month and requires $7,000 onboarding.

Free and Starter can be much less expensive, but their limits may become restrictive as your database, automation, reporting, or team grows.

## What do the Marketing Hub tiers include?

| Tier | Published price or promotion | Included contacts | Included Core Seats | Required onboarding | Important budget note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Free | $0 | Up to 1,000 stored contacts | Up to 2 users | No paid subscription | Free CRM contact limit, not a paid marketing-contact tier |
| Starter | $7/seat/month promotional price | 1,000 marketing contacts | Per-seat subscription | Confirm any service fees | $20 standard display price; promotion terms apply |
| Professional | $800/month paid annually, or $890/month with annual commitment | 2,000 marketing contacts | 3 | $3,000 | Credits included; verify overages and add-ons |
| Enterprise | $3,600/month | 10,000 marketing contacts | 5 | $7,000 | Built for larger operational complexity |

The Starter figure is promotional: $7 per seat per month is shown against a $20 standard display price. Do not use the promotional price as your long-term renewal assumption unless HubSpot confirms its duration and eligibility.

Marketing Hub Professional includes 3,000 monthly credits; Enterprise includes 5,000. The [catalog](https://legal.hubspot.com/hubspot-product-and-services-catalog) lists extra credits at $0.010 each or $10 per additional 1,000-credit monthly capacity pack. The marketing page advertises $9 per 1,000 when paying annually. Confirm that offer’s billing period and commitment; it does not mean $9 buys another 1,000 credits every month for an entire year.

## How do you calculate the first-year HubSpot cost?

Use this formula:

1. Choose the tier and billing basis.
2. Multiply the monthly subscription by 12.
3. Add the annual cost of extra seats, if required.
4. Add required onboarding.
5. Add expected credit purchases beyond the included allowance.
6. Add any contact-related or negotiated charges confirmed in writing.
7. Build a renewal case using standard, non-promotional pricing.

For Professional on the annual payment option, the displayed subscription baseline is:

**$800 × 12 = $9,600**

Adding required onboarding produces a first-year baseline of:

**$9,600 + $3,000 = $12,600**

For Enterprise:

**$3,600 × 12 + $7,000 = $50,200**

These are published-price baselines, not quotes. They exclude taxes, optional products, extra seats, negotiated discounts, and usage above included credits.

If you are comparing HubSpot with other software, keep the same structure. Our [Mailchimp pricing guide](/blog/mailchimp-pricing) and [Slack pricing guide](/blog/slack-pricing) use a similar separation between recurring subscription cost and expansion costs.

## What would two common HubSpot budgets look like?

### What is a serious small-team Professional budget?

A small marketing team that needs Professional should begin with the annual-payment baseline:

- Subscription: $9,600 per year
- Required onboarding: $3,000
- First-year baseline: **$12,600**

Professional includes 3 Core Seats and 2,000 marketing contacts. Price any extra seats by role, including workflow owners and administrators.

If monthly usage reaches 5,000 credits against a 3,000-credit allowance, the excess is 2,000 per month. At the catalog’s $0.010 rate:

**2,000 × $0.010 = $20 per month; $20 × 12 = $240 per year**

That produces a $12,840 first-year example before other extras. It assumes the same excess every month and the catalog rate, not the annual-payment offer. Confirm the credit model in your order.

### What is an Enterprise operating budget?

Enterprise has a much higher commitment:

- Subscription: $43,200 per year
- Required onboarding: $7,000
- First-year baseline: **$50,200**

Enterprise includes 5 Core Seats, 10,000 marketing contacts, and 5,000 credits.

Enterprise needs an operational justification beyond contact volume. Ask HubSpot to demonstrate which required governance, automation, reporting, or data capabilities Professional cannot provide.

## How should you model contacts and database hygiene?

Contacts are a budget variable, not merely a marketing metric. A contact who is stored, synced, and counted for marketing purposes can affect the plan you need even if that person is not currently receiving a campaign.

Before buying, classify your database:

- Active marketable contacts
- Suppressed or unsubscribed contacts
- Duplicates
- Employees and test records
- Old leads with no realistic reactivation path
- Contacts retained for sales or compliance reasons

Then separate stored contacts from contacts designated for marketing. The paid tiers include 2,000 marketing contacts for Professional and 10,000 for Enterprise; non-marketing contacts have separate storage limits. Check designation and billing rules before removing useful records merely to reduce the headline database count.

A clean database can delay an upgrade more effectively than negotiating a small discount. Export a representative sample, estimate monthly net-new contacts, and model the database at months 3, 6, 9, and 12.

## How sensitive is the budget to credits?

Credits deserve their own forecast because usage can grow faster than seats. Use monthly allowances, monthly excess, and the contracted unit price consistently; do not confuse an annual payment discount with a year's worth of monthly capacity.

Build three usage cases:

- Low: 50% of included credits
- Expected: 100%
- High: 150% or more

For the high case, distinguish pay-as-you-go from a capacity increase that lasts for the remaining commitment. Ask about allowance resets, expiration, shared usage, and which actions consume credits. Record any annual-payment discount separately from the amount of recurring capacity purchased.

## What should you take into the sales call?

Prepare a one-page worksheet with:

1. Current and projected marketing contacts at months 3, 6, 9, and 12.
2. Required Core Seats and likely users by function.
3. Expected monthly credit consumption.
4. Required hubs, integrations, automations, and reporting.
5. Annual-commitment versus monthly-cash-flow preference.
6. Onboarding requirements and implementation ownership.
7. Renewal price assumptions after any promotion ends.
8. Export, cancellation, overage, and data-retention terms.

Request written answers and a quote separating subscription, seats, contacts, credits, onboarding, discounts, taxes, and optional products.

## What is the defensible HubSpot pricing verdict?

Free or Starter can suit a small team, but Starter’s displayed price is promotional. Professional has a $12,600 first-year subscription-and-onboarding baseline before extras; Enterprise starts at $50,200 on the same basis. Neither is justified merely because a team wants more AI-generated content.

If AI-search visibility is your only goal, check HubSpot’s separate AEO offer before budgeting the full marketing suite: its pricing page lists $50 monthly or $45 per month paid annually, without another HubSpot subscription. Compare prompt coverage, engines, and monitoring cadence with your [AEO audit requirements](/blog/aeo-audit-checklist). This is a price-and-scope comparison, not a hands-on evaluation of either product’s results.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HubSpot Professional really $800 per month?

The pricing page shows $800 per month on the annual payment option, plus required $3,000 onboarding: a $12,600 first-year baseline before extras. The $890 monthly-payment option also indicates an annual commitment.

### Is the $7 Starter price permanent?

No. It is displayed as a promotion against a $20 standard price. Confirm its duration, eligibility, renewal price, and included limits before using it in a long-term budget.

### How much onboarding does HubSpot require?

Marketing Hub Professional requires $3,000 onboarding and Enterprise requires $7,000. Confirm any implementation services separately, including optional services when starting on a lower tier.

### What happens when I exceed included credits?

The catalog lists $0.010 per credit for pay-as-you-go and $10 for each additional 1,000-credit monthly capacity pack. Annual-payment offers may differ; confirm the rate and period in your order. Check overage settings, resets, and commitment before enabling credit-consuming workflows.
