## What are the generative AI statistics for 2026?

Generative AI now reaches hundreds of millions of people every single week. ChatGPT counts roughly 800 million weekly active users as of October 2025, according to TechCrunch. Google's Gemini app has surpassed 750 million monthly active users as of February 2026, also per TechCrunch. Perplexity handles about 780 million queries a month as of June 2025, per Search Engine Land.

Those three numbers alone describe a userbase in the billions, even before accounting for overlap between platforms. Pew Research adds another data point: about 34% of US adults said they had used ChatGPT, based on its 2025 survey. That's roughly one in three American adults, for one tool, in one country.

The sections below lay out exactly where each figure comes from, why the numbers from different companies don't line up cleanly, and what any of it should actually change about how you approach marketing in a world where AI tools answer questions before a search results page ever loads.

## How much is each generative AI platform used?

Three platforms currently have verifiable, sourced usage figures: ChatGPT at roughly 800 million weekly active users, Gemini at roughly 750 million monthly active users, and Perplexity at roughly 780 million monthly queries. The table below lines up each figure with its source and date, since the numbers use different units and shouldn't be added together.

| Platform / metric | Figure | Source | As of |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT weekly active users | ~800 million | TechCrunch | Oct 2025 |
| Google Gemini app monthly active users | ~750 million | TechCrunch | Feb 2026 |
| Perplexity monthly queries | ~780 million | Search Engine Land | Jun 2025 |
| US adults who have used ChatGPT | ~34% | Pew Research | 2025 |

A few things worth noticing:

- ChatGPT's figure is weekly, Gemini's is monthly, and Perplexity's is a query count, not a user count — the three aren't directly comparable.
- All four figures are self-reported by the companies (or their coverage) rather than independently audited, which is normal for the industry but worth remembering when you cite them.
- Each number is already months old by the time you read it. Generative AI usage has grown consistently since ChatGPT's public launch, so treat every figure here as a floor, not a ceiling.

## What does generative AI actually cover?

Generative AI is a broad label, and usage statistics get confusing fast unless you specify which slice you mean. In practice, the term spans at least three different product categories:

- **Chat assistants** — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and similar tools used for writing, coding, research, and general Q&A.
- **AI-powered search** — Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, and other tools that generate answers instead of, or alongside, a list of links. See our guide to [generative search](/blog/generative-search) for how this differs from a traditional results page.
- **Image, audio, and video generation** — tools like Midjourney or Sora that create media rather than text.

This page focuses specifically on the first two categories — chat assistants and AI search — because those are the surfaces where a brand can realistically be mentioned, recommended, or cited in response to a user's question. Image and video generation are a different discipline with a different set of adoption metrics, and we don't cover them here.

## Why are generative AI usage numbers so hard to pin down?

Because no two companies measure usage the same way, and none of them publish a common, audited standard. That's the short answer. The longer answer has a few specific causes.

**Different metrics, different companies.** OpenAI reports weekly active users. Google reports monthly active users for the Gemini app. Perplexity reports monthly query volume rather than a user count. Stack any two of those next to each other and you're comparing different units, not just different numbers.

**Numbers change fast.** Generative AI adoption has moved quickly enough that a statistic from early 2025 can be meaningfully out of date within a few months. That's part of why this page anchors every figure to a specific as-of date rather than presenting it as a permanent fact.

**Self-reported, not third-party audited.** Usage figures typically come from company blog posts, investor updates, or press briefings — not from an independent measurement firm. That doesn't make them wrong, but it does mean context and sourcing matter more than the raw number. Our companion piece on [LLM statistics](/blog/llm-statistics) digs further into model-level benchmarks and adoption figures if you need a deeper breakdown by platform.

**Overlap between tools.** Someone can be a weekly ChatGPT user, a monthly Gemini user, and a Perplexity searcher, all in the same week. None of the published figures account for that overlap, so you can't simply add them together to get one total generative AI users figure — despite how often that math shows up in marketing decks.

## What does generative AI adoption mean for marketers?

It means a growing share of buying-related questions never touch a traditional search results page at all. When someone asks ChatGPT to compare two products, asks Gemini to summarize reviews, or asks Perplexity to recommend the best option in a category, the answer is generated, not ranked — and your brand is either part of that generated answer or it doesn't exist for that user.

That's a real shift in how discovery works, not a hypothetical one. The usage numbers in the table above describe hundreds of millions of people who are already forming opinions and shortlists through [AI search](/blog/what-is-ai-search) instead of, or in addition to, a conventional search engine. A few implications follow directly:

- **Being cited is the new being ranked.** If an AI engine doesn't mention your brand when it answers a relevant question, you lose that customer before they ever reach your website.
- **Structured, factual content travels better.** These tools tend to pull from pages that state things clearly and back them up, not from pages optimized purely for keyword density.
- **You can't manage what you don't measure.** Knowing that ChatGPT has roughly 800 million weekly users is interesting; knowing whether ChatGPT recommends your brand when someone asks a relevant question is what actually affects revenue.

## Where can you find current generative AI statistics?

Go to primary sources whenever possible, and treat secondary roundups — including this one — as a starting point rather than the final word. The most reliable places to check are:

- **Company reports and blog posts** — OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity all publish usage updates periodically, though not on a fixed schedule.
- **Reputable tech press** — outlets like TechCrunch and Search Engine Land regularly cover and contextualize company announcements, often asking follow-up questions the original release doesn't answer.
- **Independent research organizations** — Pew Research and similar bodies survey actual usage behavior rather than relying on what a company chooses to disclose, which makes their numbers a useful cross-check.

For a broader look at how these figures compare across the wider AI landscape, our [AI usage statistics](/blog/ai-usage-statistics) page tracks adoption trends beyond just the generative AI category. Whichever source you cite, check the publication date before you use the number — in this space, current usually means current as of last quarter, not today.

## What's the real question behind these numbers?

Usage statistics answer how many people use generative AI — but they can't answer whether generative AI recommends your brand. Those are different questions, and the second one is the one that actually affects your traffic and revenue.

The platforms in the table above represent hundreds of millions of people asking questions that used to go straight to a search box. Some of those questions are about your product category right now.
