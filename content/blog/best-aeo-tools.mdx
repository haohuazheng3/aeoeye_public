"AEO tool" is suddenly on every marketing roadmap — new platforms launch monthly, all promising to get your brand quoted inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews. But the category hides a split most buyers don't notice until after they've paid: some tools *watch* your AI visibility, others try to *improve* it, and almost none do both equally well. Get the category wrong and you'll own a dashboard that tells you what's broken without ever fixing it, or a content tool with no way to check whether it worked.

## What is an AEO tool?

An AEO tool helps you get cited by AI answer engines — either by monitoring how your brand already shows up in tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity, or by optimizing your content and site structure so those engines are more likely to quote you. Most vendors specialize in one side, even when their marketing implies both.

That confusion isn't an accident. Monitoring is easier to build, easier to demo, and produces a shinier dashboard, so plenty of "AEO platforms" lean hard on the word optimization in their pitch even though the product mostly just watches. Knowing the split before your first sales call saves you a wasted quarter.

The two jobs use completely different mechanics:

- **Monitoring tools** run recurring prompts against AI engines, log whether your brand appears, and track sentiment, share of voice, and competitor mentions over time. They're closer to a rank tracker than a content tool.
- **Optimization tools** work on your actual site and content — structured data, answer-first formatting, FAQ schema, source citations — the on-page signals that make a page easy for a model to lift and quote.

AEO also isn't a replacement for SEO — see [AEO vs. SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo) for how the two actually relate.

## Monitoring vs optimization: which do you need first?

You need monitoring first — you can't optimize what you can't measure. Run an audit to see where you actually stand across AI engines, then use those results to decide whether your gaps are structural (fix with an optimization tool) or purely a visibility problem that closes once the content itself improves.

Here's what that looks like in practice. Say your product page never comes up when someone asks an AI engine to compare tools in your category. An audit tells you that much for free. What it won't tell you is *why* — for that, you look at the actual page: no FAQ schema, no clear comparison structure, nothing citable in the first two sentences. That's an optimization problem, and no amount of extra monitoring fixes it.

The sequence, in order:

1. **Audit** — find out whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and the rest already mention or recommend you, and for which queries.
2. **Diagnose** — if you're invisible everywhere, that's usually a content and structure problem, not a tooling problem.
3. **Fix** — restructure the specific pages that should be getting cited: answer-first intros, clear headings, schema markup, credible sources.
4. **Re-monitor** — check whether the fix actually moved the needle, on a schedule, not a one-off.

In our experience, this trips up small teams and enterprises differently. A solo founder or small SaaS usually only needs a one-time or occasional audit — you don't have the content volume yet to justify a monitoring subscription. A larger brand with dozens of product pages and active competitors, on the other hand, benefits from continuous monitoring, because visibility shifts week to week as AI engines update their indexes.

Buying an optimization platform before you've run a single audit is the most common wasted spend we see — you end up "optimizing" pages that were never the problem. ## How to evaluate an AEO tool

Before you buy, check five things: which engines it actually tracks, whether it shows real citations or just mention counts, how often data refreshes, whether it recommends fixes or only reports numbers, and whether you can see results before you pay.

1. **Engine coverage.** Confirm it tracks the engines your buyers actually use — ChatGPT and Perplexity are table stakes; Gemini, Google AI, and Claude coverage varies by vendor.
2. **Citation depth, not just mentions.** A tool that shows the exact quoted text and source is far more useful than one that only reports "mentioned: yes/no."
3. **Refresh frequency.** Daily or weekly tracking catches trend shifts; a stale monthly snapshot mostly just confirms what you already suspected.
4. **Recommendations, not just dashboards.** Ask whether the tool tells you what to fix, or just that something's wrong.
5. **Try before you commit.** A free tier or sample report tells you more in five minutes than any sales deck will.

None of this is complicated, but it's easy to skip when a demo looks impressive — slow down enough to ask these five questions before you sign anything. A couple of red flags worth watching for, too: a glossy "visibility score" with no explanation of what it's measuring, a vendor who can't tell you which exact prompts they ran to get their numbers, and contracts that lock you in for a year with no monthly or trial option. A tool worth using can explain, in plain language, exactly what it asked the AI engines and when.
