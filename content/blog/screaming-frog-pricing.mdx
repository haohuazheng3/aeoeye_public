Screaming Frog SEO Spider costs $279 per user per year in USD, while the canonical UK price is £199. The free version can crawl 500 URLs, but the paid license removes that cap and unlocks saved crawls, configuration, JavaScript rendering, integrations, extraction, and support.

That price makes Screaming Frog neither an impulse purchase nor an obvious expense. It is a focused desktop crawler. Value depends on how often you audit, how large the sites are, and how much manual checking your process requires.

The practical question is simple: does the license replace enough repetitive technical SEO work to justify its annual cost?

## How much does Screaming Frog cost in 2026?

One annual license is $279, £199, or €245 per user, with lower per-user USD rates beginning at five licenses; currency pricing and tax can change the final invoice.

| Licenses | Price per user, annually | Annual total |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1–4 | $279 | $279–$1,116 |
| 5–9 | $265 | $1,325–$2,385 |
| 10–19 | $249 | $2,490–$4,731 |
| 20+ | $235 | $4,700+ |

These are per-user prices, not a shared company pool. The final amount may vary with currency conversion, local taxes, and quantity at checkout. Check the current [Screaming Frog license page](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/licence/?quantity=1&wmc-currency=USD) before budgeting.

The structure favors teams that genuinely need multiple seats. A five-person team receives the first volume tier, but buying extra licenses “just in case” is wasteful if only one person performs crawls.

For a solo consultant, the decision is usually binary: remain within the free crawler’s limits or pay $279 for professional workflow features. For an agency, the real question is utilization per seat.

## What can the free version do?

The free spider is a real crawler for up to 500 URLs per crawl, but it restricts saved crawls, configuration, JavaScript rendering, integrations, and other advanced workflows.

That limit is more useful than many assume. A brochure site, local business website, landing-page collection, or early-stage startup may fit comfortably inside it. You can discover links, inspect common technical issues, and learn crawlability before spending anything.

The free version is especially sensible for learning. It shows how redirects, canonical tags, status codes, titles, headings, and indexability signals appear in a crawler.

The limit becomes restrictive when a site includes variants, faceted navigation, archives, documentation, regional folders, or other URL-generating systems. A crawl that starts under 500 URLs can also become incomplete as the site grows.

The free edition is a diagnostic instrument, not a crippled demo. It answers focused questions but is less suitable as the permanent operating system for recurring audits. The official [SEO Spider overview](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/) and [feature comparison](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/pricing/) explain the free and paid workflow.

## What does the paid license unlock?

Paid access removes the crawl ceiling and opens repeatable professional work: saving crawls, custom extraction and search, rendering JavaScript, analytics and Search Console integrations, and technical support.

The most important upgrade is not simply “more URLs.” It is repeatability. A paid workflow can preserve configurations and results, making it easier to compare a site before and after a migration, template change, redirect project, or internal-link cleanup.

Saved crawls also change collaboration. Instead of screenshots or isolated findings, an analyst can retain the crawl as an artifact for review and follow-up. That makes the work more defensible in client reporting.

JavaScript rendering matters when important content or links are assembled in the browser rather than delivered in initial HTML. It does not automatically make a site search-friendly, but it gives an auditor a closer view of the rendered page.

Custom search and extraction can turn a generic crawl into a site-specific investigation: particular templates, embedded directives, structured snippets, tracking patterns, or content elements relevant to a migration.

Analytics and Search Console integrations connect technical findings with performance context. The software remains a crawler, not a complete platform. See the official [user guide](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/user-guide/general/).

## When does the license pay for itself?

The license pays for itself when larger or repeat audits save a few hours of manual checking; calculate break-even as annual license cost divided by the hourly value of technical SEO time.

Using the single-seat price of $279:

| Hourly value of SEO time | Break-even time |
|---:|---:|
| $50 per hour | 5.58 hours |
| $100 per hour | 2.79 hours |
| $150 per hour | 1.86 hours |

These figures are a decision model, not a promise. If a paid crawl saves six hours across a year, it has exceeded the license cost at a $50 hourly value. If it saves one hour, the free version may remain the better choice.

Include preparation, crawling, filtering, exports, comparison, and reporting—not merely time waiting for a crawl. Repeating the same audit on several sites increases potential return quickly.

AEOeye’s view is that paid becomes compelling once technical SEO is a recurring service rather than an occasional curiosity. ## How do per-user rules affect agencies?

Licenses belong to individual users, not devices or offices: one person may install on multiple devices, but each person who uses the software needs a license.

This matters for agencies with shared laptops, contractors, and rotating account teams. A seat is not a floating license everyone can use. If three people independently operate the software, plan for three users.

The rule also affects hiring. A team may start with two seats, then add another when an employee takes responsibility for audits. Volume pricing reduces unit cost only after reaching the threshold.

Assign seats to people who configure crawls, analyze results, or produce deliverables. A stakeholder who only receives a report does not need to operate the crawler. The official [FAQ](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/faq/) confirms the per-user, multi-device rule and renewal behavior.

## Who should stay on free?

Stay on free when every crawl is comfortably below 500 URLs and you do not need saved projects, rendering, integrations, custom extraction, or scheduled repeatability.

“Comfortably below” matters. A site with 480 URLs may cross the limit after a new category, language folder, or archive is published. If the free version requires constant pruning to produce a useful crawl, time cost is already signaling an upgrade.

The free edition fits:

- Small websites with stable URL counts
- People learning technical SEO
- One-off checks with narrow questions
- Owners validating a new site before hiring help
- Teams with another system for saved audit history

Do not upgrade because paid software feels more professional. A paid license that sits unused is an annual subscription to unused capacity. Conversely, do not stay free when splitting a site destroys crawl context; the official [FAQ](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/faq/) documents the paid restrictions that matter.

## What should you compare before buying?

Compare annual labor saved, site size, JavaScript requirements, audit frequency, reporting workflow, and user count—not the price against a cloud suite that solves different jobs.

| Your need | Screaming Frog fit |
|---|---|
| Crawl a large site deeply | Strong |
| Preserve crawl configurations | Strong with paid access |
| Render JavaScript during audits | Strong with paid access |
| Monitor rankings daily | Not its primary purpose |
| Provide a client-facing cloud dashboard | Usually requires another workflow |
| Run a small one-off crawl | Free may be enough |

Traditional crawling explains what a site exposes technically; it does not prove whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI, or Codex recommends a brand. For that question, an [AI search data accuracy tool](/blog/ai-search-data-accuracy-tool) addresses a different visibility layer.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is one license?

One license costs $279 per user annually in USD. The listed UK price is £199 and euro price is €245. Taxes and checkout conditions can affect the final invoice.

### Is the free version useful?

Yes. It crawls up to 500 URLs and is useful for small sites, focused diagnostics, and learning technical SEO.

### Is a license per user or per device?

A license is per user, not per device. One licensed user can install the software on multiple devices, but each operator needs a license.

### What happens when it expires?

The SEO Spider returns to the restricted free version. Paid functionality and the ability to open saved crawls are unavailable until renewal.

### When does paid become cost-effective?

Paid becomes cost-effective when it saves more than $279 in annual labor or prevents a meaningful audit limitation. At $100 per hour, break-even is 2.79 hours.
