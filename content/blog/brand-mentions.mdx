For years, SEO ran on one currency: the backlink. Earn enough of them, from strong enough sites, and Google moved you up the page. That currency still works, but it now has a competitor it can't simply buy off. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini “what's the best invoicing tool for freelancers,” the engine isn't counting your backlinks. It's recalling what it has read about you — and what it read didn't need a link attached to teach it your name. That's a brand mention, and in AI search, it may be the thing worth chasing hardest.

## What are brand mentions?

A brand mention is any place your brand's name shows up online, whether or not that mention links back to your site. Linked mentions pass through as clickable references. Unlinked mentions simply state your name in context — and both teach search engines and AI models who you are.

The distinction used to decide everything:

- A **linked mention** is a mention plus a vote. It points somewhere and, in classic SEO terms, passes along some authority.
- An **unlinked mention** is the same information without the vote. Your name appears, in context, with no anchor tag attached.

Mentions live everywhere your category gets discussed, not only in places built to talk about you:

- Press coverage and “best of” roundups
- Product review sites and marketplaces
- Comparison posts (“X vs. Y”)
- Forums and community threads — Reddit, niche Slack and Discord groups, industry boards
- Social posts and creator content
- Podcast show notes and video descriptions

Most of that list produces unlinked mentions far more often than linked ones — a Reddit reply naming your product rarely links to it, and a podcast host recommending a tool mid-episode doesn't drop a URL. For years, that made those mentions feel like the consolation prize next to a real backlink. That's changing — but the honest classic-SEO answer comes first.

## Do brand mentions matter for SEO?

For classic Google rankings, links still do more work than unlinked mentions carry on their own — that's the honest answer, not the exciting one.

Google has discussed entity recognition and “implied links” for a long time, and there's a reasonable case that consistent, contextual mentions help a search engine understand what category you belong to. But across years of rank-tracking and correlation studies, links remain the stronger, more reliable signal for competitive queries. An unlinked mention on a mid-authority blog won't, by itself, move a page from deep in the results onto page one.

What unlinked mentions likely do is reinforce topical association at the margins — feeding how a search engine's entity systems connect your brand to a category, alongside every other signal saying the same thing. That's real, but modest. This is the honest state of brand mention SEO: helpful, marginal, unproven as a standalone ranking lever. Anyone telling you unlinked mentions replace a link-building strategy for classic Google rankings is overselling it.

Where the picture changes completely is the search surface that doesn't run on a link graph at all.

## Why mentions beat links in AI search

In AI search, a mention alone can do the whole job a link used to do, because AI engines answer from the text they've read — through training data and live retrieval — not from a link graph they crawl and rank in real time.

When ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, or Claude answer something like “best accounting software for a small agency,” they're drawing on patterns learned from vast amounts of text about which brands get discussed alongside which categories, plus, for engines that browse, live snippets pulled from indexed pages. What matters is that your name showed up, attached to the right topic, echoed across independent sources — we break down the fuller mechanism in our guide to [how AI assistants choose which brands to recommend](/blog/how-ai-assistants-choose-brands). A link is one way to attach your name to a topic in that system. It's not the only way, and often not even the primary one.

This is exactly what we track as [mention rate](/glossary/mention-rate) — how often a brand gets named across a set of real buyer prompts, regardless of whether the citation carries a link. A brand with a strong mention rate shows up consistently when the right questions get asked. A brand with an excellent backlink profile and a weak mention rate can still be invisible in that same conversation.

The gap between the two games shows up in the data. An Ahrefs study of 15,000 prompts found that only 12% of URLs cited by AI assistants also ranked in Google's top 10 for the same query. Read that twice — it means the citation game and the ranking game largely have different winners. Optimize only for links, and you may be winning a race that AI answers don't even run.

The backlink was the currency of ranking. The mention is the currency of being known. An AI engine doesn't need to click through to your site to describe what you do — it just needs to have seen it said, clearly and repeatedly, by independent sources, until it reads as settled fact.

## How do links and mentions actually compare?

Short version: links still win for classic rankings, mentions increasingly win for AI answers, and one of the two is much easier to buy your way into.

| | Links | Mentions (linked or not) |
|---|---|---|
| **What it earns you** | Authority passed to one specific URL, plus referral clicks | An association between your name and a topic — with or without a click |
| **Who counts it** | Search engine crawlers and backlink tools | AI models, during training and live retrieval, plus entity-recognition and listening tools |
| **Where it pays off** | Classic Google and Bing rankings | AI answers and recommendations — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews, Claude — with a smaller assist to classic rankings |
| **How hard to fake** | Historically easy, now heavily policed; paid and spammy links get discounted or penalized | Harder to fake at scale — requires independent sources, in different contexts, actually saying your name near your category |

## How do you earn more brand mentions?

You earn mentions the way you earn trust: by being worth talking about, then showing up in the places people already talk.

1. **Be quotable.** Publish original data, a clear benchmark, or a genuine point of view that other writers want to cite. Safe, generic copy doesn't get mentioned. A specific number or a specific opinion does.
2. **Do digital PR that targets people, not just links.** Pitch journalists and niche writers who cover your category, not only sites that will trade a mention for a link back. A founder quote in a trade publication is a mention whether or not it's linked.
3. **Get into comparison and review content.** “X vs. Y” and “best of” posts are exactly where AI engines pick up category associations. Being the well-documented, honestly represented option in those posts compounds every time the post gets cited.
4. **Show up where your buyers already ask questions.** Reddit threads, niche community forums, industry Slack and Discord groups — answer well, under your own name, and let the mention happen naturally instead of planting it.
5. **Keep your name consistent everywhere.** One brand name, spelled and capitalized the same way across every bio, listing, and profile, so mentions consolidate into a single entity instead of splintering into near-matches that no model can confidently merge back together.

None of this is exotic. It's the same work good brands have always done to earn word of mouth — it just now also feeds the systems deciding whether AI recommends you.
