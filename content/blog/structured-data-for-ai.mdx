Structured data does not make ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google AI cite you. Google says its AI features need no special markup, and the one large before-and-after study we found saw no citation lift. Ship clean schema anyway, because it labels who you are and what you sell. Just never let a fact live only in JSON-LD.

This guide separates what is documented, what has been measured and what nobody outside the labs knows. It reflects public documentation and studies as of October 2026.

## What is structured data, and why would AI care?

Structured data is machine-readable labeling added to a page, almost always as [JSON-LD](https://www.w3.org/TR/json-ld11/) using the [schema.org](https://schema.org/docs/about.html) vocabulary. It tells software that "49" is a price and "AEOeye" is an organization, instead of leaving it to guess from prose.

Google says it uses markup to understand the page and to learn about the people, books and companies named in it ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)). That is entity disambiguation: is this Acme the software company or the plumber? It is the one job schema does reliably, and it is the core of [entity SEO](/blog/what-is-entity-seo).

Google recommends JSON-LD over Microdata and RDFa because it is easiest to maintain at scale. Our [glossary entry](/glossary/structured-data) has the short definition.

## Do AI assistants actually use schema markup?

Only partly, and the documented part is small. Google says its AI features need no special markup, and Microsoft recommends schema for AI search. OpenAI's and Perplexity's crawler documentation says nothing about it, and no lab documents training use. Everything else is inference or third-party testing.

| Engine | What the vendor documents | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | Structured data [isn't required](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), no special markup exists, and it must match visible text ([AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)) | Documented |
| Microsoft Copilot and Bing | A Bing product manager lists schema among core practices for AI answers ([Microsoft Advertising, October 2025](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers)) | Recommended, mechanism unstated |
| ChatGPT search | [OpenAI's bot page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) covers OAI-SearchBot access, not markup | Not documented |
| Perplexity | [Perplexity's bot guide](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) covers robots.txt and IP ranges, not markup | Not documented |
| Model training | [Web Data Commons](https://webdatacommons.org/structureddata/) extracts JSON-LD from Common Crawl, but no lab says it uses it | Unknown |

We checked the OpenAI and Perplexity pages on October 3, 2026 for the words schema, JSON-LD and structured data; neither page's documentation text mentions them.

What about live fetching? On October 30, 2025, [searchVIU](https://www.searchviu.com/en/schema-markup-and-ai-in-2025-what-chatgpt-claude-perplexity-gemini-really-see/) put a price only in JSON-LD on a test page and asked five AI systems for it. None reported that price. One page and one day prove little, and searchVIU notes markup may still matter at indexing, which the test could not see.

Our reading: treat JSON-LD as an upstream hint for classification and entity matching, not a storage place for facts you want quoted. If a number matters, it must also be visible text.

## Does adding schema increase AI citations?

There is no clear evidence that it does. [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/) tracked 1,885 pages that first added JSON-LD between August 2025 and March 2026 against 4,000 control pages. Citations moved −4.6% in Google AI Overviews (small but significant), +2.4% in AI Mode and +2.2% in ChatGPT; the last two were indistinguishable from zero.

Three caveats keep that honest:

- Every page already had 100+ AI Overview citations in February 2025, so the study says nothing about pages AI has not yet seen.
- It measured 30 days, pooled all types, and tested only JSON-LD present in the page's HTML.
- Across 6 million URLs, AI-cited pages were almost three times as likely to carry JSON-LD. Ahrefs calls that correlation, since well-run sites ship schema and also earn links.

Our position: schema is a symptom of a well-maintained site, not a lever. Spend a day on it, not a quarter.

## Which schema types matter most?

Start with Organization, then the type that describes what you sell, then Article. Add BreadcrumbList cheaply and treat FAQPage as optional. This order follows what Google still documents and what defines your entity. It is not ranked by citation lift, because none has been shown.

| Priority | Type | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Organization with `sameAs` and `logo` | Homepage or about page | [Google recommends](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) it there, not on every page; no required properties; use the most specific subtype |
| 2 | SoftwareApplication or Product, with `Offer` | The page that sells it | [Google's software app rich result](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app) needs name, `offers.price` and a rating or review; never invent ratings |
| 3 | Article or BlogPosting | Every article | Google recommends `author` and `dateModified` |
| 4 | BreadcrumbList | Inner pages | Cheap, low risk |
| 5 | FAQPage | Only pages with visible Q&A | Google's [changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) says FAQ rich results stopped on May 7, 2026 |

Google's [rich result gallery](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery), updated June 2026, no longer lists FAQ or HowTo.

In AEOeye's crawler access study (October 2026, 391 well-known sites, 312 homepages fetched), 73.3% of SaaS homepages (129 of 176) had Organization markup and 68.8% had `sameAs`. FAQPage appeared on only 7.4% (13 of 176), but on 42.9% of AI-visibility vendors (18 of 42). On e-commerce homepages, Product, SoftwareApplication or Offer markup appeared on 3.3% (2 of 61); it belongs on product pages, which we did not test.

It is a convenience sample of established brands, homepages only. Per-site data is in the [study CSV](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv), and the method is on the [crawler directory hub](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory). For the wider web, the [2024 Web Almanac](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/structured-data) found JSON-LD on 41% of pages but Organization on only 7.16% of mobile pages.

## What does correct JSON-LD look like?

Correct JSON-LD sits in the server-delivered HTML, uses `https://schema.org` as its `@context`, and describes only what a visitor can see on that page. Here is what aeoeye.com ships on its homepage as of October 3, 2026, trimmed to the essentials.

Organization:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "AEOeye",
  "url": "https://aeoeye.com",
  "logo": "https://aeoeye.com/icon.svg",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.pinterest.com/AEOeye01/",
    "https://www.reddit.com/user/AEOeye/"
  ]
}
```

SoftwareApplication with an Offer, which is true because the free audit costs nothing:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "SoftwareApplication",
  "name": "AEOeye",
  "applicationCategory": "BusinessApplication",
  "operatingSystem": "Web",
  "url": "https://aeoeye.com",
  "offers": { "@type": "Offer", "price": "0", "priceCurrency": "USD" }
}
```

An Article template, with placeholder values you replace from your own page data:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Article",
  "headline": "Your visible H1",
  "datePublished": "2026-01-15",
  "dateModified": "2026-10-03",
  "author": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "Your Brand", "url": "https://example.com" }
}
```

Four rules go with them:

1. **Render it on the server.** [Vercel's December 2024 analysis](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler) found ChatGPT's and Claude's crawlers do not execute JavaScript. Google [supports Tag Manager injection](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/generate-structured-data-with-javascript), but warns generated Product markup can make Shopping crawls less reliable.
2. **Point `sameAs` at real identity pages.** Schema.org [defines it](https://schema.org/sameAs) as a page that unambiguously indicates identity, such as Wikipedia, Wikidata or an official website. We list the two profiles we run, no more.
3. **Mirror visible Q&A.** Our homepage FAQPage block repeats the questions and answers printed on the page, which we checked on October 3, 2026.
4. **Skip what you cannot back.** No `aggregateRating` without real reviews.

## What is schema drift, and how do you prevent it?

Schema drift is markup that stops matching the page it describes: a stale price, a deleted FAQ, a departed author. It is easy to cause, because templates and plugins emit markup that nobody rechecks when visible content changes.

Google's [guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) say not to mark up content readers cannot see, and breaking that can prevent a rich result or get markup treated as spam. A structured data manual action removes rich result eligibility; Google says it does not change web ranking. For AI, the risk is simpler: you hand machines a confident wrong fact.

To prevent it:

1. Generate markup from the same data that renders the page, never from a parallel copy.
2. Validate on every deploy and fail the build on errors.
3. Once a month, diff your top pages' JSON-LD against their visible text.
4. Delete FAQPage from pages that no longer show Q&A.
5. Review `sameAs` and `Offer` whenever pricing, branding or profiles change.

## What should you fix before schema?

Fix visible, quotable text first. Microsoft's Bing guidance recommends self-contained phrasing and warns against hiding answers in tabs or expandable menus, because AI systems may not render them. Markup cannot rescue a page with nothing worth quoting.

That means headings that mirror real questions, a direct answer under each, and consistent brand names. Our guides on [optimizing content for AI search](/guides/how-to-optimize-content-for-ai-search) and [what makes content quotable by AI](/answers/what-makes-content-quotable-by-ai) cover this. Google's AI guide also says Google Search does not use llms.txt; our view is in [Is llms.txt worth it?](/blog/llms-txt-is-overrated).

## How do you check your structured data?

Check three things: the markup is in the raw HTML, it validates, and it matches the visible page. Then measure outcomes, because valid markup says nothing about whether any engine recommends you.

1. **Raw HTML.** Run `curl -s https://your-site.com/ | grep -c 'application/ld+json'`. Zero means crawlers that skip JavaScript see nothing.
2. **Validity.** Use Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results) for Google features and the [Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) for general syntax. Google also advises watching Search Console's rich result reports after deployment.
3. **Match.** Compare each price, date, author and question to the page.
4. **Outcome.** Ask the buyer questions your customers ask and see who gets named. Our [free audit](/) does that for three questions on ChatGPT.
