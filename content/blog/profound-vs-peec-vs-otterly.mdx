Profound, Peec AI, and Otterly.ai keep turning up in the same searches, the same "best AI visibility tools" roundups, the same internal Slack thread where someone asks "which one do we actually buy?" But line up their pricing pages, feature lists, and target customers, and a pattern shows up fast: these three aren't fighting over the same buyer. They're three tiers of the same market — and your team size probably already knows which one you need.

## The 3 tiers at a glance

Think of these three as tiers, not rivals: Otterly.ai is the entry point for solo marketers and small teams, Peec AI is the mid-market pick for growing teams (especially in Europe), and Profound is the enterprise option for organizations with a dedicated brand-analytics budget. Comparing them feature-by-feature only makes sense once you know which tier you're actually shopping in.

That distinction matters more than any checklist. A solo marketer sitting through Profound's enterprise sales process is going to find it slow and oversized for what they need. An enterprise team trying to run brand tracking across a dozen stakeholders on Otterly is going to hit walls the tool was never built to solve. Match the tier to the team first. Compare features second.

This is also why "Profound vs Otterly" is usually the wrong question to ask. It's a bit like comparing a Shopify starter plan to a Salesforce enterprise contract — technically both are "commerce platforms," but almost nobody who actually needs one is choosing between them. The real question is which shelf you're shopping on, and that's set by team size and budget authority, not by which vendor has the longer feature list on their homepage.

## Side-by-side comparison

Here's the short version: Otterly wins on affordability and simplicity, Peec AI wins on the balance of features and price for growing teams, and Profound wins on analytical depth for enterprises who can pay for it and staff someone to actually use it.

| | Otterly.ai | Peec AI | Profound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Entry-level | Mid-market | Enterprise |
| Engine coverage | Core AI engines; some offered as add-ons | Multi-engine, growing coverage | Broadest coverage, deep per-engine analytics |
| Team size fit | Solo marketers, small teams | Growing marketing teams | Multiple stakeholders, larger orgs |
| Price tier (as of 2026) | Budget-friendly, self-serve | Mid-range, mostly self-serve | Premium, sales-led |
| Best for | Testing AEO without a big commitment | Teams that outgrew spreadsheets and need collaboration | Brand and analytics teams needing scale and depth |

Pricing moves on all three of these regularly, and none of it is worth repeating secondhand. As of 2026, check each vendor's own site before you build a budget around a number you saw in someone else's blog post.

A word on "engine coverage" specifically: all three track the major AI answer engines in some form, but how deep that tracking goes — and how fast newer engines get added — tends to scale with the tier. Enterprise tools usually move faster on adding coverage, because that breadth is exactly what enterprise buyers are paying for. Don't assume "covers ChatGPT" means the same thing across all three; ask each vendor what "coverage" includes before you compare on that line alone.

## Otterly.ai — best for solo marketers and small teams

Otterly.ai is built for marketers who want to start tracking AI visibility without sitting through an enterprise buying process — it's the fastest on-ramp to seeing how ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other engines mention your brand today.

It's priced and packaged for individuals and small teams, and some AI engines are offered as add-ons rather than bundled in from day one. If you're still validating whether AEO matters for your brand before you commit real budget, this is the low-friction way to find out.

The tradeoff is depth. What you gain in speed and affordability, you give up in the kind of granular, multi-stakeholder reporting an enterprise team would expect. That's not a flaw — it's the point. Otterly isn't trying to be Profound at a lower price; it's solving a narrower problem for a different buyer. Our [AEOeye vs Otterly.ai comparison](/vs/otterly) has the feature-level detail.

## Peec AI — best for growing mid-market teams

Peec AI earns its spot in the middle: multi-engine coverage plus the collaboration features a growing marketing team actually needs, at a price that doesn't require a procurement meeting to approve.

It's built a strong following among mid-market teams in Europe specifically, which tracks — the product feels shaped around that market's workflows and buying habits. If your team has outgrown a single-user tool but isn't ready for an enterprise sales cycle, this is the tier to start shopping in.

The tradeoff is that Peec AI isn't chasing the deepest enterprise analytics — it's optimizing for a team that needs solid multi-engine data without managing five different logins to get it. For a marketing team of five to fifteen people, that balance is usually worth more than an extra layer of enterprise reporting nobody on the team has time to read anyway. ## Profound — best for enterprises with dedicated analytics budgets

Profound is the enterprise tier: the broadest engine coverage, the deepest analytics, and a sales-led buying process that assumes you have both the budget and the stakeholder team to match.

That depth is the point, not a downside. Enterprises tracking brand visibility across multiple products, regions, or business units need the breadth Profound was built for — a lighter tool stretched past its design just adds risk at that scale.

The tradeoff is just as obvious: a longer sales cycle and a higher price than either of the other two. That's a reasonable trade for a buyer who needs the reporting to hold up in a board deck. It's a bad trade for a five-person startup that just wants to know whether ChatGPT ever mentions them. ## Which tier are you?

Your team size and buying process answer this faster than any feature list: a solo or small team with a self-serve budget points to Otterly, a growing team that needs collaboration and multi-engine coverage points to Peec AI, and an enterprise org with a dedicated budget and sales cycle points to Profound.

Use these four questions to place yourself:

- **Team size** — 1–2 people managing AI visibility: Otterly. 3–15 people: Peec AI. 15+ people or multiple stakeholders: Profound.
- **Budget process** — Self-serve, card-on-file: Otterly or Peec AI. Requires procurement or a sales cycle: Profound.
- **AEO maturity** — Just testing whether this matters at all: Otterly. Already tracking it and need more engines and seats: Peec AI. Already sold internally and need enterprise-grade reporting: Profound.
- **Reporting needs** — A dashboard you check yourself: Otterly or Peec AI. Formal reporting for stakeholders across a large org: Profound.

None of these are wrong answers — they're built for different stages of the same problem. The real mistake is buying a tier ahead of where your team actually is: overpaying for enterprise depth nobody uses, or underbuying a tool your team outgrows in two quarters and has to migrate away from anyway.
