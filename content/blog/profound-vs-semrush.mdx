You're already paying for Semrush every month, and now "Profound" keeps showing up in every AI-search roundup you read. So you're stuck on a fair question: do you actually need a second, dedicated tool just to track how ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews talk about your brand, or does the AI visibility feature already inside Semrush cover it? The honest answer isn't "buy both" or "Semrush is fine" — it depends on whether AI visibility is a number you glance at once a quarter, or a channel you're starting to manage like paid search, with its own budget and its own expectations.

## Semrush (AI visibility) at a glance

Semrush is an all-in-one SEO suite that added AI visibility features — convenient if you already live in Semrush, but AI visibility is one module among many. The core product has always been keyword research, rank tracking, site audits, and backlink analysis — the toolkit most SEO teams already open every single day.

Over the past year or two, Semrush layered an AI visibility module on top of that existing suite, tracking whether your brand gets mentioned in AI Overviews and chatbot answers, roughly alongside your normal keyword rankings. If your team already opens Semrush daily for keyword and backlink work, that's a genuinely convenient way to get a first read on AI visibility without adding another login, another invoice, or another dashboard to check. For more on how the broader SEO toolset is adapting to AI search, see our breakdown of [AI for SEO](/blog/ai-for-seo).

The tradeoff shows up the moment you actually need depth. Because AI visibility is one module inside a much bigger product, it's built to answer "are we showing up, roughly?" — not to hand you the prompt-by-prompt detail a team that lives or dies by AI visibility is going to want. It's a solid early-warning signal, not a research tool. For a solo marketer or a small in-house team, that's often exactly the right amount of AI visibility tracking — a signal, not a project.

## Profound at a glance

Profound is a dedicated AI visibility platform — deeper and more focused on how AI answers represent your brand. It wasn't bolted onto an existing SEO suite as a feature; it was built from the ground up to track brand mentions, citations, and sentiment across AI answer engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.

That singular focus shows up as depth. Instead of one dashboard tile, you get granular views of which prompts actually trigger a mention of your brand and which competing sources get cited in your place. You also see how your visibility stacks up against named competitors inside AI answers specifically — not buried as a single row in a broader SEO report. We go deeper on this exact matchup in our full [Profound review](/vs/profound), and if Profound itself isn't the right fit, [Profound alternatives](/alternatives/profound) rounds up other dedicated options worth a look.

The tradeoff is the mirror image of Semrush's. You're adding a second subscription, a second login, and a second workflow — for a tool that, by design, does one job instead of ten. For a team whose growth plan explicitly includes AI search — an agency reporting AI visibility to clients, or a brand that's watched a competitor get cited over and over in ChatGPT answers — that depth is the entire point of paying for a second tool.

## Dedicated tool vs all-in-one: the real tradeoff

An all-in-one saves you a subscription; a dedicated tool goes deeper on the one thing that's becoming a channel of its own. Laid out side by side, the practical differences look like this:

| | All-in-one (Semrush-style) | Dedicated (Profound-style) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary job | SEO suite; AI visibility is one module | AI visibility is the entire product |
| Depth on AI answers | Are you mentioned, roughly how often | Which prompts, which sources are cited instead of you, sentiment detail |
| Learning curve | Low if you already use the suite | New tool, new workflow to adopt |
| Cost structure | Bundled into an existing subscription | Separate line item on top of existing tools |
| Best fit | Teams that want an AI-visibility signal alongside SEO work | Teams treating AI visibility as its own budgeted channel |
| Roadmap focus | AI visibility improves as a side effect of SEO work | AI visibility improves because it's the whole roadmap |

Neither column wins outright — they're built to answer different questions. An all-in-one is asking "how's our SEO doing, and by the way, are we showing up in AI answers too?" A dedicated tool is asking "exactly how, where, and why are AI models representing us the way they are — and what do we do about it?" Those are different jobs, even when they're measuring an overlapping thing. ## Which should you choose?

If AI visibility is something you check once a quarter next to rankings and backlinks, Semrush's built-in module is probably enough. You're not losing much by skipping a second platform when AI search is still a minor slice of how your buyers actually find you.

A dedicated tool starts earning its cost once a few of these are true for you:

- You're already seeing measurable traffic or leads referred from ChatGPT, Perplexity, or AI Overviews.
- Your buyers are known to research or shortlist vendors by asking AI assistants before they visit anyone's website.
- AI visibility has its own line item in your marketing budget, separate from general SEO.
- You need to know exactly which prompts you're losing, and to which competitor, not just a general "mentioned sometimes."

That's a different job than a general SEO suite was ever built to do — which is also the core distinction we unpack in [AEO vs SEO](/blog/aeo-vs-seo). There's no universally correct answer here, and you should treat any source that hands you one flatly — including this one — with a little suspicion.

Most teams don't fall cleanly into either bucket — you're somewhere in the middle, where AI search clearly matters but you're not sure how much yet. In that case, the cheaper move is to start with what you already have. Turn on Semrush's AI visibility module, watch it for a month or two, and see whether the numbers move enough to justify a dedicated tool. You can always upgrade to more depth later; you can't get back a subscription you paid for and barely opened.
