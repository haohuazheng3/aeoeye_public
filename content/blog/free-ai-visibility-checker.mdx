## What a Free Checker Can (and Can't) Tell You

A free check reveals whether you're mentioned and roughly where you stand against competitors across the major AI engines; deep prompt sets, historical trend tracking, and exportable reports usually live behind a paid plan. Treat the free result as a snapshot, not a full audit — think of it the way you'd think of a single credit-score pull versus a full financial audit: directionally useful, not the whole picture.

What you should reasonably expect from a free scan:

- **Presence** — are you named at all when someone asks an AI engine a buying question in your category?
- **A competitor snapshot** — who's getting mentioned in your place, at a glance.
- **A cross-engine view** — most free tiers, including AEOeye's, check more than one engine (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI) rather than just one.

What a free tier typically won't give you:

- **A large, custom prompt set.** Free tools run a handful of representative queries, not the 50-100 prompt sets that map to your full buyer journey.
- **Historical tracking.** One snapshot doesn't tell you if you're gaining or losing ground month over month.
- **Root-cause detail.** Knowing you're absent is step one; knowing *why* — thin schema, no comparison content, weak entity signals — is usually a paid-tier or manual-audit job.

If your free result comes back thin or absent, that's useful information on its own — it just means the next move is fixing the gap, not running more free scans. Running the same free scan five times in a week won't change the underlying content and structure problems that are keeping you out of AI answers in the first place. ## How to Read Your Free Result

Read your result along three axes: whether you're mentioned at all, who's occupying the answer instead of you, and what tone the mention carries when you do show up. All three matter more than a single composite score.

1. **Presence.** Binary, per engine. Mentioned or not — this is the headline number, but it's the least interesting one on its own.
2. **Competitor occupancy.** If you're absent, who's there instead? A free scan that shows you three competitors named ahead of you is more actionable than a bare "you weren't mentioned."
3. **Tone and context.** Being named isn't the same as being recommended. AI engines sometimes mention a brand in passing, in a comparison list, or as a caveat ("X exists, but Y is more established") — the difference matters for how urgently you act.

One more thing worth knowing: a free result is a snapshot from the moment you ran it. AI engines' answers shift as they retrain, re-crawl, and adjust which sources they trust, so the same query can return a different answer next week. That's not a bug in the checker; it's the nature of the systems being measured.

A quick sanity check before you trust any score: look at an actual [sample report](/example) so you know what a real breakdown looks like versus a bare number with no supporting detail. If the tool you used can't show you why it scored you the way it did, be skeptical of the score.
