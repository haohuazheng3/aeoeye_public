## How should you read prompt and audience capacity?

A prompt is a question selected for monitoring; an audience segment distinguishes groups whose questions or needs may differ. The pricing page lists prompt allowances for Pro and Prism and five Prism segments, but does not define how they are configured or consumed.

Make a short inventory of the questions that matter: category discovery, brand evaluation, product comparisons, and recommendations. Tie each question to a decision, then decide whether separate audience groups truly ask or evaluate differently. This keeps capacity focused on distinct buyer needs instead of filling slots with near-duplicates.

The number of segments does not show how many prompts can be assigned to each, whether segments are customizable, or whether a prompt can be reused across them. Likewise, the listed prompt limit does not establish run frequency or a guaranteed answer count. Ask how prompts are scheduled and counted before using a plan limit as a forecast.

The useful budgeting unit is a repeatable prompt-and-audience question, alongside the AI platforms covered. ## Which plan fits a brand or agency?

Pro is the clearest fit for a bounded set of up to 150 prompts where ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews are the stated platform needs. Prism may fit teams needing 750 prompts and five audience segments. GEO Council is a waitlist option, not confirmed immediate access.

An in-house team should start with priority products, markets, and buyer questions. If Pro covers those questions with a useful refresh cadence, paying for larger capacity may not add value. If audience comparisons or additional coverage matter, ask whether the tier supports the required setup; the facts here do not establish Pro’s segment capabilities or Prism’s model coverage.

An agency should estimate capacity per client, including onboarding, recurring audits, client-specific prompts, and reporting cadence. Ask whether account limits are shared among clients and whether separate workspaces are available. The published prompt and segment numbers alone do not establish how many client programs a plan can support.

Choose based on whether the plan can create a useful, repeatable record of mentions, citations, and recommendations across relevant buyer questions. High answer volume without clear coverage and repeat runs can create false confidence. ## What should you verify before buying?

Confirm tier definitions, platform coverage, billing, and access before committing. The public price and capacity summaries leave operational details that affect the real cost.

Use the seven-day trial to assess workflow fit, not as proof of accuracy. This guide did not test outputs. Before the trial ends, ask:

- How often are prompts run, and can the schedule be changed?
- What counts as a prompt, run, and analyzed result?
- Which platforms are included in Prism and GEO Council?
- How are Prism’s five segments configured, and can prompts be reused across them?
- Are limits shared across brands, users, or workspaces?
- What happens when a prompt allowance is reached?
- What are the payment, renewal, and cancellation terms?
- How can a buyer join the GEO Council waitlist, and when can access be confirmed?

Test with a fixed set of realistic buyer prompts and save dated examples. Check whether a teammate can understand what was asked and how the brand appeared. For a structured review, see AEOeye’s [AEO audit checklist](/blog/aeo-audit-checklist).
