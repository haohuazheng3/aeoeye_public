If your bottleneck is making and refreshing pages, start with AirOps. If it is not knowing where AI engines name your competitors instead of you, start with AthenaHQ. This guide is based on public documentation as of October 2026 (each vendor's own pricing and product pages, fetched on October 3), and we have not tested either product hands-on.

The tidy story of "content tool versus tracking tool" is out of date. AirOps now sells intelligence and measurement on top of its agents, and AthenaHQ sells content recommendations on top of its monitoring. They still start from opposite ends.

## Do you need AirOps or AthenaHQ?

You need AirOps when the work is the problem, and AthenaHQ when the picture is the problem. Our position: measure before you scale production, because volume without a map of lost questions mostly produces pages nobody asked for.

- **AirOps fits** teams with a known backlog of pages to create or refresh and not enough hands.
- **AthenaHQ fits** teams that cannot yet say which buyer questions name competitors instead of them.
- **Both can fit** large programs that want one system for measuring and another for shipping.

## What is AirOps in October 2026?

AirOps is a platform of agents, playbooks and workflows for producing and refreshing marketing content, now framed around AI discovery. Its [platform page](https://www.airops.com/platform) describes three parts: intelligence, action and measurement. Its center of gravity is still the action layer.

What the vendor's pages state:

- **Action:** agents break growth programs into goals, hand work to specialist sub-agents and run playbooks and workflows. CMS integrations publish owned content with field mappings and item IDs preserved.
- **Review:** agents pause for human approval in an inbox, with comments, version history and rollback. Content scores check readability, claim support, information gain, SEO and AEO before publishing.
- **Intelligence:** per the [pricing page](https://www.airops.com/pricing), Solo includes ChatGPT insights only; Pro includes insights across 7+ answer engines.
- **Services:** a [September 2, 2026 announcement](https://www.airops.com/blog/the-next-chapter-for-airops) adds a hands-on team for strategy and execution.

AirOps also argues that a visibility score "often measures a brand's available prompt list, not the market." It is a fair warning, and it is the vendor's own position.

## What is AthenaHQ in October 2026?

AthenaHQ is a monitoring-first platform that tracks how AI engines describe your brand, then suggests fixes. It calls itself ["the command center for AEO and GEO"](https://athenahq.ai). Its content features center on recommended changes rather than on running publishing pipelines.

From its [pricing page](https://athenahq.ai/pricing) and homepage:

- **Monitoring:** all plans cover ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews and Copilot. Paid plans add Google AI Mode, Claude, Grok, DeepSeek and Meta AI.
- **Action:** "AthenaHQ Content" lists on-page and off-page fixes mapped to passages AI models use, and "Ask Athena" answers questions about your own data.
- **Plans:** Free ($25 credit, 300 credits), Starter at $295/month for 3,600 credits, and a custom Enterprise plan. One credit equals one AI response.
- **Integrations:** GA4, Search Console, Shopify and Webflow.

Our broader notes are in the [AthenaHQ review](/blog/athenahq-review), and the [AthenaHQ versus Profound comparison](/blog/athenahq-vs-profound) covers its closest monitoring rival.

## How do production and measurement differ in practice?

Production tools are judged by what ships; measurement tools by what AI engines then say. The table shows where each vendor's own pages put the weight. Neither column is empty, which is the point.

| | AirOps | AthenaHQ |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Agents, playbooks, workflows | Prompt and response monitoring |
| Visibility insight | Solo: ChatGPT only; Pro: 7+ engines | All plans: 5 platforms; Starter: 11 models |
| Content work | Drafts, refreshes, CMS publishing, review inbox | Recommendations and an optimization agent |
| Published price | None shown; Enterprise by quote | Free, then $295/month; Enterprise by quote |
| Usage unit | Tasks (Solo overage: $0.025/task) | Credits (1 credit = 1 AI response) |
| Buying route | Sign up, or talk to the team | Sign up, or contact sales |

Read the usage row before you budget. Tasks are counted when workflow actions complete, so heavy production adds usage quickly; credits rise with how many prompts and engines you monitor.

AirOps's FAQ also mentions "Scale" and "Agency" plans that its plan cards do not show. Ask for current terms.

## Which bottleneck do you actually have?

Look at your last quarter. If you shipped pages and cannot say what changed in AI answers, you have a measurement gap. If you know the gaps and cannot close them, you have a capacity gap.

![Aerial view of a desk with a laptop, coffee and accessories](https://images.pexels.com/photos/20955062/pexels-photo-20955062.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

Measurement gaps are easy to fall into because more pages do not guarantee more citations. [Google's documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and that there are no additional requirements or special optimizations. Throughput alone is not a strategy.

Capacity gaps are real too. A two-person team with 40 validated topics needs production help more than another dashboard.

The [GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) reports visibility gains of up to 40% from certain content changes, with results varying by domain. That is a reason to test changes on your own questions before industrializing them.

## What do the two pricing models mean for your budget?

Neither vendor lets you compute a real monthly bill from its public page. AthenaHQ publishes one price and a credit allowance; AirOps publishes allowances and an overage rate but no plan price. Ask each for a written quote before you plan a budget.

What the pages give you:

- **AthenaHQ:** $295/month for 3,600 credits on Starter, with API access and extra credits sold as add-ons at unpublished prices. Annual billing is shown as 17% off.
- **AirOps:** 35,000 tasks a month on Solo and 100,000 on Pro. Its FAQ says only specific step types count as tasks and the number depends on the step and the data processed.
- **Trials:** AirOps's FAQ describes a 14-day free trial, but names plan tiers that its plan cards do not show.

Our reading: tasks reward predictable, templated production, while credits reward a stable list of monitored prompts. A team that experiments heavily with new workflows can overshoot a task allowance quickly. A team that monitors hundreds of prompts on many engines can burn through credits just as fast.

## Can you use AirOps and AthenaHQ together?

Yes, but only if you can name which tool owns which job. Both now report visibility, so running both as dashboards duplicates cost and creates two versions of the truth.

A workable split:

1. **Measure:** one tool is the record of who AI names for your buyer questions.
2. **Decide:** use those gaps to pick pages to build or refresh, as in our [guide to optimizing content for AI search](/guides/how-to-optimize-content-for-ai-search).
3. **Produce:** run the work in the production tool, with human review.
4. **Re-check:** re-ask the same questions to see whether anything moved.

For a one-off baseline before you buy either, AEOeye's [free audit](/) asks ChatGPT three buyer questions, and the $29 one-time report covers ten questions across five engines.
