## How much does Gauge cost?

Gauge Growth costs $599 monthly, while Enterprise is custom-priced and has no public amount on the official site. The published Growth fee is a recurring monthly price, not a stated annual prepayment rate.

| Published plan detail | Amount or allowance | What the public documentation establishes |
|---|---:|---|
| Growth | $599/month | Monthly plan price |
| Prompt allowance | 600 | Run through leading models daily, per the FAQ |
| Content Engine allowance | 18 articles/month | Monthly included article volume |
| Enterprise | Custom | The site directs buyers toward a demo; no amount is published |

For a simple annual budget, $599 × 12 = **$7,188** for twelve monthly payments, before any taxes or other terms not stated on the public pricing page. There is no basis in the public information reviewed here to assume an annual discount, a particular contract term, or an Enterprise price.

Gauge presents Growth as a combined measurement and execution package, so compare it with monitoring and content workflow costs. See AEOeye’s guides to [AI visibility tools](/blog/best-ai-visibility-tools) and [AI search monitoring](/blog/ai-search-monitoring).

## What is included in Growth?

Growth includes 600 prompts run through leading models daily and 18 Content Engine articles per month. Public materials do not define every operational limit behind those allowances.

Gauge describes monitoring across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, AI Mode, and AI Overviews. It also lists GSC, GA4, DataForSEO, and PostHog integrations, plus crawler monitoring via Cloudflare and Vercel. These are vendor feature claims, not proof of visibility, traffic, or revenue outcomes.

The practical question is whether the tracking and workflow match your reporting needs. Teams that need occasional snapshots may not use daily runs or 18 article slots; teams with existing content operations should clarify what review and publishing work remains theirs.

## What does 600 daily prompts mean?

The official FAQ says the 600 prompts run through all leading models daily, but does not publicly explain whether the allowance counts unique prompt configurations, model-specific executions, or another unit. That distinction matters because one configured question tested across multiple models can create more answer observations than a single model response.

Do not divide the monthly plan price by an assumed number of responses. A transparent rough allocation is:

**$599 ÷ 600 configured prompts = about $1.00 per configured prompt per month.**

That is not a cost per model response. Gauge says prompts run across models daily, and the public pricing description does not state the exact number of model executions, how retries are counted, or whether “600” is a monthly prompt set repeated daily. Ask Gauge to clarify the allowance’s counting rules, refresh cadence, overage policy, and treatment of paused or edited prompts before forecasting capacity.

A useful prompt set reflects real buyer questions: branded and unbranded queries, category comparisons, and decision-stage needs. ## How should buyers value the 18 article allowance?

The 18-article allowance does not establish the finished cost or usefulness of an article. Public details do not define article length, revisions, source requirements, or expected human editing.

As a rough allocation, **$599 ÷ 18 = $33.28 per included article slot**. This is not a standalone writing price: the fee also covers monitoring and analytics, and the calculation says nothing about quality or production effort.

Ask whether slots roll over, how output is reviewed, and whether it fits your editorial and publishing process. If expert review, source checking, or CMS approval is the bottleneck, more drafts may not reduce production time. Compare the work removed with your current process; an included slot is not necessarily a publish-ready page.

## When does Enterprise make sense?

Enterprise is worth discussing when your required capacity, governance, or operating needs do not fit the published Growth description. Gauge labels Enterprise as custom-priced, so buyers need a vendor quote to establish the actual fee and terms.

Discuss Enterprise when you need a larger prompt set, multiple brands or teams, or terms Growth documentation does not answer. These are vendor questions, not confirmed inclusions; custom pricing does not establish higher limits, support, or service levels.

Bring a workload estimate covering brands, markets, users, prompts, reports, and articles. Request written limits and any setup fees, minimum term, renewal rules, or usage charges so the quote can be compared with Growth and separate tools.

## What should you verify before buying?

Verify allowance definitions, total cost, and remaining human work; headline figures do not resolve every operational or contract detail.

Use the demo or start-free path to resolve the details that affect your budget:

- Is 600 the number of distinct prompts, and how are daily model runs counted across the listed engines?
- What happens when a team changes prompts, reaches a limit, or needs more capacity?
- What does one of the 18 monthly articles include: format, length, revisions, source handling, and export or publishing steps?
- Which integrations are available on Growth, and what data or setup is required to use them?
- How are mentions, citations, recommendations, and competitor comparisons defined in reports?
- What are the cancellation, renewal, billing, and Enterprise contract terms?

Compare the answers with current costs and workflow. Gauge’s $599 is a bundled measurement-and-execution budget; it fits best when the team can use both recurring visibility data and content capacity.
