You typed "GEO agency" into a search bar because AI answers have quietly become the new battleground for customer attention — if ChatGPT or Perplexity recommends a competitor instead of you, that's a lost customer who never even saw your website, let alone your homepage. The catch: this category is so new that even the agencies selling it haven't agreed on a name. GEO, AEO, and "LLM SEO agency" are three labels for essentially the same pitch, and the inconsistency alone should tell you how early-stage this market still is.

## What is a GEO agency?

A GEO (generative engine optimization) agency helps brands get cited and recommended inside AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews — a service category built around a genuine shift in search behavior, but one that overlaps heavily with classic SEO rather than replacing it outright.

The core idea behind [generative engine optimization](/blog/generative-engine-optimization) isn't complicated: instead of optimizing purely for a page-one ranking, you optimize for being the source an AI model pulls from when it answers a question. [The glossary definition](/glossary/generative-engine-optimization) breaks the mechanics down in more detail.

What actually trips people up is the naming. GEO, AEO (answer engine optimization), and "LLM SEO" get used interchangeably by agencies, and none of the three has won out as the industry standard term. If you want the real distinctions — there are some, mostly in emphasis rather than substance — see [AEO vs GEO vs SEO](/compare/aeo-vs-geo-vs-seo). For hiring purposes, treat the three as synonyms until an agency proves otherwise, and ask them to define their own terminology rather than assuming you share one.

## What do GEO agencies actually do?

In practice, GEO agencies sell a bundle of four services: visibility monitoring, entity and schema cleanup, answer-shaped content, and third-party consensus building — most of which is recognizable to anyone who's hired a solid SEO or content agency before.

- **AI visibility auditing and monitoring.** Tracking whether, how often, and in what context a brand gets mentioned across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and similar tools — usually through repeated prompt testing rather than a public API, since none of these platforms publish a "your citation rate" dashboard.
- **Entity and schema work.** Structured data (FAQ, Organization, Product schema), consistent business naming across the web, and a coherent presence in the knowledge graphs AI systems draw from, so models have a cleaner signal about who you are.
- **Answer-shaped content.** Rewriting or building pages so the first sentence of a section directly answers the question it poses, using the structure — bullets, comparison tables, direct numbers — that AI answer engines tend to extract cleanly.
- **Third-party consensus building.** Getting reviews, comparisons, and mentions placed on the sites LLMs already trust and cite, since AI models weigh independent corroboration far more than they weigh your own homepage copy. This is arguably the hardest of the four to fake or rush, and it's where a good agency's existing relationships can genuinely save you months of outreach.

Here's the part agency pitch decks tend to gloss over: three of those four bullets are just good AEO/SEO execution with new packaging. That's not automatically a knock — solid execution has real value — but it means the label "GEO agency" tells you very little on its own. Evaluate the deliverables and the evidence behind them, not the name on the invoice.

## How much does a GEO agency cost?

Pricing is all over the map because the category is new and no established market rate exists yet. Commonly reported ranges run from low four figures a month up to enterprise-level retainers — but treat any number you see quoted online, including that one, as a snapshot rather than a rule.

A few reasons pricing is so inconsistent right now:

- There's no standard deliverable list, so a "GEO retainer" can mean a monthly audit report or a full content-production team.
- Agencies are still working out how to price monitoring work that depends on unpredictable, frequently-changing AI platforms.
- Many "GEO agencies" are existing SEO or content shops that repriced an adjacent service line rather than building new pricing from scratch.

Get exact scope, deliverables, and reporting cadence in writing before you sign anything — and be specific about what "done" looks like for month one.

## How to choose a GEO agency (checklist)

The fastest way to separate a real GEO practice from a relabeled SEO retainer is to ask direct questions about evidence, not strategy decks. Run every agency you're evaluating through this checklist before you sign.

1. **Which AI engines do you actually monitor?** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini behave differently — a vague answer here is a red flag.
2. **Can you show citation evidence, not just claims?** Ask for a redacted before/after example showing a client actually getting cited or recommended, including the prompts used.
3. **Will you run a baseline audit before I pay for a retainer?** You can't measure improvement without a starting point — a serious agency establishes one first.
4. **Who writes and reviews the content?** Get specifics on whether it's a dedicated writer, a generalist account manager, or an unreviewed AI pipeline.
5. **Does your reporting show citations, or just rankings?** Traditional rank-tracking dashboards rebranded as "AI visibility" reports are a common shortcut — ask to see a sample.
6. **What happens if I cancel?** Do you keep the schema markup, content, and entity work, or does the value disappear with the contract?

| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Redacted before/after citation examples | Real track record |
| Reporting shows only keyword rank | Relabeled SEO retainer |
| Refuses a pre-billing baseline audit | Selling activity, not outcomes |
| Vague on which AI engines they track | Early-stage or unproven practice |

None of this makes an agency bad by default — plenty of teams are learning this space in good faith. It just means the burden of proof sits with them, not with you. And be realistic about timelines: AI answer patterns shift as models get updated, so anyone promising permanent, guaranteed placement in a specific chatbot's response is overselling a moving target.
