You can't rank in Gemini, because there is no results list to climb. You can only be named in its answers, and the Gemini app passed [1 billion monthly users in August 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/one-billion-monthly-users/). In our reading of Google's documentation, being named depends mostly on the work behind your Google visibility: pages Google Search can find, a business Google can identify without guessing, and credible third parties saying the same thing. This guide is based on public documentation as of October 2026; we have not tested Gemini against your brand.

## How does Gemini decide what to recommend?

Gemini decides question by question whether to search Google, and when it does, it writes the answer from the results. Google does not publish how it picks among those results, so treat anything beyond the points below as inference.

What Google documents:

- **The model chooses whether to search.** In the Gemini API, the model [decides whether a Google Search can improve the answer and generates one or more queries](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search).
- **The app uses Search too.** Gemini Apps Help says Gemini [automatically uses public information from certain Google services](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13695044): Search, Flights, Hotels, Maps and YouTube.
- **Links are optional.** The app [may provide links to sources, but not every response includes them](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14143489).

Our inference: a page Google can't find or doesn't surface for the underlying question is unlikely to shape Gemini's answer. Google has not said so in those words.

One independent study tested the grounded API. In [a University of Toronto preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919), gemini-2.5-flash with Google Search grounding cited 63.4% earned media, 25.1% brand-owned pages and 11.5% social sources on ranking-style questions about well-known brands. ChatGPT's search model cited 93.5% earned and 6.5% brand. The caveats are real: an API rather than the app, 2025 model versions, and the authors' own source classification. Our takeaway: your own pages count for more in Gemini than in ChatGPT, but third-party coverage still supplies most of what it cites.

## Can you do SEO for Gemini?

Yes, and it is mostly ordinary Google SEO. Google's guide to generative AI features says SEO best practices stay relevant because those features [are rooted in its core ranking and quality systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). That guide covers AI Overviews and AI Mode and never names the Gemini app, so applying it there is our extrapolation from the grounding mechanism.

The same guide lists what you don't need: llms.txt files and other special markup, chunking content, rewriting content just for AI systems, seeking inauthentic mentions, and overfocusing on structured data. We agree. We publish an llms.txt only because it is cheap; our position is in [llms.txt is overrated](/blog/llms-txt-is-overrated). If a vendor sells a "Gemini-only" trick beyond this list, ask for the Google page that supports it.

Where Gemini genuinely differs is first-party business data. The app uses [public Google Maps information such as ratings, websites and opening hours](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16622866), and Merchant Center says free product listings [can appear on Gemini](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9199328). Business Profile and Merchant Center are the two Gemini-specific levers Google documents.

## What should you do for Gemini, step by step?

Do six things, in order of leverage. The first two are plain Google SEO; the rest make you identifiable and corroborated.

1. **Publish a rankable answer page.** Take one buyer question, such as "best payroll software for ten employees", and answer it in the first two sentences of one crawlable, indexable page. One strong page per question beats five thin ones; [how to get your business recommended by AI](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-recommended-by-ai) covers the wider playbook.
2. **Check what Google shows for that question.** If you are not in the results, fix that before anything Gemini-specific.
3. **Complete your Google Business Profile (local businesses).** Google says [complete and accurate information makes a business more likely to appear, and more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
4. **List products in Merchant Center (sellers).** Free listings need a feed with attributes such as title, link, price and availability.
5. **Make your entity unambiguous.** Google says [Organization structured data helps it understand and disambiguate your organization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization); add `sameAs` links to your profiles and keep one name everywhere. See [what is entity SEO](/blog/what-is-entity-seo).
6. **Earn independent coverage.** Reviews, roundups and comparison pages are where most grounded citations came from in the study above. Skip fake mentions: Google says they aren't as helpful as they seem.

If your buyers learn by video, note that Gemini also [uses public YouTube information](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16622858), so a clear video can be a second way in.

## What does Google-Extended actually control?

Google-Extended is a robots.txt token, not a separate crawler. Per [Google's crawler documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers), it lets you control whether content Google crawls may be used to train future Gemini models and to ground responses in Gemini Apps and Grounding with Google Search on Vertex AI. It does not affect inclusion in Google Search or act as a ranking signal.

Three points people get wrong:

- **It is not training-only.** Google's wording includes grounding in Gemini Apps, so blocking it is broader than a training opt-out.
- **It does not control AI Overviews or AI Mode.** Those have a separate [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024), which Google says doesn't affect training.
- **It won't stop user-requested fetches.** Google says [user-triggered fetchers, including Gemini Notebook, generally ignore robots.txt](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-user-triggered-fetchers).

In [AEOeye's crawler access study](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv) (October 2026, 384 sites with a readable or absent robots.txt), 31 sites (8.1%) block Google-Extended. That is 20 of 38 publishers, 5 of 20 UGC and review sites, 4 of 90 e-commerce and consumer brands, 2 of 193 SaaS brands and none of 43 AI-visibility vendors. It is a convenience sample, not the web. Our stance: unless you license your content, don't block it, and aeoeye.com explicitly allows it.

## Gemini app, AI Mode and AI Overviews: are they the same?

They share a parent company, not a product, a control or a report. Google doesn't publish whether the Gemini app and AI Mode draw on identical result sets, so don't assume they do.

| | Gemini app | AI Mode | AI Overviews |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Chat assistant that uses public Google Search, Maps and YouTube information | [Search experience that splits a question into subtopics and searches each](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537) | [AI snapshot with links, shown in Search](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683) |
| Publisher control | Google-Extended | Search generative AI control | Search generative AI control |
| Reporting | None documented in Search Console | [Generative AI performance report (impressions)](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) | Same report |

For the Search-side surfaces, see [how to rank in Google AI Mode](/blog/how-to-rank-in-google-ai-mode) and [what is an AI Overview](/blog/what-is-an-ai-overview).

## How do you check whether Gemini recommends you?

Ask Gemini the questions your buyers ask, repeat each several times, and log whether you are named. There is no rank position to read, and one answer proves little because answers vary between runs.

1. Write 10 unbranded buyer questions, such as "best CRM for a five-person agency".
2. Use a [temporary chat](https://blog.google/products/gemini/temporary-chats-privacy-controls/), which Google says isn't used to personalize responses, so your own history doesn't skew results.
3. Run each question three to five times and record whether you appear, who else does, and whether sources were shown.
4. Report a mention rate across all runs, not a position. [SparkToro's 2025 study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) of ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI found visibility percentage across many prompts a reasonable metric and ranking position unreliable. It did not test the Gemini app, so treat this as a method, not a Gemini finding.

For clicks, GA4's default channel group has an [AI Assistant channel that lists Gemini](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891) among its sources. It only sees visits that carry a referrer; [AI traffic analytics](/blog/ai-traffic-analytics) explains how to audit it.

For scale, in AEOeye's audit records (June to September 2026) Gemini named the audited brand in 2 of 74 answers. That sample is tiny and self-selected, and engines aren't comparable, so don't read it as a benchmark. The useful part is the method: test your own questions. AEOeye's [free audit](/) checks three questions in ChatGPT, and the $29 report adds Gemini; for daily monitoring across hundreds of prompts, dedicated trackers such as Profound or Peec AI are the better fit.
