AI search is search where an AI model reads sources and writes you one answer, usually with citations, instead of handing you ten links to compare. ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude with web search, and Google AI Overviews and AI Mode all work this way. For a brand the shift is simple: buyers now meet a shortlist of a few names, so being named matters more than ranking ninth.

## What is AI search, and how is it different from traditional search?

AI search puts a language model between you and the web: it finds pages, reads them, and writes the answer instead of listing links. [Wikipedia's entry on search engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine) describes the 2020s version as traditional web indexing combined with large language models.

| | Traditional search | AI search |
|---|---|---|
| Query | Short keywords: "best crm software" | Full questions: "best CRM for a five-person sales team" |
| Memory | Each query stands alone | Follow-ups keep the thread's context |
| Output | Ranked links | One written answer with source links |
| Your job | Click, compare, decide | Read the answer; the comparing is done |
| Winning means | A high position | Being named in the answer |

Traditional search still wins when you know where you are going. In [Pew Research's March 2025 browsing data](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), only 8% of one- or two-word Google searches produced an AI summary, against 53% of searches with ten or more words. AI answers show up most on long, question-shaped searches, and the "which should I pick?" kind is where buying decisions form.

## Are AI search, generative search and conversational search the same thing?

Nearly: they are three angles on one shift, not three technologies. AI search names the technology, generative search names the output, and conversational search names the interaction. Most products combine all three.

| Term | In one line |
|---|---|
| AI search | A language model sits between you and the sources |
| Generative search | The engine [synthesizes an answer from several sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) |
| Conversational search | A [sequence of dialogue turns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08808) in which follow-ups keep earlier context |

## How does AI search work?

AI search runs a loop: interpret the question, retrieve candidate pages, have a language model write an answer from them, and attach citations. The retrieve-then-write pattern is [retrieval-augmented generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) (RAG), which Google's [optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) also calls grounding. More in [what RAG is](/blog/what-is-rag).

1. **Interpret and fan out.** The model reads your question and earlier turns, then may spawn several related searches. Google's example: a question about fixing a weedy lawn can fan out into searches like "best herbicides for lawns" and "remove weeds without chemicals".
2. **Decide whether to search.** Assistants typically choose per question. [OpenAI's API documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search) says the model can choose to search or not; [Gemini's](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search) says it determines whether a Google Search can improve the answer. With no search, the answer comes from what the model learned in training.
3. **Retrieve.** The engine pulls candidate pages from an index: Google's Search index, or pages collected by search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot.
4. **Write.** The model reads the retrieved passages and composes one answer.
5. **Cite.** The answer links back to sources. Links are pointers, not proof: a [2023 human evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848) of four generative search engines found only 51.5% of generated sentences fully supported by their citations. Those products have changed since, so check what AI says about you.

Retrieval is the gate: a page the engine never retrieves cannot be quoted. For Google, a page must be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet, the site must not have opted out in Search Console, and [Google says](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) no special schema, markup or AI-specific files are needed.

## Where do people meet AI search today?

In six places and three shapes: chat assistants that can search (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude), [answer engines](/blog/what-is-an-answer-engine) built around search (Perplexity), and AI layers on a search engine (Google and [Microsoft's Copilot Search](https://blogs.bing.com/search/April-2025/Introducing-Copilot-Search-in-Bing)). They find pages through different crawlers and indexes, so check each one separately. See the [best AI search engines](/blog/best-ai-search-engines) compared.

| Surface | How it finds web pages (vendor documentation, October 2026) |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT search | [OAI-SearchBot](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) surfaces sites in ChatGPT's search features; ChatGPT-User may fetch a page when a user asks |
| Google [AI Overviews](/blog/what-is-an-ai-overview) and [AI Mode](/blog/how-to-rank-in-google-ai-mode) | Google's Search index, [one flow since May 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/); pages must be indexed and snippet-eligible, and the site not opted out |
| Gemini | Google Search grounding, per its [API documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search): the model decides whether to search and cites sources |
| Perplexity | [PerplexityBot](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) surfaces and links sites in results; Perplexity-User fetches pages on request |
| Claude | Searches [when applicable](https://claude.com/blog/web-search) with direct citations; [Claude-SearchBot](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler) indexes content for search |

The practical rule: confirm you have not opted out by accident. OpenAI says sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not appear in ChatGPT search answers, and Anthropic says disabling Claude-SearchBot may reduce visibility in search results. Google's equivalent is the [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024) in Search Console, which can exclude a site from AI Overviews and AI Mode. We did not test these crawlers hands-on.

## Is AI search replacing traditional search?

No, and "replace" is the wrong verb. Google reports record query volume while ChatGPT keeps growing, so people are adding AI to how they search, not leaving search. What AI search replaces is the shortlist: the engine now builds the comparison you used to build by opening links.

- **Google, 2026:** in June it reported [over 2.5 billion monthly active users for AI Overviews](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/) and over one billion for AI Mode. In May it said [queries hit an all-time high](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/), and in July that AI features send [billions of clicks to websites every week](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/).
- **ChatGPT, February 2026:** OpenAI said [900 million people use it weekly](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users), up from 800 million in October.
- **Clicks, Pew, March 2025:** [on Google pages with an AI summary](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, against 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary in 1%.

The median AI summary in Pew's data ran 67 words, which leaves room for a few names, not ten. AEOeye's audit records (127 audits, 892 engine answers to buyer questions, June to September 2026) point the same way: the average answer [named 2.9 competing brands](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json), and when the audited brand was named and ranked it came first in 71.4% of 147 cases. These are self-selected brands with repeat audits and unequal engine samples: a feel for how short the shortlist is, not a market statistic.

## What does AI search mean for your brand?

The contest moved from ranking on a results page to being named in an answer. The buyer meets a handful of brands, often inside one conversation, and a brand outside that handful never enters the discussion.

A typical thread shows why:

1. "What is a project management tool?" Awareness, no brand needed yet.
2. "Which ones suit a five-person design team?" A shortlist forms.
3. "Which of those has the best free plan?" The field narrows.
4. "Just tell me which one to pick." The assistant names one, maybe two.

In classic search that is four queries and four results pages. In a thread, awareness, shortlist and decision happen in one place, and Google now lets people ask a follow-up from an AI Overview and [continue in AI Mode with their context kept](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/).

![Man at a laptop in a modern office browsing an online marketplace](https://images.pexels.com/photos/1604024/pexels-photo-1604024.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

Five moves:

- **Test like a buyer.** Write 10 to 20 questions a buyer asks before choosing a product like yours, plus the follow-ups. Run them through every engine and note whether you are named first, among rivals, low or not at all. Repeat monthly; answers shift without a changelog. [AEOeye's free audit](/) runs three unbranded buyer questions through ChatGPT; the $29 one-time full report covers 10 questions across five engines.
- **Clear the gate.** Confirm no robots.txt or firewall rule blocks the search crawlers above ([Perplexity says](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) a firewall may need an allowlist) and the Search generative AI control includes your site.
- **Fewer, stronger pages.** [Google's guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says separate content for every variation of how people search, fan-out queries included, made mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses, violates its scaled content abuse policy. We merged 165 duplicate-intent pages of our own into 37 on October 3: one strong page per question beats several thin ones.
- **Be worth quoting.** The same guide says unique, non-commodity content will likely matter more than anything else it suggests, and that [llms.txt](https://llmstxt.org/) files, content chunking and AI-only rewrites are not needed for Google. Write reader-first: lead each section with the direct answer in sentences that still work when quoted alone, use specific facts, cover the follow-ups buyers ask next, and name your brand the same way everywhere.
- **Read the first-party reports.** [Search Console's Generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) shows impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, country, device and date, and Bing Webmaster Tools [reports Copilot citations](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) (in preview). For other engines, test by hand.
