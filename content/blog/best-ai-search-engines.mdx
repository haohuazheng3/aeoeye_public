The best AI search engines in 2026 are Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT search, the Gemini app, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Claude with web search. For a brand, the sharper question is which of them to prepare for. Facts below come from each vendor's own documentation, checked on 3 October 2026, and usage figures are company-reported.

## What are the best AI search engines in 2026?

Six products do AI search at scale. None wins every job, so we rank them by what they are best at, not by model quality. That is what decides which one your buyers open. For how retrieval works under the hood, see [what AI search is](/blog/what-is-ai-search).

- **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode: best for reach.** In its [Q2 2026 earnings remarks](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/) (22 July 2026), Google said AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly active users after going global in October 2025. It also said it had merged AI Overviews and AI Mode into one Search experience.
- **ChatGPT search: best for open-ended decisions.** OpenAI says ChatGPT now has [1.2 billion weekly users](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model/) (TechCrunch, 29 September 2026). That counts all ChatGPT use, not only searches.
- **Gemini app: best inside Google's ecosystem.** Google [announced](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/one-billion-monthly-users/) on 11 August 2026 that the app passed 1 billion monthly users.
- **Perplexity: best for sourced research.** It is built around linked sources, and its crawler exists to surface and link sites in its results. We found no first-party usage figure, so we print none.
- **Microsoft Copilot: best for measurability.** It is the only engine here whose maker gives site owners a citation report (more below).
- **Claude with web search: best if you already work in Claude.** It searches when a question needs current information and answers with direct citations.

## How do the AI search engines compare?

They differ in two ways that matter to a brand: where answers get their sources, and which crawler you can allow or block. Where a vendor does not document something, the table says so rather than guessing.

| Engine | Where sources come from | Crawler or token to allow | Reach (company-reported) | Your own data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews / AI Mode | Google Search index; page must be indexed and snippet-eligible | Normal Google Search crawling | AI Mode 1B+ monthly (Jul 2026) | Search Console, inside Web performance |
| Gemini app | Google Search index grounding | Google-Extended token | 1B+ monthly (Aug 2026) | No dedicated report found |
| ChatGPT search | OpenAI's search crawler; full source list not published | OAI-SearchBot | 1.2B weekly, all of ChatGPT (Sep 2026) | No dedicated report found |
| Perplexity | Its own crawler's results | PerplexityBot | No first-party figure found | No dedicated report found |
| Microsoft Copilot | Bing's search results (Copilot Search is part of Bing) | Bing's crawler | None found | Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance |
| Claude | Web search; source list not documented | Claude-SearchBot | None found | No dedicated report found |

Google's [AI features guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page needs to be indexed and snippet-eligible, with no additional technical requirements and no special schema. Its [crawler documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers) adds a detail most people miss: the Google-Extended token governs not only Gemini training but also grounding in Gemini Apps. Blocking it is not a free decision.

## Which AI search engine is best for which job?

Pick by task, not by brand loyalty. A citation-first engine suits one-off sourced facts, an assistant that keeps context suits multi-step decisions, and the search bar you already use suits quick lookups. This is our view, not a benchmark.

- **Verify a fact or compare vendors:** Perplexity or Google AI Mode, because you can open the cited pages in one click.
- **Plan something with five follow-ups:** ChatGPT, where the conversation is the product.
- **Everyday and local lookups:** Google, because AI Overviews appear inside the page you were already on.
- **Work in Google apps:** Gemini.
- **Audit who is citing your site:** Copilot, because Bing gives you the data. In February 2026 Microsoft launched, in public preview, [AI Performance in Bing Webmaster Tools](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview), which shows citation counts, cited pages and the grounding queries the AI used across Copilot and Bing's AI summaries.

## Which AI search engines should your brand prioritize?

Prioritize Google's surfaces and Gemini first, ChatGPT second, and Perplexity, Copilot and Claude third unless your buyers are known to use them. The first two groups share a lot of work, and the order is a default you should override with buyer-question tests.

1. **Google AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini.** They draw on one index, so one body of work serves three surfaces. The stakes are clicks: in a [Pew Research study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) of 900 U.S. adults (March 2025), people clicked a result link in 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% without. Being cited inside the answer is now part of the job.
2. **ChatGPT.** It has the largest assistant audience, and its search crawler is documented. OpenAI's [crawler page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) says sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers.
3. **Perplexity, Copilot, Claude.** Treat these as targeted bets. Perplexity and ChatGPT cite quite different sources; our [Perplexity vs ChatGPT comparison](/compare/perplexity-vs-chatgpt-for-brand-visibility) and [Perplexity playbook](/blog/how-to-rank-in-perplexity) cover that.

Our position: do not build an engine-by-engine strategy before the basics work. Indexable pages, a consistent description of your brand across the web, and independent mentions help in every engine. Our [engine prioritization answer](/answers/which-ai-engine-to-optimize-for-first) walks through the decision.

## Does blocking AI crawlers keep brands out of AI search?

For brands, rarely. In AEOeye's crawler access study (fetched 3 October 2026, 384 sites with a readable or absent robots.txt), only 16 sites (4.2%) blocked OAI-SearchBot, 24 (6.2%) blocked Claude-SearchBot and 35 (9.1%) blocked PerplexityBot. The per-site data is in our [public CSV](/resources/ai-crawler-access-study-2026-10.csv).

The split matters more than the average. Of 193 SaaS brands, 1 blocked any AI retrieval bot; of 38 news publishers, 27 did. Publishers and review sites are the sources AI engines lean on, so they are the ones most likely to be restricting access. The practical risk for a brand is not its own robots.txt but being absent from the pages the engines read.

Limits: it is a convenience sample of well-known sites, one fetch each, main host only, and robots.txt cannot show CDN-level blocking. Still, the 8.8% of SaaS homepages and 27.3% of e-commerce homepages that challenged our declared research crawler are worth a look in your own bot-protection logs. The full method is on our [AI crawler directory](/blog/ai-crawler-user-agent-directory).

One nuance on user-driven fetchers: OpenAI says ChatGPT-User actions are user-initiated, so robots.txt rules may not apply, and Perplexity says [Perplexity-User generally ignores robots.txt](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots). Anthropic says its [bots honor robots.txt](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler), and that disabling Claude-SearchBot may reduce visibility in Claude's search results.

## How do you check your visibility in each AI search engine?

Ask each engine the same unbranded buyer questions, log what comes back, then confirm with first-party reports. This takes about an hour and beats any assumption about where you appear.

1. Write 5 buyer questions that do not contain your brand, such as "best [category] for [use case]".
2. Ask each engine and record whether you are named, in what position, which competitors appear, and which URLs are cited.
3. Open Google Search Console: AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic is counted in the Web performance report, not in a separate report.
4. Open Bing Webmaster Tools and read AI Performance; Microsoft notes the grounding-query data is a sample.
5. Check robots.txt and your CDN logs for OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot.
