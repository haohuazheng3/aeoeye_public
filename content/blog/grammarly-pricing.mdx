## What does Grammarly cost right now?

Grammarly Free costs $0, while the official plan presentation currently shows Pro from $12 per month. The meaningful comparison is not $0 versus $12; it is basic correctness versus a recurring editing workflow.

As of August 29, 2026, the official plans page displays Pro at $12 per month in its annual presentation. Grammarly’s official suite support separately lists $30 per member per month when billed monthly and $12 per member per month when billed annually, so verify the final checkout screen before paying because packaging can change. ([official plans](https://www.grammarly.com/plans); [suite pricing support](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/40709437438733-Introducing-the-Superhuman-suite))

The current Grammarly pricing question is therefore about usage. If you only want spelling, grammar, and basic tone guidance, Free covers the core job. If you need repeated rewrites, fluency improvements, plagiarism checks, or AI-text detection, Pro is the relevant upgrade.

## What does each Grammarly plan include?

Free covers spelling, grammar, tone, and 100 AI prompts; Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, fluency, plagiarism and AI-text detection, unlimited personalized suggestions, and 2,000 AI prompts.

| Plan | Who it fits | Main coverage | Limit that triggers an upgrade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Occasional writers | Spelling, grammar, tone, 100 AI prompts | You need advanced rewrites or more prompts |
| Pro | Frequent individual writers | Full-sentence rewrites, fluency, plagiarism and AI-text detection, unlimited personalized suggestions, 2,000 AI prompts | You need organization-wide controls |
| Enterprise | Organizations | Team-level product and administration needs | Individual plans no longer fit the workflow |

The largest practical difference in Grammarly pricing plans is not the number of buttons. It is whether the tool helps you finish consequential writing with fewer editing passes. Review the official feature comparison for the current package rather than assuming older Premium or Business descriptions still apply. ([plans comparison](https://www.grammarly.com/plans); [product naming support](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/40709437438733-Introducing-the-Superhuman-suite))

## Is annual Grammarly pricing actually cheaper?

Annual billing materially lowers the effective monthly price, but it also commits a year of spend before you know whether Grammarly will become a habit.

Using the current official suite-support snapshot, annual billing is $12 × 12 = $144 per member for a year. Monthly billing is $30 × 12 = $360 over twelve months. That is simple arithmetic from the listed rates, not a promise that every checkout screen or future package will use the same terms. ([current suite terms](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/40709437438733-Introducing-the-Superhuman-suite))

The annual option is financially attractive only if you expect sustained use. A lower effective monthly rate does not help if you stop opening Grammarly after a few weeks. Check whether the checkout is presenting a standalone Pro subscription or a suite-related package, then confirm the renewal amount and billing interval.

## When is Grammarly Free enough?

Free is enough when the job is catching surface errors in ordinary email and documents; paying makes sense only when suggestions replace repeatable editing time or reduce meaningful publishing risk.

Free is a sensible choice for casual correspondence, internal notes, school drafts, and writing that already receives a human review. Its tone guidance and core correctness checks address the mistakes most users notice first, without creating a recurring subscription obligation. ([Free plan details](https://www.grammarly.com/plans))

Pro becomes easier to justify when you write frequently under deadlines and regularly need sentence-level rewrites or fluency help. It is also more relevant when plagiarism or AI-text detection is part of a documented review process. Those are usage thresholds, not universal reasons to upgrade.

For work involving [AI content writing](/blog/ai-content-writing) or [AI content optimization](/blog/ai-content-optimization), Pro may support the editing stage, but it does not replace editorial judgment, factual review, or a publishing strategy.

## What costs and privacy choices are easy to miss?

Renewal timing and data-training settings matter as much as the sticker price because the subscription auto-renews and individual product-improvement training is on by default but can be switched off.

Grammarly’s billing guidance explains that subscriptions renew according to their billing cycle. Before starting, note the renewal date, the plan duration, and the cancellation process shown in your account. Recurring-subscription guidance generally makes cancellation review an important part of informed purchasing; that principle does not imply wrongdoing by Grammarly. ([subscription billing support](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000090112-How-am-I-charged-for-my-subscription); [recurring subscription guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule))

The product-improvement and training setting is another decision point. Grammarly’s support documentation says individual training is on by default and provides steps to switch it off. If your writing is sensitive, review that setting before regular use and align it with your organization’s data-handling rules. ([training controls](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/25555503115277-Product-Improvement-and-Training-Control))

Trials may be offered periodically, but they are not something to assume will always be available. Pro is the current name for the paid individual tier, replacing older Premium terminology. ([trial support](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033548272-Does-Grammarly-offer-a-trial))

## How should a team decide before buying?

Run a seven-day workload test: count documents, accepted rewrites, avoided review cycles, and high-stakes checks instead of assigning value to an abstract feature list.

For seven days, record how often Free blocks progress. Count the number of documents where a full-sentence rewrite would save time, how many suggestions you accept, and whether plagiarism or AI-text detection is actually required. Also record whether the same benefit could be achieved through an existing human or editorial process.

A team can then compare observed usage with the annual commitment. If advanced features appear only once or twice, stay with Free. If they support recurring work across important documents, estimate whether the avoided editing effort justifies $144 per member on the current annual snapshot. Do not treat that estimate as a guaranteed return.

For an organization evaluating broader content operations, connect this decision to [SEO pricing](/blog/seo-pricing), but keep the buying question narrow: does Grammarly reduce a recurring writing bottleneck?

## Is Grammarly Pro worth it?

For frequent professional writers, Pro can be a rational editing cost; for occasional users, Free plus a human final read is usually the better default.

Choose Pro when most of these statements are true:

- You write important documents every week.
- Full-sentence rewrites or fluency suggestions repeatedly save review time.
- You will use plagiarism or AI-text detection as part of a real workflow.
- The 100-prompt Free allowance is regularly restrictive.
- You have checked renewal terms and privacy settings.

Stay with Free when correctness is the main need, writing volume is irregular, or a human reviewer already handles the difficult decisions. Students should also begin with Free unless their workload consistently requires Pro’s advanced checks; academic policies may restrict or shape how AI-related features are used.
