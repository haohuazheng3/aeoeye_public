## What does Promptwatch cost in 2026?

Promptwatch’s published USD monthly prices are $95 for Essential, $245 for Professional, and $579 for Business, verified from the official pricing page on September 23, 2026. All three figures are monthly plan prices, not a calculated annual equivalent. The page offers monthly and annual billing views, but the accessible official copy does not state a discount for annual billing.

The official page also advertises a seven-day trial. Before starting one, confirm the checkout screen’s billing interval and renewal language for your account. The terms say subscriptions auto-renew and are generally non-refundable except where required. Read the [Promptwatch Terms](https://promptwatch.com/terms-and-conditions) before entering payment details, especially if your team needs a cancellation or refund policy that differs from those defaults.

## What does each Promptwatch plan include?

Essential is the entry point at $95 monthly: 50 prompts, 6,000 responses, 500 agent credits, one project, and four models. It is the clearest fit when one brand or project needs a bounded prompt library and a relatively small operating surface. The important constraint is that its five allowances move independently: a response-heavy workflow can reach its response limit without using all 50 prompts, while an agent-credit workflow can consume a different allowance first.

Professional costs $245 monthly and raises the limits to 150 prompts, 18,000 responses, 1,500 agent credits, two projects, and five AEO articles. That combination matters for a team comparing or operating more than one project, or for a workflow that needs a defined article allowance alongside monitoring. The plan’s value is not simply “more of everything”; it changes the project and article capacity as well as the prompt and response ceilings.

Business costs $579 monthly and lists 350 prompts, 42,000 responses, 2,500 agent credits, five projects, and 10 AEO articles. It is the largest published tier on the official pricing page. Choose it only when the additional projects, prompts, responses, credits, or article allowance map to a repeatable operating plan. A higher ceiling is not evidence that your organization will obtain better AI answers or more accurate visibility measurements.

## How should you calculate Promptwatch cost per unit?

Unit calculations are useful for planning, but they are not the product’s complete economics. The simplest example is Professional at $245 per month with 150 configured prompts. Dividing the monthly price by the prompt allowance gives:

```text
$245 ÷ 150 prompts = $1.6333 per configured prompt per month
```

The same Professional plan has 18,000 responses:

```text
$245 ÷ 18,000 responses = $0.01361 per response
```

These are planning ratios, not separate pay-as-you-go prices. They assume you use the full allowance evenly and assign the entire subscription price to one resource. Neither calculation captures the value or consumption of 1,500 agent credits, two projects, or five AEO articles. It also does not tell you whether your workflow will reach a prompt ceiling, response ceiling, or credit ceiling first.

Use a constraint sheet before comparing vendors. Record, for one month:

1. The number of distinct prompts you will configure.
2. The responses you expect to collect or process.
3. Agent credits your workflow may consume.
4. Projects that need separate organization.
5. AEO articles required during the billing period.

Then compare each estimate with the corresponding Promptwatch allowance. A plan that looks cheaper per response may still be the wrong choice if it cannot hold your prompt set or project count. Conversely, purchasing a larger plan solely for unused response capacity can create avoidable cost.

## Which Promptwatch plan fits your workflow?

Select Essential when a single project, 50 prompts, 6,000 responses, and 500 agent credits cover the month’s planned work. Select Professional when you need 150 prompts, 18,000 responses, 1,500 credits, two projects, or five AEO articles. Select Business when the workflow genuinely requires up to 350 prompts, 42,000 responses, 2,500 credits, five projects, or 10 articles.

The decision should be repeatable. Write down expected monthly volumes, the first constraint likely to bind, and the consequence of exceeding it. Revisit those assumptions after the seven-day trial only if you can verify the account’s actual limits and your team’s usage.
