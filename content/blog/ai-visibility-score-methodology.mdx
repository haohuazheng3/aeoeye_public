The AEOeye AI visibility score is a 0-100 number. Seventy-five percent is how often and how prominently AI engines name your brand when buyers ask unbranded questions; 25% is eight checks on your homepage. This page mirrors the scoring code we run, with every constant left in.

## What does the AEOeye AI visibility score measure?

It measures whether AI engines name you, how high, and how favourably when someone asks a buying question without mentioning you, plus a smaller check of whether your site is easy for machines to read. It is not a traffic estimate or a domain-authority number. For the concept itself, see [what AI visibility is](/answers/what-is-ai-visibility).

**Overall = 0.75 × engine component + 0.25 × site component**, rounded and clamped to 0-100.

- **Engine component:** the average of the engine scores that actually ran.
- **Site component:** the average of eight homepage checks.
- **Missing half:** if only one component exists, it becomes the score unweighted. A brand-name-only audit has no site to scan, so its score is the engine component alone.
- **Engines that did not run:** inactive or failed engines are left out of the average, never counted as zero. The free audit shows the four non-ChatGPT engines as inactive.

## Which questions are asked, and to which engines?

The free audit asks 3 buyer questions to ChatGPT only. The $29 full report asks 10 questions to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google AI and Perplexity. The free 3 questions and their ChatGPT answers are kept as they were; 7 new questions are added.

- **Product-selection questions.** A language model reads your brand (and homepage, if you gave a URL), picks a category, and writes questions like "best X for Y", "X alternatives" or "A vs B" with named brands, each answerable with a shortlist of named products.
- **Asked blind.** Any question containing your brand name or domain is dropped by code. A navigational query measures name recognition, not recommendation.
- **Category pinned.** Re-auditing a domain reuses its earlier category, so questions don't drift between runs.
- **Default-tier engines.** Each engine is tested at the tier a free user gets. ChatGPT may search the web or not, [as OpenAI's guide says the model decides](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search); the other four run with live web search on, and Google AI is read from Google's AI Mode result.
- **One answer per question per engine per run.** ChatGPT also gets a short instruction to give a decisive shortlist of 4-6 named options, which makes answers rankable but is not how every real user phrases things.

## How is each answer scored?

A language model reads each answer and records whether your brand is mentioned, its 1-based rank among the recommended options, its sentiment, and which category competitors appear. An unmentioned brand scores 0. A mentioned brand scores **rankFactor × sentiment multiplier**.

- **rankFactor:** 1.00 at rank 1, minus 0.12 per position, floored at 0.30. Mentioned without a clear rank: 0.60.
- **Sentiment multiplier:** positive 1.00, neutral 0.85, negative 0.50.

Per-answer score out of 100:

| Rank | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 85 | 50 |
| 2 | 88 | 74.8 | 44 |
| 3 | 76 | 64.6 | 38 |
| 4 | 64 | 54.4 | 32 |
| 5 | 52 | 44.2 | 26 |
| 6 | 40 | 34 | 20 |
| 7 or lower | 30 | 25.5 | 15 |
| Mentioned, no rank | 60 | 51 | 30 |

A neutral first place (85) scores below a positive second place (88), so tone costs more than one rank step. The steep top-slot reward echoes the exponentially position-decayed citation weight in the [GEO research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), but our linear constants are our own judgement, and we have not published a validation of them.

In AEOeye's own audit records, 71.4% of 147 ranked mentions were first place, and 144 of 157 mentions were positive. Those audits are self-selected with unequal engine samples ([aggregates](/resources/aeoeye-recommendation-benchmark-2026-09.json)), so read them as direction only: being named at all is the hard part.

![Yellow tape measure and rulers showing millimetre, centimetre and inch markings.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/19585984/pexels-photo-19585984.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

Each engine's visibility score is the average of its per-answer scores × 100, rounded. Mention rate is reported beside it: answers naming you ÷ answers asked. A low rate means you are rarely named; a decent rate with a low score means you are named late or lukewarmly. The fixes differ, so we show both.

## What do the eight site signals check?

Eight homepage checks, each scored pass = 1, warn = 0.5, fail = 0, then averaged and multiplied by 100. For the score we fetch your homepage plus /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml and /llms.txt, nothing else.

| Signal | Pass | Warn | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structured data | JSON-LD with Organization, LocalBusiness or Brand | JSON-LD without those | No JSON-LD |
| llms.txt | /llms.txt returns a successful response | Otherwise | Never |
| FAQ content | FAQPage/Question markup, or "FAQ" text in the HTML | Otherwise | Never |
| Meta description | Present | Missing | Never |
| Crawlable | /robots.txt reachable | robots.txt missing | Homepage is noindex |
| XML sitemap | /sitemap.xml reachable | Otherwise | Never |
| Content depth | 500+ words | 200-499 words | Under 200 |
| Product/service markup | Product, Service or Offer in JSON-LD | Otherwise | Never |

Three consequences matter:

1. **A floor of 31.** Only three signals can fail, so the site component cannot drop below 31. A site that does everything wrong still adds about 8 overall points.
2. **"Crawlable" is narrow.** Despite the label, it does not test individual AI bots. A missing robots.txt is a warning, not a block: [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html) lets crawlers access everything when the file is unavailable.
3. **llms.txt is worth 3.1 points, and we think it is overrated.** It is a [proposal at llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org/), and we have not seen it move citations ([why](/blog/llms-txt-is-overrated)). It stays as a cheap hygiene check. Organization markup, by contrast, [helps Google disambiguate your organization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization).

## How do engine scores and site signals combine?

Take the mean of engine scores, take the mean of site checks, then weight 75/25. Every engine counts equally. Our view: any usage weighting would be a guess, so if your buyers live in one engine, read that engine's row.

An illustrative example, not a customer:

1. On ChatGPT, 4 of 10 questions name the brand: #1 positive (1.00), #2 positive (0.88), #3 neutral (0.646), unranked positive (0.60), six absent. Mean 0.3126, so visibility score **31**, mention rate **40%**.
2. Claude, Gemini, Google AI and Perplexity score 45, 12, 6 and 20. Engine component: (31+45+12+6+20) ÷ 5 = 22.8, rounded to **23**.
3. Six signals pass and two warn: 7 ÷ 8 = 87.5, rounded to **88**.
4. Overall: 0.75 × 23 + 0.25 × 88 = 39.25, rounded to **39**. Grade F, one point from D.

Competitors and gaps sit beside the score and never feed it. For each competitor we count mentions, questions it appears in, and **wins vs you**: questions where no engine named you but it was recommended. A competitor gap is such a question, high-intent ones first. The visibility ladder, recommendations and the paid 5-layer SEO audit are separate too.

## What is a good score?

A good score beats the brands AI names instead of you, on the questions your buyers ask. Grades are fixed cutoffs, not percentiles; we have not published a distribution of customer scores, so no letter means "better than most".

| Grade | Overall score | Gauge colour |
|---|---|---|
| A | 85-100 | Green |
| B | 70-84 | Green |
| C | 55-69 | Amber |
| D | 40-54 | Amber |
| F | 0-39 | Red |

The 75/25 split decides what each grade demands. With a perfect site (25 points), you need an engine component of 80 for A, 60 for B, 40 for C and 20 for D. With a half-passing site (12.5 points), roughly 97, 77, 57 and 37. An engine component of 40 means, for example, first-place positive mentions in four of ten answers.

## What are the score's limits?

The score is a repeatable snapshot, not a verdict. Treat one run as noisy.

- **Answers vary.** In SparkToro's study (600 volunteers, 2,961 runs), there was [under a 1-in-100 chance](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) that ChatGPT or Google's AI returned the same brand list twice. We take one answer per question per engine.
- **Three questions is thin.** In the free audit one answer moving from absent to a positive first place adds 33 to the ChatGPT score, about 25 overall points. The full report's 50 answers move the score by about 1.5 points each.
- **The judge is a model.** LLM judges can show [position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) while agreeing with humans over 80% of the time. Ours reads the first 1,800 characters of each answer, and a failed judgement counts as not mentioned, so errors lean low.
- **Questions are per brand.** Different brands get different generated questions, so cross-brand comparisons are weak.
- **Site signals read one page.** Your homepage.
