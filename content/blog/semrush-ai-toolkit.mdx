## What is the Semrush AI Toolkit?

As of this writing, the Semrush AI Toolkit — sometimes labeled the Semrush AI visibility toolkit in Semrush's own materials — is a paid add-on inside the Semrush platform that reports on how AI engines talk about your brand, part of Semrush's broader push from traditional SEO tracking into AI search. You'll also see this shorthanded as "Semrush AEO," with AEO standing for answer engine optimization: the practice of optimizing for what AI systems say about you, rather than only where you rank in traditional search results.

Semrush built its business on keyword rankings, backlink profiles, and site audits: the metrics that mattered when ten blue links were the whole game. As more buyers start their research in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews instead of typing a query into a search box, that world is expanding, and Semrush is extending its existing platform to cover it rather than ceding the ground to newcomers.

The job the toolkit is built for is straightforward: give existing Semrush users a way to check AI visibility and brand performance in AI answers without leaving the platform they already use for everything else. That concept — usually called AI visibility, or answer engine visibility — has its own mechanics worth understanding on their own terms, which we [cover in more detail elsewhere](/blog/measuring-ai-visibility). For a Semrush shop, the pitch is obvious: one login, one invoice, one dashboard to open in the morning instead of five.

## The all-in-one tradeoff

Platform suites like Semrush win on data breadth and having everything under one login — but bundling AI visibility into an existing suite raises real evaluation questions, and they're worth answering before you assume the bundled option is the right fit rather than just the convenient one.

None of this is a knock on Semrush specifically. It's the same set of questions worth asking about any AI module attached to a platform that was originally built for something else — including tools in our own category. Three matter most:

- **Depth vs. breadth.** Is this an AI module layered on top of an SEO platform — one more tab next to keyword rankings and backlinks — or a tool built from the ground up to do a single job: show you AI visibility? Neither answer is automatically wrong, but they produce different depth on the thing you're actually trying to see.
- **Scores or actual answers.** Does it show you the verbatim text an AI engine generated when someone asked about your category — the real sentences, the real competitor names — or does it compress that into an aggregated visibility score? A score tells you direction. The actual answer tells you why.
- **Add-on pricing.** Is AI visibility included in the tier you already pay for, or is it a separate paid add-on stacked on top of an existing subscription? That changes what "just turning it on" really costs.

## Questions to ask before paying for it

Before you add this to your Semrush bill, get a demo and ask five specific questions — the answers separate a genuinely useful feature from a repackaged dashboard.

1. **Which engines does it actually cover?** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Claude behave differently and get asked different things by different audiences. Ask for the exact list, not "AI engines" as a category.
2. **Which prompts generate the data?** Generic, templated prompts built to work for every customer will not reflect how your specific buyers actually phrase their questions.
3. **Can I use my own buyer questions?** This is the one that matters most. Category-level prompts tell you what a hypothetical shopper might ask. Your actual buyer questions — pulled from support tickets, sales calls, and search queries — tell you what your real shoppers ask.
4. **Do I see the verbatim answer, word for word?** Ask them to show it live, not a screenshot pulled from a slide deck.
5. **How often does the data refresh?** AI answers shift as models update and as the underlying web they draw from changes. Monthly data on a channel that moves weekly tells you where things stood, not where they stand now.
