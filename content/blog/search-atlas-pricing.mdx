Search Atlas lists Starter at $99, Growth at $199, Pro at $399, and Agency at $999 per month. The right comparison is not price per feature; it is cost per actively managed site after included OTTO projects, seats, credits, and likely top-ups.

SEO platforms combine several products: keyword research, content workflows, auditing, publishing, local SEO, reporting, and AI visibility. Search Atlas follows that model. Its headline price is only the first input.

The practical question is whether your team will use enough of the platform to replace existing tools and manual processes. A $99 plan can be expensive for one lightly managed project, while $399 may be efficient if it replaces several subscriptions and creates repeatable workflows. Read the paired [Search Atlas review](/blog/search-atlas-review) before treating automation capacity as value.

## How much does Search Atlas cost in 2026?

Public monthly tiers run from $99 to $999, with Starter, Growth, Pro, and Agency aimed at progressively larger site and client portfolios.

| Plan | Monthly price | Simple annualized price |
|---|---:|---:|
| Starter | $99 | $1,188 |
| Growth | $199 | $2,388 |
| Pro | $399 | $4,788 |
| Agency | $999 | $11,988 |

Growth costs about twice Starter, Pro about twice Growth, and Agency roughly 2.5 times Pro. Those jumps make sense only when added capacity is actively used.

Search Atlas presents a broad SEO operating platform rather than a single-purpose tracker. Its [features](https://searchatlas.com/features/) include research, content, analysis, local workflows, reporting, and automation. Review current scope before treating it as a direct replacement for one point tool.

Annualized figures exclude taxes, optional usage, implementation labor, and capacity beyond the plan. They also assume monthly pricing remains the relevant basis. Procurement should confirm any annual commitment or promotional terms on the [current pricing page](https://searchatlas.com/pricing/).

## What changes between the four plans?

The meaningful changes are included user seats, OTTO SEO projects, LLM coverage, authority-building allowances, white-label capability, and quotas—not access to a completely different core product.

| Plan | Included seats | Included OTTO projects | Best interpreted as |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Starter | 1 | 1 | One operator and one active site |
| Growth | 3 | 2 | Small team managing multiple sites |
| Pro | 5 | 4 | Higher-volume collaborative operation |
| Agency | Confirm current entitlement | Confirm current entitlement | Large portfolio needing agency capacity |

Starter is not merely a discounted Pro. It is structurally constrained around one user and one OTTO project. That may be right for a consultant validating a workflow but poor for a business needing separate client access, multiple domains, or review roles.

Growth adds collaboration room and another OTTO project. The jump from one to three seats can matter more than raw project count if strategists, writers, and account managers need access. Pro increases project capacity and is positioned for broader LLM coverage, authority-building allowances, and white-label delivery.

Agency should be treated as a scale tier, not a promise that every allowance is unlimited. Do not infer its exact seat, project, or credit quotas from the name. Ask for the entitlement schedule. The platform and its published [feature set](https://searchatlas.com/features/) evolve, so written confirmation should control the decision.

## How do OTTO site activations affect the bill?

OTTO is priced around active sites and usage allowances, so an agency should divide the monthly total by deployed client sites and add realistic credit top-ups before comparing alternatives.

An OTTO project is more useful as a unit of deployed capacity than a checkbox. Starter is $99 per included project. Growth is $99.50 per included project across two, and Pro is $99.75 across four.

That parity is revealing: higher tiers are not automatically cheaper per project. Their value comes from collaboration, allowances, broader workflows, and agency features. If you buy Pro and use one OTTO project, you pay for unused capacity.

Calculate three versions of cost:

1. **Contract cost per included project:** subscription divided by included OTTO projects.
2. **Realized cost per deployed site:** subscription divided by sites actually active.
3. **Fully loaded cost per client:** subscription, top-ups, labor, review, and delivery divided by clients served.

The second and third belong in a margin model. Search Atlas positions [OTTO SEO](https://searchatlas.com/otto-seo/) as a system for applying work across websites, so deployment volume and operating process matter as much as the label.

## Which hidden budget variables matter?

Top-up points, Hyperdrive credits, additional sites, onboarding labor, review time, and white-label delivery can matter more than the headline tier after the first month.

Usage-based allowances deserve attention. A plan can look comprehensive while requiring more purchases when a team runs more content generation, authority actions, or AI analysis than the quota supports.

Ask about:

- Included point or credit balances
- Hyperdrive credit consumption and replenishment
- Additional sites or OTTO activations
- Whether unused credits expire or roll over
- Limits on content, research, AI, or authority actions
- Manual review requirements
- White-label branding and client access
- Onboarding or implementation charges

Labor is another hidden variable. Automation does not eliminate strategy, fact checking, approvals, technical QA, or client communication. Estimate review time honestly. The relevant comparison is total operating cost before and after adoption.

Cancellation, suspension, payment disputes, and data access also affect risk. Read the [terms of service](https://searchatlas.com/terms-of-service/) alongside pricing before a large commitment.

## Which plan fits each buyer?

Starter fits a solo operator testing one site; Growth fits a small multi-site team; Pro fits higher-volume operations needing broader LLM and white-label support; Agency is for portfolios large enough to use its scale.

Starter is sensible when one person owns the workflow and one website is the clear priority. It is the easiest tier to evaluate without unused capacity.

Growth fits when two sites are active or three people need access. Extra seats prevent credential sharing and make review workflows practical.

Pro fits a growing consultancy or in-house SEO operation with several active sites. Choose it because the team will use broader allowances, collaboration, LLM coverage, or white-label functions—not because “Pro” sounds safer.

Agency makes sense only when the client process and site volume consume agency-scale capacity. If most capacity sits unused, a lower plan plus carefully priced additions may be better. When uncertain, model the first 90 days against the current [tier allowances](https://searchatlas.com/pricing/) using actual sites, users, projects, and expected usage.

## How should you compare Search Atlas with point tools?

Compare the cost of the workflows it replaces—research, content, audit, publishing, local, and reporting—while discounting modules your team will not actually adopt.

List every current tool and manual process, then classify each as replaced completely, reduced but still required, complementary, or unused.

Search Atlas may be compelling when consolidation reduces handoffs between research, content, technical recommendations, publishing, and reporting. It may be less compelling when the team already has strong specialist tools and wants one narrow capability.

Do not assign full savings to a module because it exists. If the team keeps its crawler, content editor, rank tracker, analytics, or reporting system, those costs remain. A feature has little value if nobody owns or knows how to operate it.

For AI visibility, compare engines covered, collection method, auditability, frequency, and whether outputs change decisions. ## What should procurement confirm before checkout?

Confirm billing cadence, included sites and seats, top-up rates, unused-credit treatment, cancellation terms, export access, and the exact trial-to-paid transition in writing.

Request a plan-specific entitlement summary covering users, OTTO projects, engines, credits, points, sites, reports, and white-label capabilities.

Clarify what happens when a limit is exceeded. Is the account paused, charged automatically, or prompted to buy an add-on? Can administrators set controls? Are credits shared across users or projects? Do unused balances expire?

Agencies should confirm client-facing rights and portability: exports, client access, extra-seat rules, and what happens to project data after cancellation. The decisive question is simple: can you name the sites, people, workflows, and usage that will consume the plan? If not, choose the smallest tier that supports a meaningful evaluation.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest Search Atlas plan?

Starter is the cheapest public monthly plan at $99. It includes one seat and one OTTO project, making it appropriate for a solo operator managing one primary site.

### Is OTTO included?

Yes. Published plans include OTTO projects, but the number varies: Starter one, Growth two, and Pro four. Confirm Agency allowances before purchase.

### How should an agency calculate per-client cost?

Divide total subscription, top-ups, onboarding, review labor, reporting, and account-management costs by the client sites actually deployed.

### Are top-ups part of the subscription?

Do not assume every allowance is unlimited. Confirm included points and credits, top-up prices, expiration, and whether added usage is automatic or approved.

### Which plan is best for one site?

Starter is usually best for one site and one operator. Consider Growth only when extra seats, a second active project, or collaboration justifies the extra $100 monthly.
